# Government of India Act, 1935

The **Government of India Act, 1935** was the most extensive constitutional legislation enacted by the British Parliament for the administration of British India.

It proposed an **All-India Federation**, introduced **provincial autonomy**, **abolished dyarchy in the provinces** and provided for the division of legislative powers between the Centre and provinces.

**Most important distinction:** The Act was only **partially implemented**. **Provincial autonomy came into operation in 1937**, but the **proposed All-India Federation and dyarchy at the Centre were never implemented**.

---

## 1. Background

The Government of India Act, 1935, emerged from a series of constitutional developments.

| Year | Development |
|---|---|
| 1927 | Appointment of the Simon Commission |
| 1930 | First Round Table Conference |
| 1931 | Second Round Table Conference |
| 1932 | Third Round Table Conference |
| 1933 | Publication of the White Paper on Indian constitutional reforms |
| 1934 | Report of the Joint Select Committee of the British Parliament |
| 1935 | Government of India Act passed |

### Important facts

- The Act received Royal Assent on **2 August 1935**.
- It was enacted by the **British Parliament**.
- It **did not establish a fully responsible government at the Centre**.
- It **did not grant Dominion Status to India**.

---

## 2. Principal Features of the Act

| Feature | Provision |
|---|---|
| All-India Federation | Proposed but never established |
| Dyarchy at the Centre | Proposed but never implemented |
| Dyarchy in provinces | Abolished |
| Provincial autonomy | Introduced; implemented in 1937 |
| Federal legislature | Proposed bicameral legislature |
| Provincial legislatures | Bicameralism introduced in six provinces |
| Legislative powers | **Federal**, **Provincial** and **Concurrent Lists** |
| **Residuary legislative powers** | **Governor-General** |
| Separate electorates | Continued and extended |
| Franchise | Expanded but remained limited |
| **Federal Court** | **Established in 1937** |
| **Public Service Commissions** | **Provided for Federal, Provincial and Joint Public Service Commissions** |
| Council of India | Abolished |
| Territorial reorganisation | **Separation of Burma**, Sind, Orissa and Aden |

---

## 3. Proposed All-India Federation

### 3.1 Composition

The proposed federation was to comprise:

1. British Indian provinces.
2. Chief Commissioners' provinces.
3. Indian princely states.

### 3.2 Accession to the Federation

| British Indian provinces | Princely states |
|---|---|
| Inclusion was compulsory | Accession was voluntary |
| No separate accession agreement was required | Accession required an Instrument of Accession |

### 3.3 Conditions for establishing the Federation

The Act prescribed minimum requirements for accession by princely states.

The acceding states had to:

- Be entitled to at least **52 seats in the proposed Council of States**.
- Represent at least **50% of the total population of the Indian states**.

These conditions were not fulfilled.

Consequently, **the proposed All-India Federation never came into existence**.

### Prelims distinction

The Government of India Act, 1935, proposed a federation, but it did not establish a functioning federal government.

---

## 4. Dyarchy at the Centre

The Act proposed introducing dyarchy at the Centre.

Under this arrangement, central subjects were divided into:

1. Reserved subjects.
2. Transferred subjects.

### 4.1 Reserved subjects

The following subjects were reserved:

| Reserved subject | Administration |
|---|---|
| Defence | Governor-General |
| External affairs | Governor-General |
| Ecclesiastical affairs | Governor-General |
| Tribal areas | Governor-General |

The Governor-General was to administer these subjects with the assistance of executive councillors.

These executive councillors were **not responsible to the federal legislature**.

### 4.2 Transferred subjects

- Subjects other than the reserved subjects were generally to be administered by ministers.
- These ministers were to be responsible to the federal legislature.
- They could be removed through loss of legislative confidence.

### Important distinction

**Dyarchy at the Centre was proposed but never implemented**, because the federal provisions did not come into operation.

---

## 5. Provincial Autonomy

Provincial autonomy was one of the most significant provisions actually implemented under the Act.

It came into operation on **1 April 1937**.

### 5.1 Abolition of provincial dyarchy

The Government of India Act, 1919, had introduced dyarchy in the provinces.

Under the 1935 Act:

- Provincial dyarchy was abolished.
- Provincial subjects were placed under ministers responsible to the provincial legislature.
- Provinces received greater administrative and financial powers.

### 5.2 Provincial executive

The provincial executive consisted of:

- Governor.
- Council of Ministers headed by a Premier.

Ministers were responsible to the provincial legislature.

### 5.3 Limitations on provincial autonomy

Provincial autonomy was not absolute.

Governors retained extensive powers, including:

- Discretionary powers.
- Special responsibilities.
- Power to reserve certain Bills for the Governor-General's consideration.
- Power to promulgate ordinances.
- Power to assume provincial administration under specified circumstances.

**Important:** The Governor was not merely a constitutional figurehead.

### 5.4 Centre versus provinces

| Feature | Centre | Provinces |
|---|---|---|
| Dyarchy | Proposed | Abolished |
| Responsible government | Proposed only for transferred subjects | Introduced for provincial subjects, subject to Governor's powers |
| Autonomy | No fully responsible central government | Provincial autonomy introduced |
| Implementation | Federal scheme not implemented | Provincial autonomy implemented in 1937 |

---

## 6. Division of Legislative Powers

The Act divided legislative subjects into three lists.

| List | Number of subjects | Legislative authority |
|---|---:|---|
| Federal List | 59 | Federal Legislature |
| Provincial List | 54 | Provincial Legislatures |
| Concurrent List | 36 | Both Federal and Provincial Legislatures |

### 6.1 Federal List

Included subjects such as:

- Defence.
- External affairs.
- Currency.
- Railways.
- Posts and telegraphs.

### 6.2 Provincial List

Included subjects such as:

- Police.
- Public health.
- Agriculture.
- Local government.

### 6.3 Concurrent List

Included subjects such as:

- Criminal law.
- Criminal procedure.
- Marriage and divorce.

### 6.4 Residuary powers

**Residuary legislative powers were assigned to the Governor-General.**

The Governor-General could determine whether the Federal Legislature or a Provincial Legislature would legislate on a matter not included in any of the three lists.

### Important distinction

| Government of India Act, 1935 | Constitution of India, 1950 |
|---|---|
| Residuary powers assigned to Governor-General | Residuary legislative powers vested in Parliament |
| Federal, Provincial and Concurrent Lists | Union, State and Concurrent Lists |

---

## 7. Proposed Federal Legislature

The Act provided for a **bicameral federal legislature**.

| Feature | Council of States | Federal Assembly |
|---|---|---|
| Position | Upper House | Lower House |
| Maximum membership | 260 | 375 |
| British Indian representation | 156 | 250 |
| Princely-state representation | 104 | 125 |
| Election from British India | Mainly direct | Indirect |
| Tenure | Permanent body | Five years |

### 7.1 Council of States

- Maximum strength: **260 members**.
- British Indian representatives: **156**.
- Princely-state representatives: **104**.
- It was to be a permanent body.
- One-third of its members were to retire every three years.

### 7.2 Federal Assembly

- Maximum strength: **375 members**.
- British Indian representatives: **250**.
- Princely-state representatives: **125**.
- British Indian representatives were to be indirectly elected.
- Princely-state representatives were to be selected by their rulers.
- Normal tenure: Five years.

### Important distinction

In the proposed federal legislature:

- The **Council of States** was to have mainly direct elections for British Indian representatives.
- The **Federal Assembly** was to have indirect elections for British Indian representatives.

This arrangement differs from the present Indian Parliament.

**Remember:** The proposed federal legislature was never established.

---

## 8. Provincial Legislatures

The Act provided for bicameral legislatures in **six provinces**.

### 8.1 Provinces with bicameral legislatures

1. Bengal.
2. Bombay.
3. Madras.
4. United Provinces.
5. Bihar.
6. Assam.

### 8.2 Structure

| House | Character |
|---|---|
| Legislative Assembly | Lower House |
| Legislative Council | Upper House |

The remaining provinces had unicameral legislatures.

### Important distinction

Bicameralism was not introduced in every province.

**Six provinces had bicameral legislatures under the 1935 Act.**

---

## 9. Powers of the Governor-General

The Governor-General remained the central figure in the proposed constitutional structure.

### Important powers

1. **Discretionary powers:** Could act independently in specified matters.
2. **Special responsibilities:** Included safeguarding peace, tranquillity and certain financial and other interests.
3. **Legislative veto:** Could withhold assent to Bills.
4. **Ordinance-making power:** Could promulgate ordinances.
5. **Financial powers:** Could restore certain reductions in grants and exercise control over important financial matters.
6. **Residuary legislative powers:** Could assign legislative authority over subjects not included in the three lists.

---

## 10. Franchise and Separate Electorates

### 10.1 Expansion of franchise

The Act considerably expanded the electorate.

However:

- Universal adult franchise was **not introduced**.
- Voting qualifications continued to depend on factors such as property, taxation, education and other prescribed conditions.
- Only a limited proportion of the population could vote.

Approximately **14% of the population** was enfranchised.

### 10.2 Separate electorates

The Act continued and extended communal and special representation.

Separate electorates were retained for communities and interests including:

- Muslims.
- Sikhs.
- Indian Christians.
- Anglo-Indians.
- Europeans.

The system of separate representation was also extended to certain other categories.

---

## 11. Federal Court

The Act provided for the establishment of a Federal Court.

| Feature | Fact |
|---|---|
| Constitutional basis | Government of India Act, 1935 |
| Inaugurated | **1 October 1937** |
| Location | Delhi |
| First Chief Justice | **Sir Maurice Gwyer** |

### Jurisdiction

The Federal Court had:

- Original jurisdiction over specified federal disputes.
- Appellate jurisdiction in specified constitutional matters.
- Advisory jurisdiction.

### Important distinction

The Federal Court was the predecessor of the Supreme Court of India.

The **Supreme Court of India was established on 28 January 1950**, after the Constitution came into force.

---

## 12. Public Service Commissions

The Act provided for:

1. Federal Public Service Commission.
2. Provincial Public Service Commissions.
3. Joint Public Service Commissions for two or more provinces.

### Important clarification

The Public Service Commission system did not originate with the 1935 Act.

- The first Public Service Commission in India was established in **1926**.
- The 1935 Act provided a more elaborate constitutional framework for public service commissions.

---

## 13. Abolition of the Council of India

The Act abolished the **Council of India**, which had been established under the Government of India Act, 1858, to advise the Secretary of State for India.

Under the 1935 Act:

- The Council of India was abolished.
- The Secretary of State was to be assisted by advisers.

**Important distinction:** The Council of India should not be confused with the Governor-General's Executive Council or the proposed Council of States.

---

## 14. Territorial Reorganisation

The Act provided for important territorial changes.

| Territory | Development |
|---|---|
| Burma | Separated from British India in 1937 |
| Sind | Separated from Bombay Presidency |
| Orissa | Established as a separate province |
| Aden | Separated from British India in 1937 |

### Important facts

- Sind and Orissa became separate provinces on **1 April 1936**.
- Burma was separated from British India on **1 April 1937**.
- Aden was separated from British India on **1 April 1937**.

**Important distinction:** These changes did not all take effect on the same date.

---

---

## 16. Nationalist Response to the Act

The Indian National Congress rejected the Government of India Act, 1935.

### Congress demands

- Complete independence.
- A Constituent Assembly to frame India's Constitution.
- Election of the Constituent Assembly on the basis of adult franchise.

### Jawaharlal Nehru's criticism

Nehru described the Act as:

> “A car, all brakes and no engine.”

The criticism reflected the extensive powers retained by British authorities despite the proposed constitutional reforms.

## 20. Important Cautions for UPSC Prelims

**1. Reserve Bank of India**

The Reserve Bank of India began functioning on **1 April 1935**, but it was established under the **Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934**, not the Government of India Act, 1935.

**2. Federal Court versus Supreme Court**

The Federal Court was established in 1937. The Supreme Court of India was established in 1950.

**3. Provincial autonomy versus complete self-government**

Provincial autonomy was introduced, but Governors retained substantial discretionary and special powers.

**4. Federal scheme versus provincial scheme**

The federal scheme was never implemented, while the provincial scheme came into operation in 1937.

**5. Dyarchy at the Centre versus provinces**

The Act abolished dyarchy in provinces but proposed it at the Centre.

**6. Federal Assembly versus present Lok Sabha**

The proposed Federal Assembly under the 1935 Act was not equivalent in composition or election method to the present Lok Sabha.

**7. Limited franchise versus universal adult franchise**

The Act expanded voting rights but did not introduce universal adult franchise.
