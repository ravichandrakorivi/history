# Constructive Swadeshi Nationalist

## Ashwini Kumar Dutta (1856–1923)

Ashwini Kumar Dutta, also spelled Aswini Kumar Datta, was an Indian nationalist, educationist and social reformer associated with the **Swadeshi Movement in Bengal (1905–1908)**.

He is particularly remembered for establishing Brajamohan School (1884), Brajamohan College (1889) and the **Swadesh Bandhab Samiti (1905) at Barisal**, in present-day Bangladesh.

He played an important role in spreading the Swadeshi Movement among students, peasants and rural communities of eastern Bengal.

### 1. Early Life and Background

| Feature                | Important fact                                         |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Born                   | 25 January 1856                                        |
| Birthplace             | Patuakhali, Bengal Presidency (present-day Bangladesh) |
| Popular name           | Ashwini Kumar Dutta                                    |
| Profession             | Educationist, nationalist and social reformer          |
| Important centre       | Barisal, Eastern Bengal                                |
| Political association  | Indian National Congress; Swadeshi Movement            |
| Important organisation | Swadesh Bandhab Samiti                                 |
| Died                   | 7 November 1923                                        |

- Belonged to the early generation of nationalist leaders in Bengal.
- Emphasised education, moral development and social service.
- Encouraged the participation of students and rural communities in nationalist activities.
- Became particularly influential in the Barisal region.

### 2. Educational Contributions

Ashwini Kumar Dutta regarded education as an important means of social and national development.

| Institution        | Year | Place   |
| ------------------ | ---- | ------- |
| Brajamohan School  | 1884 | Barisal |
| Brajamohan College | 1889 | Barisal |

### Important facts

- Established Brajamohan School in 1884.
- Established Brajamohan College in 1889.
- Both institutions were named in honour of his father, Brajamohan Dutta.
- Promoted modern education, character development and public service.

Prelims Point: Brajamohan School (1884) and Brajamohan College (1889) — Ashwini Kumar Dutta — Barisal.

### 3. Swadesh Bandhab Samiti (1905)

One of Ashwini Kumar Dutta's most important contributions was the establishment of the Swadesh Bandhab Samiti during the anti-partition movement in Bengal.

| Feature                    | Important fact         |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Organisation               | Swadesh Bandhab Samiti |
| Established                | 1905                   |
| Founder                    | Ashwini Kumar Dutta    |
| Headquarters / Main centre | Barisal                |
| Region                     | Eastern Bengal         |
| Important movement         | Swadeshi Movement      |

### Important objectives

- Promote the use of indigenous goods.
- Encourage the boycott of foreign goods.
- Spread nationalist awareness among rural communities.
- Promote national education.
- Organise social service and relief activities.
- Encourage cooperation among different religious communities.

### Important features

- Developed a network of branches, particularly in the Barisal region.
- Mobilised students, volunteers, peasants and other sections of society.
- Organised meetings and campaigns promoting Swadeshi.
- Undertook social welfare and relief activities.

The organisation became an important example of rural mobilisation during the Swadeshi Movement.

Prelims Point: Swadesh Bandhab Samiti — 1905 — Ashwini Kumar Dutta — Barisal.

### 4. Role in the Swadeshi Movement (1905–1908)

The Swadeshi Movement developed in response to the Partition of Bengal (1905) under Viceroy Lord Curzon.

### Important contributions

- Organised anti-partition activities in Barisal.
- Encouraged the boycott of British-manufactured goods.
- Promoted the use of Indian-made products.
- Mobilised students and rural communities through the Swadesh Bandhab Samiti.
- Encouraged national education and social service.
- Advocated Hindu-Muslim cooperation during the anti-partition agitation.

### Barisal as a Swadeshi Centre

Barisal emerged as an important centre of the Swadeshi Movement in eastern Bengal.

Ashwini Kumar Dutta's organisational activities helped extend the movement beyond urban political meetings into rural society.

Important distinction: While leaders such as Surendranath Banerjea were prominent in the wider Bengal anti-partition movement, Ashwini Kumar Dutta was particularly associated with Swadeshi mobilisation in Barisal.

### 5. Barisal Conference (1906)

The Barisal Provincial Conference of April 1906 became an important episode of the anti-partition movement.

### Important facts

- Held at Barisal in Eastern Bengal.
- Surendranath Banerjea was an important participant.
- The conference faced police restrictions and repression.
- Police action against nationalist participants generated widespread criticism.
- The episode intensified nationalist opposition to colonial restrictions on political activity.

Ashwini Kumar Dutta was an important local nationalist leader associated with the Barisal movement.

Prelims distinction: Barisal Conference (1906) was associated with the Swadeshi agitation and colonial police repression.

### 6. British Repression and Deportation (1908)

The British government adopted repressive measures against nationalist organisations during the Swadeshi Movement.

### Important facts

- The Swadesh Bandhab Samiti was banned in 1908.
- Ashwini Kumar Dutta was arrested and deported without trial in December 1908.
- He was detained outside Bengal and subsequently released in 1910.

Prelims Point: Ashwini Kumar Dutta — Deportation in 1908 — Swadeshi Movement.

## 8. Association with the Indian National Congress

- Ashwini Kumar Dutta was associated with the early Indian National Congress.
- Participated in nationalist politics through constitutional methods and public mobilisation.
- Emphasised education and constructive social work alongside political agitation.
- His principal contribution to the national movement was through the organisation of Swadeshi activities in eastern Bengal.

He is particularly associated with the anti-partition phase of the Swadeshi Movement, rather than the later Gandhian mass movements.

## Satish Chandra Mukherjee (1865–1948)

Satish Chandra Mukherjee, also spelled Satish Chandra Mukherji, was an Indian educationist, nationalist thinker and social reformer associated with the Swadeshi Movement and the National Education Movement in Bengal.

He is particularly remembered for founding the Dawn Society (1902), editing the journal Dawn, and playing an important role in the establishment of the **National Council of Education (1906)**.

His principal contribution to Indian nationalism was the promotion of national education as an alternative to the British colonial education system.

### 1. Early Life and Background

| Feature                | Important fact                       |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Born                   | 1865                                 |
| Birthplace             | Bengal Presidency                    |
| Profession             | Educationist and nationalist thinker |
| Important journal      | Dawn                                 |
| Important organisation | Dawn Society (1902)                  |
| Important movement     | National Education Movement          |
| Major association      | National Council of Education (1906) |
| Died                   | 1948                                 |

- Belonged to the generation of Indian thinkers who advocated educational reform during the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries.
- Emphasised the importance of combining modern scientific education with Indian cultural and intellectual traditions.
- Encouraged students to develop national consciousness, character and a spirit of public service.

### 2. Dawn Magazine (1897)

Satish Chandra Mukherjee started the English-language journal Dawn in 1897.

### Important objectives

- Promote Indian cultural and intellectual traditions.
- Encourage modern scientific and technical education.
- Criticise excessive dependence on colonial educational institutions.
- Develop national consciousness among educated Indians.

Prelims Point: Dawn — 1897 — Satish Chandra Mukherjee.

### 3. Dawn Society (1902)

The Dawn Society was established by Satish Chandra Mukherjee in 1902 at Calcutta.

### Important activities

- Organised lectures and discussions on Indian history, philosophy, science and culture.
- Promoted education based on Indian needs and national ideals.
- Encouraged scientific and technical learning.
- Fostered patriotic consciousness among students.
- Helped prepare the intellectual foundation for the National Education Movement during the Swadeshi period.

### Important associates

The Dawn Society attracted prominent intellectuals and nationalist thinkers, including Sister Nivedita and Rabindranath Tagore, who were associated with its educational and cultural activities.

Prelims Point: Dawn Society — 1902 — Satish Chandra Mukherjee — Calcutta.

### 4. Role in the Swadeshi Movement (1905)

The Partition of Bengal (1905) and the Indian Universities Act (1904) strengthened demands for an educational system free from excessive colonial government control.

Satish Chandra Mukherjee played an important role in the National Education Movement, which developed alongside the Swadeshi Movement.

His contributions included:

- Promoting Indian-managed educational institutions.
- Encouraging the boycott of government-controlled educational institutions.
- Supporting scientific and technical education.
- Mobilising students through the Dawn Society.
- Advocating education based on Indian culture and national requirements.

Important distinction: His principal contribution to the Swadeshi Movement was through national education, rather than revolutionary violence.

# Extremists / Militant Nationalists

## Bal Gangadhar Tilak (1856–1920)

Bal Gangadhar Tilak, popularly known as Lokmanya Tilak, was a prominent leader of the Extremist (Militant Nationalist) faction of the Indian National Congress. He advocated Swaraj, Swadeshi, Boycott and National Education and played a major role in the Home Rule Movement (1916).

He was part of the famous Lal–Bal–Pal trio, along with Lala Lajpat Rai and Bipin Chandra Pal.

### 1. Early life and educational activities

- Born: 23 July 1856, Ratnagiri district, Maharashtra.
- Popular title: Lokmanya (accepted by the people as their leader).
- Studied at Deccan College, Pune.
- Helped establish the New English School (1880) with G. G. Agarkar, Vishnushastri Chiplunkar and others.
- Associated with the establishment of the Deccan Education Society (1884).
- Helped establish Fergusson College (1885) in Pune.
- Died: 1 August 1920, Bombay.

### 2. Newspapers and important writings

| Publication / Book                                          | Important association                                                       |
| ----------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Kesari (1881)                                               | Marathi newspaper co-founded by Tilak and associates                        |
| Mahratta (1881)                                             | English-language newspaper co-founded by Tilak and associates               |
| Gita Rahasya                                                | Interpretation of the Bhagavad Gita emphasising Karma Yoga (path of action) |
| The Arctic Home in the Vedas (1903)                         | Proposed a theory concerning the ancient origins of the Aryans              |
| Orion, or Researches into the Antiquity of the Vedas (1893) | Work on Vedic chronology                                                    |

Prelims Point: Kesari was published in Marathi, while Mahratta was published in English.

### 3. Ganapati and Shivaji Festivals

Tilak used public festivals to encourage political awareness and nationalist mobilisation.

| Festival                 | Year | Significance                                                          |
| ------------------------ | ---- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Public Ganapati Festival | 1893 | Popularised public celebrations to encourage collective participation |
| Shivaji Festival         | 1895 | Commemorated Shivaji and promoted nationalist consciousness           |

- These festivals provided opportunities for public gatherings under colonial rule.
- Helped extend nationalist mobilisation beyond educated political circles.

### 4. Role in Militant Nationalism (1905–1908)

- One of the principal leaders of the Extremist faction of the Indian National Congress.
- Supported the Swadeshi Movement following the Partition of Bengal in 1905.
- Advocated Swaraj, Swadeshi, Boycott and National Education.
- Favoured mass mobilisation and passive resistance rather than relying exclusively on petitions and constitutional reforms.
- Associated with the Surat Split (1907) between Moderates and Extremists.
- Popularised the slogan “Swaraj is my birthright, and I shall have it.”

### 6. Home Rule Movement (1916)

- Established the Indian Home Rule League in April 1916 at Belgaum.
- Headquarters: Pune.
- Objective: Obtain self-government for India.
- Important associates included Joseph Baptista and N. C. Kelkar.
- Areas of activity included Maharashtra (excluding Bombay city), Karnataka, Central Provinces and Berar.
- The League organised political meetings and campaigns to popularise the demand for Home Rule.

## Lala Lajpat Rai (1865–1928)

Lala Lajpat Rai, popularly known as Punjab Kesari (Lion of Punjab), was a prominent Indian nationalist, social reformer and leader of the Extremist (Militant Nationalist) faction of the Indian National Congress.

He was a member of the Lal–Bal–Pal trio, along with Bal Gangadhar Tilak and Bipin Chandra Pal.

### 1. Early life and political associations

- Born: 28 January 1865, Dhudike village, Punjab.
- Popular title: Punjab Kesari (Lion of Punjab).
- Associated with the Arya Samaj, founded by Swami Dayanand Saraswati in 1875.
- Supported the Dayanand Anglo-Vedic (DAV) educational movement.
- Became a prominent nationalist leader in Punjab.
- Advocated Swadeshi, Boycott, National Education and Swaraj.

### 2. Role in Militant Nationalism (1905–1907)

- Prominent leader of the Extremist faction of the Indian National Congress.
- Supported the Swadeshi Movement (1905) against the Partition of Bengal.
- Encouraged nationalist mobilisation in Punjab.
- Associated with Ajit Singh, who organised agrarian protests in Punjab.
- 1907: Deported without trial to Mandalay, Burma, during the Punjab agrarian unrest.
- Released later in 1907.

Prelims distinction: Lala Lajpat Rai was deported to Mandalay in 1907, whereas Bal Gangadhar Tilak was imprisoned in Mandalay from 1908 to 1914.

### 3. Activities in the United States (1914–1919)

- Travelled abroad in 1914 and spent much of the First World War period in the United States.
- Established the Indian Home Rule League of America in New York in 1917.
- Published the journal Young India to promote Indian nationalism.
- Wrote Young India: An Interpretation and a History of the Nationalist Movement from Within (1916).
- Campaigned internationally for Indian self-government.

Prelims Point: The Indian Home Rule League of America (1917) was established by Lala Lajpat Rai, whereas the Indian Home Rule Leagues of Tilak and Annie Besant were established in India in 1916.

### 4. Congress Presidency and Non-Cooperation Movement (1920)

- Presided over the Special Session of the Indian National Congress at Calcutta in September 1920.
- The session approved Gandhi's programme of Non-Cooperation.
- Supported the Non-Cooperation Movement and participated in nationalist mobilisation in Punjab.
- Was imprisoned during the movement.

Prelims Point: The Calcutta Special Session (September 1920) was presided over by Lala Lajpat Rai, whereas the Nagpur Session (December 1920) was presided over by C. Vijayaraghavachariar.

### 6. Simon Commission Protest and Death (1928)

- 30 October 1928: Led a protest against the Simon Commission in Lahore.
- The Simon Commission, chaired by Sir John Simon, had no Indian members.
- Police carried out a lathi charge against the protesters.
- James A. Scott, Superintendent of Police, ordered the lathi charge.
- Lala Lajpat Rai suffered serious injuries.
- 17 November 1928: Died in Lahore, with his death attributed to injuries sustained during the protest.
- Revolutionaries associated with the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA) subsequently planned to kill Scott in retaliation.
- 17 December 1928: Bhagat Singh and his associates killed J. P. Saunders, a British police officer, after mistaking him for Scott.
Prelims distinction: Lala Lajpat Rai was injured during the protest against the Simon Commission in October 1928 and died in November 1928. Saunders was assassinated in December 1928.

## Bipin Chandra Pal (1858–1932)

Bipin Chandra Pal was a prominent Indian nationalist, journalist, social reformer and leader of the Extremist (Militant Nationalist) faction of the Indian National Congress.

He was a member of the famous Lal–Bal–Pal trio, along with Lala Lajpat Rai and Bal Gangadhar Tilak, and played an important role in the Swadeshi Movement (1905).

### 1. Early life and political associations

- Born: 7 November 1858, Sylhet district, Bengal Presidency (now in Bangladesh).
- Associated with the Brahmo Samaj and social reform activities.
- Joined the Indian National Congress in 1886.
- Initially associated with Moderate nationalism but subsequently became a prominent Extremist leader.
- Advocated Swaraj, Swadeshi, Boycott and National Education.
- Popularly described as one of the leading figures of militant nationalism in Bengal.

### 2. Role in the Swadeshi Movement (1905–1908)

- Strongly opposed the Partition of Bengal (1905) under Lord Curzon.
- Promoted the Swadeshi and Boycott movements.
- Advocated the boycott of British goods and the promotion of indigenous industries.
- Supported National Education to reduce dependence on colonial educational institutions.
- Advocated passive resistance and mass mobilisation against British rule.
- Supported the demand for complete self-government, rather than limited administrative reforms.

Prelims Point: Bipin Chandra Pal was a leading advocate of the Swadeshi Movement in Bengal, whereas Bal Gangadhar Tilak was a major Extremist leader in Maharashtra and Lala Lajpat Rai in Punjab.

### 3. Newspapers and important publications

### 4. Bande Mataram Sedition Case (1907)

- Bipin Chandra Pal was summoned as a prosecution witness.
- He refused to testify against Aurobindo Ghosh and the newspaper.
- Convicted of contempt of court and sentenced to six months' imprisonment.

- Published the journal Swaraj in London in 1909.
- Returned to India and remained active as a journalist, writer and political thinker.
- Later developed differences with Mahatma Gandhi over the methods and direction of the nationalist movement.
- Criticised Gandhi's approach to Non-Cooperation and his use of religious and spiritual ideas in politics.
- Died: 20 May 1932, Calcutta.

## Aurobindo Ghosh (1872–1950)

Aurobindo Ghosh (Sri Aurobindo) was an Indian nationalist, revolutionary, philosopher and spiritual leader. He was a prominent figure in the Swadeshi Movement and Militant Nationalism (1905–1910) before withdrawing from active politics in 1910.

### 1. Early life

- Born: 15 August 1872, Calcutta.
- Educated in England, including at King's College, Cambridge.
- Returned to India in 1893.
- Worked in the princely state of Baroda, including as a professor at Baroda College.
- Became involved in secret revolutionary activities before the Swadeshi Movement.

### 2. Role in the Swadeshi Movement (1905–1908)

- Emerged as a prominent nationalist leader following the Partition of Bengal (1905).
- Associated with the Extremist (Militant Nationalist) faction of the Indian National Congress.
- Advocated complete independence (Purna Swaraj) from British rule rather than limited constitutional reforms.
- Supported Swadeshi, Boycott, National Education and Passive Resistance as methods of nationalist struggle.
- Associated with revolutionary networks in Bengal, including the Anushilan Samiti and Jugantar.
- Became the first Principal of Bengal National College, Calcutta (1906), established as part of the National Education Movement.
- Associated with the Surat Split of the Congress (1907) and presided over the Nationalist Conference held at Surat.

### 3. Revolutionary activities and Alipore Bomb Case (1908–1909)

- His younger brother, Barindra Kumar Ghosh, was a prominent organiser of the Jugantar revolutionary group.
- Following the Muzaffarpur Bomb Incident (1908), British authorities arrested several revolutionaries.
- Aurobindo was arrested in May 1908 in connection with the Alipore Bomb Conspiracy Case.
- Imprisoned for approximately one year during the trial.
- Chittaranjan Das (C. R. Das) defended Aurobindo in the case.
- Acquitted on 6 May 1909 due to insufficient evidence.
- Barindra Kumar Ghosh was convicted in the same case.

Prelims Point: Aurobindo Ghosh was acquitted in the Alipore Bomb Case, whereas his brother Barindra Kumar Ghosh was convicted.

### 4. Newspapers and important writings

### 5. Withdrawal from politics (1910)

- After his acquittal in 1909, Aurobindo continued nationalist activities for a short period.
- Delivered the famous Uttarpara Speech on 30 May 1909, reflecting his growing spiritual outlook.
- Withdrew from active politics in 1910.
- Moved to Pondicherry (Puducherry), then under French colonial administration, arriving on 4 April 1910.
- Devoted himself to spiritual philosophy and developed Integral Yoga.
- Established the Sri Aurobindo Ashram in 1926, with the collaboration of Mirra Alfassa, popularly known as The Mother.
- Died: 5 December 1950, Pondicherry.

| ------------------- | ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Swadesamitran       | 1904                | Joined as sub-editor; newspaper founded by G. Subramania Iyer in 1882 |
| Chakravarthini      | 1905                | Tamil monthly associated with women's education and reform            |

The ownership, founding and editorial roles of these periodicals were not always the same. Bharati was especially influential as an editor and contributor.

### Important distinction: Swadesamitran vs India

| Feature                        | Swadesamitran                    | India                            |
| ------------------------------ | -------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Founded                        | 1882                             | 1906                             |
| Language                       | Tamil                            | Tamil                            |
| Founder / founding association | G. Subramania Iyer               | Mandayam nationalist associates  |
| Bharati's role                 | Sub-editor from 1904             | Principal editor from 1906       |
| Political association          | Tamil journalism and nationalism | Assertive nationalist journalism |

Prelims trap: Bharati did not found Swadesamitran. It was founded by G. Subramania Iyer in 1882.

### 6. Most Important UPSC Prelims Traps

- Mahakavi Bharathiyar — Subramania Bharati.
- Born — 11 December 1882 — Ettayapuram.
- Political classification — Extremist / Assertive nationalist.
- Political inspiration — Bal Gangadhar Tilak.
- Swadesamitran — Founded by G. Subramania Iyer (1882), not Bharati.
- India — Tamil weekly — Bharati became editor in 1906.
- Chakravarthini — Associated with women's education and reform.
- Vijaya — Tamil daily associated with Bharati in Pondicherry.
- Sister Nivedita — Important influence on Bharati.
- Pondicherry exile — 1908–1918.
- Sri Aurobindo — Arrived in Pondicherry in 1910.
- Panchali Sabatham, Kannan Pattu, Kuyil Pattu — Bharati's works.
- V.O.C. — Swadeshi Steam Navigation Company, whereas Bharati — nationalist poetry and journalism.
- Died — 11 September 1921 — Madras.

Key distinction: For UPSC Prelims, remember Subramania Bharati → Extremist nationalism → India (1906) → Sister Nivedita → Pondicherry exile (1908–1918) → Panchali Sabatham → Women's emancipation and anti-caste reform.
