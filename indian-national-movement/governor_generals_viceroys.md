# Governor-Generals and Viceroys of India

**Scope:** Important administrators, major policies and events, social and educational changes, economic administration, military expansion, and constitutional developments. Includes 24 original Prelims-style questions with explanations.

**How to read these notes:** An event occurring during a governor-general’s tenure does not mean that he personally initiated it. Parliamentary Acts, Indian reform movements, nationalist organisations and commissions must be attributed to their actual authors or institutions. Colonial “reforms” also served British revenue, commercial and strategic interests.

---

## 1. Understand the Three Titles First

| Office | Constitutional landmark | First holder | Essential distinction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Governor-General of Bengal | Regulating Act, 1773 | Warren Hastings | The new arrangement began operating in 1774; Hastings had been Governor of Bengal since 1772. |
| Governor-General of India | Charter Act, 1833 | Lord William Bentinck | Centralised legislative authority over British India. Bentinck’s overall tenure was 1828–1835 and straddled the change of title. |
| Viceroy and Governor-General of India | Transfer to Crown rule, 1858 | Lord Canning | The governor-general also represented the British Crown as viceroy. The governor-general’s title continued. |

**Date clarification:** Exam summaries commonly begin the Bengal category with 1773 and the India category with 1833, the years of the relevant Acts. Actual implementation explains references to 1774 and 1834 in more detailed chronologies. [1][2]

## 2. Background: Robert Clive

**Governor of Bengal: 1757–1760 and 1765–1767.**

- **Battle of Plassey, 1757:** Clive led the Company’s forces against Siraj-ud-Daulah. It established a decisive Company influence over Bengal’s politics.
- **Diwani grant, 1765:** Mughal emperor Shah Alam II granted the Company the revenue-collection rights of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa, as part of the settlement associated with the Treaty of Allahabad.
- **Dual Government in Bengal, 1765–1772:** The Company controlled revenue, while the nawab nominally retained responsibility for administration and law and order. Effective power and formal responsibility were separated.
- Warren Hastings ended this arrangement in **1772**.

**Prelims trap:** The Company commander at the **Battle of Buxar, 1764, was Hector Munro**, not Clive. The battle occurred before Clive’s second governorship.

## 3. Important Governor-Generals of Bengal

### 3.1 Warren Hastings — 1774–1785

*Governor of Bengal from 1772; first governor-general under the arrangement established by the Regulating Act, 1773.*

**Administration and revenue**

- Abolished the **Dual Government in 1772** and brought revenue administration under more direct Company control.
- Initially experimented with **five-year revenue farming**. This was not the Permanent Settlement.
- The judicial plan of **1772** provided district civil and criminal courts: **Diwani Adalats** and **Faujdari Adalats**.
- The **Supreme Court at Calcutta, 1774**, was established under the Regulating Act’s framework. It was distinct from the Company’s adalat system.
- **Pitt’s India Act, 1784**, was passed during his tenure; it strengthened British governmental control through the Board of Control.

**Education and culture**

- Established the **Calcutta Madrasa, 1781**, for the study of Islamic law and related subjects.
- Supported Orientalist scholarship.
- The **Asiatic Society, 1784**, was founded by **Sir William Jones** during his tenure. Hastings was a patron, not its founder.

**Military affairs**

- **Rohilla War, 1774.**
- **First Anglo-Maratha War, 1775–1782:** ended with the **Treaty of Salbai, 1782**.
- **Second Anglo-Mysore War, 1780–1784:** ended with the **Treaty of Mangalore, 1784**.

**Remember:** Warren Hastings is different from Lord Hastings, who governed in 1813–1823.

### 3.2 Lord Cornwallis — 1786–1793; brief second tenure in 1805

**Economic policy: Permanent Settlement, 1793**

- Introduced principally in **Bengal and Bihar**.
- Recognised zamindars as proprietors responsible for paying a fixed land-revenue demand to the Company.
- The **Company’s demand on zamindars** was permanently fixed; cultivators’ rents were not thereby permanently fixed.
- Failure to pay the stipulated revenue could result in the sale of the estate.
- The system sought stable revenue and a supportive landed class, but exposed cultivators to landlord demands and insecurity.

**Administrative and judicial changes**

- **Cornwallis Code, 1793:** reorganised administration and justice; separated revenue collection from judicial functions at the district level.
- Increased officials’ salaries and acted against private trade and corruption.
- Associated with the development of the **covenanted civil service**; commonly called the **“father of the civil service in India.”** This does not mean he introduced open competitive recruitment.
- Higher offices were largely reserved for Europeans, reinforcing racial exclusion.

**Military affairs**

- **Third Anglo-Mysore War, 1790–1792.**
- **Treaty of Seringapatam, 1792:** Tipu Sultan surrendered approximately half his territory and paid an indemnity.

### 3.3 Lord Wellesley — 1798–1805

**Political and military policy: Subsidiary Alliance**

- Systematically expanded the use of subsidiary alliances to subordinate Indian states.
- A ruler accepting such an alliance maintained British troops, usually paid a subsidy or ceded territory, and accepted restrictions on independent diplomacy and warfare.
- A **British Resident** was normally stationed at the ruler’s court.
- **Hyderabad, 1798**, is the standard first major example under Wellesley’s developed system.
- The state could continue under its ruler while losing substantial external sovereignty; an alliance did not necessarily mean immediate annexation.

**Wars and treaties**

- **Fourth Anglo-Mysore War, 1799:** Tipu Sultan died at Seringapatam; Mysore was partitioned and a reduced kingdom restored to the Wodeyars.
- **Treaty of Bassein, 1802:** concluded with **Peshwa Baji Rao II**; placed him under a subsidiary alliance.
- **Second Anglo-Maratha War, 1803–1805:** followed the political realignment caused by Bassein.

**Education and press**

- Established **Fort William College, Calcutta, 1800**, to train Company civil servants in Indian languages and administration.
- Introduced **press censorship in 1799**, partly in the context of anxiety about French influence.

### 3.4 Lord Hastings — 1813–1823

**Military expansion and paramountcy**

- Asserted **British paramountcy**: the Company claimed a superior position over Indian states and widened its right to intervene.
- **Anglo-Nepalese War, 1814–1816:** ended with the **Treaty of Sugauli, 1816**.
- **Third Anglo-Maratha War, 1817–1818:** defeated the Maratha powers and abolished the Peshwa’s office.
- Conducted campaigns against the **Pindaris**.

**Revenue and press**

- The **Ryotwari system** was extended in the Madras Presidency, particularly through **Thomas Munro**. Its earlier development also involved **Alexander Read**.
- **Holt Mackenzie’s Mahalwari proposals, 1822**, belonged to this period. Later revisions are associated with **Robert Merttins Bird** under Bentinck.
- Relaxed pre-publication press censorship in **1818**, though restrictions remained.

**Remember:** British paramountcy is broader than either a subsidiary alliance or the Doctrine of Lapse. [3]

William Bentinck, who next held office, is covered below because his tenure bridged the change from Governor-General of Bengal to Governor-General of India.

## 4. Important Governor-Generals of India under Company Rule

### 4.1 Lord William Bentinck — 1828–1835

**Social reform**

- **Bengal Sati Regulation, 1829:** declared sati illegal and punishable in the Bengal Presidency; the prohibition was extended to Bombay and Madras in 1830.
- **Raja Rammohan Roy’s campaign** was central to the reform; do not erase Indian reformers’ role by attributing the change solely to the governor-general.
- Organised suppression of **thuggee**, associated with **William Henry Sleeman**, expanded during his administration and continued thereafter.

**Education**

- Accepted the direction of **Macaulay’s Minute, 1835**, through a government resolution favouring English-language instruction in European literature and science.
- **Calcutta Medical College, 1835**, was founded during his tenure.

**Administration and economy**

- Pursued expenditure reduction and administrative economy.
- Became the **first Governor-General of India** under the **Charter Act, 1833**.
- The Act ended the Company’s remaining commercial functions, including its monopoly over tea and trade with China.
- **First Law Commission, 1834**, chaired by **Macaulay**, was constituted during his tenure.
- Mahalwari revenue arrangements were revised under **Robert Merttins Bird**.

**Prelims trap:** Sati prohibition belongs to **Bentinck**; the **Hindu Widows’ Remarriage Act, 1856**, belongs to **Canning’s tenure**. [4]

### 4.2 Sir Charles Metcalfe — 1835–1836, acting

- The **Press Act, 1835**, removed the restrictive licensing system associated with the 1823 regulations.
- Known as the **“liberator of the Indian press.”**
- Do not confuse this with **Ripon’s repeal of the Vernacular Press Act in 1882**.

### 4.3 Lord Auckland — 1836–1842

- Pursued an interventionist Afghan policy.
- **Tripartite Treaty, 1838:** involved the British, Ranjit Singh and Shah Shuja.
- **First Anglo-Afghan War, 1839–1842:** began under Auckland; the British retreat from Kabul in 1842 was disastrous.

### 4.4 Lord Ellenborough — 1842–1844

- Completed British withdrawal from the First Anglo-Afghan War after retaliatory expeditions.
- **Annexation of Sindh, 1843**, following campaigns led by **Charles Napier**.
- **Indian Slavery Act, 1843:** removed legal recognition and enforcement of claims based on slavery. It is conventionally associated with the abolition of slavery in British India, though coercive labour practices persisted.

### 4.5 Lord Hardinge I — 1844–1848

- **First Anglo-Sikh War, 1845–1846**.
- **Treaty of Lahore, 1846**, imposed an indemnity and territorial losses on the Sikh kingdom.
- Under the separate **Treaty of Amritsar, 1846**, Kashmir passed to **Gulab Singh** under British supremacy.

**Prelims trap:** Hardinge I is associated with the **First Anglo-Sikh War**. Hardinge II, viceroy in 1910–1916, is associated with the **1911 Delhi Durbar**.

### 4.6 Lord Dalhousie — 1848–1856

**A. Annexation and military expansion**

| Territory | Year | Basis of annexation |
|---|---|---|
| Satara | 1848 | Doctrine of Lapse |
| Punjab | 1849 | Defeat of the Sikh kingdom in the Second Anglo-Sikh War |
| Sambalpur | 1849 | Doctrine of Lapse |
| Lower Burma/Pegu | 1852 | Second Anglo-Burmese War |
| Jhansi | 1854 | Doctrine of Lapse |
| Nagpur | 1854 | Doctrine of Lapse; the succession crisis followed the ruler’s death in 1853 |
| Awadh | 1856 | Alleged misgovernment |

**Doctrine of Lapse:** The Company refused to recognise an adopted heir’s automatic right to succeed to the rulership of certain dependent states when the ruler died without a natural heir. This did not amount to a blanket prohibition on adoption for religious or private purposes.

**Most important trap:** **Awadh was not annexed under the Doctrine of Lapse.** Neither were Punjab nor Lower Burma. [3]

**B. Transport and communications**

- **First passenger railway, 1853:** Bombay to Thane. Specify “passenger railway”; earlier industrial rail experiments existed.
- Promoted railway expansion and the guarantee system, which protected returns to British investors using Indian revenues.
- Expanded the **electric telegraph network**, associated with **W. B. O’Shaughnessy**.
- **Postal reforms, 1854:** reorganised postal administration and introduced a uniform low postage rate.
- Established a separate **Public Works Department, 1854**.
- **Ganges Canal opened in 1854**, although its development had begun earlier.

**C. Education and social legislation**

- **Wood’s Despatch, 1854:** a major educational policy statement issued from Britain; Charles Wood was President of the Board of Control.
- Recommended an educational structure from vernacular primary schools to universities, education departments, teacher training and grants-in-aid.
- **Caste Disabilities Removal Act, 1850:** protected inheritance rights against forfeiture solely because of religious conversion or exclusion from caste.

**Prelims traps**

- Wood’s Despatch: **Dalhousie, 1854**; universities at Calcutta, Bombay and Madras: **Canning, 1857**.
- The Hindu Widows’ Remarriage Act was passed in **July 1856, after Canning took office**, despite preparatory work during Dalhousie’s period.

### 4.7 Lord Canning — 1856–1862

**Canning bridges Company rule and Crown rule:** Governor-General under the Company from 1856 to 1858, and first Viceroy from 1858 to 1862. [5]

**Social and educational developments**

- **Hindu Widows’ Remarriage Act, 1856**, following the campaign prominently led by **Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar**.
- Universities of **Calcutta, Bombay and Madras, 1857**.

**Political and administrative developments**

- **Revolt of 1857**.
- **Government of India Act, 1858:** transferred government from the Company to the Crown.
- **Queen Victoria’s Proclamation, 1 November 1858:** promised religious non-interference and respect for treaties and princely rights.
- **Indian Penal Code enacted in 1860**; came into operation in **1862**. Its drafting originated with the earlier Law Commission.
- **Indian Councils Act, 1861:** expanded legislative councils and permitted nomination of Indians to legislative work.
- **Indian High Courts Act, 1861:** authorised High Courts; the Calcutta, Bombay and Madras High Courts opened in **1862**, after Canning had left office.

**Economic and cultural developments**

- **Income tax introduced in 1860 by James Wilson**, the finance member, during Canning’s administration.
- **Archaeological Survey established in 1861**, associated with **Alexander Cunningham**. Curzon later strengthened and reorganised archaeological work. [6]

## 5. Important Viceroys of India

### 5.1 Lord Elgin I — 1862–1863

- Short tenure; **Calcutta, Bombay and Madras High Courts began functioning in 1862**.
- Keep separate from **Elgin II, 1894–1899**.

### 5.2 Sir John Lawrence — 1864–1869

- Associated with **“masterly inactivity”** towards Afghanistan: avoidance of unnecessary interference in Afghan succession struggles.
- **Bhutan War, 1864–1865**, followed by the **Treaty of Sinchula, 1865**.
- **Orissa famine, 1866**, exposed serious weaknesses in colonial famine relief.

### 5.3 Lord Mayo — 1869–1872

**Finance and administration**

- **Financial decentralisation, 1870:** assigned provincial governments greater responsibility for specified services and expenditure, with fixed financial allocations.
- The early census effort conventionally called the **Census of 1872** is associated with his period. It was **non-synchronous**, assembled from enumerations conducted at different times.
- Promoted education for princely elites; **Mayo College at Ajmer** is associated with his initiative, although it opened in 1875.
- Assassinated at **Port Blair in the Andaman Islands in 1872**.

**Prelims trap:** Mayo = early non-synchronous census; Ripon = **first synchronous census, 1881**. [7]

### 5.4 Lord Northbrook — 1872–1876

- **Bihar famine, 1873–1874:** substantial relief, including rice imports, helped avert mass mortality.
- **Prince of Wales visited India, 1875–1876**.
- The Gaekwad of Baroda, **Malhar Rao**, was deposed in 1875.

### 5.5 Lord Lytton — 1876–1880

**Repressive legislation**

- **Vernacular Press Act, 1878:** targeted Indian-language publications; English-language newspapers were outside its scope.
- **Arms Act, 1878:** regulated possession of arms through licensing; its operation embodied racial discrimination and exemptions favouring Europeans.

**Economy and famine**

- **Great Famine, 1876–1878:** relief policy was widely criticised for inadequacy and excessive insistence on economy.
- Reduced cotton import duties in ways favourable to Lancashire manufacturers; do not attribute every stage of their abolition to Lytton alone.
- Lowering the maximum age for the civil-service examination to **19** made access harder for Indian aspirants.

**Imperial and military affairs**

- **Delhi Durbar, 1877**, proclaimed Victoria as **Empress of India**, following the Royal Titles Act, 1876.
- **Second Anglo-Afghan War, 1878–1880**, began under him; **Treaty of Gandamak, 1879**.

### 5.6 Lord Ripon — 1880–1884

**Local government and public life**

- **Resolution on Local Self-Government, 1882:** encouraged elected non-official participation in local bodies.
- Known as the **“father of local self-government in India.”** His reforms remained subject to colonial supervision and limited franchise.
- **Repealed the Vernacular Press Act in 1882**.

**Labour and education**

- **First Factory Act, 1881:** limited child employment and working hours in covered factories and provided basic safety measures. Its coverage was restricted; it was not universal labour protection.
- **Hunter Education Commission, 1882**, chaired by **W. W. Hunter**, examined education, particularly primary and secondary education.

**Judicial equality**

- **Ilbert Bill controversy, 1883:** the bill proposed removing racial disqualification preventing qualified Indian judges from trying European British subjects in specified criminal cases.
- European opposition forced a compromise that diluted the original proposal.
- The controversy exposed racial inequality in colonial rule.

**Other associations**

- **First synchronous census, 1881**.
- **Rendition of Mysore, 1881:** restored administration to its hereditary ruler after decades of direct British management.

### 5.7 Lord Dufferin — 1884–1888

- **Indian National Congress founded in 1885**, during his tenure. It was not founded by Dufferin; **A. O. Hume** helped organise it and **W. C. Bonnerjee** presided over its first session.
- **Third Anglo-Burmese War, 1885**, followed by annexation of **Upper Burma, 1886**.
- **Bengal Tenancy Act, 1885:** strengthened certain tenant protections, including occupancy rights.
- **Aitchison Commission, 1886**, examined the organisation of the public services.

### 5.8 Lord Lansdowne — 1888–1894

- **Age of Consent Act, 1891:** raised the age of consent for girls from 10 to 12. It was not a general minimum-marriage-age law.
- **Factory Act, 1891:** extended regulation of working conditions, including protections for women and children.
- **Indian Councils Act, 1892:** enlarged councils and allowed limited budget discussion and questions; it did not establish responsible government.
- **Durand Agreement, 1893:** negotiated by **Mortimer Durand** with **Amir Abdur Rahman** of Afghanistan.

### 5.9 Lord Elgin II — 1894–1899

- **Famine of 1896–1897** and **bubonic plague outbreak in Bombay, 1896**.
- **Epidemic Diseases Act, 1897**.
- **Tirah campaign, 1897–1898**, on the north-western frontier.

### 5.10 Lord Curzon — 1899–1905

**A. Political and administrative policy**

- **Partition of Bengal, 1905**, effective **16 October 1905**; prompted the anti-partition and Swadeshi movements.
- Official justification emphasised administrative convenience; the measure also served to weaken Bengali nationalism through division.
- **Calcutta Corporation Act, 1899:** reduced elected Indian influence in municipal administration.
- **North-West Frontier Province created in 1901**.
- **Police Commission, 1902**, chaired by **Andrew Fraser**.

**B. Education and heritage**

- **Indian Universities Commission, 1902**, chaired by **Thomas Raleigh**.
- **Indian Universities Act, 1904:** tightened government supervision of universities while pursuing institutional reform.
- **Ancient Monuments Preservation Act, 1904**.
- Strengthened the Archaeological Survey; **John Marshall became Director-General in 1902**. The Survey’s origins were in 1861, not Curzon’s tenure.

**C. Agriculture, credit and transport**

- **Co-operative Credit Societies Act, 1904**.
- Agricultural research institution established at **Pusa, Bihar, in 1905**, the predecessor of the Indian Agricultural Research Institute. It moved to Delhi later.
- **Railway Board established in 1905**.

**D. Military and foreign affairs**

- **Younghusband expedition to Tibet, 1903–1904**, followed by the **Treaty of Lhasa, 1904**.
- Army reorganisation was associated with Commander-in-Chief **Lord Kitchener**.
- Conflict with Kitchener over military administration contributed to Curzon’s resignation.

**Remember:** Curzon’s administrative and conservation measures coexisted with restrictive political policies. [8]

### 5.11 Lord Minto II — 1905–1910

- **Indian Councils Act, 1909**, or **Morley–Minto Reforms**.
- Introduced **separate electorates for Muslims** in the legislative electoral system.
- **S. P. Sinha** became the first Indian member of the Viceroy’s Executive Council, as law member, in **1909**.
- **Simla Deputation, 1906**.
- **Muslim League founded at Dacca, 1906**, during his tenure.
- **Surat split of the Congress, 1907**.

**Prelims trap:** **Minto I = Treaty of Amritsar, 1809. Minto II = Morley–Minto Reforms, 1909.**

### 5.12 Lord Hardinge II — 1910–1916

- **Delhi Durbar, 1911:** attended by **George V and Queen Mary**.
- Announced the **annulment of Bengal’s partition** and transfer of the capital from **Calcutta to Delhi, 1911**.
- **Bihar and Orissa became a separate province in 1912**.
- **First World War began in 1914**; India’s troops and resources were heavily mobilised.
- **Gandhi returned to India from South Africa in January 1915**.

**Prelims trap:** The capital-transfer announcement was in **1911**; the formal inauguration of **New Delhi was in 1931, under Irwin**.

### 5.13 Lord Chelmsford — 1916–1921

**Constitutional policy**

- **Montagu Declaration, 20 August 1917:** stated the policy of gradual development of self-governing institutions and progressive realisation of responsible government.
- **Montagu–Chelmsford Report, 1918**.
- **Government of India Act, 1919:** introduced **dyarchy in provinces**, not at the Centre, and a bicameral central legislature.

**Repression and nationalist response**

- **Rowlatt Act, 1919**.
- **Jallianwala Bagh massacre, 13 April 1919**.
- **Disorders Inquiry Committee, 1919**, chaired by **Lord William Hunter**.
- **Champaran Satyagraha, 1917; Kheda Satyagraha and Ahmedabad mill strike, 1918**.
- **Khilafat and Non-Cooperation movements**, with Non-Cooperation launched in 1920.

**Military affairs**

- **Third Anglo-Afghan War, 1919**, followed by the **Treaty of Rawalpindi**.

**Prelims trap:** Hunter Education Commission, **1882 = Ripon**; Hunter inquiry into the Punjab disturbances, **1919 = Chelmsford**. They had different chairmen.

### 5.14 Lord Reading — 1921–1926

- **Chauri Chaura incident and withdrawal of Non-Cooperation, 1922**.
- **Swaraj Party formed, 1923**, by C. R. Das and Motilal Nehru.
- **Kakori action, 1925**.
- **Lee Commission, 1923**, examined the superior civil services.
- **Railway finances separated from general finances in 1924**, following the Acworth Committee’s recommendations.

### 5.15 Lord Irwin — 1926–1931

- **Simon Commission appointed in 1927; arrived in India in 1928**.
- **Nehru Report, 1928**.
- **Irwin Declaration, 1929:** referred to dominion status as the goal of constitutional development; it did not grant immediate dominion status.
- **Lahore Congress, 1929:** adopted **Purna Swaraj**.
- **Dandi March and Civil Disobedience Movement, 1930**.
- **First Round Table Conference, 1930–1931**.
- **Gandhi–Irwin Pact, 5 March 1931:** Congress agreed to suspend Civil Disobedience and participate in the next Round Table Conference; the government made concessions including release of prisoners not convicted of violence.
- **Bhagat Singh, Rajguru and Sukhdev executed, 23 March 1931**.
- **New Delhi inaugurated, 1931**.

**Prelims trap:** Gandhi attended the **Second Round Table Conference in 1931**, by which time **Willingdon** was viceroy. The pact had been signed with Irwin. [9]

### 5.16 Lord Willingdon — 1931–1936

- **Second and Third Round Table Conferences, 1931 and 1932**.
- Repression of the resumed **Civil Disobedience Movement**.
- **Communal Award, 1932**, announced by British Prime Minister **Ramsay MacDonald**.
- **Poona Pact, 1932:** substituted reserved seats in joint electorates for separate electorates for the Depressed Classes.
- **Government of India Act, 1935:** provided for provincial autonomy and an all-India federation; the proposed federation never came into operation.
- **RBI began operations on 1 April 1935**, under the **RBI Act, 1934**. [10]

**Prelims trap:** Act passed in **1935 under Willingdon**; provincial autonomy implemented in **1937 under Linlithgow**.

### 5.17 Lord Linlithgow — 1936–1943

- **Longest-serving Viceroy of India**.
- **Provincial elections and formation of Congress ministries, 1937**.
- Declared India a participant in the **Second World War, 1939**, without consulting Indian political leaders.
- **Congress provincial ministries resigned, 1939**.
- **August Offer, 1940**.
- **Individual Satyagraha, 1940**, beginning with Vinoba Bhave.
- **Cripps Mission, 1942**.
- **Quit India Movement, August 1942**, and its repression.
- **Bengal famine, 1943**, developed during his tenure and continued into Wavell’s.

### 5.18 Lord Wavell — 1943–1947

- **Wavell Plan and Simla Conference, 1945**.
- **INA trials, 1945–1946**, and **Royal Indian Navy uprising, 1946**.
- **Cabinet Mission, 1946**.
- **Direct Action Day, 16 August 1946**, followed by severe communal violence.
- **Interim Government formed, 2 September 1946**, with Nehru as Vice-President of the Executive Council.
- **Constituent Assembly first met, 9 December 1946**.
- **Attlee’s announcement, 20 February 1947**, stated that power would be transferred no later than June 1948.

**Prelims trap:** The Cabinet Mission and first meeting of the Constituent Assembly occurred under **Wavell**, not Mountbatten. Use **1943**, not 1944, as the beginning of Wavell’s tenure. [11]

### 5.19 Lord Mountbatten — Viceroy, March–August 1947

- **3 June Plan, 1947**, provided the framework for partition and transfer of power.
- **Indian Independence Act, 1947**, was passed by the British Parliament.
- Independence and partition in **August 1947**.
- **Last Viceroy** and then **first Governor-General of independent India, 1947–1948**.

### 5.20 C. Rajagopalachari — Governor-General, 1948–1950

- **First Indian and last Governor-General of independent India**.
- He was **not a viceroy**.
- The office ended when India became a republic on **26 January 1950**; **Rajendra Prasad** became President.

### 6.4 Wars: The Most Testable Associations

| War | Dates | Governor-General/Viceroy |

| First Anglo-Maratha | 1775–1782 | Warren Hastings |
| Second Anglo-Maratha | 1803–1805 | Wellesley; final settlements extended into his successors’ period |

| Second Anglo-Mysore | 1780–1784 | Warren Hastings |

## 7. High-Yield Prelims Traps

1. **Clive was not a governor-general.**
2. **Warren Hastings and Lord Hastings were different people.**
3. **Cornwallis’s civil-service reforms were not open competitive recruitment.** The Charter Act, 1853, opened the way for competitive recruitment, with examinations beginning in 1855.
4. **Permanent Settlement fixed the state demand, not all rents paid by tenants.**
5. **Subsidiary Alliance did not necessarily entail immediate annexation.**
6. **Awadh: alleged misgovernment; Punjab: conquest; Jhansi: lapse.**
7. **Wood’s Despatch: Dalhousie; 1857 universities: Canning.**
8. **Vernacular Press Act: Lytton; repeal: Ripon.**
9. **Metcalfe’s press reform was in 1835**, decades before the Vernacular Press Act.
10. **Census of 1872 was non-synchronous; 1881 was synchronous.**
11. **ASI origins: Canning; later strengthening: Curzon.**
12. **Bengal partition: Curzon; annulment: Hardinge II.**
13. **Capital transfer announced: Hardinge II, 1911; New Delhi inaugurated: Irwin, 1931.**
14. **Hunter education inquiry: Ripon, 1882; Hunter Punjab disturbances inquiry: Chelmsford, 1919.**
15. **Dyarchy in provinces: 1919 Act; provincial autonomy: 1935 Act, implemented in 1937.**
16. **First Round Table Conference: Irwin; second and third: Willingdon.**
17. **Cripps Mission: Linlithgow; Cabinet Mission: Wavell.**
18. **Mountbatten was both viceroy and, later, an independent India governor-general; Rajagopalachari was never viceroy.**

## 9. Answer Key and Explanations

| Question | Answer | Question | Answer | Question | Answer | Question | Answer |

### Q1. Correct Answer: C

Pairs 1, 2 and 3 are correct. **Mountbatten**, not Rajagopalachari, was independent India’s first governor-general. Rajagopalachari was its first **Indian** and last governor-general.

### Q2. Correct Answer: A

Statements 1 and 2 are correct. Hastings ended Dual Government in **1772**, before taking office as governor-general in 1774. Salbai was concluded in **1782**. Permanent Settlement belongs to **Cornwallis, 1793**; Fort William College to **Wellesley, 1800**.

### Q3. Correct Answer: D

Only statement 3 is correct. Permanent Settlement fixed the **Company’s demand on zamindars**, not cultivators’ rents. The demand was permanent rather than periodically revised. Revenue default could lead to an estate’s sale.

### Q4. Correct Answer: B

Statements 1, 2 and 3 are correct. Subsidiary alliances curtailed sovereignty while often preserving the ruler and dynasty. Immediate annexation was not an essential condition. Bassein was signed with **Baji Rao II in 1802**.

### Q5. Correct Answer: B

Pairs 1 and 3 are correct. The Third Anglo-Mysore War belongs to **Cornwallis**. The Second Anglo-Sikh War belongs to **Dalhousie**; Hardinge I is associated with the first.

### Q6. Correct Answer: D

**Salbai, 1782; Permanent Settlement, 1793; Bassein, 1802; Sugauli, 1816.**

### Q7. Correct Answer: A

Statements 1, 2 and 4 are correct: sati prohibition in **1829**, the First Law Commission in **1834**, and the English education resolution in **1835**. The three universities were established in **1857 under Canning**.

### Q8. Correct Answer: C

Statements 1 and 3 are incorrect. **Awadh** was annexed on the pretext of misgovernment; **Jhansi** under the Doctrine of Lapse. Statements 2 and 4 correctly associate Punjab and Lower Burma with military conquest.

### Q9. Correct Answer: D

All four occurred under Canning: widow remarriage legislation in **1856**, universities in **1857**, income tax in **1860**, and the archaeological survey in **1861**. Remember the separate roles of Vidyasagar, Wilson and Cunningham.

### Q10. Correct Answer: B

Statements 1 and 3 are correct. Wellesley’s censorship dates to **1799** and Metcalfe’s reform to **1835**. Statement 2 reverses the names: **Lytton enacted the Vernacular Press Act in 1878; Ripon repealed it in 1882**.

### Q11. Correct Answer: C

Pairs 1, 2 and 4 are correct. The Age of Consent Act, **1891**, belongs to **Lansdowne**, not Dufferin.

### Q12. Correct Answer: A

Statements 1 and 2 are correct. The original Ilbert Bill aimed to remove a racial disability affecting qualified Indian judges. European resistance diluted the proposal. The controversy arose in **1883 under Ripon**.

### Q13. Correct Answer: A

Statements 1, 2 and 4 are correct. The Universities Act and Ancient Monuments Preservation Act date to **1904**, and the NWFP to **1901**. Partition was annulled in **1911 under Hardinge II**, not Curzon.

### Q14. Correct Answer: C

Pairs 1 and 2 are correct. The **1919 Hunter inquiry** was appointed under **Chelmsford**. The **Lee Commission, 1923**, belongs to **Reading**.

### Q15. Correct Answer: B

Statements 1 and 3 are correct. The **1919 Act introduced dyarchy in the provinces**, not at the Centre. The **1935 Act’s proposed all-India federation never operated**. Provincial autonomy under that Act took effect in 1937.

### Q16. Correct Answer: D

All three statements are correct. Keep **1911’s capital-transfer announcement** separate from **1931’s inauguration of New Delhi**. Gandhi attended the Second Round Table Conference after **Willingdon succeeded Irwin in April 1931**.

### Q17. Correct Answer: C

The **Communal Award, 1932**, and **RBI commencement, 1935**, occurred under Willingdon. The pact was signed under **Irwin in March 1931**; Congress ministries were formed under **Linlithgow in 1937**.

### Q18. Correct Answer: D

**Reading, 1921–1926; Irwin, 1926–1931; Willingdon, 1931–1936; Linlithgow, 1936–1943; Wavell, 1943–1947.**

### Q19. Correct Answer: B

Pairs 1 and 3 are correct. The **Cripps Mission, 1942**, was under **Linlithgow**. The Constituent Assembly first met in **December 1946 under Wavell**.

### Q20. Correct Answer: A

Statements 3 and 4 are incorrect. Canning remained governor-general while also becoming viceroy. The office of governor-general ended with the inauguration of the Republic on **26 January 1950**. Statements 1 and 2 are correct.

### Q21. Correct Answer: D

The assertion is false: the Survey originated in **1861 during Canning’s tenure**, associated with Cunningham. The reason is true: Curzon strengthened archaeological administration and conservation, including the **1904 Act**.

### Q22. Correct Answer: A

Both statements are true, and the reason explains the assertion. Restrictions on external relations reduced sovereignty, while the absence of compulsory immediate annexation allowed a ruler to retain his throne.

### Q23. Correct Answer: C

The assertion is true. The reason reverses the proposal: the bill aimed to **remove**, not create, restrictions on qualified Indian judges’ jurisdiction over European British subjects.

### Q24. Correct Answer: B

Both statements are true, but the education commission does not explain the Factory Act’s limited protection. The Act’s narrow coverage and limited safeguards explain that limitation; the Hunter Commission concerned education.
