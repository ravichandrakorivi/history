# First Phase of Revolutionary Activities in India (1907–1917)

## 1. Revolutionary Nationalism — Basic Facts

- Revolutionary nationalism emerged prominently in the **first decade of the 20th century**.
- Revolutionary nationalists believed that British rule could be overthrown through **armed struggle, political assassinations, bomb attacks and organised revolutionary activities**.
- They rejected exclusive reliance on constitutional methods and petitions.
- Their activities included:
  - Secret revolutionary societies.
  - Political assassinations and attempted assassinations.
  - Attacks on government officials.
  - Revolutionary literature and propaganda.
  - Attempts to obtain arms and organise military uprisings.
  - Establishment of revolutionary organisations abroad.

### Revolutionary Nationalists vs Extremists

| Feature | Extremists | Revolutionary Nationalists |
|---|---|---|
| Important leaders | Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal, Lala Lajpat Rai | Aurobindo Ghosh (early association), Barindra Kumar Ghosh, Khudiram Bose, V.D. Savarkar |
| Principal methods | Swadeshi, Boycott, passive resistance, public mobilisation | Secret societies, armed actions and political assassinations |
| Organisational character | Mainly open political movements | Often clandestine organisations |
| Objective | Swaraj and national self-government | Overthrow of British rule |

**Prelims trap:** Militant or Extremist nationalism and revolutionary terrorism were related but **not identical movements**.

## 3. Revolutionary Activities in Bengal

### 3.1 Important Revolutionary Organisations

**Anushilan Samiti (1902)** was an early revolutionary organisation in Bengal (founded at **Calcutta** by **Satish Chandra Basu** and **Pramathanath Mitra**). Around 1906, two important revolutionary streams developed from the broader Anushilan network:

| Organisation | Year | Important Leader | Main Area of Activity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dacca Anushilan Samiti** | 1906 | Pulin Behari Das | Eastern Bengal (Dacca) |
| **Jugantar Group** | 1906 | Barindra Kumar Ghosh (brother of Aurobindo Ghosh) | Calcutta and Western Bengal |

- **Dacca Anushilan Samiti** developed as a major revolutionary organisation under **Pulin Behari Das**.
- **Jugantar** emerged from the Calcutta-based Anushilan revolutionary network and gradually acquired a distinct organisational identity.
- Both organisations advocated revolutionary methods against British rule, but developed different leadership structures and centres of activity.
- The **Jugantar Group** was closely associated with the Bengali revolutionary newspaper ***Jugantar***, launched in 1906.

**UPSC Prelims Point:** Jugantar was not originally an entirely unrelated organisation. It emerged from the broader Anushilan Samiti revolutionary network. However, the **Jugantar Group** and the ***Jugantar* newspaper** should not be confused.

### 3.2 Important Revolutionary Publications

| Publication | Personality / Association |

| *Bande Mataram* | Aurobindo Ghosh; English-language nationalist newspaper |
| *Sandhya* | Brahmabandhav Upadhyay |
| *Bhavani Mandir* | Aurobindo Ghosh |
| *Bartaman Rananiti* | Revolutionary literature associated with Bengal |

**Prelims distinction:** *Bande Mataram* was an English-language nationalist newspaper, whereas *Jugantar* was a Bengali revolutionary newspaper.

### 3.3 Muzaffarpur Bomb Incident (1908)

| Feature | Important Fact |
|---|---|
| Date | **30 April 1908** |
| Place | Muzaffarpur, Bihar |
| Revolutionaries | Khudiram Bose and Prafulla Chaki (Associated with Jugantar group of revolutionaries) |

### 3.4 Alipore Bomb Case (1908–1909)

Also known as the **Manicktala Conspiracy Case**.

| Feature | Important Fact |

**Important Prelims facts:**
- The case followed the Muzaffarpur bomb incident.
- **C.R. Das defended Aurobindo Ghosh**.
- Aurobindo was acquitted in 1909.
- Aurobindo later settled in **Pondicherry (1910)**.

### 3.5 Delhi Conspiracy Case (1912)

| Feature | Important Fact |
|---|---|
| Date | **23 December 1912** |
| Place | Delhi |
| Target | Viceroy Lord Hardinge II |
| Incident | Bomb thrown during a ceremonial procession |
| Important revolutionary | Rash Behari Bose |
| Other associated revolutionary | Basanta Kumar Biswas |
| Outcome | Lord Hardinge survived |

- The incident occurred during the ceremonial entry of the Viceroy into Delhi.
- Rash Behari Bose subsequently became associated with plans for a wider revolutionary uprising.

### 3.6 Bagha Jatin and the Indo-German Conspiracy

- **Bagha Jatin:** Jatindranath Mukherjee.
- Associated with the **Jugantar revolutionary network**.
- Attempted to obtain foreign assistance and arms for an anti-British uprising during the First World War.
- Died in **1915**, following an armed encounter with police near Balasore, Odisha.

**Prelims distinction:** Bagha Jatin is associated with the Bengal revolutionary movement and the Indo-German revolutionary plans during World War I.

## 4. Revolutionary Activities in Europe

### 4.1 Shyamji Krishna Varma and India House (London)

Shyamji Krishna Varma (1857–1930) was an Indian nationalist who established important organisations in London to promote Indian independence.

| Organisation / Publication | Year | Important facts                                          |
| -------------------------- | ---- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Indian Home Rule Society   | 1905 | Founded by Shyamji Krishna Varma in London               |
| India House                | 1905 | Established by Shyamji Krishna Varma in Highgate, London |
| The Indian Sociologist     | 1905 | Journal founded and published by Shyamji Krishna Varma   |

India House

- Functioned as a residence for Indian students and a centre of nationalist political activities.
- Became an important meeting place for Indian revolutionaries in Britain.
- Important associates included **V.D. Savarkar**, **Madan Lal Dhingra** and **V.V.S. Aiyar**.
- Shyamji Krishna Varma later moved to Paris and subsequently Geneva.

Important distinction: The **Indian Home Rule Society (1905)** established by **Shyamji Krishna Varma** was different from the **Home Rule Leagues** established in India in **1916** by **Bal Gangadhar Tilak** and **Annie Besant**.

### 4.2 V.D. Savarkar and Revolutionary Activities in London

Vinayak Damodar Savarkar (1883–1966) was associated with revolutionary organisations in both Maharashtra and London.

| Year | Event                                                      |
| ---- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1900 | Established Mitra Mela in Maharashtra                      |
| 1904 | Mitra Mela developed into Abhinav Bharat                   |
| 1906 | Travelled to London and became associated with India House |
| 1906 | Organised the **Free India Society** in London                 |
| 1909 | The Indian War of Independence, 1857 published             |
| 1910 | Arrested by British authorities                            |
| 1911 | Sent to Cellular Jail in the Andaman Islands               |

Important facts

- Savarkar promoted revolutionary nationalism among Indian students in London.
- His book The Indian War of Independence, 1857 interpreted the Revolt of 1857 as a national struggle against British rule.
- He was implicated in revolutionary activities connected with the **Nasik Conspiracy Case**.
- He was sentenced to transportation and imprisoned in the Cellular Jail.

Prelims distinction: **Savarkar**'s **Free India Society (1906)** should not be confused with **Shyamji Krishna Varma**'s **Indian Home Rule Society (1905)**.

## 5. Revolutionary Activities in North America

### 5.1 Background

Indian revolutionary activities in North America developed among Indian immigrants, particularly Punjabis living in the United States and Canada.

Important factors included:

- Racial discrimination and restrictive immigration policies.
- Discriminatory treatment of Indian immigrants.
- Opposition to British colonial rule in India.
- The growth of revolutionary nationalist organisations and publications abroad.

These activities eventually contributed to the establishment of the Ghadar Movement in 1913.

## 6. Komagata Maru Incident (1914)

The Komagata Maru Incident arose from discriminatory Canadian immigration policies restricting the entry of Indian immigrants.

### 6.1 Important Facts

| Feature                  | Important fact                      |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------------------- |
| Year                     | 1914                                |
| Ship                     | Komagata Maru                       |
| Organiser                | Gurdit Singh                        |
| Destination              | Vancouver, Canada                   |
| Number of passengers     | 376                                 |
| Passenger composition    | 340 Sikhs, 24 Muslims and 12 Hindus |
| Arrival at Vancouver     | 23 May 1914                         |
| Departure from Vancouver | 23 July 1914                        |
| Return to India          | September 1914                      |
| Major confrontation      | Budge Budge, near Calcutta          |

### 6.2 Canadian Immigration Restrictions

- Canadian authorities imposed restrictions on Asian immigration.
- One important restriction was the Continuous Journey Regulation (1908).
- It generally required immigrants to arrive in Canada through an uninterrupted journey from their country of origin.
- Direct steamship services from India to Canada were effectively unavailable.
- This requirement was used to restrict Indian immigration.

### 6.3 Sequence of Events

1. Gurdit Singh chartered the Komagata Maru to transport Indian passengers to Canada.
2. The ship arrived at Vancouver on 23 May 1914.
3. Canadian authorities refused entry to most passengers.
4. Following a prolonged dispute, the ship departed Vancouver on 23 July 1914.
5. It returned to India in September 1914.
6. On 29 September 1914, a confrontation occurred at Budge Budge, near Calcutta, between passengers and colonial authorities.
7. Several passengers were killed and others were arrested or detained.

### 6.4 Relationship with the Ghadar Movement

- The incident occurred during the growth of revolutionary nationalism among Indian immigrants in North America.
- Ghadar activists supported the cause of Indian immigrants facing discrimination.
- The incident intensified resentment against colonial rule and discriminatory immigration policies.

Important distinction: The Komagata Maru voyage was organised by Gurdit Singh, not by Lala Hardayal or Sohan Singh Bhakna. It was not itself a Ghadar Party military operation.

### Most important Prelims facts

- Komagata Maru — 1914.
- Gurdit Singh — organiser.
- 376 passengers.
- Destination — Vancouver, Canada.
- Continuous Journey Regulation — 1908.
- Budge Budge incident — 29 September 1914, near Calcutta.

## 7. Revolutionary Activities During the First World War (1914–1917)

### 7.1 First World War and Revolutionary Plans

- First World War: 1914–1918.
- Indian revolutionaries considered Britain's involvement in the war an opportunity to organise an anti-colonial uprising.
- They attempted to obtain arms, funds and international support.
- Plans included encouraging Indian soldiers in the British Indian Army to revolt.

Important revolutionary activities during this period included:

1. Ghadar uprising plans of 1915.
2. Indo-German revolutionary conspiracy.
3. Berlin Committee.
4. Provisional Government of India at Kabul.
5. Bagha Jatin's attempts to secure foreign arms.

### 7.2 Ghadar Uprising Plan (1915)

Following the outbreak of World War I, many Ghadar revolutionaries returned to India to organise an armed uprising.

Important personalities

- Rash Behari Bose.
- Kartar Singh Sarabha.
- Vishnu Ganesh Pingle.
- Sachindra Nath Sanyal.
- Other Ghadar revolutionaries and associates.

Plan

- Encourage Indian soldiers to revolt against British officers.
- Coordinate uprisings at military cantonments, particularly in northern India.
- Obtain weapons and military support.
- Launch a broader anti-British rebellion.

Important dates

- A coordinated uprising was initially planned for 21 February 1915.
- Following fears that the plan had been discovered, the date was advanced to 19 February 1915.
- British intelligence penetrated the conspiracy and suppressed the planned uprising.

Outcome

- The proposed uprising failed.
- Many revolutionaries were arrested.
- Several were prosecuted in the Lahore Conspiracy Cases.
- Rash Behari Bose escaped to Japan in 1915.

Prelims Point: The attempted Ghadar uprising of February 1915 was associated with Rash Behari Bose, Kartar Singh Sarabha and Vishnu Ganesh Pingle.

### 7.3 Lahore Conspiracy Cases (1915 onwards)

The Lahore Conspiracy Cases were associated with the attempted revolutionary uprising organised by Ghadar activists and their associates during the First World War.

| Feature           | Important fact                                        |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Principal period  | 1915 onwards                                          |
| Background        | Attempted Ghadar uprising                             |
| Important accused | Kartar Singh Sarabha, Vishnu Ganesh Pingle and others |
| Nature of case    | Conspiracy against British colonial rule              |
| Important outcome | Executions and long prison sentences                  |

Kartar Singh Sarabha

- Born in 1896, Punjab.
- Important young revolutionary of the Ghadar Movement.
- Associated with the Ghadar newspaper.
- Participated in plans for the 1915 uprising.
- Executed: 16 November 1915, Lahore.

Vishnu Ganesh Pingle

- Revolutionary from Maharashtra.
- Associated with the Ghadar Movement.
- Participated in preparations for the 1915 uprising.
- Executed: 16 November 1915, Lahore.

Prelims distinction: The **Lahore Conspiracy Cases of 1915** were associated with the **Ghadar uprising**. The **Lahore Conspiracy Case of 1929–1931** involved **Bhagat Singh**, **Sukhdev** and **Rajguru**.

### 7.4 Berlin Committee (1914)

The Berlin Committee, also called the Indian Independence Committee, was established by Indian revolutionaries in Germany during World War I.

| Feature                 | Important fact                                                   |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Established             | 1914                                                             |
| Place                   | Berlin, Germany                                                  |
| Other name              | **Indian Independence Committee**                                    |
| Important personalities | **Virendranath Chattopadhyaya**, Chempakaraman Pillai and associates |
| Objective               | Obtain German assistance for an uprising against British rule    |

Important activities

- Established contacts with German authorities.
- Attempted to obtain arms and financial assistance.
- Sought to coordinate revolutionary activities in India and abroad.
- Developed links with revolutionary plans involving Afghanistan and other regions.

Prelims Point: The **Berlin Committee (1914) was associated with Indian revolutionaries seeking German assistance against Britain during World War I**.

### 7.5 Indo-German Conspiracy

The Indo-German Conspiracy refers to revolutionary plans during World War I involving Indian nationalists and attempts to obtain assistance from Germany.

### Important participants and networks

- Berlin Committee.
- Ghadar revolutionaries.
- Jugantar revolutionaries.
- Bagha Jatin.
- Indian revolutionary contacts in Europe, North America and Asia.

### Objectives

- Obtain German arms and financial support.
- Organise uprisings in India.
- Encourage rebellion within the British Indian Army.
- Coordinate revolutionary activities from abroad.

Important clarification: The Indo-German Conspiracy was not a single organisation. It was a broader network of revolutionary plans and activities involving several groups.

### 7.6 Bagha Jatin and the Balasore Encounter (1915)

Bagha Jatin (1879–1915), whose full name was **Jatindranath Mukherjee**, was an important revolutionary associated with the **Jugantar** network in Bengal.

### 8.5 Most Important Prelims Traps

- India House (1905) was established by Shyamji Krishna Varma, not V.D. Savarkar.
- Free India Society (1906) was associated with Savarkar, not Shyamji Krishna Varma.
- Madan Lal Dhingra assassinated Curzon Wyllie, not Lord Curzon.
- Madame Cama unfurled an early nationalist flag at Stuttgart, Germany, in 1907.
- Ghadar Party was founded in North America in 1913, not in India.
- Sohan Singh Bhakna was the founding president of the Ghadar organisation.
- Lala Hardayal was an important organiser, not its founding president.
- Astoria, Oregon was the place of the Ghadar organisation's founding; San Francisco was its major headquarters and publication centre.
- Komagata Maru (1914) was organised by Gurdit Singh and sailed to Vancouver, Canada.
- The Ghadar uprising planned for February 1915 was discovered and suppressed by British authorities.
- Kartar Singh Sarabha was executed on 16 November 1915.
- Berlin Committee (1914) sought German assistance during World War I.
- Bagha Jatin died following the Balasore encounter in September 1915.
- Kabul Provisional Government (1915): President Raja Mahendra Pratap; Prime Minister Maulana Barkatullah.
- Kabul Government (1915) and Azad Hind Government (1943) were different organisations.
- The Lahore Conspiracy Cases of 1915 must not be confused with the later case involving Bhagat Singh and his associates.

Scope note: The HRA (1924), Kakori Train Action (1925), HSRA (1928), Bhagat Singh, Chandrashekhar Azad and the Central Legislative Assembly Bomb Incident (1929) belong to the later phase of revolutionary activities and should be covered separately.
