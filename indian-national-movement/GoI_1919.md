# Government of India Act, 1919 (Montagu–Chelmsford Reforms)
## UPSC Civil Services Preliminary Examination
### Study Notes and Practice Questions

# PART I — STUDY NOTES

## 1. Background

The **Government of India Act, 1919**, also known as the **Montagu–Chelmsford Reforms**, introduced significant constitutional changes in British India.

The reforms were associated with:

- **Edwin Samuel Montagu:** Secretary of State for India.
- **Lord Chelmsford:** Viceroy of India (1916–1921).

### Important developments

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1909 | Indian Councils Act (Morley–Minto Reforms) |
| 20 August 1917 | Montagu Declaration |
| 1918 | Montagu–Chelmsford Report |
| 1919 | Government of India Act enacted |
| 1921 | Reformed constitutional arrangements became operational |

### Montagu Declaration, 1917

On **20 August 1917**, Edwin Montagu announced the British government's policy of:

- Increasing the association of Indians in every branch of administration.
- Gradually developing self-governing institutions.
- Progressively realising responsible government in India.

However, the British government retained control over the pace and extent of constitutional reforms.

**Important:** The declaration did not promise immediate independence or Dominion Status.

---

## 2. Principal Features of the Government of India Act, 1919

| Feature | Provision |
|---|---|
| Dyarchy | Introduced in provinces |
| Provincial subjects | Divided into Reserved and Transferred categories |
| Central dyarchy | Not introduced |
| Central legislature | Bicameralism introduced |
| Legislative powers | Central and Provincial subjects |
| Provincial autonomy | Not established |
| Franchise | Expanded but remained limited |
| Separate electorates | Continued and extended |
| Governor-General | Retained extensive powers |
| Public Service Commission | Provision made for its establishment |
| Statutory Commission | Provision for review after ten years |

---

## 3. Dyarchy in Provinces

**Dyarchy was the most important feature of the Government of India Act, 1919.**

The term *dyarchy* means dual government.

Under this system, provincial subjects were divided into two categories:

1. Reserved subjects.
2. Transferred subjects.

### 3.1 Reserved Subjects

Reserved subjects were administered by the **Governor with the assistance of executive councillors**.

These councillors were not responsible to the elected provincial legislature.

Important reserved subjects included:

| Reserved subjects |
|---|
| Law and order |
| Police |
| Administration of justice |
| Land revenue |
| Irrigation |
| Finance |

### 3.2 Transferred Subjects

Transferred subjects were administered by the **Governor with the assistance of ministers**.

These ministers were responsible to the provincial legislature.

Important transferred subjects included:

| Transferred subjects |
|---|
| Education |
| Public health |
| Local self-government |
| Agriculture |
| Industries |
| Public works of a local character |

### 3.3 Reserved versus Transferred Subjects

| Feature | Reserved Subjects | Transferred Subjects |
|---|---|---|
| Administration | Governor and executive councillors | Governor and ministers |
| Responsibility to legislature | Executive councillors were not responsible | Ministers were responsible |
| Nature | Under stronger official control | Limited responsible government |
| Examples | Police, finance, land revenue | Education, public health, agriculture |

### Important distinctions

- **Police was a reserved subject.**
- **Education was a transferred subject.**
- **Finance was a reserved subject.**
- **Agriculture was a transferred subject.**
- **Public health was a transferred subject.**
- **Land revenue was a reserved subject.**

These distinctions are important for statement-based UPSC questions.

### 3.4 Limitations of Dyarchy

Dyarchy did not establish complete responsible government in the provinces.

The Governor retained important powers, including:

- Control over reserved subjects.
- Discretionary authority in specified matters.
- Powers relating to legislation and administration.
- Authority to override ministers in certain circumstances.

Consequently, elected Indian ministers exercised only limited authority.

---

## 4. Bicameral Legislature at the Centre

The Government of India Act, 1919, introduced **bicameralism at the Centre**.

The central legislature consisted of two Houses:

1. Council of State.
2. Legislative Assembly.

### 4.1 Composition

| Feature | Council of State | Legislative Assembly |
|---|---|---|
| Position | Upper House | Lower House |
| Membership | 60 | 145 |
| Elected members | 34 | 104 |
| Nominated members | 26 | 41 |
| Normal tenure | 5 years | 3 years |

The composition shown is the original arrangement under the reforms.

### 4.2 Important facts

- The **Council of State** was the Upper House.
- The **Legislative Assembly** was the Lower House.
- Both Houses contained elected and nominated members.
- The Governor-General retained extensive powers over legislation.

### Important distinction

**The Government of India Act, 1919, introduced bicameralism at the Centre, not in the provinces.**

The Government of India Act, 1935, subsequently provided for bicameral provincial legislatures in six provinces.

---

## 5. Division of Subjects Between Centre and Provinces

The Act divided administrative and legislative subjects into:

1. Central subjects.
2. Provincial subjects.

### Central Subjects

Important examples included:

- Defence.
- Foreign affairs.
- Railways.
- Currency.
- Posts and telegraphs.

### Provincial Subjects

Important examples included:

- Education.
- Agriculture.
- Public health.
- Police.
- Local self-government.

Provincial subjects were further divided into reserved and transferred categories for administrative purposes.

### Important distinction

| Government of India Act, 1919 | Government of India Act, 1935 |
|---|---|
| Central and Provincial subjects | Federal, Provincial and Concurrent Lists |
| Provincial subjects divided into reserved and transferred categories | Provincial dyarchy abolished |
| No separate Concurrent List | Concurrent List introduced |

---

## 6. Powers of the Governor-General

The Governor-General retained substantial executive and legislative authority.

Important powers included:

- Assenting to or withholding assent from legislation.
- Reserving Bills for consideration by the British Crown.
- Certifying certain Bills and grants.
- Promulgating ordinances under specified conditions.
- Exercising extensive control over central administration.

### Important distinction

The central executive was **not responsible to the central legislature**.

The Act did not establish parliamentary government at the Centre.

---

## 7. Franchise and Separate Electorates

### 7.1 Franchise

The Act expanded voting rights.

However, the electorate remained restricted by qualifications such as:

- Property.
- Taxation.
- Education.

**Universal adult franchise was not introduced.**

Only a small proportion of the population could vote.

### 7.2 Separate Electorates

The Act continued separate electorates for Muslims and extended separate representation to additional communities.

Separate electorates were extended to:

- Sikhs.
- Indian Christians.
- Anglo-Indians.
- Europeans.

### Important distinction

| Indian Councils Act, 1909 | Government of India Act, 1919 |
|---|---|
| Introduced separate electorates for Muslims | Continued and extended separate electorates |
| Associated with Morley–Minto Reforms | Associated with Montagu–Chelmsford Reforms |

---

## 8. Public Service Commission

The Government of India Act, 1919, provided for the establishment of a Public Service Commission in India.

### Important chronology

| Year | Development |
|---|---|
| 1919 | Statutory provision for a Public Service Commission |
| 1924 | Lee Commission recommended public service reforms |
| 1926 | First Public Service Commission established in India |
| 1935 | Government of India Act provided for Federal, Provincial and Joint Public Service Commissions |

### Important facts

- The first Public Service Commission was established on **1 October 1926**.
- Its first chairman was **Sir Ross Barker**.

**Important distinction:** The 1919 Act provided for a Public Service Commission, but the first commission was actually established in 1926.

---

## 9. Separation of Provincial Budgets

The Act provided for greater financial separation between the Centre and provinces.

- Provincial budgets were separated from the central budget.
- Provincial legislatures received limited financial powers.
- Governors retained important financial authority.

**Important:** Separate provincial budgets did not mean complete financial autonomy.

---

## 10. Statutory Commission

The Act provided for a statutory commission to review the working of the constitutional reforms after ten years.

### Simon Commission

| Feature | Fact |
|---|---|
| Appointed | 1927 |
| Chairman | Sir John Simon |
| Members | Seven |
| Indian members | None |
| Arrival in India | 1928 |

### Important distinction

The Simon Commission was appointed in **1927**, before the completion of the contemplated ten-year period.

Its exclusion of Indians generated widespread opposition.

---

## 11. Limitations of the Government of India Act, 1919

The Act did not establish fully responsible government.

Its principal limitations were:

1. Dyarchy divided provincial administration into two categories.
2. Governors retained extensive discretionary powers.
3. The central executive remained unaccountable to the elected legislature.
4. Franchise remained restricted.
5. Separate electorates continued.
6. Provincial ministers had limited control over finances and administration.

---

## 12. Comparison: Government of India Acts of 1909, 1919 and 1935

| Feature | 1909 Act | 1919 Act | 1935 Act |
|---|---|---|---|
| Popular name | Morley–Minto Reforms | Montagu–Chelmsford Reforms | Government of India Act, 1935 |
| Separate electorates | Introduced for Muslims | Continued and extended | Further continued and extended |
| Dyarchy in provinces | No | Introduced | Abolished |
| Dyarchy at Centre | No | No | Proposed but not implemented |
| Bicameralism at Centre | No | Introduced | New federal bicameral legislature proposed |
| Provincial autonomy | No | No | Introduced |
| Legislative lists | No statutory three-list division | Central and Provincial subjects | Federal, Provincial and Concurrent Lists |
| Federal Court | No | No | Established in 1937 |
| Public Service Commission | No statutory provision of this kind | Provision made | Federal, Provincial and Joint Commissions provided |
