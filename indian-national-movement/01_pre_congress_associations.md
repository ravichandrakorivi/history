# Beginning of Modern Nationalism, Pre-Congress Associations and the Moderate Phase (1885–1905)

## 1. Factors Responsible for the Growth of Modern Nationalism in India

| Factor | Important Prelims Facts |
|---|---|
| Colonial economic exploitation | Recognition of the conflict between Indian economic interests and British colonial interests |
| Political and administrative unification | Common administration, judiciary, codified laws, civil services, railways and telegraphs |
| Western education | Spread of liberalism, democracy, secularism, equality and nationalism |
| English language | Facilitated communication among educated Indians from different linguistic regions |
| Press and literature | English and vernacular newspapers spread nationalist ideas and criticised colonial policies |
| Rediscovery of India's past | Historical research strengthened national pride and challenged colonial claims of Indian inferiority |
| Socio-religious reform movements | Challenged social evils and encouraged social and intellectual awakening |
| Educated middle class | Lawyers, journalists, teachers and doctors led early nationalist political associations |
| International movements | French Revolution; national movements in Italy, Greece and Ireland; independence movements in Latin America |
| Racial discrimination | Discriminatory colonial policies and the Ilbert Bill controversy encouraged political opposition |

## 2. Colonial Policies and the Growth of Nationalism

### 2.1 Important Events and Colonial Policies

| Year | Event / Policy | Viceroy | Prelims Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1876 | Maximum ICS examination age reduced from 21 to 19 | Lord Lytton | Provoked nationalist opposition; Indian Association organised agitation |
| 1877 | Delhi Durbar | Lord Lytton | Queen Victoria proclaimed Empress of India; held during severe famine |
| 1878 | Vernacular Press Act | Lord Lytton | Imposed restrictions on Indian-language newspapers |
| 1878 | Arms Act | Lord Lytton | Imposed discriminatory restrictions on arms possession; Europeans enjoyed exemptions |
| 1882 | Repeal of Vernacular Press Act | Lord Ripon | Removed restrictions imposed under the 1878 Act |
| 1883 | Ilbert Bill controversy | Lord Ripon | European opposition to Indian judges trying European British subjects |

### 2.2 Ilbert Bill Controversy (1883)

- **Viceroy:** Lord Ripon.
- **Named after:** C.P. Ilbert, Law Member of the Viceroy's Council.
- **Objective:** Remove racial discrimination in the criminal jurisdiction of Indian judges over European British subjects.
- **Opposition:** Strongly opposed by the European community in India.
- **Outcome:** The Bill was modified, substantially weakening its original objective.
- **Significance:** Exposed racial discrimination and encouraged organised nationalist political agitation.

### 2.3 Important Prelims Distinctions

1. **Vernacular Press Act (1878):** Enacted under Lord Lytton; repealed under Lord Ripon in 1882.
2. **Arms Act (1878):** Associated with Lord Lytton, not Lord Ripon.
3. **Ilbert Bill (1883):** Concerned judicial powers and racial equality, not legislative representation.
4. **ICS age reduction:** Associated with Lord Lytton; the Indian Association organised agitation against it.
5. **Delhi Durbar (1877):** Associated with Lord Lytton and the proclamation of Queen Victoria as Empress of India.

## 3. Political Associations Before the Indian National Congress

### 3.1 Important Political Associations (Chronological Order)

| Organisation | Year | Place | Founders / Associated Leaders | Important Facts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bangabhasha Prakasika Sabha | 1836 | Calcutta | Associates of Raja Rammohan Roy | One of the earliest political associations in Bengal |
| Landholders' Society (Zamindari Association) | 1838 | Calcutta | Dwarkanath Tagore, Radhakanta Deb and other zamindars | Protection of landed interests; constitutional methods |
| Bengal British India Society | 1843 | Calcutta | George Thompson and other political activists | Promoted public welfare and political rights |
| British Indian Association | 1851 | Calcutta | Merger of Landholders' Society and Bengal British India Society | Demanded legislative and administrative reforms |
| Bombay Association | 1852 | Bombay | Jagannath Shankarsheth and other leaders | Early political association in Bombay Presidency |
| East India Association | 1866 | London | Dadabhai Naoroji | Sought to influence British public opinion on Indian affairs |
| Poona Sarvajanik Sabha | 1870 | Poona | Ganesh Vasudeo Joshi (father-in-law of Gopal Krishna Gokhale) and other leaders; M.G. Ranade was a prominent associate | Acted as a link between government and people |
| Indian League | 1875 | Calcutta | Sisir Kumar Ghosh | Promoted political education and nationalism |
| Indian Association (Indian National Association) | 1876 | Calcutta | Surendranath Banerjea and Ananda Mohan Bose | Organised political campaigns and the Indian National Conference |
| Madras Mahajan Sabha | 1884 | Madras | M. Viraraghavachari, G. Subramania Iyer and P. Ananda Charlu (the Hindu) | Important political association in Madras Presidency |
| Bombay Presidency Association | 1885 | Bombay | Pherozshah Mehta, Badruddin Tyabji and K.T. Telang | Important political association in Bombay Presidency |

### 3.2 British Indian Association (1851)

- **Formation:** Merger of Landholders' Society (1838) and Bengal British India Society (1843).
- **Place:** Calcutta.
- **Important demands:**
  - Establishment of a representative legislature.
  - Separation of executive and judicial functions.
  - Reduction of salaries of higher officials.
  - Abolition of salt duty, abkari (excise) duty and stamp duty.

### 3.3 Indian Association (1876)

- **Founders:** Surendranath Banerjea and Ananda Mohan Bose.
- **Place:** Calcutta.
- **Important campaigns:**
  - Opposed reduction of the maximum ICS examination age from 21 to 19 years.
  - Demanded simultaneous ICS examinations in India and Britain.
  - Advocated Indianisation of higher government services.
  - Opposed the Arms Act (1878) and Vernacular Press Act (1878).
  - Supported the Ilbert Bill (1883).

**Indian National Conference**

| Year | Event | Place |
|---|---|---|
| 1883 | First Indian National Conference | Calcutta |
| 1885 | Second Indian National Conference | Calcutta |
| 1886 | National Conference movement joined the Congress movement | Calcutta, at the second INC session |

### 3.4 Important Prelims Distinctions

1. **East India Association (1866):** Founded by Dadabhai Naoroji in **London**, not Bombay.
2. **Bombay Association (1852)** and **Bombay Presidency Association (1885)** were different organisations.
3. **British Indian Association (1851):** Formed through the merger of two earlier Bengal associations.
4. **Indian League (1875):** Associated with Sisir Kumar Ghosh; **Indian Association (1876):** Founded by Surendranath Banerjea and Ananda Mohan Bose.
5. **Poona Sarvajanik Sabha (1870):** Associated with Ganesh Vasudeo Joshi and M.G. Ranade.
6. **Indian National Conference:** First held in 1883, before the establishment of the Indian National Congress in 1885.
7. **Indian Association and INC:** The National Conference movement was absorbed into the Congress movement in 1886; avoid treating this as proof that the Indian Association itself ceased to exist in that year.

### 4.1 Establishment of the INC

|---|---|

### 4.3 Early Objectives of the INC

- Promote national unity across regions, religions and communities.
- Establish contact and cooperation among political workers from different parts of India.
- Formulate and present common political and economic demands.
- Develop and organise public opinion on important national issues.
- Promote political education and nationalist consciousness.

### 4.4 Important Prelims Distinctions

1. **A.O. Hume:** Organiser of the first INC session; **W.C. Bonnerjee:** First INC president.
2. **Poona:** Originally proposed venue; **Bombay:** Actual venue of the first session.
3. **1885:** First INC session at Bombay; **1886:** Second session at Calcutta.
4. **Badruddin Tyabji (1887):** First Muslim INC president.
5. **George Yule (1888):** First English INC president.
6. **Kadambini Ganguly (1890):** Addressed the Calcutta session; she was not the session president.
7. **Lord Dufferin:** Viceroy when the INC was founded.

## 5. Moderate Phase of the Indian National Congress (1885–1905)

### 5.1 Important Moderate Leaders

| Leader | Important Associations / Contributions |
|---|---|
| Dadabhai Naoroji | Drain of Wealth Theory; *Poverty and Un-British Rule in India* (1901) |
| Pherozshah Mehta | Prominent Bombay Moderate; associated with the Bombay Presidency Association (1885) |
| W.C. Bonnerjee | First President of INC (1885) |
| Surendranath Banerjea | Indian Association (1876); associated with *The Bengalee* newspaper |
| Dinshaw E. Wacha | Economic criticism of British rule; prominent Congress organiser |
| Gopal Krishna Gokhale | Servants of India Society (1905); political mentor of Mahatma Gandhi |
| R.C. Dutt | *The Economic History of India*; criticism of colonial land revenue and economic policies |
| Badruddin Tyabji | First Muslim President of INC (1887) |
| M.G. Ranade | Poona Sarvajanik Sabha; social and economic reform |

### 5.2 Methods of the Moderates

- Followed **constitutional and peaceful methods**: petitions, resolutions, memorials, public meetings and representations.
- Attempted to influence British Parliament and public opinion in Britain.
- Emphasised political education and the development of public opinion in India.
- Initially believed in British liberalism and gradual constitutional reforms.
- Did not demand immediate complete independence from British rule.

**Important organisation and publication**

| Year | Organisation / Publication | Important Fact |
|---|---|---|
| 1889 | British Committee of the INC | Established in London to influence British public opinion |
| 1890 | *India* | Journal associated with the British Committee of the INC |

### 5.3 Indian Councils Act, 1892

| Feature | Provision |
|---|---|
| Legislative councils | Increased the number of additional members in central and provincial legislative councils |
| Representation | Introduced an indirect electoral element through recommendations by specified bodies |
| Official majority | Retained in the Central Legislative Council |
| Budget | Members could discuss the budget but could not vote on it |
| Questions | Members could ask questions subject to prescribed conditions |
| Supplementary questions | Not permitted |
| Executive responsibility | Executive remained unaccountable to the legislative councils |

**Important Prelims Distinctions**

1. The Indian Councils Act, 1892 did **not explicitly use the word "election"**, although an indirect electoral element was introduced.
2. Members could **discuss the budget but could not vote on it**.
3. Members could ask questions but **could not ask supplementary questions**.
4. The Act did not introduce responsible government or parliamentary control over the executive.
5. The Act did not introduce direct elections to legislative councils.

## 6. Achievements and Limitations of the Moderates

### 6.1 Achievements

- Established the INC as an all-India political platform.
- Developed an economic critique of colonial rule, particularly the **Drain of Wealth Theory**.
- Popularised demands for representative institutions, Indianisation of services and civil liberties.
- Developed political awareness among educated Indians.
- Prepared the organisational and intellectual foundations for later nationalist movements.

### 6.2 Limitations

- Social base largely confined to educated, urban and middle-class Indians.
- Limited participation by peasants, workers and women.
- Continued faith in British liberalism and gradual constitutional reform.
- Relied mainly on petitions and constitutional agitation rather than mass mobilisation.
- Failed to secure substantial political power or responsible government during the early phase.

## 7. British Government's Attitude Towards the INC

### 7.1 Important Personalities and Organisations

| Personality / Organisation | Year | Important Prelims Fact |
|---|---|---|
| Lord Dufferin | 1884–1888 | Viceroy when the INC was founded; later criticised Congress and its political demands |
| United Indian Patriotic Association | 1888 | Associated with Sir Syed Ahmad Khan and Raja Shiv Prasad Singh; opposed Congress politics |
| Lord Curzon | 1899–1905 | Expressed the hope that Congress would decline and disappear |

### 7.2 Important Facts

- The colonial government's attitude towards Congress became increasingly hostile as its demands expanded.
- Lord Dufferin criticised Congress and its claim to represent Indian public opinion.
- The United Indian Patriotic Association opposed the Congress and sought to counter its political influence.
- British authorities encouraged divisions among Indian political and social groups to weaken nationalist unity.

### 7.3 Prelims Distinctions

1. **Lord Dufferin:** Viceroy during the establishment of INC in 1885.
2. **Lord Curzon:** Viceroy during the Partition of Bengal (1905).
3. **United Indian Patriotic Association (1888):** Associated with Sir Syed Ahmad Khan and Raja Shiv Prasad Singh.
4. **British Committee of INC (1889):** Established in London to influence British public opinion.
5. **Indian Councils Act (1892):** Permitted budget discussion, not budget voting.
6. **Moderates:** Initially demanded constitutional reforms and greater Indian participation, not immediate complete independence.

## UPSC CSE Prelims Practice Questions : Beginning of Modern Nationalism, Pre-Congress Associations and the Moderate Phase (1885–1905)

### Q1. Which of the following best explains the growth of modern Indian nationalism?

<div>
<strong>A.</strong> It arose solely from the introduction of Western education.<br>
<strong>B.</strong> It was primarily a revival of ancient political institutions.<br>
<strong>C.</strong> It developed from several factors, including colonial unification and resistance to colonial exploitation.<br>
<strong>D.</strong> It began only after the establishment of the Indian National Congress.
</div>

<br>

**Answer: C**

> **Explanation:** Nationalism grew through multiple influences: economic contradictions under colonialism, administrative unification, modern education, press, reform movements and opposition to racial discrimination.
