# Indian Councils Act, 1909 (Morley–Minto Reforms)

# PART I — STUDY NOTES

## 1. Background

The **Indian Councils Act, 1909**, popularly known as the **Morley–Minto Reforms**, introduced important changes in the legislative councils of British India.

Its most significant provision was the **introduction of separate electorates for Muslims**.

The Act expanded legislative councils and permitted limited Indian participation in legislative proceedings, but it did not establish responsible government.

### Important personalities

| Personality | Position |
|---|---|
| **Lord Morley (John Morley)** | Secretary of State for India |
| **Lord Minto II** | Viceroy of India (1905–1910) |

### Important developments

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1892 | Indian Councils Act expanded legislative councils |
| 1905 | Partition of Bengal |
| 1906 | Simla Deputation met Lord Minto |
| 1906 | All India Muslim League established at Dacca |
| 1907 | Surat Split in the Indian National Congress |
| 1909 | Indian Councils Act enacted |

### Simla Deputation, 1906

- Date: **1 October 1906**.
- Led by **Aga Khan III**.
- Met Viceroy Lord Minto at Simla.
- Demanded separate political representation for Muslims, including separate electorates.

**Prelims distinction:** The Simla Deputation preceded the establishment of the All India Muslim League in December 1906.

## 2. Principal Features of the Indian Councils Act, 1909

| Feature | Provision |
|---|---|
| Legislative councils | Expanded at the Centre and in provinces |
| Elections | Introduced an expanded indirect electoral system |
| Separate electorates | Introduced for Muslims |
| Indian representation | Increased in legislative councils |
| Official majority at Centre | Retained |
| Non-official majority in provinces | Permitted |
| Legislative powers | Expanded discussion and questioning rights |
| Budget | Could be discussed, but not voted upon as a whole |
| Responsible government | Not introduced |
| Dyarchy | Not introduced |
| Parliamentary executive | Not established |

---

## 3. Expansion of Legislative Councils

The Act enlarged both the Imperial Legislative Council and provincial legislative councils.

### 3.1 Central Legislative Council

The maximum number of additional members of the Governor-General's Legislative Council was increased from **16 to 60**.

| Feature | Provision |
|---|---|
| Maximum additional members | 60 |
| Official majority | Retained |
| Indian representation | Increased |
| Election | Indirect electoral arrangements expanded |

**Important:** The figure of 60 refers to the maximum number of additional members, not necessarily the total membership including ex officio members.

### 3.2 Provincial Legislative Councils

- Membership was substantially increased.
- Non-official members could constitute a majority.
- However, non-official members included both elected and nominated members.

### Important distinction

**Non-official majority does not necessarily mean elected majority.**

| Central Legislative Council | Provincial Legislative Councils |
|---|---|
| Official majority retained | Non-official majority permitted |
| British executive retained control | Governors retained extensive powers |
| No responsible government | No responsible government |

---

## 4. Introduction of Separate Electorates for Muslims

**This was the most significant feature of the Indian Councils Act, 1909.**

### 4.1 Meaning of Separate Electorate

Under a separate electorate:

- Muslim voters elected representatives to seats reserved for Muslims.
- Voters belonging to other communities did not participate in electing representatives from these Muslim constituencies.

Thus, electoral representation was organised partly on religious lines.

### 4.2 Separate Electorate versus Reserved Seat

| Separate Electorate | Reserved Seat under a Joint Electorate |
|---|---|
| Only voters belonging to the specified community elect its representatives | All eligible voters of the constituency participate in the election |
| Electorate is separated by community | Electorate remains common |
| Introduced for Muslims in 1909 | Different from the present system of SC/ST reservation in legislatures |

### 4.3 Historical significance

- The Act formally introduced communal electorates for Muslims into the constitutional framework of British India.
- Separate electorates were subsequently extended to additional communities under later reforms.

### Important chronology

| Act | Separate electorates |
|---|---|
| Indian Councils Act, 1909 | Introduced for Muslims |
| Government of India Act, 1919 | Continued and extended |
| Government of India Act, 1935 | Further continued and extended |

**Prelims distinction:** The Indian Councils Act, 1909, did not introduce separate electorates for all religious communities.

---

## 5. Electoral System

The Act introduced a more elaborate system of elections to legislative councils.

However, elections were largely **indirect**.

### Important features

- Members were elected through specified electoral bodies and constituencies.
- Representation was provided to various interests, including landholders and commercial bodies.
- Voting rights were limited.
- Universal adult franchise was not introduced.

### Important distinction

The Act expanded the electoral principle but did not establish direct elections based on universal adult franchise.

---

## 6. Powers of Legislative Councils

The Act enlarged the deliberative functions of legislative councils.

### Important powers

Members could:

1. Discuss the annual financial statement.
2. Move resolutions on the budget and matters of general public interest, subject to restrictions.
3. Ask supplementary questions, subject to procedural rules.
4. Participate more actively in legislative discussions.

### Limitations

- Resolutions were generally recommendatory rather than binding.
- Members could not compel the executive to accept their recommendations.
- The executive remained outside legislative control.
- The legislature could not remove the government through a vote of no-confidence.

### Important distinction

**The Act expanded legislative discussion, not legislative control over the executive.**

---

## 7. Indian Representation in Executive Councils

An important development associated with the Morley–Minto reform period was the appointment of an Indian to the Viceroy's Executive Council.

### Satyendra Prasanna Sinha

| Feature | Fact |
|---|---|
| Name | Satyendra Prasanna Sinha |
| Appointment | 1909 |
| Position | Law Member of the Viceroy's Executive Council |
| Significance | First Indian appointed to the Viceroy's Executive Council |

### Important clarification

S.P. Sinha's appointment was an executive development associated with the reform period. It should not be confused with an elected position in the Imperial Legislative Council.

---

## 8. Limitations of the Indian Councils Act, 1909

The Act did not establish responsible government.

Its principal limitations were:

1. **Separate electorates:** Introduced communal representation for Muslims.
2. **Limited franchise:** Most Indians remained excluded from voting.
3. **Official majority at Centre:** Government control over the central legislature continued.
4. **Limited legislative powers:** Resolutions were not binding on the executive.
5. **No executive responsibility:** The executive was not accountable to elected representatives.
6. **Indirect elections:** Popular participation remained restricted.

---
