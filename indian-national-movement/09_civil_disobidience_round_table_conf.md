# Civil Disobedience Movement (1930–1934) and Round Table Conferences (1930–1932)

## 1. Background to the Civil Disobedience Movement

### 1.1 Calcutta Congress Session, December 1928

- President: Motilal Nehru.
- The Nehru Report recommended Dominion Status for India.
- Younger Congress leaders, including Jawaharlal Nehru and Subhas Chandra Bose, demanded Purna Swaraj (complete independence) instead.
- A compromise was reached: the British government was given one year, until the end of 1929, to accept the demand for Dominion Status.
- If the demand was not accepted, Congress would adopt complete independence as its objective and launch civil disobedience.

### 1.2 Irwin Declaration, 31 October 1929

- Issued by Lord Irwin, Viceroy of India.
- Declared that Dominion Status was the natural outcome of India's constitutional progress contemplated under British policy.
- Promised a Round Table Conference after the Simon Commission submitted its report.
- No definite timetable was announced for granting Dominion Status.

### 1.3 Delhi Manifesto, 2 November 1929

Prominent Indian political leaders laid down conditions for participating in the proposed Round Table Conference:

1. Dominion Status should be accepted immediately as the basis for constitutional discussions, and the conference should frame a constitution to implement it.
2. Congress should receive majority representation at the conference.
3. Political prisoners should receive general amnesty, accompanied by a conciliatory government policy.

Outcome: Lord Irwin did not accept these conditions.

Prelims distinction: The Delhi Manifesto of 1929 must not be confused with the Delhi Proposals of 1927, associated with Muhammad Ali Jinnah.

## 2. Lahore Congress Session, December 1929

- President: Jawaharlal Nehru.
- Venue: Lahore.
- Congress formally adopted Purna Swaraj as its objective.

### Major resolutions

- Complete independence would replace Dominion Status as Congress's objective.
- The proposed Round Table Conference would be boycotted.
- The Congress Working Committee was authorised to launch a Civil Disobedience Movement, including non-payment of taxes.
- Congress members of legislatures were asked to resign.
- 26 January 1930 was designated Independence Day.

### Important events

- 31 December 1929: Jawaharlal Nehru hoisted the tricolour on the banks of the River Ravi.
- 26 January 1930: Independence Day was observed throughout India, and the Independence Pledge was taken.

The pledge asserted India's right to complete independence and criticised British economic, political and cultural domination.

Prelims distinction: The observance of 26 January 1930 as Independence Day preceded the coming into force of the Constitution on 26 January 1950.

## 3. Gandhi's Eleven Demands, 1930

Gandhi placed eleven demands before the British government as a final attempt to secure concessions before launching civil disobedience.

| Category                   | Demands                                                                                                                                                                               |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Administrative and general | Reduce expenditure on the army and civil services by 50%; introduce prohibition; reform the CID; modify the Arms Act; release political prisoners; accept the Postal Reservation Bill |
| Trade and economic         | Reduce the rupee–sterling exchange ratio to 1 shilling 4 pence; protect Indian textiles; reserve coastal shipping for Indians                                                         |
| Agrarian and popular       | Reduce land revenue by 50%; abolish the salt tax and government salt monopoly                                                                                                         |

### Why was salt selected?

- Salt was a necessity consumed by rich and poor alike.
- The salt tax affected the poorest sections disproportionately.
- Defying the salt law provided a simple and widely understandable form of protest.
- It allowed ordinary people to participate directly in civil disobedience.

Important: Salt was chosen as a symbol of resistance to colonial taxation and monopoly, not because the movement was restricted to salt-related grievances.

## 4. Dandi March and Salt Satyagraha, 1930

| Feature              | Fact                        |
| -------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Leader               | Mahatma Gandhi              |
| Starting point       | Sabarmati Ashram, Ahmedabad |
| Destination          | Dandi, Gujarat              |
| Started              | 12 March 1930               |

| Initial companions   | 78 selected satyagrahis     |

- Gandhi informed Lord Irwin of his proposed action on 2 March 1930.
- The march passed through villages of Gujarat.
- On 6 April, Gandhi broke the salt law at Dandi, marking the beginning of nationwide salt-law defiance.
- Gandhi encouraged salt manufacture, boycott of foreign cloth, picketing of liquor shops and non-payment of taxes where people were sufficiently organised.

### Difference between Non-Cooperation and Civil Disobedience

| Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–22)                                                          | Civil Disobedience Movement (1930–34)                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Primarily withdrawal of cooperation from colonial institutions                              | Deliberate violation of colonial laws                                   |
| Objective initially expressed as Swaraj alongside redress of Punjab and Khilafat grievances | Explicit objective of Purna Swaraj                                      |
| Boycott of schools, courts, councils and foreign goods prominent                            | Salt-law violations, no-tax campaigns and forest-law defiance prominent |
| Stronger Muslim participation associated with the Khilafat Movement                         | Muslim participation more uneven, though substantial in some regions    |
| Gandhi suspended the movement after Chauri Chaura                                           | Movement suspended under the Gandhi–Irwin Pact and later resumed        |

Both movements were based on mass mobilisation and the principle of non-violence.

### 5.2 No-revenue versus no-rent campaigns

| Campaign          | Who withheld payment?                     | Payment withheld from whom?   |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| No-revenue        | Landholders or revenue-paying cultivators | Colonial government           |
| No-rent           | Tenants                                   | Landlords or zamindars        |
| No-chaukidari-tax | Rural residents liable for the tax        | Colonial local administration |

The no-chaukidari-tax movement was particularly important in Bihar and Bengal.

### 5.3 Peshawar and the Khudai Khidmatgars

- Leader: Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan, also called Frontier Gandhi or Badshah Khan.
- Organised the Khudai Khidmatgars, popularly known as the Red Shirts.
- The organisation emphasised non-violence, social reform and resistance to colonial rule.
- Ghaffar Khan was associated with the Pashto publication Pukhtoon.
- 23 April 1930: Arrests of Congress leaders triggered major demonstrations in Peshawar.
- Soldiers of the Garhwal Rifles refused to fire on unarmed demonstrators.

Prelims significance: The movement demonstrated substantial participation by Muslims in the North-West Frontier Province, despite relatively lower Muslim participation in the Civil Disobedience Movement elsewhere.

### 5.4 Sholapur uprising, May 1930

- Followed Gandhi's arrest.
- Textile workers and other residents attacked symbols of colonial authority.
- A temporary breakdown of colonial administration occurred.
- Martial law was imposed to restore control.

### 5.5 Dharasana Salt Satyagraha, 21 May 1930

- Gandhi was arrested before the proposed action at Dharasana Salt Works.
- Sarojini Naidu, Imam Sahib and Manilal Gandhi were associated with its leadership.
- Peaceful satyagrahis faced severe police repression.
- American journalist Webb Miller reported the violence, attracting international attention.

### 5.6 Rani Gaidinliu and the Naga resistance

- Rani Gaidinliu was associated with the Heraka movement.
- She followed the leadership of Haipou Jadonang.
- The movement combined religious and social reform with opposition to British rule.
- Jadonang was executed in 1931.
- Gaidinliu was arrested in 1932 and sentenced to life imprisonment.
- She was released in 1947, following orders issued by the Interim Government in 1946.

Important distinction: This resistance had its own regional and religious origins and should not be treated simply as a Congress-organised Salt Satyagraha.

## 6. Participation and Impact of the Civil Disobedience Movement

### Major participating groups

- Women: Participated in salt satyagraha, picketing of liquor and foreign-cloth shops, and public demonstrations. Sarojini Naidu and Kamaladevi Chattopadhyay were prominent participants.
- Peasants: Active in Gujarat, Bihar and the United Provinces through revenue, rent and tax resistance.
- Merchants and business groups: Supported the boycott of foreign goods, although enthusiasm declined as the movement continued.
- Students and youth: Participated in demonstrations, boycotts and volunteer activities.
- Workers: Participated in several industrial centres, notably Sholapur.
- Tribal and forest-dependent communities: Defied forest restrictions in several provinces.
- Muslims: Participation was uneven and generally lower than during Non-Cooperation, but substantial in the North-West Frontier Province and some other areas.

### Impact

- Imports of foreign cloth declined.
- Government revenue from liquor, excise and land revenue was affected.
- Women entered nationalist public activity on an unprecedented scale.
- Congress expanded its influence among peasants and other social groups.
- The movement attracted international attention, particularly after the Dandi March and Dharasana Satyagraha.

The movement did not immediately achieve independence, but it increased the political pressure on the colonial government.

## 7. Gandhi–Irwin Pact, 5 March 1931

Also called the Delhi Pact.

Signed between Mahatma Gandhi and Viceroy Lord Irwin.

### British government's concessions

1. Release of political prisoners not convicted of violence.
2. Withdrawal of emergency ordinances and prosecutions connected with the movement.
3. Remission of fines not yet collected.
4. Return of confiscated property not already sold to third parties.
5. Lenient treatment of government employees who had resigned.
6. Permission for coastal residents to manufacture salt for personal consumption.
7. Permission for peaceful, non-aggressive picketing.

### Congress's commitments

- Suspend the Civil Disobedience Movement.
- Participate in the Second Round Table Conference.

### Demands not accepted

- A public inquiry into police excesses.
- Commutation of the death sentences of Bhagat Singh, Rajguru and Sukhdev.
- Immediate grant of complete independence.

Prelims distinction: The pact suspended the movement; it did not permanently terminate the Civil Disobedience Movement.

## 8. Karachi Congress Session, March 1931

- President: Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel.
- Endorsed the Gandhi–Irwin Pact.
- Reaffirmed Purna Swaraj.
- Adopted two important resolutions:
  - Fundamental Rights
  - National Economic Programme

Bhagat Singh, Rajguru and Sukhdev had been executed on 23 March 1931, shortly before the session.

### 8.1 Resolution on Fundamental Rights

Included:

- Freedom of speech and expression.
- Freedom of the press.
- Freedom of association and peaceful assembly.
- Equality before the law, irrespective of caste, creed or sex.
- Universal adult franchise.
- State neutrality in religious matters.
- Free and compulsory primary education.
- Protection of minority languages, scripts and cultures.

### 8.2 National Economic Programme

Included:

- Reduction of land revenue and agricultural rent.
- Relief from agricultural indebtedness.
- Control of usury.
- Living wages and reasonable working hours.
- Protection of women workers.
- Rights of workers and peasants to form unions.
- State ownership or control of key industries, mines and transport.

Prelims significance: The Karachi resolutions linked political independence with civil liberties, social equality and economic justice.

## 9. Round Table Conferences, 1930–1932

Three Round Table Conferences were held in London to discuss constitutional reforms in British India.

| Feature                | First RTC                                           | Second RTC                          | Third RTC                        |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Year                   | 1930–31                                             | 1931                                | 1932                             |
| Period                 | November 1930–January 1931                          | September–December 1931             | November–December 1932           |
| Congress participation | No                                                  | Yes                                 | No                               |
| Gandhi's participation | No                                                  | Yes                                 | No                               |
| B.R. Ambedkar          | Attended                                            | Attended                            | Attended                         |
| Major issue            | Federal structure and constitutional responsibility | Minorities and separate electorates | Further constitutional proposals |

### 9.1 First Round Table Conference

- Opened on 12 November 1930 by King George V.
- Chaired by British Prime Minister Ramsay MacDonald.
- Congress boycotted it while the Civil Disobedience Movement continued.
- Representatives included princely states, minority communities, Liberals, labour organisations and other interests.

Important participants:

| Representative                                   | Association           |
| ------------------------------------------------ | --------------------- |
| B.R. Ambedkar and Rettamalai Srinivasan          | Depressed Classes     |
| Tej Bahadur Sapru and V.S. Srinivasa Sastri      | Liberals              |
| N.M. Joshi                                       | Labour                |
| Begum Jahanara Shahnawaz and Radhabai Subbarayan | Women                 |
| Aga Khan III                                     | Muslim representation |

Outcome:

- General agreement emerged on the idea of an Indian federation.
- British safeguards concerning defence and finance remained important.
- No satisfactory constitutional settlement was achieved.
- British authorities recognised the importance of Congress participation.

### 9.2 Second Round Table Conference

- Held from 7 September to 1 December 1931.
- Gandhi attended as the sole official representative of the Indian National Congress, following the Gandhi–Irwin Pact.
- Other Indian delegates attended independently of the Congress delegation.
- Lord Willingdon had replaced Lord Irwin as Viceroy.

Important participants:

- Mahatma Gandhi — Congress.
- B.R. Ambedkar and Rettamalai Srinivasan — Depressed Classes.
- Muhammad Ali Jinnah and Aga Khan III — Muslim representation.
- Sarojini Naidu, Begum Jahanara Shahnawaz and Radhabai Subbarayan — women delegates.
- Tej Bahadur Sapru — Liberal representation.
- N.M. Joshi and V.V. Giri — labour representation.

### Why did the Second RTC fail?

- Disagreement over minority representation and separate electorates.
- Gandhi opposed separate electorates for the Depressed Classes.
- Ambedkar argued for independent political safeguards for the Depressed Classes.
- Disagreements persisted over federal arrangements, responsibility and British safeguards.
- The British government did not accept the central nationalist demands.

Outcome: The conference ended without a constitutional settlement.

### 9.3 Third Round Table Conference

- Held from 17 November to 24 December 1932.
- Congress did not participate.
- Gandhi did not attend.
- B.R. Ambedkar participated.

Subsequent constitutional developments:

Round Table Conferences (1930–32)

White Paper (1933)

Joint Select Committee examination

Government of India Act, 1935

Important: The Round Table Conferences did not themselves enact the Government of India Act, 1935. Their deliberations contributed to the subsequent constitutional process.

## 10. Resumption of the Civil Disobedience Movement, 1932–1934

### Background

- Gandhi returned from London in December 1931.
- The Congress Working Committee decided to resume civil disobedience on 29 December 1931.
- Viceroy Willingdon refused Gandhi's request for discussions.
- Gandhi was arrested on 4 January 1932.

### British repression

- Congress organisations were declared unlawful.
- Political leaders and volunteers were arrested.
- Nationalist publications were restricted.
- Properties were confiscated.
- Public gatherings and demonstrations faced severe repression.

### Character of the second phase

The movement continued through:

- Salt-law violations.
- Picketing of liquor and foreign-cloth shops.
- Non-payment of taxes.
- Forest-law defiance.
- Illegal public meetings and flag-hoisting ceremonies.

However, it gradually weakened because of sustained repression, organisational difficulties and exhaustion among participants.

### Withdrawal

- Gandhi withdrew the Civil Disobedience Movement in April 1934.
- Its withdrawal did not mean Congress had abandoned the demand for independence.

## 11. Communal Award, 16 August 1932

- Announced by British Prime Minister Ramsay MacDonald.
- Associated with the work of the Indian Franchise Committee, commonly called the Lothian Committee.
- Provided communal and special-interest representation in legislatures.

### Important provisions

- Continued or extended separate-electorate arrangements for various religious and social communities.
- Proposed separate electorates for the Depressed Classes.
- Provided a form of double voting for the Depressed Classes: participation in their separate electorate as well as the general electorate.
- Provided representation for certain occupational and economic interests.
- Included provisions for women's representation.

### Gandhi's objection

Gandhi strongly opposed separate electorates for the Depressed Classes because he believed they would institutionalise divisions within Hindu society.

He began a fast in Yeravada Jail on 20 September 1932.

### Ambedkar's position

Ambedkar argued that the Depressed Classes required independent political representation to protect their interests against caste discrimination and domination.

Prelims distinction: Gandhi's opposition centred particularly on separate electorates for the Depressed Classes; the Communal Award covered several other communities and interests as well.

## 12. Poona Pact, 24 September 1932

- Agreement reached between representatives of the Depressed Classes led by B.R. Ambedkar and caste-Hindu representatives, prominently Madan Mohan Malaviya.
- Replaced separate electorates for the Depressed Classes with reserved seats within joint electorates.
- Increased the number of reserved provincial legislative seats for the Depressed Classes.

### Comparison

| Feature                                         | Communal Award                                   | Poona Pact                                                  |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Year                                            | 1932                                             | 1932                                                        |
| Separate electorate for Depressed Classes       | Proposed                                         | Abandoned                                                   |
| Electorate arrangement                          | Separate electorate with additional general vote | Joint electorate, with a special primary-election procedure |
| Reserved provincial seats for Depressed Classes | 71                                               | 148                                                         |
| Central legislature                             | Special representation contemplated              | 18% of British Indian seats in the Central Legislature      |

The Poona Pact was subsequently accepted by the British government as a modification of the Communal Award.

Important: Reserved seats and separate electorates are not the same. A reserved seat may be filled through a joint electorate.

## 13. Gandhi's Anti-Untouchability Campaign, 1932–1934

### Major developments

| Year / period           | Event                                                                   |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| September 1932          | All India Anti-Untouchability League established                        |
| 1932                    | Organisation subsequently became known as the Harijan Sevak Sangh       |
| February 1933           | Gandhi began publishing the weekly Harijan                              |
| November 1933–July 1934 | Gandhi conducted an extensive anti-untouchability tour                  |
| 1933–34                 | Campaigns for temple entry, social reform and removal of untouchability |

The textbook records that Gandhi travelled approximately 20,000 km during his campaign.

### Objectives

- Eradication of untouchability.
- Opening public facilities and temples to formerly excluded communities.
- Improvement of education, hygiene and social conditions.
- Persuasion of caste Hindus to reject discriminatory practices.

### Gandhi and Ambedkar: Important distinctions

| Issue                | Gandhi                                                                                              | Ambedkar                                                              |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Untouchability       | Opposed it as a grave social evil                                                                   | Opposed it as a manifestation of caste oppression                     |
| Caste                | Distinguished removal of untouchability from abolition of the entire caste order; his views evolved | Advocated annihilation of caste                                       |
| Political safeguards | Opposed separate electorates for the Depressed Classes                                              | Sought effective independent political representation                 |
| Principal approach   | Moral persuasion and social reform                                                                  | Legal, political and constitutional safeguards                        |
| Village society      | Emphasised village-centred self-rule                                                                | Criticised caste hierarchy and inequality within traditional villages |

Both opposed social discrimination, but they differed significantly over the methods required to secure equality.

## Section I: Background and Launch of the Civil Disobedience Movement

### Q1. Consider the following statements regarding the Calcutta Congress Session of 1928:

1. Motilal Nehru presided over the session.
2. Congress gave the British government one year to accept the demand for Dominion Status.
3. Congress formally adopted Purna Swaraj as its objective at this session.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

A. 1 and 3 only\
B. 2 and 3 only\
C. 1 and 2 only\
D. 1, 2 and 3

Answer: C

> Explanation: The Calcutta Session, presided over by Motilal Nehru, gave a one-year ultimatum concerning Dominion Status. Purna Swaraj was formally adopted at Lahore in December 1929.

### Q2. Consider the following statements regarding the Irwin Declaration of 31 October 1929:

1. It recognised Dominion Status as the natural outcome of India's constitutional progress.
2. It announced a definite date for granting Dominion Status.
3. It proposed a Round Table Conference following the Simon Commission's report.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

A. 1 and 3 only\
B. 1 and 2 only\
C. 2 and 3 only\
D. 1, 2 and 3

Answer: A

> Explanation: The declaration referred to Dominion Status and a Round Table Conference but provided no definite timetable for granting Dominion Status.

### Q3. Which of the following were among the demands of the Delhi Manifesto of November 1929?

1. Dominion Status should form the basis of constitutional discussions.
2. Congress should receive majority representation at the proposed Round Table Conference.
3. Political prisoners should receive a general amnesty.
4. Separate electorates should immediately be abolished for all communities.

Select the correct answer.

A. 1, 2 and 4 only\
B. 2, 3 and 4 only\
C. 1, 3 and 4 only\
D. 1, 2 and 3 only

Answer: D

> Explanation: The Delhi Manifesto demanded a constitution-making conference based on Dominion Status, adequate Congress representation and a conciliatory policy including amnesty. Abolition of separate electorates was not one of its principal conditions.

### Q4. Consider the following statements regarding the Lahore Congress Session of 1929:

### Q5. Consider the following events:

### Q7. Consider the following statements regarding Gandhi's Eleven Demands:

### Q8. Why did Gandhi select salt as the initial issue for the Civil Disobedience Movement?

### Q10. Which one of the following correctly distinguishes the Civil Disobedience Movement from the Non-Cooperation Movement?

> Explanation: Deliberate, non-violent violation of laws was the distinguishing feature of civil disobedience. Both movements used boycott and non-cooperation.
