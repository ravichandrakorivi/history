# Moderates / Moderate Nationalists (Moderate phase of the Indian National Congress)

## Ananda Mohan Bose (1847–1906)

Ananda Mohan Bose was an Indian nationalist (**Moderate Nationalist**), social reformer, educationist and mathematician associated with the **Bengal Renaissance**, **Brahmo Samaj** and the **early Indian national movement**.

He is particularly remembered as a co-founder of the **Indian Association (1876)** with **Surendranath Banerjea**, a founder of the **Sadharan Brahmo Samaj (1878)**, and the President of the Indian National Congress at its Madras Session (1898).

### 1. Early Life and Education

| Feature               | Important fact                                                  |
| --------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Born                  | 23 September 1847                                               |
| Birthplace            | Mymensingh district, Bengal Presidency (present-day Bangladesh) |
| Full name             | Ananda Mohan Bose (also spelt Ananda Mohan Basu)                |
| Education             | Presidency College, Calcutta; University of Cambridge           |
| Important distinction | First Indian Wrangler at Cambridge (1874)                       |
| Profession            | Mathematician, lawyer, educationist and nationalist             |
| Died                  | 20 August 1906                                                  |

- Studied mathematics at Cambridge University.
- In 1874, became the first Indian Wrangler in the Mathematical Tripos examination at Cambridge.
- Subsequently became involved in educational, social and political reform activities in Bengal.

Prelims Point: Ananda Mohan Bose is associated with the distinction of being the first Indian Wrangler at Cambridge.

### 2. Indian Association (1876)

The Indian Association, also known as the **Indian National Association**, was an important political organisation established before the formation of the **Indian National Congress**.

| Objective   | Promote political awareness and organise Indians around common political demands |

### Important activities

- Promoted political unity among educated Indians.
- Sought wider political participation beyond a narrow elite.
- Opposed racial discrimination in colonial administration.
- Campaigned against the reduction in the maximum age for the Indian Civil Service examination.
- Helped organise political meetings and campaigns across India.

Prelims Point: The **Indian Association (1876)** was founded by **Surendranath Banerjea** and **Ananda Mohan Bose**, not by **A.O. Hume**.

### 4. Sadharan Brahmo Samaj (1878)

Ananda Mohan Bose was one of the important founders of the Sadharan Brahmo Samaj, established following disagreements within the Brahmo movement.

| Feature            | Important fact                                                         |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Established        | 15 May 1878                                                            |
| Place              | Calcutta                                                               |
| Important founders | Ananda Mohan Bose, Sivanath Sastri, Umesh Chandra Datta and associates |
| Background         | Split from Keshab Chandra Sen's Brahmo Samaj of India                  |
| Nature             | Religious and social reform organisation                               |

### Background of the split

- 1866: Keshab Chandra Sen established the Brahmo Samaj of India following a split with Debendranath Tagore.
- 1878: Controversy arose over the marriage of Keshab Chandra Sen's underage daughter, Suniti Devi, to the Maharaja of Cooch Behar.
- Reformers criticised the marriage as inconsistent with the principles advocated by the Brahmo movement.
- Dissidents established the Sadharan Brahmo Samaj on 15 May 1878.

### Important objectives

- Religious and social reform.
- Opposition to caste discrimination and social orthodoxy.
- Promotion of women's education.
- Greater democratic participation in the organisation's management.

Prelims Point: Sadharan Brahmo Samaj (1878) is associated with Ananda Mohan Bose and Sivanath Sastri, whereas Brahmo Samaj of India (1866) was established by Keshab Chandra Sen.

## Surendranath Banerjea (1848–1925)

Surendranath Banerjea (also spelt Surendranath Banerjee) was an Indian nationalist, political leader, journalist and educationist associated with the early phase of Indian nationalism and the **Moderate phase of the Indian National Congress**.

He is particularly remembered for founding the **Indian Association (1876)** with **Ananda Mohan Bose**, organising the **Indian National Conference (1883)**, and presiding over the Indian National Congress sessions of 1895 and 1902.

He was popularly known as Rashtraguru and was an important leader of the **Swadeshi Movement against the Partition of Bengal (1905)**.

### 1. Early Life and Education

| Feature               | Important fact                                                     |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Born                  | 10 November 1848                                                   |
| Birthplace            | Calcutta, Bengal Presidency                                        |
| Father                | Durga Charan Banerjee                                              |
| Education             | University of Calcutta; higher studies in England                  |
| Important distinction | Among the earliest Indians to **qualify for the Indian Civil Service** |
| Popular title         | Rashtraguru                                                        |
| Political association | **Indian Association**; **Indian National Congress**                       |
| Died                  | 6 August 1925                                                      |

### Indian Civil Service (ICS)

- Travelled to England to appear for the **Indian Civil Service examination**.
- Passed the competitive examination in 1869.
- Appointed to the ICS and served as an assistant magistrate at Sylhet.
- Dismissed from service in 1874 following an administrative controversy.
- Subsequently became involved in education and nationalist politics.

Prelims Point: **Surendranath Banerjea passed the ICS examination in 1869**, but did not continue in the service because he was dismissed in 1874.

### 2. Indian Association (1876)

The Indian Association, also known as the **Indian National Association**, was one of the most important political organisations established before the Indian National Congress.

| Feature     | Important fact                                                |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |

| Place       | Calcutta                                                      |
| Founders    | Surendranath Banerjea and Ananda Mohan Bose                   |
| Nature      | Political organisation                                        |
| Objective   | Promote political unity and political awareness among Indians |

### Important Objectives and Activities

- Promote political unity among Indians across different regions.
- Organise public opinion against discriminatory colonial policies.
- Demand greater Indian representation in administration.
- Oppose racial discrimination.
- Mobilise educated Indians and sections of the wider population.
- Campaign against the reduction in the **maximum age for the Indian Civil Service examination**.

### Indian Civil Service Age Controversy

- The **maximum age for the ICS competitive examination was reduced from 21 to 19 years in 1876**.
- The reduction disadvantaged Indian candidates because the examination was held in England.
- Surendranath Banerjea organised political campaigns against this restriction.
- He travelled to different parts of India to mobilise public opinion.

Prelims Point: Indian Association (1876) — Surendranath Banerjea and Ananda Mohan Bose.

### Important Distinction

Prelims Point: Surendranath Banerjea was a Moderate leader who actively participated in the anti-Partition and Swadeshi agitations.

### 8. Later Political Career

### Constitutional Reforms and Legislative Office

- Supported constitutional and representative reforms.
- Became associated with the **Indian National Liberal Federation**, established in 1918.
- **Accepted the constitutional framework introduced by the Government of India Act, 1919**.
- Served as **Minister for Local Self-Government in Bengal (1921–1923) under the dyarchy system**.
- Was **knighted by the British government in 1921**.

### Political Differences

- The rise of Gandhian mass movements created differences between Banerjea and the newer nationalist leadership.
- He **did not support Gandhi's Non-Cooperation Movement**.
- Continued to favour constitutional and gradual political reform.

Prelims Point: Surendranath Banerjea was associated with the Indian National Liberal Federation, and later served as a minister under the dyarchy introduced by the Government of India Act, 1919.

## Womesh Chunder Bonnerjee (1844–1906)

Womesh Chunder Bonnerjee (W.C. Bonnerjee) was an Indian lawyer and nationalist leader associated with the early Moderate phase of the Indian National Congress (INC).

He is best remembered as the **first President of the Indian National Congress**, presiding over its inaugural session at Bombay in December 1885.

He also presided over the Allahabad Session of the INC in 1892.

| Political approach    | Moderate nationalism          |
| Died                  | 21 July 1906, England         |

- Studied law in England and became a barrister.
- Practised law at the Calcutta High Court.
- Became one of the prominent Indian lawyers of his time.
- Supported constitutional reforms and greater Indian participation in administration.

### 2. First Session of the Indian National Congress (1885)

Prelims Point: First INC Session — Bombay — 1885 — W.C. Bonnerjee — 72 delegates.

### 3. Objectives of the Indian National Congress

In his presidential address at the first INC session, Bonnerjee outlined four principal objectives.

1. Promote personal acquaintance and friendship among nationalist workers from different parts of India.
2. Remove prejudices arising from differences of race, religion and province, and strengthen national unity.
3. Discuss and formulate the considered opinions of educated Indians on important public questions.
4. Determine the methods and directions for political work during the following year.

These objectives reflected the Congress's initial emphasis on national unity, political consultation and constitutional agitation.

Bonnerjee belonged to the early generation of Moderate nationalists.

### Important features

- Advocated greater Indian representation in legislative councils and public services.
- Favoured political petitions, resolutions and discussions.

## Dadabhai Naoroji (1825–1917)

Dadabhai Naoroji, popularly known as the **Grand Old Man of India**, was a prominent Moderate nationalist leader, economist and one of the founding members of the Indian National Congress (1885).

He is particularly remembered for developing the **Drain of Wealth Theory**, founding the **East India Association (1866)**, becoming the **first Indian elected to the British House of Commons (1892)** and presiding over three sessions of the Indian National Congress.

### 1. Early life and political associations

- Born: 4 September 1825, Bombay Presidency.
- Belonged to the Parsi community.
- Educated at Elphinstone Institution, Bombay.
- Became one of the first Indians appointed as a professor at Elphinstone Institution.
- Associated with the early development of Indian nationalism and economic criticism of British colonial rule.
- Died: 30 June 1917.

### 2. Important organisations

| Organisation               | Year | Association                                                                      |
| -------------------------- | ---- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Rahnumai Mazdayasnan Sabha | 1851 | Co-founder; organisation for Parsi religious and social reform                   |
| East India Association     | 1866 | Founded in London to discuss Indian affairs and influence British public opinion |
| Indian National Congress   | 1885 | One of the founding members                                                      |
| London Indian Society      | 1865 | Associated with its establishment in London                                      |

Prelims Point: The East India Association (1866) was founded by Dadabhai Naoroji in London, whereas the Indian Association (1876) was founded by Surendranath Banerjea and Ananda Mohan Bose in Calcutta.

### 3. Drain of Wealth Theory

- Dadabhai Naoroji was one of the earliest Indian nationalists to systematically explain the economic exploitation of India under British rule.
- He argued that a substantial portion of India's wealth was transferred to Britain without an adequate economic return to India.
- This continuous transfer contributed to poverty and economic underdevelopment in India.
- His analysis formed an important foundation of economic nationalism in India.

#### Major channels of wealth drain

1. Salaries, pensions and allowances of British officials paid from Indian revenues.
2. Remittances of savings and earnings by British officials to Britain.
3. Profits transferred to Britain by British businesses operating in India.
4. Interest payments on public debt held abroad.
5. Home Charges — expenditure incurred in Britain and charged to Indian revenues, including administrative and military expenses.

Prelims Point: The Drain of Wealth Theory was associated particularly with Dadabhai Naoroji, while R. C. Dutt also analysed the economic consequences of British colonial policies.

## Gopal Krishna Gokhale (1866–1915)

Gopal Krishna Gokhale was a prominent Moderate nationalist leader, social reformer and member of the Indian National Congress. He advocated constitutional methods, gradual political reforms and the expansion of education in India.

He was a political mentor of Mahatma Gandhi and founded the Servants of India Society in 1905.

### 1. Early life and political associations

- Born: 9 May 1866, Maharashtra.
- Associated with the Moderate faction of the Indian National Congress.
- Influenced by Mahadev Govind Ranade (M. G. Ranade), who was his political mentor.
- Associated with the Poona Sarvajanik Sabha and the Deccan Education Society.
- Worked at **Fergusson College, Pune, as a teacher and later as its principal**.
- Became a **member of the Bombay Legislative Council (1899) and the Imperial Legislative Council (1902)**.

### 2. Role in the Indian National Congress

- Joined the Indian National Congress in 1889.
- Became President of the Congress at the **Banaras Session (1905)**.
- Supported constitutional agitation, legislative reforms and greater Indian participation in administration.
- Advocated the use of petitions, resolutions, legislative debates and public opinion to secure reforms.
- Opposed revolutionary violence as a method of political struggle.

#### Important Congress sessions

Prelims Point: Gokhale presided over the Banaras Session (1905), not the Surat Session (1907).

### 3. Servants of India Society (1905)

- Founded: 12 June 1905.
- Founder: Gopal Krishna Gokhale.
- Place: Pune.
- Objective: Train dedicated workers for national service and promote the social, educational and political advancement of Indians.
- Members were expected to devote themselves to public service.
- Emphasised education, social reform and constitutional political work.

Important distinction: The Servants of India Society (1905) was founded by Gokhale, whereas the Deccan Education Society (1884) was founded by a group of educationists including Bal Gangadhar Tilak, G. G. Agarkar and V. S. Apte.

### 4. Role in legislative and educational reforms

- Served in the Imperial Legislative Council from 1902.
- Criticised excessive military expenditure and burdensome taxation.
- Advocated increased government expenditure on education and public welfare.
- Supported the expansion of primary education.
- Introduced the Elementary Education Bill in 1911 to promote compulsory primary education.
- The Bill was defeated in 1912.

Prelims Point: Gokhale's Elementary Education Bill (1911) sought to expand compulsory primary education, but it did not become law.

### 5. Gokhale and Mahatma Gandhi

- Gokhale became an important political mentor of Mahatma Gandhi.
- Gandhi admired his commitment to public service, constitutional politics and social reform.
- **Gokhale visited South Africa in 1912 and examined the problems faced by Indians there**.
- Gandhi returned permanently to India on 9 January 1915, following which he undertook a journey to understand Indian conditions.
- Gandhi described Gokhale as his political guru.

## Rash Behari Ghose (1845–1921)

Sir Rash Behari Ghose, also spelled Rash Bihari Ghosh, was an Indian lawyer, educationist, philanthropist and nationalist leader associated with the Moderate wing of the Indian National Congress.

He is particularly remembered for his association with the **Surat Split of 1907**, presiding over the Madras Congress Session of 1908, and serving as the **first President of the National Council of Education, Bengal (1906)**.

### 1. Early Life and Background

| Feature                | Important fact                        |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Born                   | 23 December 1845                      |
| Birthplace             | Burdwan district, Bengal Presidency   |
| Profession             | Lawyer, politician and philanthropist |
| Education              | Presidency College, Calcutta          |
| Political affiliation  | Indian National Congress              |
| Political approach     | Moderate nationalism                  |
| Important organisation | National Council of Education         |
| INC association        | Surat (1907), Madras (1908)           |
| Died                   | 1921                                  |

- Became a prominent lawyer at the Calcutta High Court.
- Obtained the degree of Doctor of Laws (LL.D.) in 1884.
- Was associated with Calcutta University as a legal scholar and benefactor.
- Supported constitutional reforms, education and scientific development.

### 2. Indian National Congress — Surat Session (1907)

Rash Behari Ghose was the Moderate leadership's choice for President of the Surat Session of the Indian National Congress in December 1907.

| Feature             | Important fact                  |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Year                | 1907                            |
| Place               | Surat                           |
| President-designate | Rash Behari Ghose               |
| Important event     | Surat Split                     |
| Main groups         | Moderates and Extremists        |
| Outcome             | Session disrupted and suspended |

### Background

The conflict between Moderates and Extremists intensified after the Calcutta Congress Session of 1906, presided over by Dadabhai Naoroji.

The differences concerned the methods of nationalist struggle, particularly Swadeshi, Boycott, National Education and Swaraj.

### Surat Split

- The Congress session was originally proposed to be held at Nagpur, but the venue was shifted to Surat.
- Moderate leaders supported Rash Behari Ghose for the presidency.
- Extremist leaders opposed the Moderate leadership's handling of the session and its proposed programme.
- Disputes over the presidency and Congress resolutions led to disorder.
- The session was suspended without completing its normal proceedings.
- The Congress consequently split into Moderate and Extremist factions.

Prelims Point: Surat Split — 1907 — Rash Behari Ghose — Moderates vs Extremists.

### 3. Indian National Congress — Madras Session (1908)

Rash Behari Ghose also presided over the Madras Session of the Indian National Congress in 1908.

| Feature               | Important fact                          |
| --------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Year                  | 1908                                    |
| Place                 | Madras                                  |
| President             | Rash Behari Ghose                       |
| Dominant faction      | Moderates                               |
| Important development | Adoption of a new Congress constitution |

### Important facts

- The Congress remained under Moderate control after the Surat Split.
- A new organisational constitution consolidated Moderate control over the Congress.
- The session adopted a more cautious position on Swadeshi and National Education.
- The boycott resolution was omitted.
- The session considered the constitutional reforms associated with Morley and Minto.

### 4. National Council of Education (1906)

Rash Behari Ghose played an important role in the National Education Movement during the Swadeshi period.

The National Council of Education (NCE), Bengal, was established in 1906 to promote Indian-controlled education.

| Feature               | Important fact                          |

### Important contributions

- Served as the first President of the National Council of Education.
- Supported the development of national educational institutions.
- Promoted scientific and technical education.
- Made substantial financial contributions to educational institutions.
- The educational initiatives associated with the NCE eventually contributed to the establishment of Jadavpur University (1955).

Prelims Point: National Council of Education (1906) — First President: Rash Behari Ghose.

### 5. Contributions to Education and Science

Rash Behari Ghose was also a major philanthropist.

- Made substantial donations to Calcutta University.
- Established an endowment of approximately ₹10 lakh in 1913 to promote scientific education and research.
- Supported the development of the National Council of Education.
- Contributed to the advancement of modern science and technical education in Bengal.

He was also associated with the Tagore Law Lectureship at Calcutta University and wrote on the law of mortgages in India.

### 6. Legislative and Political Activities

- Served in the Bengal Legislative Council.
- Also served in the Imperial Legislative Council.
- Supported constitutional reforms and greater Indian participation in administration.
- Favoured gradual political change through legislative and constitutional methods.
- Participated in Congress political activities and delegations.

He was associated with the Moderate leaders Gopal Krishna Gokhale and Pherozeshah Mehta.
