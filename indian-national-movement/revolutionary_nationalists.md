# First Phase of Revolutionary Activities in India (1907–1917)

## Barindra Kumar Ghosh (1880–1959)

Barindra Kumar Ghosh, also known as Barin Ghosh, was an Indian revolutionary nationalist, journalist and writer associated with the first phase of revolutionary activities in Bengal (1905–1917).

He is particularly remembered for his role in organising the Jugantar revolutionary group (1906), his association with the Maniktala (Muraripukur) revolutionary centre, and his involvement in the Alipore Bomb Case (1908–1909).

He was the younger brother of Sri Aurobindo (Aurobindo Ghose).

| Birthplace               | Croydon, England              |

| Political classification | Revolutionary nationalist     |

| Important case           | Alipore Bomb Case (1908–1909) |
| Died                     | 18 April 1959                 |

- Grew up in Bengal and was influenced by the rise of Indian nationalism.
- Came into contact with revolutionary nationalist ideas through his elder brother, Sri Aurobindo.
- Became involved in organising secret revolutionary activities in Bengal.
- Advocated armed resistance against British colonial rule.

### 2. Jugantar Group and Newspaper (1906)

Barindra Kumar Ghosh was one of the principal organisers of the Jugantar revolutionary movement in Bengal.

### Important facts

- Barindra Kumar Ghosh helped establish the Bengali weekly Jugantar in 1906.
- Bhupendranath Datta, the younger brother of Swami Vivekananda, was another important associate of the newspaper.
- The Jugantar revolutionary group emerged from the wider Anushilan Samiti network.
- The group recruited young revolutionaries and organised clandestine activities against British colonial rule.
- Bagha Jatin (Jatindranath Mukherjee) became another important leader of the Jugantar revolutionary network.

Prelims distinction: Jugantar was both the name of a Bengali nationalist newspaper and a revolutionary group.

### 4. Muzaffarpur Bombing (1908)

The attempted assassination of British magistrate Douglas Kingsford at Muzaffarpur was a major event preceding the Alipore Bomb Case.

### Important facts

- The carriage instead carried two British women, who died.
- Prafulla Chaki shot himself while evading arrest.
- Khudiram Bose was arrested and subsequently executed on 11 August 1908.
- The investigation led to the arrest of Barindra Kumar Ghosh and other revolutionaries.

Important distinction: Khudiram Bose and Prafulla Chaki carried out the Muzaffarpur bombing; Barindra Kumar Ghosh was an important organiser of the associated revolutionary network.

## Khudiram Bose (1889–1908)

Khudiram Bose was a young Indian revolutionary freedom fighter associated with the **Jugantar revolutionary organisation** in Bengal. He is particularly remembered for his involvement in the **Muzaffarpur Bomb Incident (1908)**, along with **Prafulla Chaki**.

He was executed by the British authorities on 11 August 1908, at the age of 18.

### 1. Early life and revolutionary activities

- Born: 3 December 1889, Midnapore district, Bengal Presidency.
- Associated with revolutionary nationalism in Bengal.
- Influenced by the Swadeshi Movement (1905) following the Partition of Bengal.
- Joined revolutionary networks associated with Jugantar.
- Participated in anti-British revolutionary activities during his teenage years.

### 2. Muzaffarpur Bomb Incident (1908)

- Date: 30 April 1908.
- Place: Muzaffarpur, Bihar.
- Revolutionaries: **Khudiram Bose** and **Prafulla Chaki**.
- Intended target: **Douglas Kingsford**, a British judicial officer who had earlier served as Chief Presidency Magistrate in Calcutta and was then **District Judge at Muzaffarpur**.
- Reason for targeting: Kingsford had become unpopular among nationalists for imposing harsh punishments on political activists.
- The revolutionaries threw a bomb at a carriage believed to be carrying Kingsford.
- However, Kingsford was not in the carriage.
- The explosion killed Mrs. Kennedy and her daughter, the wife and daughter of barrister Pringle Kennedy.

Prelims Point: The intended target was Douglas Kingsford, but he survived. The two people killed were members of the Kennedy family.

### 3. Arrest and execution (1908)

- After the bombing, Khudiram Bose and Prafulla Chaki fled separately.
- 1 May 1908: Khudiram Bose was arrested near Waini railway station.
- Prafulla Chaki died by suicide at Mokamaghat to avoid capture by British police.
- Khudiram Bose was tried and sentenced to death.
- 11 August 1908: Executed by hanging at Muzaffarpur, aged 18.

### 4. Connection with the Alipore Bomb Case (1908–1909)

- The Muzaffarpur bombing prompted British authorities to investigate revolutionary networks in Bengal.
- Police raided revolutionary premises at **Maniktala (Muraripukur), Calcutta**.
- Several revolutionaries, including **Aurobindo Ghosh** and **Barindra Kumar Ghosh**, were arrested.
- These arrests led to the **Alipore Bomb Conspiracy Case** (1908–1909).
- Aurobindo Ghosh was acquitted, while Barindra Kumar Ghosh was convicted.

## Rash Behari Bose (1886–1945)

Rash Behari Bose was an Indian revolutionary freedom fighter associated with **revolutionary activities in northern India**, the **Ghadar Movement** and the formation of the **Indian Independence League** in **East Asia**. He played an important role in **organising** the **Indian National Army (INA)** before Subhas Chandra Bose assumed its leadership.

### 1. Early revolutionary activities

- Born on 25 May 1886 in Bengal.
- Worked at the Forest Research Institute, Dehradun.
- Associated with revolutionary networks in Bengal and northern India.
- Played an important role in extending revolutionary activities to Punjab, Delhi and the United Provinces.

### 2. Delhi Conspiracy Case (1912)

- Date: 23 December 1912.
- Target: **Viceroy Lord Hardinge**.
- Event: A bomb was thrown at Lord Hardinge during his ceremonial entry into Delhi.
- **Rash Behari Bose**: One of the principal organisers of the assassination attempt.
- **Basanta Kumar Biswas**: Associated with the actual bomb attack.
- Result: Lord Hardinge was injured but survived.

### 3. Ghadar Movement and Revolutionary Conspiracy (1915)

- Rash Behari Bose coordinated with revolutionaries associated with the **Ghadar Movement**.
- Planned an armed uprising involving Indian soldiers serving in the British Indian Army during the First World War.
- The **uprising was planned for February 1915**.
- The plan was discovered by British intelligence before its execution.
- The conspiracy failed, and several revolutionaries were arrested.

Important associates: **Sachindra Nath Sanyal**, Kartar Singh Sarabha and Vishnu Ganesh Pingle.

### 4. Escape to Japan (1915)

- Escaped to Japan in 1915 to avoid arrest by British authorities.
- Continued revolutionary activities from Japan.
- Developed connections with Japanese supporters of Indian independence.
- Later acquired Japanese citizenship.

### 5. Indian Independence League and INA

| 1942 | The first INA was organised under Captain Mohan Singh, with support from the Indian Independence League.                   |
| 1943 | Subhas Chandra Bose arrived in East Asia and assumed leadership of the Indian Independence League and the reorganised INA. |

Important distinction: Rash Behari Bose and Subhas Chandra Bose were two different revolutionaries. Rash Behari Bose helped lay the organisational foundation for the INA, while Subhas Chandra Bose subsequently reorganised and led it.

Basanta Kumar Biswas was an Indian revolutionary nationalist from Bengal, best known for his involvement in the **Delhi Conspiracy Case (1912)**, also called the **Hardinge Bomb Case**.

## Sachindra Nath Sanyal (1893–1942)

Sachindra Nath Sanyal was an Indian revolutionary freedom fighter associated with the revolutionary movement in northern India, the **Ghadar conspiracy of 1915** and the establishment of the **Hindustan Republican Association (HRA) in 1924**.

### 1. Early revolutionary activities

- Born in 1893 in Benares (Varanasi).
- Associated with revolutionary activities in Bengal and northern India.
- Worked closely with **Rash Behari Bose** in organising revolutionary activities against British rule.
- Helped **establish revolutionary networks in the United Provinces and Punjab**.

### 2. Ghadar Conspiracy (1915)

- Associated with **Rash Behari Bose** in planning an **armed uprising against British rule during the First World War**.
- The planned uprising **sought to mobilise Indian soldiers serving in the British Indian Army**.
- The proposed uprising in February 1915 failed after British authorities discovered the conspiracy.
- Sanyal was subsequently arrested and sentenced to transportation for life to the Cellular Jail in the Andaman Islands.

**Note:** 

The Ghadar Conspiracy was a specific, major military operation executed within the broader, global framework of the **Indo-German Conspiracy**.

### 3. Hindustan Republican Association (1924)

- Year: 1924.
- Place: Kanpur.
- Important founders: **Sachindra Nath Sanyal**, **Ram Prasad Bismil** and **Jogesh Chandra Chatterjee**.
- Objective: Establish an independent, republican government in India through organised revolutionary action.
- Sanyal was a principal ideologue and organiser of the HRA.
- Authored the revolutionary manifesto The Revolutionary (1925), associated with the HRA.

### 4. Kakori Conspiracy Case (1925)

- Date: 9 August 1925.
- Event: HRA revolutionaries seized government money being transported by train near Kakori, close to Lucknow.
- Important participants: **Ram Prasad Bismil**, Ashfaqulla Khan, **Rajendra Lahiri** and **Chandrashekhar Azad**.
- Sachindra Nath Sanyal: Associated with the HRA and convicted in connection with the Kakori Conspiracy Case, although he was not one of the revolutionaries who carried out the train robbery.
- Sentenced to transportation for life.

### 5. Bandi Jeevan

- Sachindra Nath Sanyal wrote Bandi Jeevan (A Life of Captivity).
- It is an autobiographical account of his revolutionary experiences and imprisonment.
- It became an influential work among Indian revolutionaries.

### 6. HRA vs HSRA

### Ghadar Conspiracy of 1915

The Ghadar Conspiracy of 1915 was an ambitious plan by the Ghadar Party and Indian revolutionaries to trigger a pan-Indian armed mutiny within the British Indian Army and overthrow British colonial rule.

- **Objective:** Spark a widespread revolt in military cantonments across northern India (including Lahore, Ferozepur, and Meerut) on February 21, 1915, taking advantage of British forces being tied up in World War I.

- **Global Network:** The plot was organized by the **Ghadar Party** in **North America** (founded in 1913 by **Sohan Singh Bhakna** and **Lala Har Dayal**), the **Berlin Committee in Germany**, and local underground networks in India coordinated by figures like **Rash Behari Bose** and Kartar Singh Sarabha.

- **German Support:** The German Foreign Office attempted to supply weapons and funds (including plans via ships like the Annie Larson and The Maverick) as part of the broader Hindu–German Conspiracy.

- **Betrayal and Failure:** British intelligence infiltrated the Ghadar ranks through a mole (Kushal Singh/Kirpal Singh), allowing authorities to move the uprising date and arrest leaders before the mutiny could start.

- **Trials and Execution:** The crackdown led to the first **Lahore Conspiracy Case trials in 1915**, resulting in numerous life sentences and executions, including prominent young revolutionary.

## Bagha Jatin (1879–1915)

Bagha Jatin, whose real name was **Jatindranath Mukherjee**, was an Indian revolutionary freedom fighter and a prominent leader of the **Jugantar** revolutionary organisation in Bengal.

He is particularly remembered for his role in the **Indo-German Conspiracy** during the **First World War** and his final armed encounter with British police near Balasore, Odisha, in 1915.

### 1. Early life and revolutionary activities

- Born: 7 December 1879, in Kushtia district (now in Bangladesh).
- Real name: Jatindranath Mukherjee.
- Popular name: Bagha Jatin, meaning Tiger Jatin, associated with his reputed encounter with a tiger.
- Associated with the Anushilan Samiti revolutionary network and later became an important leader of Jugantar.
- Helped organise secret revolutionary groups and coordinate activities across Bengal and other regions.

### 2. Role in the Jugantar Organisation

- Jugantar emerged from the broader Anushilan revolutionary network around 1906.
- Early Jugantar leaders included Barindra Kumar Ghosh and other Bengal revolutionaries.
- **Following the British crackdown after the Alipore Bomb Case (1908), Bagha Jatin became a major organiser of the Jugantar revolutionary network**.
- Promoted coordinated revolutionary action against British rule.

Prelims distinction: Barindra Kumar Ghosh was associated with the early Jugantar group and the Alipore Bomb Case, while Bagha Jatin became particularly prominent in the later phase of Jugantar's activities.

### 3. Indo-German Conspiracy (1914–1915)

- During the First World War, Indian revolutionaries sought German assistance to organise an armed uprising against British rule.
- The conspiracy is also known as the **Indo–German Conspiracy**.
- Bagha Jatin coordinated revolutionary activities in Bengal through Jugantar.
- **Rash Behari Bose** was associated with plans for a military uprising in northern India.
- Narendra Nath Bhattacharya (later known as **M. N. Roy**) was an important associate of Bagha Jatin who contacted German representatives in Batavia (present-day Jakarta).
- The revolutionaries **planned to obtain German arms and ammunition through shipments** to the Indian coast, including Balasore in Odisha.
- The conspiracy failed after British authorities discovered the plans and disrupted the arms shipments.

### 4. Battle of Balasore / Buribalam (1915)

- Date: 9 September 1915.
- Place: Chashakhand, near the Buribalam (Burha Balang) River, Balasore, Odisha.
- Bagha Jatin and four associates fought an armed encounter against British police forces.
- Chittapriya Ray Chaudhuri was killed during the encounter.
- Bagha Jatin was seriously wounded and died on 10 September 1915 at Balasore hospital.

## Pulin Behari Das (1877–1949)

Pulin Behari Das was an Indian revolutionary nationalist associated with the first phase of revolutionary activities in Bengal.

He is particularly remembered for founding the **Dacca Anushilan Samiti in 1906**, which became an important **centre of revolutionary activities in Eastern Bengal following the Partition of Bengal (1905)**.

### 1. Early life and political associations

- Born: 24 January 1877, Bengal Presidency (present-day Bangladesh).
- Popular name: Pulin Behari Das.
- Political association: Revolutionary nationalist movement in Bengal.
- Major organisation: Dacca Anushilan Samiti (1906).
- Was skilled in lathi training and physical exercises.
- Advocated physical training and organised revolutionary activity against British colonial rule.
- Died: 17 August 1949, Calcutta.

Prelims Point: Pulin Behari Das is primarily associated with the Dacca Anushilan Samiti (1906), not the original Calcutta Anushilan Samiti (1902).

### 2. Background — Partition of Bengal (1905)

- Partition of Bengal: 16 October 1905.
- Viceroy: Lord Curzon.
- The partition created the province of Eastern Bengal and Assam, with Dacca as its capital.
- The partition intensified the Swadeshi Movement and revolutionary nationalism in Bengal.
- Revolutionary organisations recruited young people and promoted physical training, political education and armed resistance.

Prelims Point: The Dacca Anushilan Samiti developed during the Swadeshi Movement following the Partition of Bengal (1905).

### 3. Dacca Anushilan Samiti (1906)

- Established: October 1906.
- Place: Dacca (present-day Dhaka, Bangladesh).
- Founder and principal organiser: Pulin Behari Das.
- Initial membership: Approximately 80 young men.
- Important influence: Pramathanath Mitra, associated with the earlier Calcutta Anushilan Samiti.
- Developed an extensive network of branches throughout Eastern Bengal.

### Important activities

- Organised physical training in lathi fighting and other martial exercises.
- Recruited and trained young revolutionaries.
- Established a National School in Dacca for training and nationalist education.
- Developed a secret revolutionary network.
- Associated with efforts to acquire arms and funds for revolutionary activities.

Government of India historical records identify Pulin Behari Das as the founder-president of the Dacca Anushilan Samiti.

### 4. Anushilan Samiti — Calcutta vs Dacca

| Year                    | 1902                                             | 1906                                             |

| Important personalities | Satish Chandra Basu, Pramathanath Mitra          | Pulin Behari Das                                 |
| Region                  | Primarily Western Bengal                         | Primarily Eastern Bengal                         |
| Activities              | Physical training and revolutionary organisation | Physical training and revolutionary organisation |

### 5. Barrah Dacoity (1908)

- Date: 2 June 1908.
- Place: Barrah (Bara), Dacca district.
- An armed robbery was carried out to obtain funds.
- The incident was investigated in connection with revolutionary activities in Eastern Bengal.

The prosecution linked the incident to the Dacca Anushilan Samiti, but the court record noted that the evidence did not establish the involvement of the organisation or its individual members. Therefore, it should not be treated as conclusively proved that Pulin Behari Das personally organised the robbery.

**Note:** The **Barrah Dacoity** was a major **armed robbery** carried out in 1908 in the Dhaka district of East Bengal (now Bangladesh).  It was executed in broad daylight in 1908 (often noted around June 1908) **at the residence of a local zamindar in Barrah, Dhaka district, East Bengal**.

### 6. Arrest and Dacca Conspiracy Case

- 1908: British authorities banned the Anushilan Samiti and detained Pulin Behari Das.
- He was sent to Montgomery Jail in Punjab.
- Later prosecuted in the Dacca Conspiracy Case, resulting in a sentence of transportation for life.
- Imprisoned in the Cellular Jail, Andaman Islands.
- Released after the First World War, although restrictions continued for some time.

Prelims Point: Pulin Behari Das was associated with the Dacca Conspiracy Case, not the Alipore Bomb Case (1908).

### 7. Later Activities

### Bharat Sevak Sangh (1920)

- Established by Pulin Behari Das around 1920.
- Sought to continue his programme of revolutionary nationalist organisation.
- Das disagreed with Gandhi's emphasis on non-violent Non-Cooperation.
- Associated with the publications Hak Katha and Swaraj.

### Bangiya Byayam Samiti

- Established in Calcutta during the 1920s.
- Promoted physical education, martial training and exercise.
- Pulin Behari Das gradually withdrew from active revolutionary politics.

Government of India historical material records his association with the Bharat Sevak Sangh and Bangiya Byayam Samiti.

## V. D. Savarkar (1883–1966)

Vinayak Damodar Savarkar, popularly known as Veer Savarkar, was an Indian revolutionary, political thinker, writer and a prominent leader of the **Hindu Mahasabha**. He played an important role in revolutionary activities in Maharashtra and London during the early twentieth century.

### 1. Early life

- Born: 28 May 1883, Bhagur, near Nashik, Maharashtra.
- Full name: Vinayak Damodar Savarkar.
- Elder brother: Ganesh Damodar Savarkar (Babarao Savarkar), also a revolutionary.
- Studied at Fergusson College, Pune.
- Travelled to London in 1906 to study law at Gray's Inn.
- Influenced by Italian revolutionary nationalist Giuseppe Mazzini.

### 2. Mitra Mela and Abhinav Bharat

- 1900: Founded **Mitra Mela**, a secret revolutionary organisation in Nashik.
- 1904: Reorganised Mitra Mela as **Abhinav Bharat Society**, with the involvement of his **brother Ganesh Damodar Savarkar**.
- Abhinav Bharat **advocated armed revolution to overthrow British colonial rule**.
- Its activities extended from Maharashtra to revolutionary networks abroad.
- The organisation was influenced by Giuseppe Mazzini's Young Italy movement.

### 3. Revolutionary activities in London (1906–1910)

- Associated with **India House**, established in London in 1905 by **Shyamji Krishna Varma**.
- Became an important leader of the revolutionary network centred on India House.
- Established the **Free India Society** in **London** in 1906 to mobilise Indian students for independence.
- Associated with revolutionaries including **Madan Lal Dhingra**, **V. V. S. Aiyar** and **Madame Bhikaji Cama**.
- Advocated revolutionary methods against British colonial rule.

Prelims distinction: India House was founded by Shyamji Krishna Varma, not V. D. Savarkar. Savarkar subsequently became one of its prominent leaders.

### 4. Madan Lal Dhingra and Curzon Wyllie Assassination (1909)

- Date: 1 July 1909.
- Place: Imperial Institute, London.
- Revolutionary: Madan Lal Dhingra.
- British official assassinated: Sir William Hutt Curzon Wyllie.
- Dhingra was associated with the revolutionary circle around India House and Savarkar.
- Dhingra was executed on 17 August 1909.

Prelims point: Curzon Wyllie was assassinated by Madan Lal Dhingra, not Savarkar.

### 5. Nashik Conspiracy Case (1909–1910)

- Date of assassination: 21 December 1909.
- Victim: A. M. T. Jackson, Collector of Nashik.
- Assassin: Anant Laxman Kanhere.
- The assassination was connected with revolutionaries associated with Abhinav Bharat.
- Savarkar was implicated in the conspiracy and prosecuted by British authorities.
- Arrested in London in March 1910.
- During his transfer to India, attempted to escape from a ship at Marseilles, France, in July 1910.
- Subsequently sentenced to two terms of transportation for life, amounting to 50 years.

### 6. Cellular Jail and subsequent release

- 1911: Transported to the Cellular Jail in the Andaman Islands.
- Submitted petitions to British authorities seeking clemency or release during imprisonment.
- 1921: Transferred from the Andamans to prisons on the Indian mainland.
- 1924: Released from prison under restrictions, including confinement to Ratnagiri and limitations on political activities.
- 1937: Restrictions on his movement and political participation were lifted.

### 7. Important books and writings

| Mazi Janmathep (My Transportation for Life) | Autobiographical account of imprisonment                                         |
| Six Glorious Epochs of Indian History       | Work on Indian history                                                           |
| Hindu Pad-Padshahi                          | Historical work associated with the Maratha period                               |

### 8. Hindu Mahasabha and later political activities

- Became a prominent leader of the **Hindu Mahasabha** after the removal of restrictions in 1937.
- Became its **President in 1937**.
- Advocated Hindutva, a conception of Hindu national identity.
- **Opposed the Quit India Movement (1942**) and **supported Indian recruitment into the British Indian armed forces during the Second World War**.
- Was **charged in connection with the assassination of Mahatma Gandhi (1948)** but was acquitted by the trial court in 1949 because the evidence was insufficient to establish his guilt.
- Died: 26 February 1966, Bombay.

## Lala Har Dayal (1884–1939)

Lala Har Dayal, also written as Har Dayal, was an Indian revolutionary nationalist, intellectual and writer associated with the Indian revolutionary movement abroad, particularly in the United States.

He is best known for his role in establishing the **Ghadar Movement in 1913**, which sought to **overthrow British colonial rule in India through an armed revolution**.

He was also associated with **India House in London**, founded by **Shyamji Krishna Varma in 1905**.

### 1. Early Life and Background

| Birthplace               | Delhi, British India                                                           |
| Education                | St. Stephen's College, Delhi; Government College, Lahore; University of Oxford |
| Political classification | Revolutionary nationalist                                                      |
| Important influences     | Shyamji Krishna Varma and Indian revolutionary nationalism abroad              |
| Important organisation   | **Ghadar Party** / **Hindustan Association of the Pacific Coast**                      |
| Important publication    | Hindustan Ghadar                                                               |
| Died                     | 4 March 1939                                                                   |
| Place of death           | Philadelphia, United States                                                    |

### Important facts

- Received a scholarship to study at Oxford University.
- Renounced his government scholarship and abandoned his studies in Britain.
- Became involved in revolutionary nationalist activities abroad.
- Worked among Indian immigrants, particularly in North America.

### 2. Association with India House, London

Har Dayal became associated with India House, an important centre of Indian revolutionary nationalism in London.

| Feature                          | Important fact                                        |
| -------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| India House established          | 1905                                                  |
| Founder                          | Shyamji Krishna Varma                                 |
| Location                         | Highgate, London                                      |
| Har Dayal's association          | Indian nationalist activities during his Oxford years |
| Government scholarship renounced | 1907                                                  |

- Har Dayal entered St John's College, Oxford, in 1905.
- He came into contact with Shyamji Krishna Varma and visited India House.
- He resigned his government scholarship in 1907 because of his opposition to British colonial rule.
- He returned to India in 1908 and subsequently moved to Paris.

Prelims distinction: Shyamji Krishna Varma founded India House; Lala Har Dayal was associated with it.

### 3. Revolutionary Activities in Paris (1909)

Har Dayal was also associated with Indian revolutionary nationalists in Paris.

- Moved to Paris in 1909.
- Became editor of Bande Mataram, a revolutionary nationalist journal published from Paris.
- Worked within a network of expatriate Indian revolutionaries, including Bhikaji Cama and S. R. Rana.
- Later moved to the United States, where he became a central organiser of the Ghadar Movement.

- He was released on bail and subsequently left the country.
- Consequently, he was not personally directing the attempted February 1915 uprising in India.

## Baba Gurdit Singh (1860–1954)

Baba Gurdit Singh was an Indian nationalist and businessman from Punjab, best known for organising the voyage of the **Komagata Maru** in **1914**.

The incident **exposed racial discrimination in Canada's immigration policies** and became an important event in the history of the Indian nationalist movement abroad.

### 1. Early Life and Background

| Feature                    | Important fact                                         |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Born                       | 1860                                                   |
| Birthplace                 | Sarhali village, Amritsar district, Punjab             |
| Popular name               | Baba Gurdit Singh                                      |
| Profession                 | Businessman and nationalist activist                   |
| Most important association | Komagata Maru incident (1914)                          |
| Political classification   | Nationalist associated with overseas Indian resistance |
| Died                       | 1954                                                   |

- Belonged to a Sikh family in Punjab.
- Conducted business in Southeast Asia, particularly in Malaya.
- Became concerned about the discriminatory restrictions imposed on Indian immigrants travelling to Canada.
- Organised the Komagata Maru voyage in 1914 to challenge these restrictions.

Prelims classification: Gurdit Singh is best remembered as the organiser of the Komagata Maru voyage, rather than as a founding leader of the Ghadar Party.

### 2. Komagata Maru Incident (1914)

The Komagata Maru was a **Japanese** steamship chartered by Baba Gurdit Singh to transport Indian passengers from Asia to Canada.

| Feature               | Important fact                               |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Ship                  | Komagata Maru                                |
| Year                  | 1914                                         |
| Chartered by          | Baba Gurdit Singh                            |
| Departure             | 4 April 1914, **Hong Kong**                      |
| Destination           | **Vancouver, Canada**                            |
| Arrival at Vancouver  | 23 May 1914                                  |
| Total passengers      | 376                                          |
| Departure from Canada | 23 July 1914                                 |
| Return to India       | 29 September 1914, Budge Budge near Calcutta |

Composition of passengers:
- 340 Sikhs
- 24 Muslims
- 12 Hindus

All 376 passengers were of Indian origin.

### 3. Why Was the Ship Denied Entry into Canada?

The Canadian government had introduced immigration restrictions that discriminated against Asian immigrants, particularly Indians.

### Important restrictions

| Restriction                          | Provision                                                                                               |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Continuous Journey Regulation (1908) | Immigrants were required to travel directly from their country of origin to Canada without interruption |
| Financial requirement                | Certain Asian immigrants were required to possess at least 200 Canadian dollars                         |

There was no practical direct passenger shipping service between India and Canada, making the continuous journey requirement especially restrictive.

Gurdit Singh chartered the ship to challenge these discriminatory regulations.

When it reached Vancouver, Canadian authorities refused entry to most passengers. The ship remained in the harbour for approximately two months before being forced to return to India.

### 4. Budge Budge Incident (29 September 1914)

The Komagata Maru returned to India and reached Budge Budge, near Calcutta, on 29 September 1914.

### Important facts

- British authorities suspected the returning passengers of having revolutionary connections.
- The authorities attempted to send the passengers to Punjab by a special train.
- A confrontation occurred between the passengers and British Indian police.
- Police opened fire, killing approximately 19–20 passengers.
- Many passengers were arrested.
- Gurdit Singh escaped arrest and remained in hiding for several years.

The Government of India's Ministry of Information and Broadcasting records 19 deaths, while Parks Canada records 20.

Prelims Point: Budge Budge Incident — 29 September 1914 — Near Calcutta — Associated with the return of the Komagata Maru.

### 5. Relationship with the Ghadar Movement

The Komagata Maru incident became an important source of revolutionary mobilisation among overseas Indians.

- It exposed the unequal treatment of Indians within the British Empire.
- Ghadar activists used the incident to strengthen anti-colonial mobilisation.
- The incident contributed to the atmosphere of revolutionary activity that culminated in the attempted Ghadar uprising of 1915.

Important distinction: Gurdit Singh organised the Komagata Maru voyage, but he was not the founding President of the Ghadar Party. That position was held by Sohan Singh Bhakna.

### 6. Later Life

- Gurdit Singh remained underground after the Budge Budge incident.
- He surrendered to British authorities in 1922, reportedly following advice from Mahatma Gandhi.
- He was imprisoned and later released.
- He died in 1954.

## Virendranath Chattopadhyaya (1880–1937)

Virendranath Chattopadhyaya, popularly known as Chatto, was an Indian revolutionary nationalist and international political activist associated with the Indian independence movement abroad, particularly in Germany and Soviet Russia.

He is best known for his leadership in the **Berlin Committee (Indian Independence Committee)**, established in **1914**, which sought German assistance to overthrow British colonial rule during the First World War.

Before the First World War, Virendranath Chattopadhyaya became associated with Indian revolutionary circles in Britain.

## Madame Bhikaji Cama (1861–1936)

Madame Bhikaji Cama, also known as Madam Cama, was an Indian revolutionary nationalist who played an important role in the Indian independence movement abroad, particularly in Britain, France and Germany.

She is best known for **unfurling an early Indian nationalist flag** at the **International Socialist Congress** in **Stuttgart, Germany**, on 22 August **1907**.

She was also associated with **Shyamji Krishna Varma**, S. R. Rana and other Indian revolutionaries in Europe.

### Important facts

## Sardar Ajit Singh (1881–1947)

Sardar Ajit Singh was an Indian revolutionary nationalist and an important leader of the agrarian agitation in Punjab in 1907 against British colonial policies.

He is best known for his association with the Pagri Sambhal Jatta movement (1907), which mobilised peasants against colonial agrarian legislation.

He was the **paternal uncle of Bhagat Singh**, the revolutionary associated with the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA).

### 1. Early Life and Background

| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------ |

| Nephew                   | Bhagat Singh                                           |
| Political classification | Revolutionary nationalist and agrarian movement leader |
| Important organisation   | Bharat Mata Society                                    |
| Important movement       | Pagri Sambhal Jatta movement (1907)                    |
| Important associate      | Lala Lajpat Rai                                        |
| Died                     | 15 August 1947                                         |
| Place of death           | Dalhousie, Himachal Pradesh (then Punjab Province)     |

Prelims classification: Ajit Singh was an early revolutionary nationalist associated with Punjab's agrarian unrest, particularly during the period of militant nationalism (1905–1909).

### 2. Bharat Mata Society (1906–1907)

Ajit Singh helped organise the Bharat Mata Society, also known as the Bharat Mata Association, to mobilise opposition to British colonial rule in Punjab.

| Feature              | Important fact                                                      |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Organisation         | Bharat Mata Society                                                 |

The society helped circulate nationalist literature and organise public meetings. Ajit Singh's brothers Kishan Singh and Swaran Singh were also involved in nationalist activities.

### 3. Pagri Sambhal Jatta Movement (1907)

This is Ajit Singh's most important association for UPSC Prelims.

The Pagri Sambhal Jatta movement was a major peasant agitation in Punjab in 1907, directed against British agrarian legislation and increases in land revenue and irrigation charges.

### Causes of the movement

| British measure                              | Why it was opposed                                                                   |
| -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Punjab Land Alienation Act, 1900             | Restricted transfers of agricultural land between specified groups                   |
| Punjab Land Colonisation Bill, 1906          | Proposed restrictions affecting inheritance and landholding rights in canal colonies |
| Increased canal-water rates and land revenue | Increased the financial burden on cultivators                                        |

The Punjab Land Colonisation Bill of 1906, particularly its proposed provisions affecting settlers in the Chenab Colony, was a central immediate cause of the agitation.

### Important personalities

| Personality       | Role                                              |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Sardar Ajit Singh | Principal leader of the agitation                 |
| Lala Lajpat Rai   | Supported the Punjab agrarian agitation           |
| Sufi Amba Prasad  | Revolutionary nationalist associate of Ajit Singh |
| Banke Dayal       | Wrote the poem Pagri Sambhal Jatta                |

### Origin of the slogan

- The phrase Pagri Sambhal Jatta means approximately Protect your turban, O farmer.
- The turban symbolised the dignity and honour of Punjab's peasants.
- Banke Dayal, associated with the newspaper Jhang Sial, composed the poem.
- It was recited at a major peasant gathering in Lyallpur on 22 March 1907.
- The poem became the rallying cry of the agitation.


Prelims distinction: Ajit Singh led the movement; Banke Dayal wrote the poem that gave the movement its name.

### 4. Deportation to Mandalay (1907)

British authorities viewed the growing peasant agitation as a threat to colonial administration.

- Lala Lajpat Rai was arrested on 9 May 1907.
- Ajit Singh was arrested on 2 June 1907.
- Both were deported to Mandalay in Burma (present-day Myanmar).
- Their detention was carried out under Regulation III of 1818, which permitted detention without an ordinary criminal trial.
- Both were released in November 1907, following the withdrawal of the deportation orders.

Prelims Point: Ajit Singh and Lala Lajpat Rai — Mandalay deportation — 1907.

### 5. Revolutionary Activities Abroad (1909–1947)

Ajit Singh left India in 1909 to avoid renewed British prosecution and spent approximately 38 years abroad.

### Association with Sufi Amba Prasad

- Ajit Singh and Sufi Amba Prasad were associated with revolutionary activities in Persia.
- Ajit Singh helped establish a revolutionary centre at Shiraz.
- A Persian-language revolutionary journal called Hayat was launched in 1910.

# Ram Prasad Bismil (1897–1927)

Ram Prasad Bismil was an Indian revolutionary nationalist, poet and writer associated with the revolutionary movement in northern India.

He is particularly remembered for his role in the Mainpuri Conspiracy (1918), the formation of the Hindustan Republican Association (HRA, 1924) and the Kakori Train Action (1925).

He was one of the principal organisers of the HRA and worked closely with Ashfaqulla Khan, Sachindra Nath Sanyal, Rajendra Lahiri and other revolutionaries.

## 1. Early Life and Background

| Feature                  | Important fact                                             |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Born                     | 11 June 1897                                               |
| Birthplace               | Shahjahanpur, United Provinces (present-day Uttar Pradesh) |
| Father                   | Murlidhar                                                  |
| Mother                   | Moolmati Devi                                              |
| Pen name                 | Bismil                                                     |
| Political classification | Revolutionary nationalist                                  |
| Important organisations  | Matrivedi; Hindustan Republican Association                |
| Important events         | Mainpuri Conspiracy (1918), Kakori Train Action (1925)     |
| Executed                 | 19 December 1927                                           |
| Place of execution       | Gorakhpur Jail                                             |

Bismil was influenced by the nationalist and reformist ideas of the Arya Samaj, founded by Swami Dayananda Saraswati in 1875.

He advocated Indian independence through revolutionary organisation and armed resistance to British colonial rule.

## 2. Mainpuri Conspiracy (1918)

Before the formation of the HRA, Ram Prasad Bismil was associated with revolutionary activities in the United Provinces.

### Important facts

- Bismil worked with Genda Lal Dixit, a schoolteacher and revolutionary organiser.
- They recruited young nationalists in Shahjahanpur, Mainpuri, Etawah and neighbouring districts.
- Bismil distributed the nationalist pamphlet Deshvasiyon Ke Naam Sandesh and the poem Mainpuri Ki Pratigya in 1918.
- Revolutionary activities included attempts to obtain funds for arms and organisation.
- Following police investigations, Bismil went underground and evaded arrest.&#x20;

## 3. Hindustan Republican Association (1924)

The Hindustan Republican Association (HRA) was a revolutionary organisation established in 1924 to overthrow British colonial rule and establish a republic in India.

## 4. Kakori Train Action (1925)

The Kakori Train Action, also called the Kakori Conspiracy or Kakori Train Robbery, was organised by HRA revolutionaries to obtain government funds for revolutionary activities.

| Feature             | Important fact                                                            |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Date                | 9 August 1925                                                             |

| Consequence         | Arrest and prosecution of HRA revolutionaries                             |

### Important facts

- Revolutionaries stopped the 8-Down Saharanpur–Lucknow passenger train near Kakori.
- They seized money being transported for the government treasury.
- The objective was to finance revolutionary activities.
- A passenger, Ahmed Ali, was accidentally killed during the operation.
- British authorities arrested several revolutionaries following the incident.
- Chandrashekhar Azad escaped arrest and continued revolutionary activities.&#x20;
