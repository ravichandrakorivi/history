## Raja Rammohan Roy (1772–1833)

Raja Rammohan Roy was an Indian social and religious reformer, educationist, journalist and early advocate of modern political ideas in India.

He is popularly known as the Father of the **Indian Renaissance** and is particularly remembered for founding the **Atmiya Sabha (1815)** and **Brahmo Sabha (1828)**, **campaigning against the practice of Sati**, and promoting **modern education and freedom of the press**.

### 1. Early life and political associations

- Born: 22 May 1772, Radhanagar village, Hooghly district, Bengal Presidency.
- Popular title: Raja Rammohan Roy.
- Religious outlook: Monotheism, rationalism and criticism of religious orthodoxy.
- Studied Sanskrit, Persian and Arabic, among other languages.
- Worked for a period in the service of the East India Company.
- Advocated women's rights, modern education and freedom of expression.
- Died: 27 September 1833, Bristol, England.
- Burial: Arnos Vale Cemetery, Bristol.

Prelims Point: Rammohan Roy was associated with the Bengal Renaissance and early nineteenth-century socio-religious reform movements.

### 2. Atmiya Sabha (1815)

- Founded: 1815.
- Founder: Raja Rammohan Roy.
- Place: Calcutta.
- Meaning: Society of Friends.
- Objective: Promote discussions on religion, philosophy and social reform.

### Important features

- Encouraged monotheism (belief in one God).
- Criticised idol worship and religious superstition.
- Discussed Hindu scriptures, particularly the Upanishads.
- Opposed social practices such as Sati.

Prelims Point: The Atmiya Sabha (1815) preceded the establishment of the Brahmo Sabha (1828).

### 3. Brahmo Sabha (1828)

- Founded: 20 August 1828.
- Founder: Raja Rammohan Roy.
- Place: Calcutta.
- Established as a religious reform organisation promoting monotheism.
- Subsequently became known as the Brahmo Samaj.
- A trust deed for its place of worship was executed on 8 January 1830.
- The new prayer hall was inaugurated on 23 January 1830.

### Important principles

- Belief in one supreme, formless God.
- Opposition to idol worship.
- Rejection of religious rituals involving animal sacrifice.
- Emphasis on morality, reason and spiritual understanding.
- Respect for different religious traditions.

### Brahmo Samaj — later developments

| ---- | ----------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| 1828 | Brahmo Sabha founded                      | Raja Rammohan Roy                             |

Prelims Point: Raja Rammohan Roy founded the Brahmo Sabha in 1828, whereas Debendranath Tagore founded the Tattwabodhini Sabha in 1839.

### 4. Campaign against Sati and its Abolition (1829)

Rammohan Roy campaigned against the practice of Sati, in which a widow was burned on her husband's funeral pyre.

- Criticised the practice through writings, petitions and public debate.
- Argued that Sati was not a compulsory religious duty under Hindu scriptures.
- Supported government intervention to prohibit the practice.
- His campaign contributed to the abolition of Sati under Governor-General Lord William Bentinck.

### Bengal Sati Regulation, 1829

- Date: 4 December 1829.
- Governor-General: **Lord William Bentinck** (**first Governor General of India under Charter Act 1833**).
- Regulation: Bengal Regulation XVII of 1829.
- Declared Sati illegal and punishable under criminal law.
- Initially applied to the Bengal Presidency; prohibition was subsequently extended to the Madras and Bombay Presidencies in 1830.

Prelims Point: Rammohan Roy was a major campaigner against Sati, but the legal prohibition was enacted by Lord William Bentinck in 1829.

### 5. Other Social Reforms

Rammohan Roy advocated:

- Women's inheritance and property rights.
- Opposition to polygamy and child marriage.
- Criticism of caste discrimination.
- Promotion of women's education.
- Religious tolerance and rational inquiry.
- Opposition to harmful customs justified in the name of religion.

Important distinction: Rammohan Roy advocated women's rights, but the **Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act, 1856** is especially associated with **Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar**.

### 6. Contributions to Modern Education

- Supported modern education in science, mathematics and other useful subjects.
- Advocated English education alongside the study of Indian philosophical traditions.
- Established the Anglo-Hindu School in 1822.
- Founded Vedanta College in 1825, combining the study of Vedantic philosophy with modern learning.
- Wrote to Lord Amherst in 1823, arguing for modern scientific education rather than exclusive reliance on traditional Sanskrit learning.

### Hindu College (1817)

- Established in Calcutta in 1817.
- Rammohan Roy supported the cause of modern education and was associated with the educational initiatives surrounding its establishment.
- David Hare was a leading figure in the founding of Hindu College.

Prelims Point: Vedanta College (1825) was founded by Rammohan Roy. Do not confuse it with Hindu College (1817), whose establishment involved several reformers and patrons.

### 7. Journalism and Freedom of the Press

| Newspaper / Publication | Year | Language |
| ----------------------- | ---- | -------- |
| Sambad Kaumudi          | 1821 | Bengali  |
| Mirat-ul-Akhbar         | 1822 | Persian  |
| Brahmanical Magazine    | 1821 | English  |

- Sambad Kaumudi promoted social reform and public awareness.
- Mirat-ul-Akhbar discussed political, religious and social issues.
- Opposed restrictions on press freedom.
- Discontinued Mirat-ul-Akhbar in 1823 in protest against the restrictive press regulations introduced under John Adam.

Prelims Point: Sambad Kaumudi (Bengali) and Mirat-ul-Akhbar (Persian) are associated with Raja Rammohan Roy.

### 9. Visit to England (1830–1833)

- Travelled to England in 1830, arriving in 1831.
- Represented the interests of Mughal Emperor Akbar II.
- The title Raja was conferred upon him by Akbar II.
- Sought to advance the Mughal emperor's claims regarding his allowance and status.
- Supported the continuation of the prohibition on Sati.
- Presented views on Indian administrative and legal matters during discussions surrounding the renewal of the East India Company's charter.
- Died in Bristol in 1833.

Prelims Point: The **title Raja was conferred on Rammohan Roy by Mughal Emperor Akbar II**, not by the British government.

## Dwarkanath Tagore (1794–1846)

Dwarkanath Tagore was an Indian entrepreneur, industrialist, philanthropist and social reform supporter associated with the Bengal Renaissance.

He is particularly remembered for his association with Raja Rammohan Roy, his support for the Brahmo Samaj, his role in establishing the Landholders' Society (1838), and his pioneering contributions to modern business and industry in colonial India.

He was the father of Debendranath Tagore and the grandfather of Rabindranath Tagore.

### 1. Early Life and Background

| Feature         | Important fact                       |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Born            | 1794                                 |
| Birthplace      | Calcutta, Bengal Presidency          |
| Family          | Tagore family of Jorasanko, Calcutta |
| Father          | Rammani Tagore                       |
| Adoptive father | Ramlochan Tagore                     |
| Son             | Debendranath Tagore                  |
| Grandson        | Rabindranath Tagore                  |
| Popular title   | Prince Dwarkanath Tagore             |
| Died            | 1 August 1846, London                |

- Belonged to the influential Tagore family of Bengal.
- Became a prominent Indian entrepreneur during the early nineteenth century.
- Engaged in banking, trade, coal mining, shipping and other commercial activities.
- Supported modern education and social reform.

### 2. Association with Raja Rammohan Roy

Dwarkanath Tagore was a close associate and supporter of Raja Rammohan Roy.

### Important contributions

- Supported Rammohan Roy's religious and social reform initiatives.
- Associated with the early Brahmo Sabha, established in 1828.
- Supported efforts to challenge social and religious orthodoxy.
- Promoted modern education and intellectual development.
- Supported reformist journalism and public discussion.

### Important distinction

| Personality         | Important association                                          |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Raja Rammohan Roy   | Founded Brahmo Sabha in 1828                                   |
| Dwarkanath Tagore   | Important supporter and patron of early Brahmo reform          |
| Debendranath Tagore | Founded Tattwabodhini Sabha in 1839; strengthened Brahmo Samaj |
| Rabindranath Tagore | Poet; Nobel Prize in Literature (1913)                         |

Prelims Point: Dwarkanath Tagore was an important supporter of the Brahmo movement, but Raja Rammohan Roy founded the Brahmo Sabha in 1828.

### 3. Landholders' Society (1838)

Dwarkanath Tagore was associated with the establishment of the Landholders' Society, one of the earliest political associations in British India.

| Feature                           | Important fact                                                                 |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Organisation                      | Landholders' Society                                                           |
| Also known as                     | Zamindari Association                                                          |
| Established                       | 1838                                                                           |
| Place                             | Calcutta                                                                       |
| Important founders and associates | Dwarkanath Tagore, Radhakanta Deb, Prasanna Kumar Tagore and other landholders |
| Principal objective               | Protect the interests of landholders                                           |

### Important features

- Represented the interests of zamindars and landholders.
- Sought to influence colonial policies affecting landed property.
- Used petitions and constitutional methods to communicate demands to the British government.
- Was among the earliest organised political associations in British India.

### Historical significance

The Landholders' Society is regarded as an important predecessor of later political associations in India.

However, its membership and demands were primarily concerned with landed interests, rather than broad-based national political mobilisation.

Prelims Point: Landholders' Society (1838) — Dwarkanath Tagore and other Bengal zamindars.

### 4. Industrial and Commercial Activities

Dwarkanath Tagore was among the early Indian entrepreneurs who participated extensively in modern commercial and industrial enterprises.

### Carr, Tagore and Company (1834)

| Feature            | Important fact                                                |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------- |

| Important founders | Dwarkanath Tagore and William Carr                            |
| Nature             | Indo-European business partnership                            |
| Areas of activity  | Coal mining, trade, shipping and other commercial enterprises |

### Important contributions

- Developed business partnerships with European merchants.
- Invested in coal mining, particularly in the Raniganj coalfields.
- Participated in shipping and commercial trade.
- Helped expand Indian participation in modern industrial enterprises.

Prelims Point: Carr, Tagore and Company (1834) — Dwarkanath Tagore and William Carr.

### 5. Banking Activities

Dwarkanath Tagore was associated with early modern banking institutions in Bengal.

### Union Bank (1829)

| Feature               | Important fact       |
| --------------------- | -------------------- |

- The bank was part of the expanding commercial and financial activities of nineteenth-century Calcutta.
- It should not be confused with the present-day Union Bank of India, established in 1919.

Prelims Point: Union Bank (1829), Calcutta — associated with Dwarkanath Tagore.

### 6. Contributions to Modern Education

Dwarkanath Tagore supported the spread of modern education in Bengal.

### Important associations

- Supported Western education and scientific learning.
- Associated with the educational reform activities of Raja Rammohan Roy.
- Supported institutions promoting modern learning.
- Was among the Indian supporters of the establishment of Calcutta Medical College (1835).

## Debendranath Tagore (1817–1905)

Debendranath Tagore was an Indian religious and social reformer associated with the Brahmo Samaj and the **Bengal Renaissance**.

He is particularly remembered for founding the **Tattwabodhini Sabha (1839)**, revitalising the **Brahmo Samaj** after Raja Rammohan Roy's death, and promoting monotheism and the study of the Upanishads.

He was popularly known as Maharshi Debendranath Tagore.

### 1. Early life and associations

- Born: 15 May 1817, Calcutta, Bengal Presidency.
- Father: Dwarkanath Tagore.
- Son: Rabindranath Tagore.
- Popular title: Maharshi.
- Religious association: Brahmo Samaj.
- Major organisation founded: Tattwabodhini Sabha (1839).
- Died: 19 January 1905, Calcutta.

Prelims Point: Debendranath Tagore was the father of Rabindranath Tagore, not the founder of the Brahmo Samaj.

### 2. Tattwabodhini Sabha (1839)

- Founded: 6 October 1839.
- Founder: Debendranath Tagore.
- Place: Calcutta.
- Originally known as the Tattvaranjini Sabha.
- Promoted the study of the Upanishads, religious philosophy and ethical teachings.
- Encouraged monotheism and rational religious inquiry.
- Played an important role in revitalising the Brahmo movement.

### Important objectives

- Promote the study of Hindu philosophical texts, particularly the Upanishads.
- Spread belief in one God.
- Encourage religious and moral reform.
- Oppose superstition and ritualistic practices.
- Promote education and intellectual discussion.

Prelims Point: Tattwabodhini Sabha (1839) was founded by Debendranath Tagore, whereas Atmiya Sabha (1815) was founded by Raja Rammohan Roy.

### 3. Association with the Brahmo Samaj

### Background

- Brahmo Sabha founded: 1828.
- Founder: Raja Rammohan Roy.
- Rammohan Roy died in 1833.
- The organisation subsequently experienced a period of reduced activity.

### Debendranath's contribution

- Formally joined the Brahmo Samaj in 1843.
- Revitalised the organisation through the activities of the Tattwabodhini Sabha.
- Encouraged the study of the Upanishads and monotheistic religious principles.
- Developed a more organised system of religious instruction and initiation.
- Emphasised the authority of reason and spiritual understanding.

Prelims Point: Debendranath revitalised the Brahmo Samaj; he did not found it.

### 4. Tattwabodhini Patrika (1843)

- Established: August 1843.
- Language: Bengali.
- Associated organisation: Tattwabodhini Sabha.
- First editor: Akshay Kumar Datta.
- Principal patron: Debendranath Tagore.

### Importance

- Promoted religious and social reform.
- Published discussions on philosophy, science and education.
- Helped disseminate Brahmo Samaj ideas.
- Encouraged rational inquiry and intellectual debate.

Prelims Point: Tattwabodhini Patrika (1843) was edited by Akshay Kumar Datta, not Debendranath Tagore.

### 5. Religious and Social Reform Ideas

| Area                | Debendranath Tagore's position                                  |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Monotheism          | Supported belief in one God                                     |
| Idol worship        | Opposed                                                         |
| Upanishads          | Emphasised their philosophical teachings                        |
| Religious rituals   | Opposed elaborate ritualism and superstition                    |
| Religious authority | Emphasised spiritual understanding and reason                   |
| Social reform       | Supported moral and educational reform                          |
| Vedas               | Eventually rejected the doctrine that the Vedas were infallible |

### Important distinction

Debendranath drew heavily upon the Upanishads but did not ultimately accept the infallibility of the Vedas.

This distinguished his position from that of Dayananda Saraswati, who regarded the Vedas as infallible.

### 6. Brahmo Samaj Split (1866)

Differences developed between Debendranath Tagore and Keshab Chandra Sen concerning religious practices and the pace of social reform.

### Split of 1866

- Year: 1866.
- Debendranath Tagore: Led the Adi Brahmo Samaj.
- Keshab Chandra Sen: Established the Brahmo Samaj of India.

### Important distinction

| Organisation          | Year | Associated personality                        |
| --------------------- | ---- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Brahmo Sabha          | 1828 | Raja Rammohan Roy                             |
| Tattwabodhini Sabha   | 1839 | Debendranath Tagore                           |
| Adi Brahmo Samaj      | 1866 | Debendranath Tagore                           |
| Brahmo Samaj of India | 1866 | Keshab Chandra Sen                            |
| Sadharan Brahmo Samaj | 1878 | Ananda Mohan Bose, Sivanath Sastri and others |

Prelims Point: The 1866 split involved Debendranath Tagore and Keshab Chandra Sen, whereas the 1878 split led to the establishment of the Sadharan Brahmo Samaj.

### 7. Santiniketan

- Year: 1863.
- Founder: Debendranath Tagore.
- Location: Birbhum district, Bengal (present-day West Bengal).
- Established a spiritual retreat at Santiniketan.
- The name Santiniketan means Abode of Peace.

### Later developments

- 1901: Rabindranath Tagore established a school at Santiniketan.
- 1921: Rabindranath Tagore established Visva-Bharati.
- 1951: Visva-Bharati became a Central University and an Institution of National Importance.

Prelims Point: Debendranath Tagore established the Santiniketan retreat, whereas Rabindranath Tagore founded Visva-Bharati (1921).

### Most important Prelims traps

## Keshab Chandra Sen (1838–1884)

Keshab Chandra Sen was an Indian religious and social reformer and an important leader of the Brahmo Samaj during the nineteenth-century **Bengal Renaissance**.

He is particularly remembered for establishing the **Brahmo Samaj of India (1866)**, founding the **Indian Reform Association (1870)**, advocating **women's education and social reform**, and establishing the **New Dispensation (Nava Vidhan)** in 1880.

### 1. Early life and associations

- Born: 19 November 1838, Calcutta, Bengal Presidency.
- Religious association: Brahmo Samaj.
- Joined Brahmo Samaj: 1858.
- Important associate: Debendranath Tagore.
- Advocated monotheism, social equality, women's education and reform of marriage customs.
- Promoted a universal religious outlook incorporating teachings from different religions.
- Died: 8 January 1884, Calcutta.

Prelims Point: Keshab Chandra Sen was an important leader of the Brahmo Samaj, but Raja Rammohan Roy founded the original Brahmo Sabha in 1828.

### 2. Association with Brahmo Samaj

- Joined the Brahmo Samaj in 1858.
- Worked with Debendranath Tagore to promote religious and social reform.
- Encouraged greater participation in the Brahmo movement.
- Opposed caste discrimination, idol worship and hereditary social restrictions.
- Supported women's education, widow remarriage and inter-caste marriage.
- Favoured a more universal and activist approach to religious reform.

### Differences with Debendranath Tagore

Disagreements emerged concerning:

- The pace and extent of social reform.
- The relationship between Hindu traditions and universal religion.
- Marriage customs and caste restrictions.
- Religious practices and organisational leadership.

These disagreements resulted in a split in 1866.

### 3. Brahmo Samaj of India (1866)

- Established: 1866.
- Founder: Keshab Chandra Sen.
- Place: Calcutta.
- Formed following a split with Debendranath Tagore.
- Promoted universal monotheism and progressive social reform.
- Emphasised the spiritual teachings of different religious traditions.

### Brahmo Samaj split of 1866

| Organisation          | Associated leader   |
| --------------------- | ------------------- |
| Adi Brahmo Samaj      | Debendranath Tagore |
| Brahmo Samaj of India | Keshab Chandra Sen  |

Prelims Point: Keshab Chandra Sen founded the Brahmo Samaj of India in 1866, not the original Brahmo Sabha of 1828.

### 4. Indian Reform Association (1870)

- Founded: 1870.
- Founder: Keshab Chandra Sen.
- Place: Calcutta.
- Established following his visit to England.
- Sought to promote practical social reforms.

### Important objectives

- Women's education.
- Adult and general education.
- Temperance and opposition to alcohol abuse.
- Publication of affordable literature.
- Social service and charitable activities.

Prelims Point: The Indian Reform Association (1870) was founded by Keshab Chandra Sen, not Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar.

### 5. Marriage Reforms and the Native Marriage Act (1872)

Keshab Chandra Sen campaigned against child marriage and supported inter-caste marriage and women's rights.

### Native Marriage Act, 1872

- Also known as Act III of 1872.
- Commonly associated with the Brahmo Marriage Act.
- Legalised civil marriages under specified conditions.
- Permitted marriages outside conventional religious and caste restrictions.
- Prescribed minimum marriage ages of:
  - 14 years for girls
  - 18 years for boys
- Required the consent of the parties and compliance with statutory conditions.

Important clarification: The Act did not automatically apply to all Hindu marriages. Its provisions concerned marriages registered under the special legal framework, including a declaration that the parties did not profess specified religions.

Prelims Point: The Native Marriage Act, 1872 is associated with the reform efforts of Keshab Chandra Sen.

### 6. Cooch Behar Marriage Controversy (1878)

- Year: 1878.
- Keshab Chandra Sen arranged the marriage of his daughter Suniti Devi to Nripendra Narayan, the Maharaja of Cooch Behar.
- Both were below the minimum ages prescribed by the Native Marriage Act of 1872.
- The marriage also involved traditional Hindu ceremonies.
- This generated controversy because Sen had previously advocated marriage reform.

### Consequence

- Reformist members separated from Keshab Chandra Sen's organisation.
- They established the Sadharan Brahmo Samaj in 1878.

Prelims Point: The Cooch Behar marriage controversy (1878) was an important immediate cause of the formation of the Sadharan Brahmo Samaj.

### 7. Sadharan Brahmo Samaj (1878)

- Established: 15 May 1878.
- Place: Calcutta.
- Formed by reformist members who separated from Keshab Chandra Sen's organisation.

### Important founding personalities

- Sivanath Sastri

- Other reformist Brahmo leaders.

## Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar (1820–1891)

Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar was an Indian social reformer, educationist, Sanskrit scholar and writer associated with the **Bengal Renaissance**.

He is particularly remembered for his campaign for **Hindu widow remarriage**, which contributed to the enactment of the **Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act, 1856**, during the Governor-Generalship of **Lord Canning** (the **last Governor-General of India and the first Viceroy of India**).

He also promoted women's education, opposed child marriage and polygamy, and contributed significantly to the development of modern Bengali prose.

### 1. Early Life and Education

| Mother        | Bhagavati Devi                                                                    |
| Institution   | Sanskrit College, Calcutta                                                        |
| Title         | Vidyasagar, meaning Ocean of Knowledge                                            |
| Died          | 29 July 1891                                                                      |

- Received his education at Sanskrit College, Calcutta.
- Earned the title Vidyasagar for his scholarship in Sanskrit.
- Became one of the prominent intellectual figures of the Bengal Renaissance.

### 2. Campaign for Hindu Widow Remarriage

Vidyasagar strongly advocated the remarriage of Hindu widows, challenging prevailing social restrictions.

### Contributions

- Argued that Hindu widow remarriage was supported by interpretations of Hindu scriptures.
- Used religious texts, particularly the Parashara Smriti, to support his arguments.
- Organised petitions demanding legal recognition of widow remarriage.
- Published writings supporting the reform.
- Helped arrange widow remarriages following the enactment of the law.

### Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act, 1856

Important provisions

- Recognised the validity of marriages involving Hindu widows.
- Children born from such marriages were considered legitimate.
- A widow who remarried generally lost her rights in her deceased husband's property, subject to the Act's provisions.

Important distinction: The Act of 1856 legalised widow remarriage; it did not make widow remarriage compulsory.

# Other Indian Socio-Religious Reformers

## Swami Dayananda Saraswati (1824–1883)

Swami Dayananda Saraswati was an Indian religious and social reformer and the founder of the **Arya Samaj (1875)**.

He is particularly remembered for advocating a **return to the Vedas**, **opposing idol worship and caste discrimination based on birth, promoting women's education and widow remarriage**, and writing **Satyarth Prakash (1875)**.

He was an important figure in the socio-religious reform movements of nineteenth-century India.

### 1. Early life and religious associations

- Born: 12 February 1824, Tankara, Morvi State, Kathiawar (present-day Gujarat).
- Original name: Mool Shankar (Mul Shankar).
- Popular name: Swami Dayananda Saraswati.
- Religious tradition: Hindu reform movement based on the authority of the Vedas.
- Spiritual teacher: Swami Virajananda, a Sanskrit scholar based in Mathura.
- Major organisation: Arya Samaj (1875).
- Major work: Satyarth Prakash (The Light of Truth).
- Died: 30 October 1883, Ajmer, Rajasthan.

Prelims Point: Dayananda Saraswati founded the Arya Samaj, whereas Raja Rammohan Roy founded the Brahmo Sabha.

### 2. Arya Samaj (1875)

- Founded: 10 April 1875.
- Founder: Swami Dayananda Saraswati.
- Place: Bombay (present-day Mumbai).
- Objective: Reform Hindu society by promoting the teachings of the Vedas.
- Motto: Krinvanto Vishwam Aryam — commonly translated as “Make the world noble.”
- The organisation promoted religious reform, social equality and education.

### Important principles

- The Vedas were regarded as the authoritative source of true knowledge.
- Belief in one formless God.
- Opposition to idol worship, superstition and elaborate ritualism.
- Opposition to caste discrimination based on birth.
- Support for women's education and widow remarriage.
- Opposition to child marriage.
- Promotion of education and social service.

Prelims Point: The Arya Samaj was founded in Bombay in 1875, although Lahore later became an important centre of its activities.

### 3. Satyarth Prakash (1875)

- Author: Swami Dayananda Saraswati.
- First published: 1875.
- Language: Hindi.
- Meaning: The Light of Truth.
- Considered the principal work of Dayananda Saraswati.
- Presented his interpretation of Vedic teachings and criticism of religious practices he considered inconsistent with them.
- Discussed religious, social and educational reform.

Prelims Point: Satyarth Prakash (1875) was written by Dayananda Saraswati, not Raja Rammohan Roy or Swami Vivekananda.

### 4. Religious Reforms

### Return to the Vedas

Dayananda advocated a return to the teachings of the Vedas, commonly summarised by the slogan “Back to the Vedas.”

He regarded the Vedas as a source of spiritual and moral knowledge and rejected later practices he believed were inconsistent with them.

### Opposition to idol worship

- Rejected idol worship.
- Opposed priestly domination and elaborate rituals.
- Advocated worship of one formless God.
- Rejected religious superstition.

### Caste system

- Opposed caste distinctions based solely on birth.
- Supported a varna system based on qualities, conduct and occupation, rather than hereditary status.
- Criticised untouchability and caste discrimination.

Important distinction: Dayananda did not reject the concept of varna altogether; he advocated its interpretation based on merit and conduct rather than birth.

### 5. Social Reforms

| Area                       | Dayananda's position                   |
| -------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Child marriage             | Opposed                                |
| Widow remarriage           | Supported                              |
| Women's education          | Supported                              |
| Female infanticide         | Opposed                                |
| Untouchability             | Opposed                                |
| Hereditary caste hierarchy | Criticised                             |
| Education                  | Promoted Vedic and modern learning     |
| Religious conversion       | Supported reconversion through Shuddhi |

Prelims Point: The Arya Samaj supported widow remarriage and women's education, but its religious outlook differed from the Brahmo Samaj.

### 6. Shuddhi Movement

- Meaning: Purification.
- Associated with Dayananda Saraswati and the Arya Samaj.
- Sought to bring people who had converted to other religions back into the Hindu fold.
- Later became an important programme of the Arya Samaj.
- Swami Shraddhanand played a prominent role in the Shuddhi Movement in the early twentieth century.

Prelims Point: The Shuddhi Movement is associated with the Arya Samaj, particularly Dayananda Saraswati and later Swami Shraddhanand.

### 7. Educational Contributions

The Arya Samaj promoted education through two important institutional traditions.

### Dayanand Anglo-Vedic (DAV) Institutions

- First DAV School: Established in 1886, at Lahore.
- Important leader: Lala Hansraj (Mahatma Hansraj).
- Combined modern Western education with Vedic teachings.
- Developed after Dayananda's death in 1883.

### Gurukul Kangri

- Established: 1902.
- Place: Haridwar (present-day Uttarakhand).
- Founder: Swami Shraddhanand (Munshi Ram).
- Promoted education based on the traditional Gurukul system.

### DAV vs Gurukul

| Educational emphasis  | Modern education combined with Vedic learning | Traditional Gurukul-based Vedic education |

Prelims Point: Dayananda Saraswati did not personally establish DAV School (1886) or Gurukul Kangri (1902), as both were founded after his death in 1883.

### 8. Arya Samaj and the National Movement

Although Dayananda died before the establishment of the Indian National Congress in 1885, the Arya Samaj subsequently influenced several nationalist leaders.

### Important personalities

### Political ideas

- Encouraged Swadeshi and indigenous education.
- Criticised colonial dependence.
- Supported the ideal of self-government.
- Used the term Swaraj in his writings before it became a central demand of the national movement.

Prelims Point: Dayananda Saraswati is among the early Indian thinkers associated with the use of the term Swaraj, although the later organised national movement developed the demand in different political contexts.

### 9. Arya Samaj vs Brahmo Samaj

## Jyotirao Phule (1827–1890)

Jyotirao Govindrao Phule, popularly known as Mahatma Jyotiba Phule, was an Indian social reformer, educationist, writer and anti-caste thinker associated with the nineteenth-century social reform movement in Maharashtra.

He is particularly remembered for founding the **Satyashodhak Samaj (1873)**, **promoting women's education alongside his wife Savitribai Phule**, **opposing caste discrimination and untouchability**, and writing Gulamgiri (1873).

### 1. Early Life and Background

| Feature                | Important fact                  |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Born                   | 11 April 1827                   |
| Birthplace             | Pune (Poona), Bombay Presidency |
| Full name              | Jyotirao Govindrao Phule        |
| Popular names          | Jyotiba Phule; Mahatma Phule    |
| Wife                   | Savitribai Phule                |
| Social background      | Mali community                  |
| Important organisation | Satyashodhak Samaj (1873)       |
| Died                   | 28 November 1890, Pune          |

- Belonged to the Mali community, traditionally associated with gardening and cultivation.
- Criticised caste hierarchy and Brahmanical social dominance.
- Advocated education, social equality and the rights of oppressed communities.
- Worked closely with Savitribai Phule, a pioneer of women's education in India.
- Received the honorific Mahatma in 1888.

### 2. Contribution to Women's Education

Jyotirao and Savitribai Phule were pioneers of modern girls' education in Maharashtra.

### Girls' School (1848)

| Feature             | Important fact                      |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Established         | 1848                                |
| Place               | Bhide Wada, Pune                    |
| Important founders  | Jyotirao Phule and Savitribai Phule |
| Important associate | Fatima Sheikh                       |
| Objective           | Promote education among girls       |

### Important contributions

- Established a pioneering school for girls at Bhide Wada, Pune, in 1848.
- Savitribai Phule worked as a teacher in the schools.
- Fatima Sheikh supported their educational initiatives and taught alongside Savitribai.
- Established additional schools for girls and children from socially marginalised communities.
- Challenged restrictions on education based on caste and gender.

Prelims Point: Girls' School at Bhide Wada, Pune (1848) — Jyotirao Phule and Savitribai Phule.

Important distinction: The Phules' school at Pune in 1848 was an important pioneering institution for girls' education, but it should not be described without qualification as the first girls' school anywhere in India, since earlier girls' schools existed.

### 3. Satyashodhak Samaj (1873)

The Satyashodhak Samaj, meaning Society of Truth Seekers, was founded by Jyotirao Phule to promote social equality and challenge caste-based discrimination.

| Feature             | Important fact                                         |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Organisation        | Satyashodhak Samaj                                     |
| Established         | 24 September 1873                                      |
| Place               | Pune, Maharashtra                                      |
| Founder             | Jyotirao Phule                                         |
| Meaning             | Society of Truth Seekers                               |
| Principal objective | Social equality and opposition to caste discrimination |

### Important Objectives

- Oppose caste hierarchy and untouchability.
- Challenge Brahmanical dominance in religious and social life.
- Promote equality among people of different castes.
- Encourage education among women and oppressed communities.
- Oppose social practices that perpetuated inequality.
- Promote religious and social practices without compulsory dependence on Brahmin priests.

### Satyashodhak Marriage

- Encouraged marriages conducted without Brahmin priests.
- Opposed expensive rituals and unnecessary marriage expenditure.
- Promoted simpler marriage ceremonies based on social equality.

Prelims Point: Satyashodhak Samaj (1873) — Jyotirao Phule, not Savitribai Phule or B.R. Ambedkar.

### 4. Campaign Against Caste Discrimination

Jyotirao Phule was an important early critic of caste-based inequality.

### Important contributions

- Opposed untouchability and restrictions imposed on oppressed communities.
- Criticised the caste hierarchy and hereditary social privileges.
- Advocated education as a means of social advancement.
- Promoted the dignity and rights of cultivators, labourers and marginalised communities.
- Criticised the exploitation of peasants by moneylenders and dominant social groups.

### Public Access to Water (1868)

- In 1868, Phule opened the water tank at his residence to people from communities subjected to untouchability.
- This challenged caste-based restrictions on access to water.

Prelims Point: Opening of his household water tank to oppressed castes — 1868 — Jyotirao Phule.

### 5. Widow Remarriage and Social Reform

Jyotirao and Savitribai Phule also worked against practices that disadvantaged widows and women.

### Important contributions

- Supported widow remarriage.
- Opposed child marriage.
- Criticised the mistreatment of widows.
- Opposed female infanticide.
- Established a shelter for pregnant widows and women facing social abandonment.

### Gulamgiri (1873)

- One of Jyotirao Phule's most important works.
- Criticised caste oppression and social inequality.

### 7. Views on British Rule and Education

Phule's attitude towards British rule differed from that of many early political nationalists.

- Criticised caste-based inequalities in Indian society.
- Viewed access to modern education and certain colonial legal reforms as opportunities to challenge traditional social hierarchies.
- Also criticised administrative failures and the exploitation of peasants under colonial rule.
- Placed strong emphasis on social reform and education, rather than primarily on constitutional political agitation.

Important distinction: Jyotirao Phule was principally associated with the anti-caste and social reform movement.

# Sir Syed Ahmad Khan (1817–1898)

Sir Syed Ahmad Khan was an Indian Muslim social reformer, educationist, writer and intellectual associated with the Aligarh Movement of the nineteenth century.

He is particularly remembered for promoting modern Western and scientific education among Indian Muslims, establishing the Scientific Society (1864) and founding the Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental College at Aligarh (1875–1877), which later developed into Aligarh Muslim University (1920).

He also wrote Asbab-e-Baghawat-e-Hind (1858–1859), examining the causes of the Revolt of 1857.

## 1. Early Life and Background

| Feature               | Important fact                                          |

| Born                  | 17 October 1817                                         |

| Profession            | Civil servant, educationist, social reformer and writer |
| Important movement    | Aligarh Movement                                        |
| Principal institution | Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental College                       |
| Knighthood            | 1888                                                    |
| Died                  | 27 March 1898, Aligarh                                  |

- Born into a Muslim family associated with the Mughal court.
- Entered the service of the East India Company in 1838.
- Served in judicial and administrative positions.
- Witnessed the Revolt of 1857 while serving at Bijnor.
- Subsequently devoted considerable attention to educational and social reform.

## 2. Aligarh Movement

The Aligarh Movement was a nineteenth-century Muslim educational and social reform movement associated with Sir Syed Ahmad Khan.

### Important objectives

- Promote modern Western and scientific education among Indian Muslims.
- Encourage the study of English and modern subjects.
- Reconcile Islamic religious thought with modern scientific knowledge.
- Promote social and educational reform within Muslim society.
- Improve relations between Indian Muslims and the British government.

- Supported constitutional and gradual reform rather than confrontational politics.

# Periyar E.V. Ramasamy (1879–1973)

Erode Venkata Ramasamy, popularly known as Periyar, was an Indian social reformer, rationalist and political activist associated with the Self-Respect Movement and the development of the Dravidian Movement in Tamil Nadu.

He is particularly remembered for founding the Self-Respect Movement (1925), participating in the Vaikom Satyagraha (1924–1925), leading the Justice Party, and transforming it into the Dravidar Kazhagam (1944).

He advocated the abolition of caste discrimination, women's rights, rationalism and opposition to Brahminical social dominance.

## 1. Early Life and Background

| Feature                | Important fact                                    |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Born                   | 17 September 1879                                 |

| Popular name           | Periyar, meaning respected elder                  |

| Wife                   | Nagammai; later Maniammai                         |
| Principal movement     | Self-Respect Movement                             |
| Important organisation | Dravidar Kazhagam                                 |
| Died                   | 24 December 1973                                  |

- Initially participated in the Indian National Congress.
- Later criticised caste discrimination and the unequal representation of non-Brahmin communities.
- Developed a social reform programme centred on rationalism, equality and self-respect.

## 2. Vaikom Satyagraha (1924–1925)

The Vaikom Satyagraha was an important anti-untouchability movement in the princely state of Travancore, in present-day Kerala.

| Feature                               | Important fact                                                                 |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Period                                | 1924–1925                                                                      |
| Place                                 | Vaikom, Travancore                                                             |

| Periyar's role                        | Participated and led agitation during the movement                             |
| Popular title associated with Periyar | Vaikom Veerar                                                                  |

### Important facts

- The movement challenged caste-based restrictions on access to public roads near the temple.
- Periyar joined the agitation in 1924 and was imprisoned for his participation.
- The movement helped secure access to several previously restricted roads.
- It did not immediately secure unrestricted entry into the temple itself.

Important distinction: The Vaikom Satyagraha concerned access to public roads around a temple, whereas the Travancore Temple Entry Proclamation of 1936 concerned temple entry for communities previously excluded on caste grounds.

Prelims Point: Vaikom Satyagraha (1924–1925) — Travancore — Periyar, T.K. Madhavan and other reformers.

## Swami Vivekananda (1863–1902)

Swami Vivekananda, born Narendranath Datta, was an Indian Hindu monk, philosopher and social reformer associated with the Neo-Vedanta movement and the spread of Vedanta in India and abroad.

He was the foremost disciple of Ramakrishna Paramahamsa and is particularly remembered for his address at the World's Parliament of Religions in Chicago (1893) and the establishment of the Ramakrishna Mission (1897).

He emphasised Practical Vedanta, religious harmony, service to humanity, education and national regeneration.

| Religious philosophy   | Vedanta, especially Advaita Vedanta |
| Important organisation | Ramakrishna Mission                 |
| Died                   | 4 July 1902, Belur Math             |

- Studied at General Assembly's Institution, now Scottish Church College, Calcutta.
- Was initially influenced by Western philosophy and rational inquiry.
- Had an association with the Brahmo Samaj during his early intellectual development.
- Became a disciple of Ramakrishna Paramahamsa, who profoundly influenced his spiritual outlook.
- Travelled extensively throughout India before visiting the United States in 1893.

### 6. Important Philosophical and Social Ideas

### Practical Vedanta

Vivekananda advocated applying Vedantic principles to everyday life and social service.

Important ideas:

- Divinity is inherent in every human being.
- Service to humanity is a form of worship.
- Education should develop character, self-confidence and human potential.
- Spiritual development and social welfare should complement each other.
- Religious harmony is possible despite differences in religious practices.

### Neo-Vedanta

Vivekananda is prominently associated with the modern reinterpretation and popularisation of Advaita Vedanta, often described as Neo-Vedanta.

| Concept             | Important association                                                |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Advaita Vedanta     | Philosophically systematised by Adi Shankaracharya                   |
| Neo-Vedanta         | Modern interpretation associated with Vivekananda and other thinkers |
| Practical Vedanta   | Application of spiritual principles to life and service              |
| Service to humanity | Important teaching of Vivekananda and Ramakrishna Mission            |

Important clarification: Vivekananda did not originate Advaita Vedanta. He reinterpreted and popularised Vedantic ideas in a modern context.

## Sir William Jones (1746–1794)

Sir William Jones was a British judge, linguist, Orientalist and scholar who made important contributions to the study of Indian languages, literature, history and law.

He is best known for founding the Asiatic Society of Bengal in Calcutta on 15 January 1784 and for recognising the historical linguistic relationship between Sanskrit, Greek and Latin.

### 2. Asiatic Society of Bengal (1784)

The Asiatic Society of Bengal was established in Calcutta on 15 January 1784 to promote systematic study of Asian history, languages, literature, philosophy, law and culture.

### 3. Contribution to Comparative Linguistics (1786)

In his Third Anniversary Discourse, delivered on 2 February 1786, William Jones argued that Sanskrit, Greek and Latin shared a common linguistic origin.

### Important facts

- He identified similarities in vocabulary and grammatical structures among Sanskrit, Greek and Latin.
- He also suggested connections with Gothic, Celtic and ancient Persian.
- His observations contributed significantly to the later development of comparative linguistics and the study of the Indo-European language family.
- He was not the first scholar to notice similarities between these languages, but his 1786 address became especially influential.

Prelims Point: William Jones — 1786 — Common origin of Sanskrit, Greek and Latin.

## James Prinsep (1799–1840)

James Prinsep was a British scholar, antiquarian, numismatist and administrator who made important contributions to the study of ancient Indian history, inscriptions and coins.

He is best known for **deciphering the Brahmi script in 1837**, a major breakthrough that **enabled historians to read the inscriptions of Emperor Ashoka** and reconstruct important aspects of ancient Indian history.

### 1. Early Life and Background

| Feature               | Important fact                                      |
| --------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Born                  | 20 August 1799                                      |
| Birthplace            | London, England                                     |
| Nationality           | British                                             |
| Profession            | Scholar, numismatist, antiquarian and administrator |
| Arrived in India      | 1819                                                |
| Important institution | Asiatic Society of Bengal                           |
| Important position    | Secretary of the Asiatic Society of Bengal          |
| Major achievement     | Decipherment of Brahmi script (1837)                |
| Important journal     | Journal of the Asiatic Society of Bengal            |
| Died                  | 22 April 1840                                       |
| Place of death        | London, England                                     |

Prelims classification: James Prinsep was a British scholar of ancient Indian inscriptions and coins, not an Indian nationalist or social reformer.

### 3. Decipherment of Brahmi Script (1837)

This is James Prinsep's most important contribution for UPSC Prelims.

In 1837, Prinsep successfully deciphered the ancient Brahmi script, enabling scholars to read many inscriptions of Emperor Ashoka.

### How did he decipher Brahmi?

- Studied short inscriptions found on the railings of the Sanchi Stupa.
- Identified recurring letters forming the word danam, meaning gift or donation.
- Used these discoveries to identify other Brahmi characters.
- Applied his findings to inscriptions on Ashokan pillars and rocks.
- Published his findings in the Journal of the Asiatic Society of Bengal.

His breakthrough built upon the earlier work of other scholars, including Charles Wilkins.

Prelims Point: James Prinsep — Brahmi script — 1837 — Ashokan inscriptions.

### 4. Identification of Ashoka in Inscriptions

Prinsep discovered that several ancient inscriptions referred to a ruler using the titles:

- **Devanampiya** — Beloved of the Gods.
- **Piyadasi (Priyadarshi)** — One who looks upon others with affection or favour.

However, the inscriptions did not initially provide a clear identification of the ruler.

### Role of George Turnour

- **George Turnour**, a British scholar working in Ceylon (present-day Sri Lanka), studied Buddhist historical chronicles.
- These chronicles helped establish that **Devanampiya Piyadasi** was associated with **Emperor Ashoka**.
- This identification **helped connect Ashokan inscriptions** with the history of the **Mauryan Empire**.


Prelims distinction: **James Prinsep deciphered Brahmi**; **George Turnour's research helped identify Piyadasi with Ashoka**.

### 5. Contribution to Kharosthi Script

Prinsep also made important contributions to the decipherment of the Kharosthi script, used in northwestern parts of the Indian subcontinent.

| Feature                               | Brahmi                                    | Kharosthi                        |
| ------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Principal region                      | Much of ancient India                     | Northwestern Indian subcontinent |
| Direction of writing                  | Left to right                             | Right to left                    |
| Important associated scholar          | James Prinsep                             | James Prinsep and other scholars |
| Important evidence                    | Ashokan inscriptions, Sanchi inscriptions | Bilingual Indo-Greek coins       |
| Period of major decipherment advances | 1837                                      | 1830s                            |

The decipherment of Kharosthi was a collaborative process involving several scholars. Prinsep made major contributions, but it would be inaccurate to credit him alone with its complete decipherment.

# Theosophical Society (1875)

The Theosophical Society was a religious-philosophical organisation founded in New York, USA, on 17 November 1875, principally by Helena Petrovna Blavatsky and Henry Steel Olcott.

It promoted universal brotherhood, comparative study of religions, philosophy and science, and the investigation of unexplained laws of nature and human capabilities.

In India, the Society contributed to the revival of interest in ancient Indian religious and philosophical traditions. Its most prominent leaders included H. S. Olcott and Annie Besant.

Prelims distinction: The Theosophical Society was founded in New York in 1875, not in India. Its international headquarters was subsequently established at Adyar in 1882.

## 2. Objectives of the Theosophical Society

The Society adopted three principal objectives:

1. To form a nucleus of the Universal Brotherhood of Humanity, without distinction of race, creed, sex, caste or colour.
2. To encourage the comparative study of religion, philosophy and science.
3. To investigate the unexplained laws of nature and powers latent in human beings.

### Important characteristics

- Recognised the value of ancient religious and philosophical traditions.
- Encouraged the comparative study of Hinduism, Buddhism and other religions.
- Promoted the concepts of karma, reincarnation and spiritual evolution.
- Rejected discrimination based on caste, religion, race or sex in its ideal of universal brotherhood.
- Did not require members to profess a single religion.

Prelims point: The Theosophical Society was not established exclusively to promote Hinduism. Its stated objectives were universal and comparative, although its Indian activities strongly influenced the revival of interest in Hindu and Buddhist traditions.
