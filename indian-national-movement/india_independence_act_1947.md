# Indian Independence Act, 1947

# PART I — STUDY NOTES

## 1. Background

The **Indian Independence Act, 1947** was enacted by the British Parliament to provide for the independence and partition of British India.

It created two independent Dominions:

1. **India**
2. **Pakistan**

Both Dominions came into existence on **15 August 1947**.

The Act marked the end of British rule in India and provided the legal framework for transferring power to the two Dominions.

### Important developments

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 20 February 1947 | British Prime Minister Clement Attlee announced the decision to transfer power to Indian hands by June 1948 |
| 24 March 1947 | Lord Mountbatten assumed office as the last Viceroy of British India |
| 3 June 1947 | Mountbatten Plan announced |
| 4 July 1947 | Indian Independence Bill introduced in the British House of Commons |
| 18 July 1947 | Indian Independence Act received Royal Assent |
| 15 August 1947 | India and Pakistan became independent Dominions under the Act |
| 26 January 1950 | India became a Republic |

### Important personalities

| Personality | Significance |
|---|---|
| Clement Attlee | British Prime Minister during the transfer of power |
| Lord Mountbatten | Last Viceroy of British India |
| Jawaharlal Nehru | First Prime Minister of independent India |
| Muhammad Ali Jinnah | First Governor-General of Pakistan |
| Lord Mountbatten | First Governor-General of independent India |
| C. Rajagopalachari | Last Governor-General of India (1948–1950) |

**Important distinction:** Lord Mountbatten was the last Viceroy of British India and the first Governor-General of independent India.

---

## 2. Mountbatten Plan, 3 June 1947

The Indian Independence Act, 1947, was based on the **Mountbatten Plan**, also known as the **3 June Plan**.

### Important provisions

1. Partition of British India into two Dominions.
2. Partition of Punjab and Bengal, subject to decisions of their respective legislative assemblies.
3. Referendum in the North-West Frontier Province (NWFP).
4. Referendum in Sylhet district of Assam regarding its separation from Assam and incorporation into East Bengal.
5. Separate Constituent Assemblies for India and Pakistan.
6. Transfer of power earlier than the previously announced June 1948 deadline.

### Important distinction

The **Mountbatten Plan was announced on 3 June 1947**, whereas the **Indian Independence Act received Royal Assent on 18 July 1947**.

---

## 3. Principal Features of the Indian Independence Act, 1947

| Feature | Provision |
|---|---|
| Independence | Two independent Dominions created |
| Appointed day | 15 August 1947 |
| Partition | British India divided into India and Pakistan |
| British sovereignty | Ended over the new Dominions |
| Governor-General | Each Dominion to have a Governor-General |
| Constituent Assemblies | Empowered to frame their respective Constitutions |
| Legislative authority | Dominion legislatures received full law-making powers |
| British Parliament | Its subsequent Acts would not automatically extend to the Dominions |
| Princely states | British paramountcy lapsed |
| Secretary of State for India | Office abolished |
| Government of India Act, 1935 | Continued as an interim constitutional framework, subject to adaptations |
| British monarch | Ceased to use the title Emperor of India |

---

## 4. Creation of Two Independent Dominions

Section 1 of the Act provided for the establishment of two independent Dominions.

### Important facts

- **India and Pakistan were established as independent Dominions on 15 August 1947 under the Act.**
- The Dominions were legally distinct from British India.
- Each Dominion could establish its own constitutional system.

### Dominion Status

Dominion Status meant that the new states were independent but initially retained the British monarch as their constitutional head of state.

The monarch was represented in each Dominion by a Governor-General.

### Important distinction

| 15 August 1947 | 26 January 1950 |
|---|---|
| India became an independent Dominion | India became a Republic |
| British monarch remained the constitutional head of state | President became the constitutional head of state |
| Governor-General represented the monarch | President became the head of state |
| Adapted Government of India Act, 1935 served as the interim constitutional framework | Constitution of India came into force |

**Independence and becoming a Republic were two different constitutional developments.**

---

## 5. Partition of British India

The Act provided for the division of British India between the two Dominions.

### 5.1 Partition of Punjab and Bengal

The existing provinces of Punjab and Bengal were divided.

| Province before partition | Result |
|---|---|
| Punjab | East Punjab and West Punjab |
| Bengal | West Bengal and East Bengal |

East Punjab and West Bengal became part of India.

West Punjab and East Bengal became part of Pakistan.

### 5.2 Boundary Commission

The boundaries were determined through commissions chaired by **Sir Cyril Radcliffe**.

| Feature | Fact |
|---|---|
| Chairman | Sir Cyril Radcliffe |
| Commissions | Punjab Boundary Commission and Bengal Boundary Commission |
| Boundary settlement | Radcliffe Award |
| Award publicly announced | 17 August 1947 |

**Important distinction:** The Radcliffe Award was publicly announced after independence.

---

## 6. Lapse of British Paramountcy over Princely States

One of the most significant provisions of the Act was the termination of British paramountcy over Indian princely states.

### Meaning of Paramountcy

British paramountcy referred to the superior authority exercised by the British Crown over princely states through treaties, agreements and political arrangements.

### Provisions of the Act

From 15 August 1947:

- British paramountcy over princely states lapsed.
- Treaties and agreements between the British Crown and princely states ceased to operate, subject to specified transitional arrangements.
- The British Crown's obligations towards princely states ended.

### Important distinction

**The Indian Independence Act, 1947, did not automatically merge all princely states with India or Pakistan.**

Their accession and integration involved subsequent political and legal processes.

### Important personalities associated with integration

| Personality | Role |
|---|---|
| Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel | Minister of States; led the political process of integration |
| V.P. Menon | Secretary in the States Department; played an important administrative and negotiating role |

---

## 7. Constituent Assemblies and Legislative Powers

The Act recognised the authority of the Constituent Assemblies of the two Dominions.

### 7.1 Constitution-making powers

Each Dominion could frame its own Constitution.

The Constituent Assemblies were empowered to undertake this task.

### 7.2 Legislative powers

The new Dominion legislatures possessed extensive law-making powers.

They could:

- Make laws for their respective Dominions.
- Amend or repeal existing laws.
- Make laws inconsistent with earlier British legislation applicable to India.
- Determine their future constitutional arrangements.

### 7.3 British Parliament's authority

The Act provided that **no Act of the British Parliament passed after 15 August 1947 would extend to either Dominion as part of its law unless extended by the law of that Dominion**.

### Important distinction

The Constituent Assembly of India performed two functions after independence:

1. Framing the Constitution.
2. Exercising legislative functions for the Dominion.

When functioning as the Dominion legislature, it operated in a legislative capacity distinct from its constitution-making work.

---

## 8. Governor-General under the Act

The Act provided for a Governor-General for each Dominion.

### Important provisions

- Each Governor-General was appointed by the British monarch.
- The Governor-General represented the monarch.
- The Governor-General exercised constitutional functions under the interim arrangements.

The Act also permitted one person to serve as Governor-General of both Dominions if such an arrangement were made.

However, separate Governors-General were appointed.

### First Governors-General

| Dominion | First Governor-General |
|---|---|
| India | Lord Mountbatten |
| Pakistan | Muhammad Ali Jinnah |

### Last Governor-General of India

**C. Rajagopalachari** served as Governor-General from 1948 to 1950.

He was:

- The first Indian Governor-General of independent India.
- The last Governor-General of India.

The office ceased when India became a Republic on **26 January 1950**.

---

## 9. Abolition of the Office of Secretary of State for India

The Act abolished the office of the **Secretary of State for India**.

### Background

- The office had been established under the Government of India Act, 1858.
- It was responsible for overseeing Indian administration on behalf of the British government.

After independence, this arrangement was no longer appropriate.

### Important distinction

| Government of India Act, 1858 | Indian Independence Act, 1947 |
|---|---|
| Established the office of Secretary of State for India | Abolished the office |
| Transferred administration from the East India Company to the British Crown | Ended British rule over the new Dominions |

---

## 10. Government of India Act, 1935 as the Interim Constitution

The Indian Independence Act, 1947, did not immediately replace the entire existing constitutional framework.

Instead, the Government of India Act, 1935, continued to operate with necessary adaptations.

### Important facts

- The 1935 Act served as the principal interim constitutional framework.
- Provisions were modified to reflect the independence of the Dominions.
- The Governor-General exercised powers to adapt existing laws.
- India continued under the adapted framework until the Constitution came into force on 26 January 1950.

### Important distinction

**The Indian Independence Act, 1947, did not itself introduce the Constitution of India.**

The Constitution was adopted on **26 November 1949** and came into force on **26 January 1950**.

---

## 11. End of the British Crown's Title of Emperor of India

The Act provided for the removal of the title **Emperor of India** from the British monarch's royal titles.

### Important distinction

This did not mean that the British monarch immediately ceased to be India's constitutional head of state.

The monarch continued as head of state of the independent Dominion of India until 26 January 1950.

---
