# Simon Commission and the Nehru Report (1927–1929)

The Simon Commission (1927) and the Nehru Report (1928) were major developments in India's constitutional struggle.

The Simon Commission was appointed by the British government to review the working of the Government of India Act, 1919. Its exclusion of Indians provoked widespread opposition. Indian political leaders subsequently prepared the Nehru Report, proposing a constitutional framework for self-government.

## 1. Background: Government of India Act, 1919

- The Government of India Act, 1919, introduced dyarchy in the provinces.
- Provincial subjects were divided into:
  - Reserved subjects: Administered by the Governor and his executive councillors.
  - Transferred subjects: Administered by ministers responsible to the provincial legislature, subject to the Governor's powers.
- The Act provided for a statutory commission to review the working of the constitutional system after ten years.
- The British government appointed the Simon Commission in 1927, earlier than the expected review.

### Why was the Simon Commission appointed early?

- The Conservative government in Britain, led by Stanley Baldwin, feared losing power to the Labour Party.
- It wanted to influence the constitutional review before a possible change of government.
- Difficulties in implementing the 1919 reforms had already prompted several inquiries.

### Important commissions

| Commission                        | Subject                                            |
| --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Lee Commission (1924)             | Superior civil services and recruitment            |
| Muddiman Committee (1924)         | Working of dyarchy under the 1919 reforms          |
| Linlithgow Commission (1926–1928) | Indian agriculture                                 |
| Simon Commission (1927)           | Constitutional reforms and working of the 1919 Act |

Prelims distinction: The Muddiman Committee examined the working of dyarchy, whereas the Simon Commission conducted a broader statutory review of constitutional reforms.

## 2. Simon Commission (1927)

### 2.1 Appointment and composition

| Feature                      | Fact                        |
| ---------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Official name                | Indian Statutory Commission |
| Appointed                    | 8 November 1927             |
| British Prime Minister       | Stanley Baldwin             |
| Secretary of State for India | Lord Birkenhead             |
| Viceroy of India             | Lord Irwin                  |
| Chairman                     | Sir John Simon              |
| Total members                | Seven                       |
| Indian members               | None                        |
| Arrival in India             | 3 February 1928, Bombay     |
| Report published             | May 1930                    |

- All seven members were British parliamentarians.
- It was popularly called the All-White Commission.
- The commission was tasked with reviewing constitutional progress and recommending further reforms.

### 2.2 Why was the commission opposed?

1. No Indian was included in a commission examining India's constitutional future.
2. Indian leaders regarded the exclusion as an insult to Indian political aspirations.
3. Nationalists objected to Britain deciding India's readiness for self-government without Indian participation.
4. The commission was seen as inconsistent with the principle of self-determination.

The principal slogan was “**Simon Go Back.**”

## 3. Political Responses to the Simon Commission

| Organisation / group                             | Response                 |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------ |
| Indian National Congress                         | Boycotted                |
| Muslim League faction led by Muhammad Ali Jinnah | Boycotted                |
| Muslim League faction led by Muhammad Shafi      | Cooperated               |
| Hindu Mahasabha                                  | Opposed                  |
| Liberal Federation                               | Opposed                  |
| Justice Party                                    | Cooperated               |
| Unionist Party in Punjab                         | Did not join the boycott |

### Congress decision

- The **Madras Congress Session of December 1927**, presided over by **M.A. Ansari**, decided to **boycott the Simon Commission**.
- The session also adopted a **resolution favouring complete independence, associated with Jawaharlal Nehru**.

### Muslim League divisions

- Jinnah faction: Opposed the commission.
- Muhammad Shafi faction: Favoured cooperation.

Prelims distinction: The Simon Commission was opposed by most major nationalist organisations, but not by every political group.

## 4. Anti-Simon Commission Protests (1928)

### 4.1 Nationwide protests

- The commission arrived in Bombay on 3 February 1928.
- Hartals, black-flag demonstrations and protest meetings were organised.
- Students and youth organisations played an important role.
- The protests strengthened radical nationalist mobilisation.

### 4.2 Lala Lajpat Rai and Lahore protest

| Event                              | Date             |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------------- |
| Anti-Simon demonstration in Lahore | 30 October 1928  |
| Death of Lala Lajpat Rai           | 17 November 1928 |
| Saunders assassination             | 17 December 1928 |

- Lala Lajpat Rai led an anti-Simon demonstration in Lahore.
- Police used lathis against the protesters.
- Rai sustained injuries and subsequently died.
- Revolutionaries including Bhagat Singh, Rajguru and Chandrashekhar Azad participated in the operation that killed police officer J.P. Saunders.
- The intended target had been James A. Scott, whom the revolutionaries held responsible for the lathi charge.

### 4.3 Lucknow protests

- Jawaharlal Nehru and Govind Ballabh Pant were injured during police action against anti-Simon demonstrations in Lucknow.

### 4.4 Political impact

The protests:

- Encouraged student and youth mobilisation.
- Strengthened demands for complete independence.
- Provided opportunities for socialist and radical political organisations to expand.
- Revived mass nationalist political activity.

## 5. Dr B.R. Ambedkar and the Simon Commission

Ambedkar appeared before the Simon Commission in October 1928 and submitted demands concerning the political rights of the Depressed Classes.

### Major demands

1. Universal adult franchise for men and women.
2. Provincial autonomy.
3. Constitutional safeguards for the Depressed Classes.
4. Political representation for the Depressed Classes.
5. Safeguards relating to education and public employment.

### Reserved seats and separate electorates

- Ambedkar argued that the Depressed Classes required distinct political safeguards.
- He favoured reserved seats if universal adult franchise was introduced.
- If universal adult franchise was not introduced, he advocated separate electorates for the Depressed Classes.
- He submitted a memorandum on behalf of the Bahishkrit Hitakarini Sabha.

Important distinction: Ambedkar's position in 1928 on reserved seats and separate electorates was linked to whether universal adult franchise would be granted.

## 6. Recommendations of the Simon Commission (1930)

The commission published its report in May 1930.

### 6.1 Provincial government

- Abolition of provincial dyarchy.
- Establishment of responsible government in provinces.
- Provincial autonomy subject to safeguards.
- Governors to retain discretionary and special powers.
- Expansion of provincial legislative councils.

### 6.2 Central government

- The commission did not recommend immediate responsible parliamentary government at the Centre.
- The Governor-General would retain substantial executive authority.
- The central executive would not be responsible to the legislature in the manner envisaged by Indian nationalists.

### 6.3 Federal structure

- Accepted the idea of an eventual all-India federation.
- Suggested a Consultative Council of Greater India involving representatives of British Indian provinces and princely states.
- Did not propose immediate establishment of a fully responsible federal government.

### 6.4 Franchise and communal representation

- Recommended expansion of the electorate.
- Did not recommend universal adult franchise.
- Favoured continuation of separate communal electorates.

### 6.5 Territorial and administrative recommendations

| Subject     | Recommendation                                                   |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Sind        | Separation from Bombay Presidency                                |
| Burma       | Separation from British India                                    |
| NWFP        | Local legislature and representation at Centre                   |
| Baluchistan | Local legislature and representation at Centre                   |
| Indian Army | Greater Indianisation while retaining British military personnel |

Prelims distinction: The Simon Commission recommended abolition of dyarchy in the provinces but did not recommend full responsible government at the Centre.

## 7. Background to the Nehru Report (1928)

### 7.1 Lord Birkenhead's challenge

- Lord Birkenhead questioned whether Indian political leaders could agree upon a constitutional framework.
- Indian leaders responded by organising discussions among different political organisations.
- The objective was to prepare an Indian constitutional scheme.

### 7.2 All Parties Conference

- The All Parties Conference met in 1928 to consider constitutional reforms.
- A committee under Motilal Nehru was established to prepare a constitutional framework.
- The report was finalised in August 1928.

### 7.3 Nehru Committee

| Position / association | Personality         |
| ---------------------- | ------------------- |
| Chairman               | Motilal Nehru       |
| Secretary              | Jawaharlal Nehru    |
| Prominent member       | Tej Bahadur Sapru   |
| Prominent member       | Subhas Chandra Bose |
| Prominent member       | Ali Imam            |
| Prominent member       | M.S. Aney           |
| Prominent member       | Mangal Singh        |
| Prominent member       | G.R. Pradhan        |
| Prominent member       | Shoaib Qureshi      |

The Nehru Report represented an important Indian effort to prepare a constitutional framework.

Prelims distinction: Motilal Nehru chaired the committee; Jawaharlal Nehru was its secretary.

## 8. Nehru Report (1928): Major Recommendations

### 8.1 Dominion Status

- Recommended Dominion Status for India within the British Commonwealth.
- Proposed a parliamentary system of government.
- The executive would be responsible to the legislature.

Important distinction: The Nehru Report recommended Dominion Status, not immediate complete independence.

### 8.2 Federal structure

- Proposed a federal constitutional structure.
- Residuary powers were to vest in the Centre.
- British Indian provinces would eventually be linked with princely states on a federal basis.

Residuary powers refer to legislative subjects not specifically assigned to any constitutional list or level of government.

### 8.3 Responsible government

- Responsible government at both the Centre and provincial levels.
- Executive councils would be responsible to their respective legislatures.
- The Governor-General would act as constitutional head on the advice of the responsible central executive.

### 8.4 Proposed central legislature

| Feature                              | Recommendation           |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------ |
| Structure                            | Bicameral legislature    |
| Lower House                          | House of Representatives |
| Lower House membership               | 500                      |
| Lower House tenure                   | 5 years                  |
| Upper House                          | Senate                   |
| Upper House membership               | 200                      |
| Upper House tenure                   | 7 years                  |
| Election to House of Representatives | Adult suffrage           |
| Election to Senate                   | By provincial councils   |

### 8.5 Provincial legislatures

- Provincial councils were to have a tenure of five years.
- Provincial Governors would act on the advice of responsible provincial executive councils.

These were proposed arrangements, not provisions actually implemented in 1928.

## 9. Fundamental Rights in the Nehru Report

The Nehru Report proposed 19 fundamental rights.

Important provisions included:

- Equality before the law.
- Freedom of expression.
- Freedom of conscience and religion.
- Freedom of association.
- Right to form unions.
- Free elementary education.
- Equal rights for women.
- Protection against discrimination.
- Universal adult franchise.

### Secular state

- The State would have no official religion.
- Religious freedom would be guaranteed.
- The State would be separated from religion.

### Linguistic provinces

- The report supported the reorganisation of provinces on linguistic lines.

Prelims distinction: The Nehru Report proposed constitutional rights and universal adult franchise decades before the Constitution of independent India came into force.

## 10. Communal Representation in the Nehru Report

### 10.1 Joint electorates

The report:

- Rejected separate electorates.
- Recommended joint electorates.
- Provided for reservation of Muslim seats at the Centre and in provinces where Muslims were a minority.
- Did not recommend Muslim reservation in Punjab and Bengal, where Muslims formed a majority.
- Rejected communal weightage.

### Important terms

| Term                 | Meaning                                                                                        |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Separate electorates | Voters of a specified community elect representatives through a separate electoral arrangement |
| Joint electorates    | Voters participate in a common electorate                                                      |
| Reserved seats       | Seats earmarked for representatives of a particular community                                  |
| Communal weightage   | Representation exceeding a community's population proportion                                   |

Prelims distinction: Joint electorates and reserved seats can coexist.

## 11. Delhi Proposals (1927)

Before the Nehru Report, Muslim political leaders associated with Jinnah attempted to reach an agreement on communal representation.

The Delhi Proposals of 1927 involved acceptance of joint electorates subject to constitutional safeguards.

### Four principal proposals

| No. | Proposal                                                                           |
| --- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Joint electorates instead of separate electorates, with reserved seats for Muslims |
| 2   | One-third Muslim representation in the Central Legislative Assembly                |
| 3   | Muslim representation in Punjab and Bengal proportionate to population             |
| 4   | Separation of Sind from Bombay and constitutional reforms in NWFP and Baluchistan  |

- The proposals sought a constitutional compromise.
- The Madras Congress Session of December 1927 accepted them.

Important distinction: The Delhi Proposals of 1927 accepted joint electorates subject to safeguards. Jinnah's Fourteen Points of 1929 demanded continuation of separate electorates.

## 12. Hindu Mahasabha and Constitutional Disagreements

- Hindu Mahasabha representatives opposed certain demands for Muslim representation.
- They objected to proposals that would strengthen Muslim political majorities in Punjab and Bengal.
- They also opposed the proposed creation of additional Muslim-majority provinces.
- These disagreements complicated efforts to obtain agreement on a common constitutional scheme.

The Nehru Report therefore became controversial over communal representation and the distribution of political powers.

## 13. Jinnah's Proposed Amendments (December 1928)

At the All Parties Convention in Calcutta, Jinnah proposed three important amendments to the Nehru Report.

1. One-third Muslim representation in the Central Legislature.
2. Muslim reservation in Punjab and Bengal according to population, until adult franchise was introduced.
3. Residuary powers to vest in the provinces.

These amendments were not accepted.

## 14. Jinnah's Fourteen Points (1929)

Jinnah formulated his Fourteen Points in March 1929, setting out constitutional demands.

### Major provisions

1. Federal constitution with residuary powers vested in provinces.
2. Full provincial autonomy.
3. No constitutional amendment affecting the federation without concurrence of the constituent units.
4. Adequate Muslim representation in legislatures without reducing Muslim majorities in Muslim-majority provinces.
5. Adequate Muslim representation in public services and local self-government bodies.
6. At least one-third Muslim representation in the Central Legislature.
7. At least one-third Muslim representation in cabinets at the Centre and in provinces.
8. Continuation of separate electorates, with provision for voluntary abandonment.
9. No bill or resolution to proceed if three-fourths of the members of a minority community in the legislature considered it harmful to that community's interests.
10. Territorial changes must not affect Muslim majorities in Punjab, Bengal and NWFP.
11. Separation of Sind from Bombay.
12. Constitutional reforms in NWFP and Baluchistan.
13. Full freedom of religion for all communities.
14. Protection of Muslim religious, cultural, educational and linguistic rights.

### Important numerical facts

| Provision                                    | Figure                                                                |
| -------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Muslim representation in Central Legislature | At least one-third                                                    |
| Muslim representation in cabinets            | At least one-third                                                    |
| Minority objection safeguard                 | Three-fourths of members of the minority community in the legislature |

## 15. Nehru Report vs Jinnah's Fourteen Points

| Issue                           | Nehru Report, 1928                                 | Jinnah's Fourteen Points, 1929                |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Residuary powers                | Centre                                             | Provinces                                     |
| Electorates                     | Joint                                              | Separate, with voluntary abandonment possible |
| Muslim representation at Centre | Reservation proportionate to population            | At least one-third                            |
| Punjab and Bengal               | No Muslim reservation in Muslim-majority provinces | Protection of Muslim majority representation  |
| Cabinet representation          | No one-third Muslim requirement                    | At least one-third Muslims                    |
| Minority objection safeguard    | No corresponding three-fourths provision           | Three-fourths objection safeguard             |
| Provincial autonomy             | Supported                                          | Supported                                     |
| Religious freedom               | Supported                                          | Supported                                     |

## 16. Responses to the Nehru Report

### 16.1 Jawaharlal Nehru and Subhas Chandra Bose

- Both opposed the recommendation of Dominion Status.
- They demanded complete independence.
- They were associated with the **Independence for India League**, established in **1928** (different from **Indian Independence League** set up by **Rash Behari Bose** in **1940** to organize Indians living outside the country and remove British colonial rule). 
- They represented the increasingly assertive younger nationalist leadership.

### 16.2 Calcutta Congress Session, December 1928

| Feature                  | Fact                                                                |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| President                | Motilal Nehru                                                       |
| Main controversy         | Dominion Status versus complete independence                        |
| Compromise               | British government was given one year by INC to accept Dominion Status         |
| Consequence of rejection | Congress would move towards complete independence and mass struggle |

Gandhi supported the compromise.

### 16.3 Lahore Congress Session, December 1929

- President: Jawaharlal Nehru.
- Congress formally adopted Purna Swaraj as its objective.
- Congress decided to observe 26 January 1930 as Independence Day.
- Preparations were authorised for civil disobedience.

### 16.4 Independence Day, 1930

- 26 January 1930 was observed as Independence Day.
- Independence pledges were taken at public gatherings.
- The Civil Disobedience Movement began later in 1930.

## 20. Most Important Prelims Distinctions

1. Simon Commission: Appointed in 1927, arrived in 1928, reported in 1930.
2. Simon Commission: All British members; no Indian representation.
3. Ambedkar: Appeared before the commission and demanded political safeguards for the Depressed Classes.
4. Simon Commission: Recommended abolition of provincial dyarchy, not immediate responsible government at the Centre.
5. Nehru Report: Recommended Dominion Status, not immediate Purna Swaraj.
6. Nehru Report: Residuary powers with Centre; Jinnah's Fourteen Points placed them with provinces.
7. Delhi Proposals (1927): Accepted joint electorates with safeguards; Jinnah's Fourteen Points (1929) supported continuation of separate electorates.
8. Nehru Report: Proposed 19 fundamental rights, universal adult franchise and linguistic provinces.
9. Madras Congress (1927): Simon Commission boycott and independence resolution.
10. Calcutta Congress (1928): One-year ultimatum regarding Dominion Status.
11. Lahore Congress (1929): Formal adoption of Purna Swaraj.
12. 26 January 1930: Observed as Independence Day, not the date India achieved independence.
