# Other Influential Figures

## Satyendranath Tagore (1842–1923)

Satyendranath Tagore was an Indian civil servant, writer, translator and social reformer associated with the Bengal Renaissance.

He is particularly remembered as the first Indian to qualify for the Indian Civil Service (ICS) through the competitive examination, in 1863.

He belonged to the prominent Tagore family of Bengal and was the elder brother of Rabindranath Tagore.

| Died                  | 9 January 1923                                              |

- Belonged to the Tagore family of Jorasanko, Calcutta.
- Studied at Hindu College, later associated with Presidency College, Calcutta.
- Was among the early students of Calcutta University, established in 1857.
- Supported women's education, social reform and greater participation of Indians in public administration.

### 2. First Indian to Qualify for the ICS (1863)

The most important fact about Satyendranath Tagore for UPSC Prelims is his achievement in the Indian Civil Service examination.

| Feature                | Important fact                                                  |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Examination            | Indian Civil Service (ICS)                                      |
| Year of success        | 1863                                                            |
| Personality            | Satyendranath Tagore                                            |
| Historical distinction | First Indian to qualify through the competitive ICS examination |
| Joined service         | 1864                                                            |

### Important background

- The Charter Act of 1853 introduced the principle of open competition for recruitment to the Company's civil services.
- The Macaulay Committee (1854) recommended arrangements for competitive recruitment.
- The first open competitive ICS examination was held in London in 1855.
- Satyendranath Tagore passed the examination in 1863 and joined the service in 1864.

Important distinction: The Charter Act of 1853 introduced open competition, whereas Satyendranath Tagore became the first Indian to qualify in 1863.

### 3. Association with the Brahmo Samaj

Satyendranath Tagore was associated with the Brahmo Samaj and the religious reform movement in Bengal.

- His father, Debendranath Tagore, was an important leader of the Brahmo Samaj.
- Satyendranath supported its ideas of religious and social reform.
- In 1861, he participated with Keshab Chandra Sen in propagating Brahmo ideas at Krishnanagar.
- Advocated social reform, education and greater freedom for women.

### 4. Important ICS-Related Personalities

Important distinction: Satyendranath Tagore was the first Indian ICS officer, not the first officer of the post-independence Indian Administrative Service (IAS).

## Rabindranath Tagore (1861–1941)

Rabindranath Tagore, popularly known as Gurudev, was an Indian poet, writer, philosopher, educationist and nationalist thinker associated with the Bengal Renaissance and the Indian freedom movement.

He is particularly remembered as the **first Asian Nobel laureate (Literature, 1913)**, the **composer of India's national anthem Jana Gana Mana**, the founder of Visva-Bharati (1921), and for **renouncing his British knighthood in 1919 in protest against the Jallianwala Bagh massacre**.

### 1. Early Life and Background

| Feature           | Important fact          |
| ----------------- | ----------------------- |
| Born              | 7 May 1861              |
| Birthplace        | Jorasanko, Calcutta     |
| Father            | Debendranath Tagore     |
| Grandfather       | Dwarkanath Tagore       |
| Elder brother     | Satyendranath Tagore    |
| Wife              | Mrinalini Devi          |
| Popular title     | Gurudev                 |
| Literary language | Primarily Bengali       |
| Nobel Prize       | Literature, 1913        |
| Died              | 7 August 1941, Calcutta |

- Belonged to the prominent Tagore family of Bengal.
- His father, Debendranath Tagore, was an important leader of the Brahmo Samaj.
- Began writing poetry at an early age.
- Used the literary pseudonym Bhanusimha for some of his early poems.
- Made important contributions to literature, music, education and social thought.

### 2. Nobel Prize in Literature (1913)

| Feature                        | Important fact             |
| ------------------------------ | -------------------------- |
| Year                           | 1913                       |
| Category                       | Nobel Prize in Literature  |
| Recipient                      | Rabindranath Tagore        |
| Principal literary association | Gitanjali (Song Offerings) |
| Historical distinction         | First Asian Nobel laureate |

### Important facts

- Tagore received the Nobel Prize for his poetry, particularly associated with the English-language collection Gitanjali: Song Offerings (1912).
- The original Bengali Gitanjali was published in 1910.
- The English collection included translations of poems from Gitanjali and other Bengali works.
- He became the first Asian to receive a Nobel Prize.

Prelims Point: Rabindranath Tagore — Nobel Prize in Literature (1913) — Gitanjali.

### 3. Santiniketan and Visva-Bharati

### Santiniketan

- Debendranath Tagore established a spiritual retreat at Santiniketan in 1863.
- Rabindranath Tagore established an experimental school there in 1901.
- The school emphasised education in a natural environment, creativity and cultural learning.

### 4. Role in the Swadeshi Movement (1905)

Rabindranath Tagore actively participated in the Swadeshi Movement against the Partition of Bengal (1905).

### Important contributions

- Opposed the partition of Bengal introduced by Lord Curzon.
- Promoted unity between Hindus and Muslims.
- Encouraged the Rakhi Bandhan ceremony as a symbol of unity.
- Composed patriotic songs, including Amar Sonar Bangla.
- Emphasised Swadeshi, self-reliance, education and rural reconstruction.
- Later criticised violent nationalism and narrow forms of nationalism.

### Rakhi Bandhan (16 October 1905)

- The Partition of Bengal came into effect on 16 October 1905.
- Tagore encouraged people from different religious communities to tie rakhis as a symbol of solidarity.
- The ceremony represented the unity of Bengal despite its administrative partition.

### 5. National Anthems of India and Bangladesh

Rabindranath Tagore wrote songs that became the national anthems of two countries.

| Country    | National anthem   | Important facts                                                                        |
| ---------- | ----------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| India      | Jana Gana Mana    | Composed in 1911; adopted on 24 January 1950                                           |
| Bangladesh | Amar Sonar Bangla | Composed during the Swadeshi Movement in 1905; adopted as Bangladesh's national anthem |

### Jana Gana Mana

- Written originally in Bengali.
- First sung publicly at the Calcutta Session of the Indian National Congress on 27 December 1911.
- Adopted as India's national anthem by the Constituent Assembly on 24 January 1950.
- The national anthem consists of the first stanza of the original composition.

### Amar Sonar Bangla

- Composed during the agitation against the Partition of Bengal in 1905.
- Later became the national anthem of Bangladesh.


Important distinction: Vande Mataram was written by Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay, not Rabindranath Tagore.

### 6. Renunciation of Knighthood (1919)

| Feature              | Important fact                            |
| -------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Knighthood awarded   | 1915                                      |
| Knighthood renounced | 1919                                      |
| Immediate cause      | Jallianwala Bagh massacre (13 April 1919) |
| Viceroy              | Lord Chelmsford                           |

### Important facts

- Tagore received a knighthood from the British government in 1915.
- Following the Jallianwala Bagh massacre, he renounced the honour in May 1919.
- He communicated his decision in a letter to Viceroy Lord Chelmsford.
- His action was a protest against British repression in Punjab.

Prelims Point: Rabindranath Tagore — Renounced knighthood in 1919 — Jallianwala Bagh massacre.

### 7. Political Ideas and Nationalism

Tagore supported India's freedom from colonial rule but criticised aggressive and exclusionary nationalism.

### Important ideas

- Supported the Swadeshi Movement and Indian self-reliance.
- Opposed colonial repression and racial discrimination.
- Emphasised education, social reform and rural reconstruction.
- Advocated international cooperation and cultural exchange.
- Criticised violent nationalism and excessive political narrowness.

### Association with Mahatma Gandhi

- Tagore and Gandhi shared mutual respect but differed on certain political and educational questions.
- Tagore is widely associated with popularising the title Mahatma for Gandhi.
- Gandhi referred to Tagore as Gurudev.
- Tagore expressed reservations about some aspects of the Non-Cooperation Movement, including the boycott of educational institutions.

Important distinction: Tagore supported Indian freedom but was not an uncritical supporter of every programme of the Indian National Congress.
