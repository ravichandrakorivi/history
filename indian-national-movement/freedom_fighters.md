# Home Rule League Movement Leaders

## Annie Besant (1847–1933)

Annie Besant was a British-born theosophist, educationist, social reformer, journalist and Indian nationalist who played an important role in the **Indian Home Rule Movement (1916)** and the development of nationalist education in India.

She is best known for establishing the **All India Home Rule League** in **September 1916** and becoming the **first woman President of the Indian National Congress (1917)**.

She was also a prominent leader of the **Theosophical Society**, headquartered at **Adyar, Madras**.

Prelims classification: Annie Besant was associated with **Theosophy**, nationalist education, the Home Rule Movement and the Indian National Congress.

### 2. Theosophical Society

The Theosophical Society was founded in **New York** in **1875** by **Helena Petrovna Blavatsky** and **Henry Steel Olcott**, along with other associates.

It promoted the study of comparative religion, philosophy and the idea of universal brotherhood.

| Feature                       | Important fact                   |

### Important facts

- Annie Besant joined the Theosophical Society in 1889, before arriving in India.
- She became its second international President in 1907, succeeding Henry Steel Olcott.
- She promoted the study of Indian religious and philosophical traditions.
- Her activities contributed to renewed interest in India's cultural heritage.

Prelims distinction: Annie Besant did not found the Theosophical Society. She became its President in 1907.

### 5. Home Rule Movement (1916)

This is Annie Besant's most important political contribution for UPSC Prelims.

The Home Rule Movement demanded self-government for India, initially within the British Empire.

Two major Home Rule Leagues were established in 1916.

| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |

### Important facts

- Tilak established his league in April 1916.
- Annie Besant established the All India Home Rule League in September 1916.
- The leagues were separate organisations, although they pursued a common political objective.
- They organised public meetings, lectures, pamphlets and political campaigns.
- Their objective was self-government, not an immediate declaration of complete independence.

Prelims distinction: The Home Rule Leagues of 1916 were different from the Indian Home Rule Society founded by Shyamji Krishna Varma in London in 1905.

### 6. Arrest and Release (1917)

- In June 1917, the Madras Government interned Annie Besant along with B. P. Wadia and George Arundale.
- Her detention provoked widespread nationalist protests.
- The agitation strengthened support for the Home Rule Movement.
- She was released in September 1917.

The **Montagu Declaration of 20 August 1917** announced the **British government's policy of progressively developing self-governing institutions in India, with responsible government as its eventual objective**.

Prelims distinction: The Montagu Declaration (1917) was a British government announcement, not a declaration issued by Annie Besant.

### 8. Relationship with Mahatma Gandhi

Annie Besant initially supported the growing nationalist movement but later disagreed with Gandhi's methods.

- She criticised the Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–1922).
- She preferred constitutional political methods and gradual institutional reform.
- Her political influence declined as mass politics under Gandhi became dominant.

Prelims distinction: Annie Besant was associated with the Home Rule Movement, but she was not a leader of Gandhi's Non-Cooperation Movement.

## Sir S. Subramania Iyer (1842–1924)

Sir Subramania Iyer, also known as Sir Subbier Subramania Iyer, was an Indian **lawyer**, **judge**, nationalist and social reformer associated with the early **Indian National Congress** and the **Home Rule Movement**.

He is particularly remembered for his association with the **Madras High Court**, his support for **Annie Besant's Home Rule Movement (1916)**, and his role in the establishment of the **Home Rule League in Madras**.

He should not be confused with **G. Subramania Iyer (1855–1916)**, the **journalist** who co-founded The **Hindu** and founded **Swadesamitran**.

| Political association | Indian National Congress                       |
| Important movement    | Home Rule Movement                             |
| Important associate   | Annie Besant                                   |

- Became a prominent lawyer in the Madras Presidency.
- Was associated with the early nationalist movement in southern India.
- Supported constitutional reforms and greater Indian participation in government.
- Later became an important supporter of the Home Rule Movement.

### 2. Judicial Career

Sir S. Subramania Iyer was an important Indian judge during British rule.

Prelims Point: S. Subramania Iyer — First Indian Acting Chief Justice of the Madras High Court.

### 3. Association with the Indian National Congress

- Was **associated with the early organisational activities leading to the establishment of the Indian National Congress in 1885**.
- Participated in early Congress activities in the Madras Presidency.
- Supported constitutional reforms and greater Indian representation in administration.
- Served as **Chairman of the Reception Committee** for the 1914 Madras session of the INC.
- Advocated village panchayats and measures to address the difficulties of cultivators.

Important distinction: S. Subramania Iyer was Chairman of the Reception Committee at the 1914 Madras Congress session. The session's President was Bhupendra Nath Bose.

### 4. Association with the Theosophical Society

### Important facts

# Khilafat & Non-Cooperation Movement

## Mohammad Ali Jauhar (1878–1931)

Maulana Mohammad Ali Jauhar was an Indian nationalist, journalist, Islamic scholar and political leader who played a major role in the **Khilafat Movement (1919–1924)** and the **Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–1922)**.

He is best known for his association with the **Ali Brothers**, his newspapers The **Comrade** and **Hamdard**, and his **presidency** of the **Indian National Congress** at its **Kakinada Session** in **1923**.

He was also one of the important founders of **Jamia Millia Islamia (1920)**.

He participated in the historic 1906 convention in Dhaka where the Muslim League was officially established.

He later served as the President of the **Muslim League** in 1918 (and was active in its leadership for years thereafter).

He joined the **Indian National Congress** in 1919 and became its president in 1923, while continuing a dynamic role in Indian nationalist and Khilafat politics.

### 2. The Ali Brothers

The term Ali Brothers refers to:

1. Maulana Mohammad Ali Jauhar
2. Maulana Shaukat Ali

Both played leading roles in the Khilafat Movement and worked with Mahatma Gandhi during the Non-Cooperation Movement.

Their mother, Abadi Bano Begum, popularly known as Bi Amman, was also involved in nationalist mobilisation.

Prelims point: Ali Brothers — Mohammad Ali Jauhar and Shaukat Ali.

Mohammad Ali Jauhar used journalism to express nationalist views and discuss political developments affecting Indian Muslims.

### Important facts

- Hamdard was launched in Delhi in 1913.

- During the First World War, the colonial government imposed restrictions on his publications and political activities.

Prelims distinction: The **Comrade — Mohammad Ali Jauhar (1911)**; **Al-Hilal — Abul Kalam Azad (1912)**.

### 4. Khilafat Movement (1919–1924)

This is Mohammad Ali Jauhar's most important political association for UPSC Prelims.

The Khilafat Movement emerged in India following the First World War, when Indian Muslim leaders opposed the proposed treatment of the Ottoman Caliph and the Ottoman Empire by the victorious Allied powers.

### Important leaders

### Important developments

- 1919: All-India Khilafat mobilisation gained momentum.
- 1920: A Khilafat delegation travelled to England; Dr. M. A. Ansari led the delegation, with Mohammad Ali Jauhar among its members.
- 1920: **Gandhi linked the Khilafat cause with the Non-Cooperation Movement**.
- 1921: Mohammad Ali and Shaukat Ali were imprisoned.
- 1922: **Gandhi withdrew the Non-Cooperation Movement following the Chauri Chaura incident**.
- 1924: The **Turkish Republic abolished the Caliphate, removing the central institution that the Khilafat Movement sought to protect**.

### 5. Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–1922)

- Mohammad Ali Jauhar and Shaukat Ali supported Gandhi's Non-Cooperation programme.
- They encouraged participation in the boycott of colonial institutions and government-controlled education.
- The Ali Brothers were imprisoned in 1921 and released in 1923.
- Mohammad Ali later developed differences with Gandhi and Congress leaders, particularly following the withdrawal of Non-Cooperation in 1922.

## Maulana Shaukat Ali (1873–1938)

Maulana Shaukat Ali was an Indian nationalist and prominent leader of the **Khilafat Movement (1919–1924)** and the **Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–1922)**.

Along with his younger brother, Maulana Mohammad Ali Jauhar, he formed the famous Ali Brothers, who played an important role in mobilising Indian Muslims against British colonial rule.

He was also associated with the **All-India Muslim League** and the **All-India Khilafat Committee**.

### 1. Early Life and Background

| Feature                | Important fact                                                |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Born                   | 10 March 1873                                                 |
| Birthplace             | Rampur, present-day Uttar Pradesh                             |
| Mother                 | Abadi Bano Begum (Bi Amman)                                   |
| Younger brother        | **Maulana Mohammad Ali Jauhar**                                   |
| Education              | Aligarh                                                       |
| Political association  | **Khilafat Movement, Indian National Congress and Muslim League** |
| Important organisation | All-India Khilafat Committee                                  |
| Important movement     | Khilafat–Non-Cooperation Movement                             |
| Died                   | 26 November 1938                                              |
| Place of death         | Delhi                                                         |

### 2. The Ali Brothers

The term Ali Brothers refers to:

1. Maulana Shaukat Ali — Elder brother.
2. Maulana Mohammad Ali Jauhar — Younger brother.

Both were prominent leaders of the Khilafat Movement.

Their mother, Abadi Bano Begum, popularly known as Bi Amman, also participated in nationalist mobilisation.

| Personality         | Important association                                                        |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Shaukat Ali         | Khilafat leadership and mass mobilisation                                    |
| Mohammad Ali Jauhar | Khilafat Movement; The Comrade, Hamdard; INC President (1923)                |
| Bi Amman            | Mother of Ali Brothers; participation in Khilafat and nationalist activities |

Prelims distinction: The Comrade (1911) and Hamdard (1913) were founded by Mohammad Ali Jauhar, not Shaukat Ali.

### 3. Khilafat Movement (1919–1924)

This is Shaukat Ali's most important association for UPSC Prelims.

The Khilafat Movement emerged in India following the First World War (1914–1918).

Indian Muslim leaders opposed the proposed treatment of the Ottoman Empire and its Caliph by the victorious Allied powers.

### Important facts

- Shaukat Ali helped organise public meetings and mobilise support for the Khilafat cause.
- The All-India Khilafat Committee coordinated the agitation.
- Gandhi supported the movement as an opportunity for Hindu–Muslim political cooperation.
- The abolition of the Caliphate by the Turkish Republic in March 1924 removed the central institution the movement sought to defend.

Prelims distinction: The Khilafat Movement concerned the Ottoman Caliphate, not the restoration of Mughal rule in India.

### 4. Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–1922)

Shaukat Ali and Mohammad Ali Jauhar supported Gandhi's programme of Non-Cooperation.

### Important activities

- Encouraged the boycott of British government-controlled institutions.
- Participated in political meetings alongside Gandhi.
- Promoted the Khilafat and Non-Cooperation causes among Indian Muslims.
- Supported the boycott of government schools, courts, honours and other colonial institutions.

The cooperation between Gandhi and the Ali Brothers became an important feature of the Khilafat–Non-Cooperation phase.

Prelims point: Gandhi and the Ali Brothers — Khilafat–Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–1922).

### 5. Arrest and Imprisonment (1921)

- British authorities arrested Shaukat Ali and Mohammad Ali Jauhar in September 1921.
- They were prosecuted for speeches and activities considered seditious by the colonial government.
- Both were sentenced to imprisonment and released in 1923.

The arrests occurred during the Non-Cooperation Movement, which Gandhi subsequently withdrew in February 1922 following the Chauri Chaura incident.

### 6. Relationship with the Indian National Congress

Shaukat Ali cooperated with Congress leaders during the Khilafat and Non-Cooperation movements.

However, his relationship with the Congress changed over time.

- Supported Gandhi's Non-Cooperation programme during 1920–1922.
- Became critical of aspects of Congress politics after the decline of the Khilafat Movement.
- Later became more closely associated with Muslim political organisations and communal representation issues.
- Participated in political debates surrounding constitutional reforms and Hindu–Muslim relations.

Prelims distinction: Mohammad Ali Jauhar was INC President at Kakinada in 1923; Shaukat Ali was not an INC President.

### 7. All-India Muslim League

Shaukat Ali was also associated with the All-India Muslim League.

| Shaukat Ali's association | Muslim political leadership, particularly in later years |

Shaukat Ali's political activities therefore extended beyond the Khilafat Movement.

## Hakim Ajmal Khan (1868–1927)

Hakim Ajmal Khan was an Indian freedom fighter, Unani physician, educationist and nationalist leader associated with the **Khilafat Movement, Non-Cooperation Movement**, **Muslim league** and **Indian National Congress**.

He is particularly remembered for serving as **President of the Indian National Congress at the Ahmedabad Session (1921)**, being a founder and the **first Chancellor of Jamia Millia Islamia (1920)**, and promoting the Unani system of medicine.

He was a founding member of the All-India Muslim League when it was established in Dhaka on December 30, 1906, and seconded the proposal for its name.

He helped broker the historic **1916 Lucknow Pact** between the Muslim League and the Congress. He later moved away from the Muslim League and joined the Indian National Congress around 1917, eventually serving as the Congress President in 1921.

### 1. Early life and political associations

- Born: 11 February 1868, Delhi, British India.
- Popular title: Masih-ul-Mulk (Healer of the Nation).
- Profession: Unani physician, educationist and nationalist leader.
- Belonged to a family of distinguished Unani physicians.
- Associated with the Indian National Congress, Khilafat Movement and nationalist education.
- Worked with Mahatma Gandhi, Maulana Abul Kalam Azad, Muhammad Ali Jauhar and M. A. Ansari.
- Supported Hindu–Muslim unity and the freedom struggle.
- Died: 29 December 1927, Delhi.

### 2. Indian National Congress — Ahmedabad Session (1921)

- Year: 1921.
- Place: Ahmedabad, Gujarat.
- President: Hakim Ajmal Khan.
- Presided over the Congress during the Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–1922).
- The session took place against the background of the Khilafat and Non-Cooperation movements.

Prelims Point: The Ahmedabad Congress Session (1921) was presided over by Hakim Ajmal Khan.

Gandhi presided over the Congress only once, at the Belgaum Session (1924).

### 3. Role in the Khilafat Movement (1919–1924)

- Ajmal Khan was an important leader of the Khilafat Movement.
- Associated with the All India Khilafat Committee.
- Worked with the Ali brothers, Mahatma Gandhi, Maulana Azad and M. A. Ansari.
- Supported the alliance between the Khilafat and Non-Cooperation movements.
- Advocated Hindu–Muslim unity during the national movement.

He also held leadership positions in the All India Muslim League and the All India Khilafat Committee, in addition to the Indian National Congress.

### 4. Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–1922)

- Supported Gandhi's Non-Cooperation Movement.
- Encouraged the boycott of British-controlled educational institutions.
- Promoted Swadeshi, khadi and national education.
- Supported the establishment of educational institutions independent of colonial government control.
- Helped strengthen cooperation between nationalist Muslim leaders and the Congress.

Prelims Point: Ajmal Khan supported both the Khilafat Movement and the Non-Cooperation Movement.

### 5. Jamia Millia Islamia (1920)

- Established: 29 October 1920.
- Original location: Aligarh, United Provinces.
- Foundation stone laid by: Maulana Mahmud Hasan.
- First Chancellor: Hakim Ajmal Khan (1920–1927).
- First Vice-Chancellor: Muhammad Ali Jauhar.
- Later Chancellor: M. A. Ansari (1928–1936).
- Moved to Delhi: 1925.
- Became a Central University: 1988.

Ajmal Khan was one of the principal founders and provided important financial and organisational support during Jamia's early years.

### Important Jamia Millia Islamia personalities

| Personality          | Association                                         |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |

| Zakir Husain         | Vice-Chancellor from 1926; later President of India |

Prelims Point: Hakim Ajmal Khan was the first Chancellor, not the first Vice-Chancellor, of Jamia Millia Islamia.

### 6. Contributions to Unani Medicine

Ajmal Khan was a distinguished practitioner and promoter of the Unani system of medicine.

### Ayurvedic and Unani Tibbia College, Delhi

- Associated with the establishment and development of Ayurvedic and Unani Tibbia College.
- Promoted education and research in traditional systems of medicine.
- Encouraged cooperation between practitioners of Ayurveda and Unani.
- Established the All India Ayurvedic and Unani Tibbi Conference in 1910.
- Supported institutions such as Hindustani Dawakhana, associated with indigenous medicine.

The Delhi government's history of Tibbia College identifies Ajmal Khan as a central figure in its development.

Prelims Point: Ajmal Khan is associated with Unani medicine, Ayurvedic and Unani Tibbia College, and the All India Ayurvedic and Unani Tibbi Conference (1910).

### 7. Journalism

- Associated with the Urdu weekly Akmal-ul-Akhbar, started by his family in 1886.
- His involvement in journalism contributed to his entry into public and political life.

Prelims distinction: Akmal-ul-Akhbar is associated with Hakim Ajmal Khan's family, whereas Al-Hilal (1912) was founded by Maulana Abul Kalam Azad.

## Dr. Mukhtar Ahmad Ansari (1880–1936)

Dr. Mukhtar Ahmad Ansari (M. A. Ansari) was an Indian nationalist, physician and political leader who played an important role in the Khilafat Movement, Non-Cooperation Movement, Indian National Congress, All-India Muslim League and nationalist education.

He is best known for leading the Khilafat delegation to England in 1920, serving as President of the Indian National Congress at its Madras Session in 1927, serving as All-India Muslim League's president in 1918 and 1920, and helping establish Jamia Millia Islamia in 1920.

He was also associated with the All-India Muslim League and efforts to promote Hindu–Muslim political cooperation.

Prelims classification: M. A. Ansari was a nationalist physician and Congress leader associated with the Khilafat–Non-Cooperation Movement, not an armed revolutionary.

### 2. Medical Career and Balkan Wars (1912–1913)

Before becoming a prominent nationalist leader, Ansari established himself as a physician.

- Studied medicine in India and received further medical training in Britain.
- Worked as a surgeon in London.
- Organised and led an Indian medical mission to Turkey during the Balkan Wars (1912–1913).
- The mission provided medical assistance to Ottoman soldiers and civilians.

Prelims point: M. A. Ansari — Indian medical mission to Turkey — Balkan Wars (1912–1913).

### 3. Khilafat Movement (1919–1924)

Ansari was an important leader of the Khilafat Movement, which sought to protect the position of the Ottoman Caliph after the First World War.

### Khilafat delegation to England (1920)

## Maulana Abul Kalam Azad (1888–1958)

Maulana Abul Kalam Azad, popularly known as Maulana Azad, was an Indian nationalist leader, Islamic scholar, journalist, freedom fighter and independent India's **first Education Minister (1947–1958)**.

He is particularly remembered for his role in the **Khilafat and Non-Cooperation movements**, his **opposition to the partition of India**, and serving as **President of the Indian National Congress during the Quit India Movement (1942)**.

He was a prominent advocate of **Hindu–Muslim unity** and composite Indian nationalism.

### 1. Early life and political associations

- Born: 11 November 1888, Mecca (then part of the Ottoman Empire; present-day Saudi Arabia).
- Original name: Abul Kalam Ghulam Muhiyuddin Ahmed.
- Popular name: Maulana Azad.
- Pen name: Azad.
- His family subsequently settled in Calcutta.
- Received education primarily in Islamic theology, Arabic, Persian and other subjects.
- Became associated with nationalist journalism and the Indian freedom struggle.
- Supported Hindu–Muslim unity and opposed the demand for Partition.
- Died: 22 February 1958, New Delhi.

Prelims Point: Maulana Azad was born in Mecca, not in India. His birth anniversary, 11 November, is observed as National Education Day in India.

### 2. Journalism and nationalist publications

| Publication | Year | Language | Important fact                                                  |
| ----------- | ---- | -------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Al-Hilal    | 1912 | Urdu     | Founded by Azad in Calcutta; promoted anti-colonial nationalism |
| Al-Balagh   | 1915 | Urdu     | Started after the British government suppressed Al-Hilal        |

- Al-Hilal criticised British colonial policies and encouraged political awakening.
- British authorities suppressed Al-Hilal in 1914 under the press laws.
- Azad subsequently launched Al-Balagh in 1915.
- British authorities imposed restrictions on Azad, and he was interned at Ranchi from 1916 to 1920.

Prelims Point: Al-Hilal (1912) and Al-Balagh (1915) were both associated with Maulana Abul Kalam Azad.

### 3. Role in the Khilafat and Non-Cooperation Movements (1919–1922)

### Khilafat Movement

- Azad was an important leader of the Khilafat Movement (1919–1924).
- Supported the protection of the Ottoman Caliphate following the First World War.
- Worked with leaders such as **Muhammad Ali Jauhar**, **Shaukat Ali**, **Hakim Ajmal Khan** and **M. A. Ansari**.
- **Supported Gandhi's efforts to bring the Khilafat and Non-Cooperation movements together**.

### Non-Cooperation Movement

- **Actively supported Gandhi's Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–1922)**.
- Encouraged the **boycott of British-controlled educational institutions**.
- Supported the **establishment of national educational institutions**.
- Was imprisoned for his participation in nationalist activities.

Prelims Point: Azad supported both the Khilafat Movement and Non-Cooperation Movement, unlike **Muhammad Ali Jinnah**, who **opposed Gandhi's Non-Cooperation programme**.

### 4. Role in Jamia Millia Islamia (1920)

- Established: 29 October 1920, at Aligarh.
- Azad was associated with the nationalist educational movement that supported Jamia Millia Islamia.
- Other important personalities included Hakim Ajmal Khan, Muhammad Ali Jauhar, M. A. Ansari and Abdul Majeed Khwaja.
- Jamia Millia Islamia was established as an institution independent of British government control.

### Important distinctions

| Personality             | Association                                  |

| Hakim Ajmal Khan        | First Chancellor                             |
| Muhammad Ali Jauhar     | First Vice-Chancellor                        |
| M. A. Ansari            | Co-founder and later Chancellor              |

### 5. President of the Indian National Congress

Maulana Azad presided over the Congress on two important occasions.

| Year | Congress Session      | Important fact                                          |
| ---- | --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1923 | Delhi Special Session | Became one of the youngest Congress Presidents, aged 35 |
| 1940 | Ramgarh Session       | Began his second presidency, which continued until 1946 |

### Delhi Special Session (1923)

- President: Maulana Abul Kalam Azad.
- Place: Delhi.
- Became one of the youngest persons to preside over an INC session.
- The session occurred during the debate over legislative council entry following the withdrawal of the Non-Cooperation Movement.

### Ramgarh Session (1940)

- President: Maulana Abul Kalam Azad.
- Place: Ramgarh, Bihar (present-day Jharkhand).
- Held during the Second World War.
- Azad continued as Congress President until 1946, as regular annual elections were disrupted by wartime conditions and repression.

Prelims Point: **Azad was Congress President during the Quit India Movement (1942)**, although the Congress presidency under which he began this term was the Ramgarh Session (1940).

### 6. Role in the Quit India Movement (1942)

- **8 August 1942**: The **All India Congress Committee** adopted the **Quit India Resolution** at **Bombay**.
- **Congress President**: Maulana Abul Kalam Azad.
- Mahatma Gandhi delivered the famous **Do or Die call**.
- 9 August 1942: Azad was arrested along with other senior Congress leaders.
- Imprisoned at Ahmednagar Fort.
- Released in 1945.

### Important personalities during Quit India

| Personality             | Association                                  |

# Swarajists (Pro-Changers)

## Motilal Nehru (1861–1931)

Motilal Nehru was a prominent Indian nationalist leader, lawyer and member of the Indian National Congress. He is particularly remembered for co-founding the Swaraj Party (1923) with Chittaranjan Das, chairing the Nehru Report Committee (1928) and serving as President of the Indian National Congress in 1919 and 1928.

He was the father of Jawaharlal Nehru.

### 1. Early life and political associations

- Born: 6 May 1861, Agra, British India.
- Became a prominent lawyer at the Allahabad High Court.
- Associated with the Indian National Congress.
- Initially favoured constitutional methods of political reform.
- Later participated in the Non-Cooperation Movement and gave up his legal practice.
- Died: 6 February 1931, Lucknow.

### 2. Congress Presidency

Motilal Nehru served as President of the Indian National Congress twice.

| Year | Session  | Important fact                                              |
| ---- | -------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1919 | Amritsar | Held after the Jallianwala Bagh Massacre                    |
| 1928 | Calcutta | Debated the Nehru Report and the demand for Dominion Status |

### Calcutta Session (1928)

- President: Motilal Nehru.
- The Nehru Report (1928) was considered.
- Congress accepted Dominion Status as an immediate constitutional demand, subject to a time limit.
- The younger nationalist group, including Jawaharlal Nehru and Subhas Chandra Bose, advocated complete independence.
- Congress decided that if Britain did not accept the demand for Dominion Status within one year, it would adopt complete independence as its goal.

Prelims Point: Motilal Nehru presided over the Calcutta Session (1928), whereas Jawaharlal Nehru presided over the Lahore Session (1929).

### 3. Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–1922)

- Supported Mahatma Gandhi's Non-Cooperation Movement.
- Gave up his lucrative legal practice as part of the boycott of British institutions.
- Participated in nationalist mobilisation.
- Was imprisoned during the movement.
- Following its withdrawal in February 1922, supported participation in legislative councils as a new political strategy.

### 4. Swaraj Party (1923)

- Founded: January 1923.
- Founders: Motilal Nehru and Chittaranjan Das (C. R. Das).
- Also known as the Congress-Khilafat Swarajya Party.
- Formed following disagreements within Congress over legislative council entry.
- Objective: Enter colonial legislative councils and obstruct British policies from within.
- Motilal Nehru became a prominent leader of the Swaraj Party in the Central Legislative Assembly.

- Year: 1928.

- Drafted a proposed constitutional framework for India.
- Sought Dominion Status, rather than immediate complete independence.
- Proposed a parliamentary system with responsible government.
- Recommended fundamental rights and a federal structure.
- Favoured joint electorates, with reservation of seats for minorities in specified circumstances.

## Chittaranjan Das (C. R. Das) (1870–1925)

Chittaranjan Das, popularly known as Deshbandhu (Friend of the Nation), was a prominent Indian nationalist leader, lawyer and freedom fighter. He is particularly remembered for defending Aurobindo Ghosh in the Alipore Bomb Case (1908–1909), founding the Swaraj Party (1923) with Motilal Nehru and serving as the political mentor of Subhas Chandra Bose.

### 1. Early life and political associations

- Born: 5 November 1870, Calcutta, Bengal Presidency.
- Popular title: Deshbandhu (Friend of the Nation).
- Studied law in England and became a barrister.
- Became a prominent lawyer in Calcutta.
- Associated with the Indian National Congress and the nationalist movement in Bengal.
- Political disciple: Subhas Chandra Bose.
- Died: 16 June 1925, Darjeeling.

### 2. Alipore Bomb Case (1908–1909)

- The Alipore Bomb Conspiracy Case followed the Muzaffarpur Bomb Incident of 30 April 1908.
- Aurobindo Ghosh and several other revolutionaries were prosecuted.
- C. R. Das defended Aurobindo Ghosh during the trial.
- Aurobindo was acquitted in May 1909.
- The case helped establish Das's reputation as a prominent nationalist lawyer.

Prelims Point: C. R. Das was the defence lawyer of Aurobindo Ghosh in the Alipore Bomb Case, not one of the revolutionaries who carried out the Muzaffarpur bombing.

### 3. Role in the Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–1922)

- Supported Mahatma Gandhi's Non-Cooperation Movement (1920).
- Gave up his lucrative legal practice to participate in the freedom struggle.
- Promoted the boycott of colonial institutions and foreign goods.
- Played an important role in organising the movement in Bengal.
- Was arrested and imprisoned by British authorities in 1921.

### 4. Congress Presidency — Gaya Session (1922)

- Year: 1922.
- Session: Gaya.
- President: Chittaranjan Das.
- Following the withdrawal of the Non-Cooperation Movement in February 1922, differences emerged within Congress over future political strategy.
- Das advocated entering legislative councils to oppose colonial policies from within.
- His proposal was defeated at the Gaya Session.
- Subsequently resigned from the Congress presidency and helped establish the Swaraj Party.

Prelims Point: C. R. Das presided over the Gaya Session (1922), whereas Hakim Ajmal Khan presided over the Ahmedabad Session (1921).

### 5. Formation of the Swaraj Party (1923)

- Founded: January 1923.
- Founders: Chittaranjan Das and Motilal Nehru.
- Also known as the Congress-Khilafat Swarajya Party.
- Established after differences within Congress regarding participation in legislative councils.
- Objective: Enter colonial legislative councils and obstruct British policies from within.
- The party contested elections under the Government of India Act, 1919.
- Swarajists sought to expose the limitations of colonial constitutional reforms through legislative opposition.

### Swarajists vs No-Changers

Important distinction: The Swarajists and No-Changers differed mainly over council entry, not over the ultimate objective of achieving Swaraj.

### 6. Calcutta Corporation and Subhas Chandra Bose

- Following the Calcutta Municipal Act of 1923, Swarajists gained control of the Calcutta Corporation.
- 1924: C. R. Das became the first Mayor of Calcutta under the reorganised municipal system.
- Appointed Subhas Chandra Bose as Chief Executive Officer of the Calcutta Corporation.
- Das became an important political mentor of Bose.

Prelims Point: C. R. Das was the Mayor, while Subhas Chandra Bose was the Chief Executive Officer of the Calcutta Corporation in 1924.

### 7. Bengal Pact (1923)

- C. R. Das proposed the Bengal Pact in 1923.
- Sought political cooperation between Hindus and Muslims in Bengal.
- Proposed arrangements concerning representation in legislative bodies, local institutions and government employment.
- Reflected his efforts to promote Hindu–Muslim political cooperation.
- The Pact faced opposition and was not accepted by the Congress at the national level.

# Revolutionaries

## Captain Mohan Singh (1909–1989)

Captain Mohan Singh was an Indian military officer and freedom fighter who founded and became the first commander of the Indian National Army (INA) in 1942, during the Second World War.

### 1. Early military career

- Born in 1909 in Punjab, British India.
- Served as an officer in the British Indian Army.
- Was deployed in Malaya during the Second World War.
- Following the Japanese invasion of Malaya in December 1941, he came into contact with Japanese military authorities and Indian prisoners of war.

### 2. Formation of the First INA (1942)

- Year: 1942.
- Region: Southeast Asia, particularly Malaya and Singapore.
- Founder and first commander: Captain Mohan Singh.
- Composition: Primarily Indian soldiers of the British Indian Army captured by Japanese forces.
- Objective: Fight British colonial rule and secure India's independence with Japanese military assistance.
- The fall of Singapore to Japan in February 1942 provided a large number of Indian prisoners of war who could be recruited into the INA.

### 3. Role of Rash Behari Bose

- Rash Behari Bose helped organise and coordinate the Indian independence movement in East Asia.
- The Indian Independence League (IIL) provided political support to the INA.
- At the Bangkok Conference (June 1942), Rash Behari Bose emerged as the principal leader of the IIL, while Mohan Singh remained the military commander of the INA.

### 4. Dissolution of the First INA (1942)

- Differences developed between Mohan Singh and Japanese military authorities over the INA's independence and control.
- Mohan Singh opposed Japanese interference in the INA's functioning.
- In December 1942, the first INA effectively collapsed, and Mohan Singh was detained by the Japanese.

### 5. Reorganisation under Subhas Chandra Bose (1943)

- Subhas Chandra Bose arrived in Singapore in July 1943 and assumed leadership of the Indian Independence League and the INA.
- The INA was reorganised under his leadership.
- 21 October 1943: Subhas Chandra Bose proclaimed the Provisional Government of Azad Hind in Singapore.
- The reorganised INA subsequently participated in military operations against British forces, including the Imphal–Kohima campaign (1944).

### 6. Important Personalities Associated with the INA

## Bhagat Singh (1907–1931)

Bhagat Singh was an Indian revolutionary nationalist associated with the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA) and the revolutionary movement of the 1920s and early 1930s.

He is particularly remembered for his association with the Naujawan Bharat Sabha (1926), the killing of British police officer J. P. Saunders (1928), the Central Legislative Assembly Bomb Incident (1929), and the Lahore Conspiracy Case.

He was executed on 23 March 1931, along with Shivaram Rajguru and Sukhdev Thapar.

### 1. Early life and political associations

- Born: 28 September 1907, Banga village, Lyallpur district, Punjab, British India (present-day Pakistan).
- Father: Kishan Singh.
- Mother: Vidyavati.
- Uncle: Ajit Singh, associated with the agrarian agitation in Punjab in 1907.
- Education: National College, Lahore, associated with Lala Lajpat Rai.
- Ideological orientation: Revolutionary nationalism, socialism and opposition to communalism.
- Important organisations: Naujawan Bharat Sabha and HSRA.
- Popular slogan associated with him: Inquilab Zindabad (Long Live the Revolution).
- Executed: 23 March 1931, Lahore Central Jail, aged 23.

Prelims Point: Bhagat Singh was associated with the HSRA, not the Ghadar Party or the Indian National Army.

### 2. Early revolutionary influences

Bhagat Singh grew up in a family associated with anti-colonial activities.

- Ajit Singh: His uncle, associated with the Punjab agrarian agitation of 1907.
- Ghadar Movement: The revolutionary activities of Ghadar leaders, particularly Kartar Singh Sarabha, influenced him.
- Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (1919): Deeply influenced his political outlook.
- Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–1922): Participated in the nationalist atmosphere generated by Gandhi's movement.
- Withdrawal of Non-Cooperation (1922): Contributed to his growing interest in revolutionary methods.

Prelims Point: Kartar Singh Sarabha was an important revolutionary influence on Bhagat Singh.

### 3. Naujawan Bharat Sabha (1926)

- Established: March 1926.
- Place: Lahore.
- Important founder: Bhagat Singh, along with revolutionary associates.
- Objective: Mobilise young people for the struggle against British colonial rule.

### Important features

- Promoted nationalism and anti-imperialism.
- Encouraged secularism and opposition to communal divisions.
- Mobilised students and young workers.
- Promoted political education and revolutionary ideas.
- Worked through public meetings and other forms of political mobilisation.

Prelims Point: Naujawan Bharat Sabha (1926) is associated with Bhagat Singh and Lahore.

### 4. HRA and HSRA

### Hindustan Republican Association (HRA), 1924

- Established: 1924.
- Important founders: Ram Prasad Bismil, Sachindra Nath Sanyal, Jogesh Chandra Chatterjee and associates.
- Objective: Establish a republican government by overthrowing British colonial rule.
- Important event: Kakori Train Action (9 August 1925).

### Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA), 1928

- Reorganisation: September 1928.
- Place: Feroz Shah Kotla, Delhi.
- Important personalities: Chandrashekhar Azad, Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev, Bhagwati Charan Vohra and other revolutionaries.
- The organisation adopted an explicitly socialist orientation.
- Chandrashekhar Azad served as a leading military organiser.

Prelims Point: Bhagat Singh was associated with the 1928 socialist reorganisation of the HRA. He was not an original founder of the HRA in 1924.

### 5. Saunders Killing (1928)

### Background — Simon Commission protests

- Simon Commission appointed: 1927.
- Arrived in India: 3 February 1928.
- Lahore protest: 30 October 1928.
- Protest leader: Lala Lajpat Rai.
- Lajpat Rai was injured during a police lathi charge.
- Died: 17 November 1928.

### Saunders killing

- Date: 17 December 1928.
- Place: Lahore.
- Victim: John P. Saunders, a British police officer.
- Important revolutionaries: Bhagat Singh, Shivaram Rajguru, Chandrashekhar Azad and associates.

Prelims Point: The revolutionary target was James A. Scott, but the officer killed was J. P. Saunders.

### 6. Central Legislative Assembly Bomb Incident (1929)

- Date: 8 April 1929.
- Place: Central Legislative Assembly, Delhi.
- Revolutionaries: Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt.
- Organisation: HSRA.

### Background

The action was intended as a protest against colonial repression, particularly the proposed:

- Public Safety Bill
- Trade Disputes Bill

### Important features

- Two bombs were thrown into the Assembly chamber.
- The bombs were intended as a political demonstration rather than a mass-casualty attack.
- Revolutionary leaflets were distributed.
- The slogan Inquilab Zindabad was raised.
- Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt deliberately remained at the scene and were arrested.

Their subsequent trial was used to publicise revolutionary political ideas.

Prelims Point: The Central Legislative Assembly Bomb Incident (1929) involved Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt, not Rajguru or Sukhdev.

### 7. Hunger Strike in Jail (1929)

Bhagat Singh and other imprisoned revolutionaries protested against discriminatory prison conditions.

### Major demands

- Better food and clothing.
- Access to newspapers and books.
- Improved sanitary conditions.
- Equal treatment of Indian and European prisoners.
- Recognition of their status as political prisoners.

### Jatin Das

- Full name: Jatindra Nath Das.
- Associated with: Revolutionary movement and Lahore Conspiracy Case.
- Died: 13 September 1929.
- Cause: Death following a prolonged hunger strike, commonly recorded as lasting 63 days.

Prelims Point: Jatin Das died during the Lahore Jail hunger strike in 1929. He was not one of the three revolutionaries executed in March 1931.

### 8. Lahore Conspiracy Case

- Concerned the revolutionary conspiracy surrounding the killing of Saunders and related activities.
- Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev and Rajguru were convicted and sentenced to death.

### 9. Political and ideological ideas

Bhagat Singh's revolutionary outlook increasingly emphasised:

- Socialism: Opposition to economic exploitation and class inequality.
- Secularism: Opposition to communal divisions.
- Republicanism: Opposition to colonial rule and hereditary political privilege.
- Atheism and rationalism: Emphasis on reason and critical inquiry.
- Mass political mobilisation: Importance of peasants, workers and organised political education.

His later writings stressed that revolution required wider social and economic transformation, rather than merely replacing British rulers with Indian rulers.

## Chandrashekhar Azad (1906–1931)

Chandrashekhar Azad was an Indian revolutionary nationalist and a prominent leader of the Hindustan Republican Association (HRA) and the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA).

He is particularly remembered for his involvement in the Kakori Train Action (1925), his role in reorganising the HRA into the HSRA (1928), and his association with revolutionaries such as Bhagat Singh, Rajguru and Sukhdev.

He died on 27 February 1931 during an encounter with British police at Alfred Park, Allahabad.

### 1. Early life and political associations

- Born: 23 July 1906, Bhabra village, Alirajpur State (present-day Madhya Pradesh).
- Original name: Chandrashekhar Tiwari.
- Popular name: Chandrashekhar Azad.
- Father: Sitaram Tiwari.
- Mother: Jagrani Devi.
- Early political involvement: Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–1922).
- Major organisations: HRA and HSRA.
- Important associates: Ram Prasad Bismil, Bhagat Singh, Rajguru, Sukhdev and Bhagwati Charan Vohra.
- Died: 27 February 1931, Allahabad (present-day Prayagraj), aged 24.

Prelims Point: Chandrashekhar Azad's original surname was Tiwari. He adopted the name Azad during the Non-Cooperation Movement.

### 2. Origin of the name Azad

- Participated in the Non-Cooperation Movement as a young student.
- Arrested by British authorities.
- According to the widely recounted incident, when questioned by a magistrate, he gave:
  - Name: Azad (Free).
  - Father's name: Swatantrata (Freedom).
  - Residence: Jail.
- The name Azad became permanently associated with him.

Prelims Point: Azad's early nationalist activity began during the Non-Cooperation Movement, before his involvement in revolutionary organisations.

### 3. Hindustan Republican Association (HRA)

- Established: 1924.
- Important founders: Ram Prasad Bismil, Sachindra Nath Sanyal, Jogesh Chandra Chatterjee and associates.
- Objective: Overthrow British colonial rule and establish a republican government.
- Chandrashekhar Azad subsequently became an important member.

### Important activities

- Organised revolutionary networks.
- Participated in operations intended to obtain funds and arms.
- Trained revolutionaries.
- Helped sustain the organisation after the arrests and executions of several senior leaders.

Prelims Point: Azad was an important HRA member and later leader, but the organisation's original founders included Bismil, Sanyal and Jogesh Chandra Chatterjee.

### 4. Kakori Train Action (1925)

- Date: 9 August 1925.
- Place: Near Kakori, close to Lucknow, United Provinces.
- Organisation: HRA.
- Objective: Seize government funds being transported by train to finance revolutionary activities.

### Important revolutionaries

- Ram Prasad Bismil.
- Ashfaqulla Khan.
- Rajendra Nath Lahiri.
- Roshan Singh.
- Chandrashekhar Azad.
- Manmath Nath Gupta and other associates.

### Consequences

- British authorities arrested several HRA revolutionaries.
- Four revolutionaries were executed in December 1927:
  - Ram Prasad Bismil
  - Ashfaqulla Khan
  - Rajendra Nath Lahiri
  - Roshan Singh
- Chandrashekhar Azad escaped arrest and continued underground revolutionary activities.

Prelims Point: Chandrashekhar Azad participated in the Kakori Train Action (1925) but was not among the four revolutionaries executed in 1927.

### 5. Formation of HSRA (1928)

### Background

After the Kakori arrests and executions, Chandrashekhar Azad and younger revolutionaries reorganised the revolutionary movement.

- Year: September 1928.
- Place: Feroz Shah Kotla, Delhi.
- Organisation: Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA).
- Important personalities: Chandrashekhar Azad, Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev and associates.
- Ideological emphasis: Socialism and revolutionary republicanism.

### Azad's role

- Became the leading military organiser of the HSRA.
- Frequently described as its Commander-in-Chief.
- Organised revolutionary training and underground operations.
- Helped coordinate activities across different regions.

| Feature              | HRA                         | HSRA                        |
| -------------------- | --------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Year                 | 1924                        | 1928                        |
| Important leaders    | Bismil, Sanyal, Chatterjee  | Azad, Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev |
| Ideological emphasis | Revolutionary republicanism | Revolutionary socialism     |

Prelims Point: The HSRA emerged through the reorganisation of the HRA in 1928, rather than as an entirely unrelated revolutionary organisation.

### 6. Saunders Killing (1928)

### Background

- Simon Commission: Arrived in India in February 1928.
- Lahore protest: 30 October 1928.
- Leader: Lala Lajpat Rai.
- Lajpat Rai was injured during a police lathi charge.
- Died: 17 November 1928.

### Saunders killing

- Date: 17 December 1928.
- Place: Lahore.
- Victim: British police officer J. P. Saunders.
- Important revolutionaries: Bhagat Singh, Rajguru, Chandrashekhar Azad and associates.

The revolutionaries originally intended to kill James A. Scott, whom they held responsible for the lathi charge. However, Saunders was killed instead.

### Azad's role

- Participated in planning and providing protection for the operation.
- Shot police constable Chanan Singh, who pursued the revolutionaries during their escape.
- Remained underground afterward.

Prelims Point: In the Saunders incident, Rajguru and Bhagat Singh shot Saunders, while Azad provided cover during the escape.

- Place: Central Legislative Assembly, Delhi.
- Organisation: HSRA.

- Was associated with the organisation's planning and underground activities.
- Did not personally throw the bombs in the Assembly.

Prelims Point: The Assembly Bomb Incident was carried out by Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt, not Chandrashekhar Azad.

### 8. Death at Alfred Park (1931)

- Date: 27 February 1931.
- Place: Alfred Park, Allahabad (present-day Prayagraj).
- The park is now known as Chandrashekhar Azad Park.
- Azad was surrounded by British police.
- He engaged in an armed encounter and died during the confrontation.

According to the widely accepted nationalist account, Azad shot himself with his last bullet rather than surrender. However, some historical accounts dispute the precise circumstances of his final moments.

Prelims Point: Chandrashekhar Azad died in 1931 during a police encounter. He was not executed by hanging, unlike Bhagat Singh, Rajguru and Sukhdev.

## Batukeshwar Dutt (1910–1965)

Batukeshwar Dutt was an Indian revolutionary nationalist associated with the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA).

He is particularly remembered for throwing bombs in the Central Legislative Assembly in Delhi on 8 April 1929, alongside Bhagat Singh, to protest against British colonial legislation.

Unlike Bhagat Singh, Rajguru and Sukhdev, Batukeshwar Dutt was not executed in 1931. He survived imprisonment and died in 1965.

### 1. Early life and political associations

- Born: 18 November 1910, Oari village, Bengal Presidency (present-day West Bengal).
- Popular names: B. K. Dutt, Batukeshwar Dutta.
- Revolutionary organisation: Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA).
- Important associate: Bhagat Singh.
- Political ideology: Revolutionary nationalism and socialism.
- Died: 20 July 1965, New Delhi.

Prelims Point: Batukeshwar Dutt is principally associated with the Central Legislative Assembly Bomb Incident (1929).

### 2. Association with HSRA

### Background

- Hindustan Republican Association (HRA): Established in 1924.
- Important founders: Ram Prasad Bismil, Sachindra Nath Sanyal, Jogesh Chandra Chatterjee and associates.
- Reorganised as HSRA: September 1928.
- Place: Feroz Shah Kotla, Delhi.
- Important leaders: Chandrashekhar Azad, Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev and other revolutionaries.

### Dutt's role

- Joined revolutionary nationalist circles.
- Became associated with Bhagat Singh and other HSRA members.
- Participated in revolutionary activities against British colonial rule.
- Was selected to accompany Bhagat Singh in the Assembly Bomb Incident.

Prelims Point: Batukeshwar Dutt was an HSRA revolutionary, not a founder of the original HRA in 1924.

### 3. Central Legislative Assembly Bomb Incident (1929)

- Date: 8 April 1929.
- Place: Central Legislative Assembly, Delhi.
- Revolutionaries: Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt.
- Organisation: HSRA.

### Background

The incident was organised to protest against two controversial legislative measures:

1. Public Safety Bill: Intended to strengthen colonial powers against political activities regarded as threats to public order.
2. Trade Disputes Bill: Sought to restrict certain forms of labour agitation and industrial action.

### What happened?

- Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt threw two bombs into the Assembly chamber.
- The bombs were intended to create a political demonstration rather than cause mass casualties.
- Revolutionary leaflets were distributed.
- They raised slogans, including Inquilab Zindabad.
- Both deliberately remained at the scene and surrendered to the authorities.

### Objective

The revolutionaries intended to attract public attention to colonial repression and use their trial to communicate revolutionary political ideas.

Prelims Point: The Central Legislative Assembly Bomb Incident (8 April 1929) involved Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt, not Rajguru or Sukhdev.

### 4. Arrest, Trial and Imprisonment

- Arrested: 8 April 1929, immediately after the Assembly Bomb Incident.
- Prosecuted in the Assembly Bomb Case.
- Sentence: Transportation for life.
- Sent to prison and subsequently associated with protests over prison conditions.

### Important distinction

| Revolutionary    | Outcome                                                        |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Bhagat Singh     | Executed on 23 March 1931 in the Lahore Conspiracy Case        |
| Batukeshwar Dutt | Sentenced to transportation for life in the Assembly Bomb Case |
| Rajguru          | Executed on 23 March 1931                                      |
| Sukhdev          | Executed on 23 March 1931                                      |

Prelims Point: Although Bhagat Singh and Dutt were arrested together, they did not receive the same ultimate punishment.

### 5. Prison Hunger Strike (1929)

Batukeshwar Dutt participated in protests by imprisoned revolutionaries against discriminatory prison conditions.

### Major demands

- Better food and clothing.
- Improved sanitary conditions.
- Access to newspapers and books.
- Equal treatment of Indian and European prisoners.
- Recognition of their status as political prisoners.

### Jatin Das

- Full name: Jatindra Nath Das.
- Died: 13 September 1929.
- Cause: Death following a prolonged hunger strike, commonly recorded as lasting 63 days.

Prelims Point: Jatin Das died during the Lahore Jail hunger strike in 1929, while Batukeshwar Dutt survived imprisonment.

### 6. Later Life and Political Activities

- Released from imprisonment in 1938.
- Continued to face restrictions and surveillance.
- Participated in activities associated with the Quit India Movement (1942).
- Was imprisoned again during the freedom struggle.
- Lived in independent India after 1947.
- Served as a member of the Bihar Legislative Council during the 1960s.
- Died on 20 July 1965 in New Delhi.

### Cremation

- His remains were cremated at Hussainiwala, Punjab, near the memorial site associated with Bhagat Singh, Rajguru and Sukhdev.

Prelims Point: Batukeshwar Dutt survived until 1965, unlike Bhagat Singh, Rajguru and Sukhdev, who were executed in 1931.

### 7. Important Slogans

### Inquilab Zindabad

- Meaning: Long Live the Revolution.
- Originally coined by Maulana Hasrat Mohani.
- Popularised by Bhagat Singh and other revolutionaries.
- Raised during the Central Legislative Assembly Bomb Incident.

### Other slogans

- Samrajyavad Murdabad: Down with Imperialism.

Prelims Point: Inquilab Zindabad was popularised by Bhagat Singh and his associates, but its origin is associated with Hasrat Mohani.

## Jatin Das (1904–1929)

Jatin Das, also known as Jatindra Nath Das, was an Indian revolutionary nationalist associated with the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA) and the revolutionary movement led by Bhagat Singh and his associates.

He is particularly remembered for dying on 13 September 1929 after a 63-day hunger strike in Lahore Jail, protesting against the discriminatory treatment of Indian political prisoners under British rule.

### 1. Early life and political associations

- Born: 27 October 1904, Calcutta, Bengal Presidency.
- Full name: Jatindra Nath Das.
- Popular name: Jatin Das.
- Revolutionary association: Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA).
- Important associates: Bhagat Singh, Batukeshwar Dutt and other revolutionaries.
- Participated in nationalist activities during the Non-Cooperation Movement.
- Was associated with revolutionary organisations in Bengal.
- Died: 13 September 1929, Lahore Jail.
- Age at death: 24 years.

Prelims Point: Jatin Das is primarily associated with the Lahore Jail Hunger Strike (1929), not the Central Legislative Assembly Bomb Incident.

### 2. Early Revolutionary Activities

- Participated in the Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–1922).
- Became associated with the revolutionary movement in Bengal.
- Had connections with the Anushilan Samiti.
- Acquired knowledge of explosives and bomb-making.
- Later became associated with the revolutionary network of the HSRA.

### Earlier imprisonment

- Arrested in 1925 for revolutionary activities.
- Imprisoned in Mymensingh Jail.
- Undertook a hunger strike against prison conditions.
- Released after a prolonged protest.

Prelims Point: Jatin Das had participated in prison hunger strikes before the famous Lahore hunger strike of 1929.

### 3. Association with HSRA

### Background

- Hindustan Republican Association (HRA): Established in 1924.
- Reorganised as HSRA: September 1928.
- Place: Feroz Shah Kotla, Delhi.

### 4. Lahore Jail Hunger Strike (1929)

- Better quality food and clothing.
- Improved sanitation and hygiene.

Prelims Point: Subhas Chandra Bose was associated with the funeral arrangements of Jatin Das in 1929.

## Bhagwati Charan Vohra (1904–1930)

Bhagwati Charan Vohra was an Indian revolutionary nationalist and an important member of the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA).

He is particularly remembered for his association with Bhagat Singh, Chandrashekhar Azad and Sukhdev, his role in revolutionary writings, and the manifesto The Philosophy of the Bomb (1930).

He died on 28 May 1930, following an accidental bomb explosion near the Ravi River in Lahore.

### 1. Early life and political associations

- Born: 1904, Lahore, Punjab, British India.
- Full name: Bhagwati Charan Vohra.
- Wife: Durga Devi Vohra, popularly known as Durga Bhabhi.
- Revolutionary organisations: Hindustan Republican Association (HRA) and Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA).
- Important associates: Bhagat Singh, Chandrashekhar Azad, Sukhdev and Yashpal.
- Political outlook: Revolutionary nationalism and socialism.
- Died: 28 May 1930, Lahore.

Prelims Point: Bhagwati Charan Vohra was an important HSRA revolutionary and writer, while his wife Durga Devi Vohra (Durga Bhabhi) also participated in revolutionary activities.

### 2. Naujawan Bharat Sabha (1926)

- Established: March 1926.
- Place: Lahore.
- Important personalities: Bhagat Singh, Bhagwati Charan Vohra and other revolutionary associates.
- Objective: Mobilise young Indians against British colonial rule.

### Vohra's contributions

- Helped organise the activities of the Sabha.
- Served as an important organiser and propaganda secretary.
- Helped prepare the Naujawan Bharat Sabha Manifesto (1928), associated with Bhagat Singh and Vohra.
- Advocated the mobilisation of students and young people.
- Promoted socialism, secularism and opposition to communal divisions.

Prelims Point: Bhagwati Charan Vohra was associated with the Naujawan Bharat Sabha and its 1928 manifesto.

### 3. Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA)

### Background

- HRA established: 1924.
- Important founders: Ram Prasad Bismil, Sachindra Nath Sanyal and Jogesh Chandra Chatterjee.
- Reorganised as HSRA: September 1928.
- Place: Feroz Shah Kotla, Delhi.
- Important leaders: Chandrashekhar Azad, Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev and other revolutionaries.

### Vohra's role

- Became an important organiser and ideological writer of the HSRA.
- Served as its propaganda secretary.
- Helped prepare revolutionary manifestos and political literature.
- Participated in underground revolutionary planning.

Prelims Point: Vohra was particularly associated with the ideological and propaganda activities of the HSRA, rather than the original establishment of the HRA in 1924.

### 4. Attempt to Bomb the Viceroy's Train (1929)

- Date: 23 December 1929.
- Location: Near Delhi, on the railway route used by the Viceroy.
- Target: Viceroy Lord Irwin.
- Important revolutionaries: Bhagwati Charan Vohra, Yashpal and associates.
- Organisation: HSRA.

### What happened?

- Revolutionaries attempted to blow up the train carrying Lord Irwin.
- An explosive device detonated as the train passed.
- Lord Irwin survived.

The incident became the subject of a public debate over revolutionary violence and non-violent resistance.

Prelims Point: The attempt to bomb Lord Irwin's train occurred on 23 December 1929 and is associated with Bhagwati Charan Vohra and Yashpal.

### 5. The Philosophy of the Bomb (1930)

- Title: The Philosophy of the Bomb.
- Principal author: Bhagwati Charan Vohra.
- Year: January 1930.
- Organisation: HSRA.
- Prepared in consultation with Chandrashekhar Azad.

### Background

Following the attempted bombing of Lord Irwin's train, Mahatma Gandhi criticised revolutionary violence in an article titled The Cult of the Bomb.

In response, Bhagwati Charan Vohra wrote The Philosophy of the Bomb, explaining and defending the revolutionary methods of the HSRA.

### Important distinctions

| Publication                | Author                | Year |
| -------------------------- | --------------------- | ---- |
| The Cult of the Bomb       | Mahatma Gandhi        | 1930 |
| The Philosophy of the Bomb | Bhagwati Charan Vohra | 1930 |

The Philosophy of the Bomb was circulated in the name of the HSRA. Historical research identifies Vohra as its principal author, writing in consultation with Azad.

Prelims Point: The Philosophy of the Bomb was written by Bhagwati Charan Vohra, not Bhagat Singh or Chandrashekhar Azad.

### 6. Durga Devi Vohra (Durga Bhabhi)

- Full name: Durga Devi Vohra (Durgawati Devi).
- Husband: Bhagwati Charan Vohra.
- Popular name: Durga Bhabhi.
- Revolutionary association: HSRA.

### Important contributions

- Provided shelter and assistance to revolutionaries.
- Helped Bhagat Singh escape from Lahore following the Saunders killing in December 1928.

### 7. Death During Bomb Testing (1930)

- Date: 28 May 1930.
- Place: Banks of the Ravi River, near Lahore.

- Purpose of testing: Preparation for a proposed operation to rescue Bhagat Singh and other imprisoned revolutionaries.

### What happened?

- Vohra was testing an explosive device near the Ravi River.
- The bomb exploded prematurely.

## Ram Prasad Bismil (1897–1927)

Ram Prasad Bismil was an Indian revolutionary nationalist, poet and one of the principal founders of the Hindustan Republican Association (HRA) in 1924.

He is particularly remembered for his role in the Mainpuri Conspiracy Case (1918), the establishment of the HRA, and the Kakori Train Action (9 August 1925).

He was executed by the British authorities on 19 December 1927, following his conviction in the Kakori Conspiracy Case.

### 1. Early life and political associations

- Born: 11 June 1897, Shahjahanpur, United Provinces (present-day Uttar Pradesh).
- Full name: Ram Prasad Bismil.
- Pen names: Bismil, Ram and Agyat.
- Religious and intellectual influence: Arya Samaj and the teachings of Swami Dayananda Saraswati.
- Important organisations: Matrivedi and Hindustan Republican Association (HRA).
- Important associates: Ashfaqulla Khan, Sachindra Nath Sanyal, Jogesh Chandra Chatterjee and Chandrashekhar Azad.
- Executed: 19 December 1927, Gorakhpur Jail.

Prelims Point: Ram Prasad Bismil was associated with the HRA and Kakori Train Action, not the Ghadar Party or Indian National Army.

### 2. Matrivedi and Mainpuri Conspiracy Case (1918)

### Matrivedi

- Organisation: Matrivedi (also written Matri Vedi).
- Important personalities: Ram Prasad Bismil and Pandit Genda Lal Dixit.
- Region: United Provinces, particularly Mainpuri, Shahjahanpur and surrounding districts.
- Objective: Organise young revolutionaries against British colonial rule.

### Mainpuri Conspiracy (1918)

- Bismil participated in revolutionary activities involving the acquisition of funds and organisation of armed resistance.
- He published the pamphlet Deshwasiyon Ke Naam and the poem Mainpuri Ki Pratigya in January 1918.
- British authorities investigated the activities of Matrivedi and associated revolutionaries.
- Bismil evaded arrest and remained underground for a period.

Prelims Point: Mainpuri Conspiracy Case (1918) is associated with Ram Prasad Bismil and Genda Lal Dixit.

### 3. Hindustan Republican Association (HRA), 1924

- Established: 1924.
- Important founders: Ram Prasad Bismil, Sachindra Nath Sanyal, Jogesh Chandra Chatterjee and other revolutionary associates.
- Important organisational meeting: Kanpur, October 1924.
- Objective: Overthrow British colonial rule and establish a republican government in India.

### Important features

- Advocated revolutionary struggle against British rule.
- Sought to establish a federal republic.
- Supported universal adult suffrage.
- Opposed political and economic exploitation.
- Established underground revolutionary networks.

The HRA constitution was developed in 1923–24, and its manifesto was circulated in early 1925.

### HRA manifesto — The Revolutionary (1925)

- Title: The Revolutionary.
- Year: 1925.
- Organisation: HRA.
- Principal author commonly identified: Sachindra Nath Sanyal.
- Bismil was closely associated with the organisation and circulation of its revolutionary programme.

Prelims Point: The HRA manifesto The Revolutionary (1925) is generally attributed to Sachindra Nath Sanyal. Do not confuse it with The Philosophy of the Bomb (1930) by Bhagwati Charan Vohra.

### 4. Kakori Train Action (1925)

- Date: 9 August 1925.
- Place: Near Kakori, close to Lucknow, United Provinces.
- Organisation: HRA.
- Principal organiser: Ram Prasad Bismil.

### Objective

The revolutionaries intended to seize government treasury money being transported by train and use it to finance revolutionary activities.

### What happened?

- HRA members stopped the 8-Down Saharanpur–Lucknow passenger train near Kakori.
- They seized government money from the train.
- A passenger was accidentally killed during the operation.
- British authorities launched a major investigation and arrested several revolutionaries.

### Important participants

- Ram Prasad Bismil
- Ashfaqulla Khan
- Rajendra Nath Lahiri
- Sachindra Nath Bakshi
- Chandrashekhar Azad
- Manmath Nath Gupta
- Other HRA associates

Prelims Point: The Kakori Train Action (9 August 1925) was organised by the HRA, not the HSRA, which emerged in 1928.

### 5. Kakori Conspiracy Case and Executions (1927)

Following the Kakori incident, British authorities prosecuted several HRA revolutionaries.

### Four revolutionaries executed

| Revolutionary        | Date             | Place of execution    |
| -------------------- | ---------------- | --------------------- |
| Rajendra Nath Lahiri | 17 December 1927 | Gonda Jail            |
| Ram Prasad Bismil    | 19 December 1927 | Gorakhpur Jail        |

## Jogesh Chandra Chatterjee (1895–1969)

Jogesh Chandra Chatterjee, also known as Jogesh Chandra Chattopadhyay, was an Indian revolutionary nationalist and one of the principal founders of the Hindustan Republican Association (HRA) in 1924.

He is particularly remembered for his association with the Anushilan Samiti, his role in establishing the HRA alongside Sachindra Nath Sanyal and Ram Prasad Bismil, and his imprisonment in connection with the Kakori Conspiracy Case (1925).

### 1. Early life and political associations

- Born: 1895, Dacca district, Bengal Presidency (present-day Bangladesh).
- Other name: Jogesh Chandra Chattopadhyay.
- Revolutionary associations: Anushilan Samiti and Hindustan Republican Association (HRA).
- Important associates: Sachindra Nath Sanyal, Ram Prasad Bismil and other revolutionary nationalists.
- Worked to establish revolutionary networks beyond Bengal, particularly in the United Provinces.
- Died: 1969.

Prelims Point: Jogesh Chandra Chatterjee was a founder of the HRA, whereas Bhagat Singh and Sukhdev became prominent in its later socialist reorganisation as the HSRA.

### 2. Association with Anushilan Samiti

- Jogesh Chandra Chatterjee became involved in the Anushilan Samiti, a revolutionary organisation in Bengal.
- The organisation promoted physical training, nationalist activities and revolutionary resistance to British rule.
- He developed contacts with revolutionaries in Bengal and northern India.
- His organisational experience helped connect Bengal's revolutionary networks with the emerging HRA.

- Supported the development of an all-India revolutionary network.

## Subhas Chandra Bose (1897–1945)

Subhas Chandra Bose, popularly known as Netaji, was a prominent Indian nationalist leader, twice elected President of the Indian National Congress, founder of the Forward Bloc (1939) and leader of the Indian National Army (INA) during the Second World War.

He established the Provisional Government of Azad Hind on 21 October 1943 in Singapore.

### 1. Early life and political associations

- Born: 23 January 1897, Cuttack, Odisha (then Orissa Division, Bengal Presidency).
- Popular title: Netaji.
- Studied at Presidency College and Scottish Church College, Calcutta.
- Travelled to England and passed the Indian Civil Service (ICS) examination in 1920.
- Resigned from the ICS in 1921 before entering service, to participate in the freedom struggle.
- Political mentor: Chittaranjan Das (C. R. Das).
- Associated with the Indian National Congress and the nationalist movement in Bengal.
- Served as Chief Executive Officer of the Calcutta Corporation in 1924, under Mayor C. R. Das.

### 2. Role in the Indian National Congress

- Emerged as a prominent Congress leader advocating complete independence.
- Supported stronger mass mobilisation against British rule.
- Became President of the All India Youth Congress in 1928.
- Opposed the acceptance of Dominion Status as India's ultimate political objective.
- Favoured industrialisation and economic planning.

### Congress presidency

| Year | Session  | Important fact                                                |
| ---- | -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1938 | Haripura | Elected Congress President                                    |
| 1939 | Tripuri  | Re-elected Congress President, defeating Pattabhi Sitaramayya |

### Haripura Session (1938)

- Presided over the Haripura Session of the INC in Gujarat.
- Supported economic planning and industrial development.
- Established the National Planning Committee in 1938, with Jawaharlal Nehru as Chairman.

### Tripuri Session (1939)

- Bose defeated Pattabhi Sitaramayya in the Congress presidential election.
- Developed serious differences with the Gandhi-aligned leadership over political strategy and the composition of the Congress Working Committee.
- Resigned from the Congress presidency in April 1939.
- Rajendra Prasad succeeded him as Congress President.

Prelims Point: Bose was elected Congress President in 1938 and 1939, but resigned after the Tripuri Session because of internal disagreements.

### 3. Formation of the Forward Bloc (1939)

- Founded: 3 May 1939.
- Founder: Subhas Chandra Bose.
- Initially formed as a political grouping within the Indian National Congress.
- Sought to unite left-wing and radical nationalist elements.
- Advocated immediate independence and a more confrontational struggle against British colonial rule.
- Subsequently developed into a separate political organisation.


Prelims Point: The Forward Bloc was founded by Subhas Chandra Bose in 1939, not 1942 or 1943.

### 4. Escape from India and activities abroad (1941–1943)

- January 1941: Escaped from house arrest in Calcutta.
- Travelled through Afghanistan and the Soviet Union to Germany.
- Reached Germany in 1941 and sought assistance against British rule.
- Associated with Azad Hind Radio, which broadcast messages to Indians.
- Helped organise the Indian Legion (Free India Legion) from Indian prisoners of war in Europe.
- 1943: Travelled from Europe to East Asia using German and Japanese submarines.
- Reached Singapore in July 1943.

### 5. Indian National Army (INA)

### Formation of the first INA (1942)

- The first INA was organised in 1942 under Captain Mohan Singh.
- Consisted mainly of Indian soldiers captured by Japanese forces in Southeast Asia.
- Received political support from the Indian Independence League, led by Rash Behari Bose.
- The first INA collapsed in December 1942 following disagreements between Mohan Singh and Japanese authorities.

### Reorganisation under Subhas Chandra Bose (1943)

- 4 July 1943: Rash Behari Bose handed over leadership of the Indian Independence League to Subhas Chandra Bose in Singapore.
- 5 July 1943: Bose publicly announced the reorganised INA and gave the call Delhi Chalo.
- Became the Supreme Commander of the INA.
- Organised the INA to fight alongside Japanese forces against British rule.

### Important INA personalities

Prelims Point: Subhas Chandra Bose did not originally found the INA. Captain Mohan Singh organised the first INA in 1942.

### 6. Provisional Government of Azad Hind (1943)

- Established: 21 October 1943.
- Place: Singapore.
- Founder and Head of Government: Subhas Chandra Bose.
- Also known as the Provisional Government of Free India.
- Established with support from Japan and recognised by several Axis-aligned governments.
- Bose served as Head of State, Prime Minister and Minister for War and Foreign Affairs.
- The INA functioned as its military force.

### Andaman and Nicobar Islands (1943)

- The islands were under Japanese occupation from 1942.
- Japan announced their transfer to the Azad Hind Government in November 1943.
- Bose renamed the islands:
  - Andaman — Shaheed
  - Nicobar — Swaraj
- 30 December 1943: Bose hoisted the Indian national flag at Port Blair.
- Despite the symbolic transfer, Japanese authorities retained substantial military and administrative control.

### 7. INA military campaigns (1944–1945)

- The INA fought alongside Japanese forces in Burma and northeastern India.
- Participated in the Imphal–Kohima campaign (1944).
- INA forces entered Indian territory during the campaign.
- Moirang, Manipur: INA officer Shaukat Ali Malik hoisted the Azad Hind flag in April 1944.
- The campaign ultimately failed as Japanese and INA forces suffered military setbacks.
- The INA collapsed following Japan's defeat in 1945.

### 9. Death (1945)

- Bose is generally reported to have died on 18 August 1945, following an aircraft crash at Taihoku (now Taipei), Taiwan.
- The circumstances of his reported death have been disputed, and different inquiries have reached differing conclusions.
- The Government of India's Ministry of Culture acknowledges the continuing controversy.

## Madan Lal Dhingra (1883–1909)

Madan Lal Dhingra was an Indian revolutionary nationalist associated with India House in London and the revolutionary network of V.D. Savarkar.

He is particularly remembered for the assassination of Sir William Hutt Curzon Wyllie on 1 July 1909 in London.

He was executed by the British authorities on 17 August 1909 at Pentonville Prison, London.

### 1. Early Life and Political Associations

- Born: 18 September 1883, Amritsar, Punjab, British India.
- Full name: Madan Lal Dhingra.
- Revolutionary association: India House, London.
- Important associates: V.D. Savarkar and other India House revolutionaries.
- Education: Studied mechanical engineering at University College London.
- Executed: 17 August 1909, Pentonville Prison, London.

Prelims Point: Madan Lal Dhingra was associated with revolutionary activities in London, rather than the Bengal-based Anushilan Samiti or Jugantar group.

### Who was Curzon Wyllie?

- A British Army officer and political official.
- Served as a political aide-de-camp to the Secretary of State for India.
- Had connections with British official circles dealing with Indian affairs.

### What happened?

- On 1 July 1909, Dhingra attended a gathering at the Imperial Institute in London.
- He shot and killed Sir William Hutt Curzon Wyllie.
- Dr Cawas Lalcaca, a Parsi physician who intervened, was also fatally shot.
- Dhingra was arrested at the scene.

- Method: Hanging.

## Udham Singh (1899–1940)

Udham Singh was an Indian revolutionary nationalist associated with the anti-colonial movement in Punjab and revolutionary activities abroad.

He is particularly remembered for the assassination of Sir Michael O'Dwyer in London on 13 March 1940, which he carried out in retaliation for the Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (13 April 1919).

He was executed by the British authorities on 31 July 1940 at Pentonville Prison, London.

### 1. Early Life and Political Associations

- Born: 26 December 1899, Sunam, Punjab (present-day Sangrur district).
- Birth name: Sher Singh.
- Popular name: Shaheed Udham Singh.
- Associated revolutionary movement: Ghadar Movement.
- Important influence: Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (1919).
- Assumed name: Ram Mohammad Singh Azad.
- Executed: 31 July 1940, Pentonville Prison, London.

Prelims Point: Udham Singh is associated with the assassination of Michael O'Dwyer (1940), not the assassination of Curzon Wyllie (1909).

### 2. Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (1919)

### Background

- Date: 13 April 1919.
- Place: Jallianwala Bagh, Amritsar, Punjab.
- Occasion: Baisakhi.
- Background: Protests against the Rowlatt Act and the arrest of nationalist leaders.
- Important arrested leaders: Dr Saifuddin Kitchlew and Dr Satyapal.

### Important British Officials

| Official                                     | Position / Role                                             |
| -------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Brigadier-General Reginald Edward Harry Dyer | Ordered troops to fire on the gathering at Jallianwala Bagh |
| Sir Michael O'Dwyer                          | Lieutenant-Governor of Punjab during the massacre           |

### What happened?

- A large gathering assembled at Jallianwala Bagh on 13 April 1919.
- General Dyer ordered troops to open fire on the crowd.
- Hundreds of people were killed.
- The massacre generated widespread opposition to British colonial rule.

### Udham Singh's association

- Udham Singh was deeply influenced by the massacre.
- Accounts of his life associate him with Amritsar and the events surrounding Jallianwala Bagh.
- He later targeted Michael O'Dwyer, whom he held responsible for supporting the repressive policies in Punjab.

Prelims Point: General Dyer ordered the firing, whereas Michael O'Dwyer was the Lieutenant-Governor of Punjab.

### 3. Association with the Ghadar Movement

Udham Singh became associated with revolutionary nationalist circles abroad, including networks connected with the Ghadar Movement.

- Was associated with Ghadar-linked revolutionary networks.
- Participated in activities directed against British colonial rule.
- Returned to India and was arrested for possessing prohibited arms and revolutionary literature.

Prelims Point: Udham Singh was associated with Ghadar-linked revolutionary activities, but he was not a founder of the Ghadar Party in 1913.

### 4. Arrest and Imprisonment (1927)

- Year: 1927.
- Place: Amritsar.
- Arrested by British authorities for illegal possession of firearms and prohibited revolutionary literature.
- Sentenced to five years' imprisonment.
- Released in 1931.

Following his release, he remained under police surveillance and later travelled abroad.

Prelims Point: Udham Singh was imprisoned in India before the assassination of Michael O'Dwyer in London.

### What happened?

- On 13 March 1940, Udham Singh attended a meeting at Caxton Hall in London.
- Michael O'Dwyer was among the participants.
- Udham Singh shot and killed Michael O'Dwyer.
- Other individuals were also injured.
- Udham Singh was arrested at the scene.

### Why was Michael O'Dwyer targeted?

Udham Singh regarded Michael O'Dwyer as responsible for the repressive colonial administration in Punjab during the Jallianwala Bagh Massacre.

Important distinction: Michael O'Dwyer was not the officer who personally ordered the firing at Jallianwala Bagh. That order was given by General Reginald Dyer.

### 6. Trial and Execution

- Trial: Central Criminal Court (Old Bailey), London.
- Year: 1940.
- Sentence: Death.
- Execution: 31 July 1940.
- Place: Pentonville Prison, London.
- Method: Hanging.

Udham Singh was buried in Britain following his execution. His remains were subsequently repatriated to India in 1974.

Prelims Point: Udham Singh was executed at Pentonville Prison, the same prison where Madan Lal Dhingra had been executed in 1909.

## Vallabhbhai Patel (1875–1950)

Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel, popularly known as the Iron Man of India, was a prominent nationalist leader, freedom fighter and one of the principal architects of India's political integration after independence.

He is particularly remembered for leading the Kheda Satyagraha (1918) and Bardoli Satyagraha (1928), serving as President of the Karachi Session of the Indian National Congress (1931), and integrating princely states into the Indian Union after independence.

He served as independent India's first Deputy Prime Minister and first Home Minister (1947–1950).

### 1. Early life and political associations

- Born: 31 October 1875, Nadiad, Gujarat, Bombay Presidency.
- Profession: Lawyer.
- Studied law in England and became a barrister.
- Influenced by Mahatma Gandhi and participated in the Indian national movement.
- Associated with the Gujarat Sabha, an important political organisation in Gujarat.
- Elder brother: Vithalbhai Patel, who became President of the Central Legislative Assembly in 1925.
- Died: 15 December 1950, Bombay.

### 2. Kheda Satyagraha (1918)

- Year: 1918.
- Place: Kheda district, Gujarat.
- Important leaders: Mahatma Gandhi, Vallabhbhai Patel and other local activists.
- Cause: Crop failure and economic distress made it difficult for peasants to pay land revenue.
- Peasants demanded suspension of revenue collection under applicable remission rules.
- Patel played an important organisational role in mobilising peasants.
- The agitation resulted in concessions concerning revenue collection.

Prelims Point: The Kheda Satyagraha (1918) was associated with both Mahatma Gandhi and Vallabhbhai Patel, whereas the Bardoli Satyagraha (1928) was led principally by Patel.

### 3. Bardoli Satyagraha (1928)

- Year: 1928.
- Place: Bardoli taluka, Surat district, Gujarat.
- Leader: Vallabhbhai Patel.
- Cause: The Bombay Presidency government increased land revenue assessment by approximately 22%.
- Peasants organised a non-violent campaign refusing to pay the increased assessment.
- Patel coordinated the movement and maintained discipline among the participants.
- Following negotiations and an inquiry, the revenue increase was substantially reduced.

### Origin of the title Sardar

- Following the Bardoli Satyagraha, Patel became widely known as Sardar.
- The title is associated with the recognition given to his leadership by the women of Bardoli.

Prelims Point: The title Sardar is particularly associated with the Bardoli Satyagraha of 1928.

### 4. Role in the Indian National Congress

- Participated in the Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–1922).
- Played an important organisational role in the Civil Disobedience Movement (1930–1934).
- Supported Gandhi's leadership and the use of non-violent mass mobilisation.
- Served as Congress President at the Karachi Session (1931).
- Participated in the Quit India Movement (1942) and was imprisoned.

### Karachi Congress Session (1931)

- Year: 1931.
- President: Vallabhbhai Patel.
- Held shortly after the Gandhi–Irwin Pact (5 March 1931).
- Congress endorsed the Gandhi–Irwin Pact.
- Adopted important resolutions on:
  - Fundamental Rights
  - National Economic Programme
- The resolutions addressed civil liberties, equality, labour rights and economic justice.

Prelims Point: The Karachi Session (1931) is associated with Vallabhbhai Patel and the resolutions on Fundamental Rights and the National Economic Programme.

### 5. Integration of Princely States (1947–1950)

- After independence, Patel served as Deputy Prime Minister and Minister of Home Affairs.
- Also headed the States Department, responsible for relations with princely states.
- Worked closely with V. P. Menon, Secretary of the States Department.
- Played a central role in integrating more than 560 princely states into the Indian Union.
- Most rulers signed Instruments of Accession, initially covering Defence, External Affairs and Communications.
- Further agreements and administrative measures brought the states into a more unified political structure.

### Important princely states

Important distinction: Jammu and Kashmir's accession was signed by Maharaja Hari Singh, while Patel played a central role in the wider integration of princely states. The integration process involved several leaders and officials, including Jawaharlal Nehru, V. P. Menon and Lord Mountbatten.

Prelims Point: Patel chaired the main Advisory Committee, not the Fundamental Rights Sub-Committee, which was chaired by J. B. Kripalani.

### 7. Contribution to All India Services

- Strongly supported the continuation of an independent, professional civil service after British rule.
- Advocated the establishment of the All India Administrative Service and All India Police Service, later known as the IAS and IPS.
- Emphasised the importance of administrative continuity, impartiality and security of tenure for civil servants.
- Is often called the Father of All India Services.

Prelims Point: The constitutional framework for All India Services is contained in Article 312, which empowers Parliament to create such services following a Rajya Sabha resolution supported by not less than two-thirds of members present and voting.

## Rajendra Prasad (1884–1963)

Dr. Rajendra Prasad, popularly known as Desh Ratna, was a prominent Indian nationalist leader, freedom fighter and the first President of independent India (1950–1962).

He is particularly remembered for his participation in the Champaran Satyagraha (1917), his role in the Indian National Congress, and serving as President of the Constituent Assembly (1946–1950).

### 1. Early life and political associations

- Born: 3 December 1884, Ziradei village, Saran district, Bihar (present-day Siwan district).
- Popular title: Desh Ratna.
- Profession: Lawyer.
- Studied at Presidency College, Calcutta.
- Practised law at the Calcutta High Court and later at the Patna High Court.
- Associated with the Indian National Congress.
- Influenced by Mahatma Gandhi and participated in the national movement.
- Died: 28 February 1963, Patna.

### 2. Champaran Satyagraha (1917)

- Year: 1917.
- Place: Champaran, Bihar.
- Principal leader: Mahatma Gandhi.
- Cause: Exploitation of indigo cultivators under the Tinkathia System.
- Rajendra Prasad assisted Gandhi in organising the movement and investigating peasants' grievances.
- Other important associates included Brajkishore Prasad, J. B. Kripalani and Mazharul Haque.
- The movement led to reforms in the indigo cultivation system.

Prelims Point: The Champaran Satyagraha (1917) was Gandhi's first major satyagraha in India. Rajendra Prasad was an important associate, not its principal leader.

### 3. Role in the Indian National Congress

Rajendra Prasad served as Congress President on three occasions.

| Year | Session / Circumstance                  | Important fact                                          |
| ---- | --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1934 | Bombay Session                          | Elected Congress President                              |
| 1939 | After Tripuri Session                   | Succeeded Subhas Chandra Bose following his resignation |
| 1947 | Following J. B. Kripalani's resignation | Became Congress President again                         |

### Tripuri Congress Crisis (1939)

- Subhas Chandra Bose defeated Pattabhi Sitaramayya in the Congress presidential election.
- Bose subsequently resigned in April 1939 because of disagreements within the Congress leadership.
- Rajendra Prasad succeeded Bose as Congress President.
- Bose subsequently formed the Forward Bloc in May 1939.

Prelims Point: Rajendra Prasad did not defeat Bose in the 1939 presidential election. He succeeded Bose after Bose resigned.

### 4. Role in the National Movement

- Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–1922): Gave up his legal practice and supported Gandhi's boycott programme.
- Bihar Vidyapeeth (1921): Associated with establishing this institution to promote national education.
- Civil Disobedience Movement (1930): Participated in the movement and was imprisoned.
- Bihar Earthquake (1934): Organised relief activities following the devastating earthquake.
- Quit India Movement (1942): Arrested and imprisoned; released in 1945.

### 5. Interim Government (1946)

- Joined the Interim Government headed by Jawaharlal Nehru in 1946.
- Held the portfolio of Food and Agriculture.
- Promoted agricultural production through the Grow More Food campaign.

Prelims Point: Rajendra Prasad was the Minister of Food and Agriculture in the Interim Government (1946), before becoming India's first President.

### 6. President of the Constituent Assembly (1946–1950)

- 9 December 1946: First meeting of the Constituent Assembly.
- Temporary President: Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha.
- 11 December 1946: Rajendra Prasad was elected permanent President of the Constituent Assembly.
- Presided over the Assembly during the drafting and adoption of the Constitution.
- 26 November 1949: Constitution adopted.
- 24 January 1950: Rajendra Prasad was elected India's first President by the Constituent Assembly.
- 26 January 1950: Constitution came fully into effect, and Rajendra Prasad assumed office as President of India.

### 7. First President of India (1950–1962)

- Term: 26 January 1950 to 13 May 1962.
- First election: 1950, by the Constituent Assembly.
- Re-elected: 1952 and 1957 under the Constitution.
- Served as President for approximately 12 years and 3 months.
- Remains the only President of India to have served two full elected terms.
- Successor: Dr. Sarvepalli Radhakrishnan.

Important distinction: The 1950 election was conducted by the Constituent Assembly under transitional arrangements. The 1952 and 1957 presidential elections were conducted under the constitutional electoral-college system.

## C. Rajagopalachari (1878–1972)

Chakravarti Rajagopalachari, popularly known as Rajaji and C. R., was a prominent Indian nationalist leader, freedom fighter, statesman and close associate of Mahatma Gandhi.

He is particularly remembered for leading the Vedaranyam Salt Satyagraha (1930), proposing the C. R. Formula (1944) to resolve the Congress–Muslim League deadlock, becoming the last Governor-General of India (1948–1950) and founding the Swatantra Party (1959).

He was also the first and only Indian to serve as Governor-General of independent India.

### 1. Early life and political associations

- Born: 10 December 1878, Thorapalli, near Hosur, in the Madras Presidency (present-day Tamil Nadu).
- Popular names: Rajaji, C. R.
- Profession: Lawyer.
- Associated with the Indian National Congress and the freedom struggle in the Madras Presidency.
- Became a close associate of Mahatma Gandhi.
- Participated in the Non-Cooperation and Civil Disobedience movements.
- Died: 25 December 1972, Madras.

### 2. Vedaranyam Salt Satyagraha (1930)

- Year: 1930.
- Place: Vedaranyam, Tamil Nadu (then Madras Presidency).
- Leader: C. Rajagopalachari.
- Organised as part of Gandhi's Civil Disobedience Movement.
- Began the march from Tiruchirappalli on 13 April 1930.
- Reached Vedaranyam and broke the salt law on 30 April 1930.
- Rajagopalachari was subsequently arrested.

### Dandi March vs Vedaranyam March

| Feature         | Dandi March      | Vedaranyam March       |
| --------------- | ---------------- | ---------------------- |
| Year            | 1930             | 1930                   |
| Leader          | Mahatma Gandhi   | C. Rajagopalachari     |
| Starting point  | Sabarmati Ashram | Tiruchirappalli        |
| Destination     | Dandi, Gujarat   | Vedaranyam, Tamil Nadu |
| Salt law broken | 6 April          | 30 April               |

Prelims Point: The Vedaranyam Salt Satyagraha was the important salt-law protest led by Rajagopalachari in the Madras Presidency.

### 3. Premier of Madras Presidency (1937–1939)

- Became Premier of Madras Presidency in July 1937, following provincial elections under the Government of India Act, 1935.
- Introduced prohibition measures and supported legislation facilitating temple entry.
- Introduced compulsory Hindi instruction in certain schools, which triggered strong opposition.
- His ministry resigned in October 1939, along with other Congress provincial ministries, after Britain involved India in the Second World War without consulting Indian representatives.

### 4. Position during the Quit India Movement (1942)

- Rajagopalachari differed from the Congress leadership over its strategy during the Second World War.
- Opposed the Quit India Movement (1942).
- Favoured negotiations with the Muslim League to resolve the constitutional deadlock.
- Resigned from the Congress in April 1942 over these differences.

Prelims Point: Rajagopalachari was a prominent nationalist leader who opposed the Quit India Movement, although he had actively participated in earlier Gandhian movements.

### 5. C. R. Formula (1944)

- Proposed by: C. Rajagopalachari.
- Year: 1944.
- Objective: Resolve the political deadlock between the Indian National Congress and the Muslim League.
- Sought Muslim League cooperation in securing Indian independence through a proposed arrangement addressing the Pakistan demand.

### Main proposals

1. The Muslim League would support the demand for Indian independence and cooperate in forming an interim government.
2. After the war, a commission would demarcate Muslim-majority districts in the north-western and north-eastern parts of India.
3. A plebiscite would determine whether the people of the demarcated areas wished to separate from India.
4. In the event of separation, agreements would be made on defence, communications, commerce and other essential matters.
5. The arrangements would operate after the transfer of power by Britain.

- The proposals became an important basis for the Gandhi–Jinnah Talks (September 1944).
- The talks failed to produce an agreement.

Prelims Point: The C. R. Formula (1944) was proposed by Rajagopalachari, not by C. R. Das.

### 6. Important offices after independence

| Office                         | Period    | Important fact                           |
| ------------------------------ | --------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Governor of West Bengal        | 1947–1948 | Served after independence                |
| Governor-General of India      | 1948–1950 | First and only Indian to hold the office |
| Union Home Minister            | 1950–1951 | Succeeded Vallabhbhai Patel              |
| Chief Minister of Madras State | 1952–1954 | Succeeded by K. Kamaraj                  |

### Last Governor-General of India (1948–1950)

- Predecessor: Lord Mountbatten.
- Assumed office: 21 June 1948.
- Last day in office: 26 January 1950.
- The office of Governor-General ceased when India became a republic.
- Dr. Rajendra Prasad became the first President of India on 26 January 1950.

Important distinction:

- Lord Mountbatten: First Governor-General of independent India (1947–1948).
- C. Rajagopalachari: Last Governor-General of India (1948–1950) and the only Indian to hold the office after independence.
- Rajendra Prasad: First President of the Republic of India (1950–1962).

### 7. Swatantra Party (1959)

- Founded: 1959.
- Principal founder: C. Rajagopalachari, along with leaders including N. G. Ranga and Minoo Masani.
- Established as an opposition party to the Congress.
- Opposed excessive state control, economic centralisation and the licence-permit system.
- Favoured private enterprise, individual freedom and a market-oriented economy.
- Opposed the Congress's increasing commitment to a socialist pattern of development.

Prelims Point: The Swatantra Party (1959) was associated with Rajagopalachari, whereas the Swaraj Party (1923) was founded by C. R. Das and Motilal Nehru.

## Mukhtar Ahmed Ansari (M. A. Ansari) (1880–1936)

Dr. Mukhtar Ahmed Ansari, commonly known as M. A. Ansari, was a physician, Indian nationalist leader and prominent participant in the Khilafat and Non-Cooperation movements.

He is particularly remembered for serving as President of the Indian National Congress at the Madras Session (1927), presiding over the All-India Muslim League Session (1918), and helping establish Jamia Millia Islamia (1920).

### 1. Early life and political associations

- Born: 25 December 1880, Yusufpur-Mohammadabad, Ghazipur district, present-day Uttar Pradesh.
- Profession: Physician and surgeon.
- Studied medicine in India and Britain.
- Associated with the Indian National Congress, the All-India Muslim League and the Khilafat Movement.
- Supported Hindu–Muslim unity and the Indian independence movement.
- Was a close associate of Mahatma Gandhi.
- Died: 10 May 1936.

### 2. Medical Mission to Turkey (1912–1913)

- Led an Indian medical mission to Turkey in 1912 during the Balkan Wars.
- The mission provided medical assistance to wounded Ottoman soldiers.
- Strengthened his association with Indian Muslim political activism and international humanitarian work.

Prelims Point: M. A. Ansari was associated with the Indian Medical Mission to Turkey during the Balkan Wars, before the Khilafat Movement began in India.

### 3. Role in the Khilafat Movement (1919–1924)

- Actively supported the Khilafat Movement, which sought to protect the position of the Ottoman Caliph after the First World War.
- Worked with the Ali brothers, Muhammad Ali and Shaukat Ali, and other Khilafat leaders.
- 1920: Led a Khilafat delegation to Britain to present Indian Muslim concerns to the British government.
- Supported the alliance between the Khilafat Movement and Gandhi's Non-Cooperation Movement.
- Advocated Hindu–Muslim political cooperation.

### 4. President of the All-India Muslim League

- Presided over the All-India Muslim League sessions in 1918 and 1920.
- Supported cooperation between the Muslim League and the Indian National Congress.
- Opposed communal divisions within the nationalist movement.

Prelims Point: M. A. Ansari served as President of both the All-India Muslim League and the Indian National Congress, but in different years.

### 5. Madras Congress Session (1927)

- Year: 1927.
- Place: Madras (present-day Chennai).
- President: M. A. Ansari.

### Important resolutions

1. Boycott of the Simon Commission: Congress resolved to boycott the all-British Simon Commission, appointed in 1927.
2. Independence Resolution: Jawaharlal Nehru moved a resolution calling for complete national independence.
3. Opposition to overseas deployment of Indian troops: Congress objected to the use of Indian troops for imperial purposes abroad.

Important distinction: The Madras Session (1927) adopted an independence resolution, but the Congress formally adopted Purna Swaraj as its objective at the Lahore Session (1929) under Jawaharlal Nehru.

### 6. Role in Jamia Millia Islamia (1920)

- Founded: 29 October 1920, at Aligarh.
- M. A. Ansari was one of its founders.
- Other important personalities included Hakim Ajmal Khan, Muhammad Ali Jauhar and Abdul Majeed Khwaja.
- First Chancellor: Hakim Ajmal Khan.
- First Vice-Chancellor: Muhammad Ali Jauhar.
- M. A. Ansari served as Chancellor from 1928 to 1936.
- Helped raise funds and sustain the institution.
- Jamia Millia Islamia shifted to Delhi in 1925, and later established its campus at Okhla.

Prelims Point: M. A. Ansari was a founder and later Chancellor of Jamia Millia Islamia, not its first Chancellor.

### 7. Other contributions

- Participated in the Non-Cooperation and Civil Disobedience movements.
- Held important organisational positions in the Congress.
- Supported national educational institutions, including Kashi Vidyapith.
- His Delhi residence, Dar-us-Salam, served as a meeting place for nationalist leaders.
- Authored the medical work The Regeneration of Man (1935).

## Mahatma Gandhi (1869–1948)

Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi, popularly known as Mahatma Gandhi and Bapu, was a prominent leader of the Indian national movement who developed and popularised Satyagraha, based on truth and non-violence.

He is particularly remembered for the Champaran Satyagraha (1917), Kheda Satyagraha (1918), Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–1922), Civil Disobedience Movement (1930–1934) and Quit India Movement (1942).

### 1. Early life and political associations

- Born: 2 October 1869, Porbandar, Kathiawar, Gujarat.
- Father: Karamchand Gandhi.
- Mother: Putlibai Gandhi.
- Wife: Kasturba Gandhi.
- Profession: Lawyer.
- Studied law in London and was called to the Bar in 1891.
- Travelled to South Africa in 1893.
- Returned permanently to India on 9 January 1915.
- Political mentor: Gopal Krishna Gokhale.
- Died: 30 January 1948, New Delhi.

Prelims Point: Gandhi's birthday, 2 October, is observed as the International Day of Non-Violence. His return to India on 9 January 1915 is commemorated as Pravasi Bharatiya Divas.

### 2. Gandhi in South Africa (1893–1914)

Gandhi developed his methods of Satyagraha and non-violent resistance while opposing racial discrimination against Indians in South Africa.

| Year | Event / Organisation                | Importance                                                  |
| ---- | ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1893 | Arrival in South Africa             | Began his work against racial discrimination                |
| 1894 | Natal Indian Congress               | Founded to defend Indian interests                          |
| 1903 | Indian Opinion                      | Newspaper established in South Africa                       |
| 1904 | Phoenix Settlement                  | Established near Durban                                     |
| 1906 | First organised Satyagraha campaign | Resistance to discriminatory registration laws in Transvaal |
| 1910 | Tolstoy Farm                        | Established near Johannesburg                               |
| 1913 | Satyagraha campaign                 | Protest involving Indian workers and discriminatory laws    |
| 1914 | Indian Relief Act                   | Addressed some grievances raised during the campaigns       |

### Important associates

- Henry Polak: Associate of Gandhi in South Africa.
- Hermann Kallenbach: Associated with the establishment of Tolstoy Farm.
- Kasturba Gandhi: Participated in South African Satyagraha.
- Thambi Naidoo: Important organiser of Indian resistance.

Prelims Point: The first organised Satyagraha campaign was launched in South Africa in 1906, whereas Gandhi's first major Satyagraha in India was Champaran (1917).

### 3. Return to India and early activities (1915–1916)

- 9 January 1915: Returned to India from South Africa.
- Political mentor: Gopal Krishna Gokhale.
- 1915: Established the Satyagraha Ashram at Kochrab, Ahmedabad.
- 1917: Shifted the ashram to Sabarmati, near Ahmedabad.
- 1916: Delivered an important speech at the inauguration of Banaras Hindu University.
- Initially travelled across India to understand the country's social, economic and political conditions.

### 4. Early Satyagrahas in India (1917–1918)

| Movement              | Year | Place   | Main issue                                   |
| --------------------- | ---- | ------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Champaran Satyagraha  | 1917 | Bihar   | Exploitation of indigo cultivators           |
| Ahmedabad Mill Strike | 1918 | Gujarat | Wage dispute between mill workers and owners |
| Kheda Satyagraha      | 1918 | Gujarat | Land revenue collection despite crop failure |

### Champaran Satyagraha (1917)

- Gandhi's first major Satyagraha in India.
- Raj Kumar Shukla persuaded Gandhi to visit Champaran.
- Concerned the Tinkathia System, under which cultivators were compelled to grow indigo on 3/20 of their land.
- Important associates included Rajendra Prasad, Brajkishore Prasad and J. B. Kripalani.
- The agitation contributed to the abolition of the Tinkathia System.

### Ahmedabad Mill Strike (1918)

- Concerned a wage dispute between textile mill workers and owners.
- Gandhi undertook a fast during the agitation.
- Important associates included Anasuya Sarabhai.
- The dispute was settled through arbitration.

### Kheda Satyagraha (1918)

- Concerned the inability of peasants to pay land revenue because of crop failure.
- Gandhi supported the demand for suspension of revenue collection.
- Vallabhbhai Patel played an important organisational role.

Prelims distinction: Champaran concerned indigo cultivation, Ahmedabad concerned industrial workers' wages, and Kheda concerned land revenue.

### 5. Rowlatt Satyagraha (1919)

- Rowlatt Act: Passed in 1919.
- Based on the recommendations of the Sedition Committee chaired by Justice Sidney Rowlatt.
- Allowed extraordinary measures against persons suspected of revolutionary activities, including detention without ordinary trial safeguards.
- Gandhi launched a nationwide Satyagraha against the Act.
- 6 April 1919: Nationwide hartal was observed.
- 13 April 1919: Jallianwala Bagh Massacre occurred at Amritsar.
- Gandhi subsequently suspended the Rowlatt Satyagraha amid violence.

Prelims Point: The Rowlatt Satyagraha (1919) was Gandhi's first major nationwide Satyagraha campaign.

### 6. Khilafat and Non-Cooperation Movement (1919–1922)

### Khilafat Movement

- Important leaders included Muhammad Ali, Shaukat Ali, Hakim Ajmal Khan and M. A. Ansari.
- Concerned the position of the Ottoman Caliph following the First World War.
- Gandhi supported the Khilafat cause to promote Hindu–Muslim cooperation.

### Non-Cooperation Movement

- August 1920: Non-Cooperation campaign began.
- September 1920: Calcutta Special Session, presided over by Lala Lajpat Rai, approved Gandhi's Non-Cooperation programme.
- December 1920: Nagpur Session, presided over by C. Vijayaraghavachariar, adopted the programme and reorganised Congress.
- Main methods included boycott of government schools, law courts, legislative councils, foreign cloth and official titles.
- Encouraged Swadeshi, khadi and national education.

### Withdrawal (1922)

- 5 February 1922: Chauri Chaura incident, Gorakhpur district, United Provinces.
- A violent confrontation resulted in the deaths of 22 policemen.
- Gandhi withdrew the mass Non-Cooperation Movement in February 1922.
- Arrested in March 1922 and sentenced to six years' imprisonment for sedition; released in 1924.

Prelims Point: The Non-Cooperation Movement was withdrawn because of the Chauri Chaura incident (1922), not the Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (1919).

### 7. Belgaum Congress Session (1924)

- Year: 1924.
- Place: Belgaum (present-day Belagavi, Karnataka).
- President: Mahatma Gandhi.
- This was the only annual session of the Indian National Congress presided over by Gandhi.

### 8. Civil Disobedience Movement and Dandi March (1930)

### Dandi March

- Started: 12 March 1930.
- Starting point: Sabarmati Ashram, Ahmedabad.
- Destination: Dandi, Gujarat.
- Gandhi began the march with 78 selected volunteers.
- 6 April 1930: Broke the salt law at Dandi.
- Marked the beginning of the Civil Disobedience Movement.

### Eleven demands (1930)

Before launching Civil Disobedience, Gandhi presented eleven demands to Viceroy Lord Irwin, including abolition of the salt tax, reduction of land revenue and military expenditure, prohibition and changes in economic policies.

Prelims Point: Gandhi's Dandi March began on 12 March 1930 and the salt law was broken on 6 April 1930.

### 9. Gandhi–Irwin Pact (1931)

- Signed: 5 March 1931.
- Parties: Mahatma Gandhi and Viceroy Lord Irwin.
- Congress agreed to suspend the Civil Disobedience Movement.
- The government agreed to release political prisoners not convicted of violence, withdraw certain ordinances and permit limited peaceful salt manufacture.
- Gandhi agreed to attend the Second Round Table Conference.

### Round Table Conferences

| ---------- | ---- | --------------------------------------- |

| Second     | 1931 | Attended as the Congress representative |

- Gandhi attended the Second Round Table Conference in London.
- The negotiations did not produce a satisfactory constitutional settlement.
- Civil Disobedience resumed after his return to India.

Prelims Point: Gandhi attended only the Second Round Table Conference (1931).

### 10. Communal Award and Poona Pact (1932)

### Communal Award (1932)

- Announced: 16 August 1932.
- British Prime Minister: Ramsay MacDonald.
- Provided separate electorates for several communities, including the Depressed Classes.
- Gandhi opposed separate electorates for the Depressed Classes and began a fast in Yeravada Jail.

### Poona Pact (1932)

- Signed: 24 September 1932.
- Principal negotiators: B. R. Ambedkar and Madan Mohan Malaviya, with Gandhi's approval.
- Replaced separate electorates for the Depressed Classes with reserved seats in joint electorates.
- Increased the number of reserved seats for the Depressed Classes in provincial legislatures from 71 under the Communal Award to 148 under the Pact.

Prelims Point: The Poona Pact provided reserved seats within joint electorates, not separate electorates.

### 11. Constructive Programme and Social Reform

Gandhi emphasised social reform alongside political independence.

### Important organisations and initiatives

- Harijan Sevak Sangh (1932): Established to work against untouchability; Gandhi was closely associated with its formation.
- Wardha Scheme of Basic Education (1937): Associated with Gandhi's educational ideas; the committee preparing the scheme was chaired by Zakir Husain.
- Sevagram Ashram (1936): Established near Wardha, Maharashtra.

### 12. Individual Satyagraha (1940)

- Launched by Gandhi during the Second World War.
- Intended to assert the right to oppose India's involuntary participation in the war through peaceful expression.
- It was a limited, individual protest, rather than an immediate nationwide mass movement.

## Jawaharlal Nehru (1889–1964)

Jawaharlal Nehru, popularly known as Pandit Nehru and Chacha Nehru, was a prominent Indian nationalist leader, freedom fighter and the first Prime Minister of independent India (1947–1964).

He is particularly remembered for presiding over the Lahore Congress Session (1929), which adopted Purna Swaraj (Complete Independence) as the Congress objective; serving as Chairman of the National Planning Committee (1938); and leading India during the early years after independence.

### 1. Early life and political associations

- Born: 14 November 1889, Allahabad, United Provinces (present-day Uttar Pradesh).
- Father: Motilal Nehru.
- Mother: Swarup Rani Nehru.
- Wife: Kamala Nehru.
- Daughter: Indira Gandhi.
- Educated at Harrow School and Trinity College, Cambridge, England.
- Studied law at the Inner Temple, London.
- Returned to India in 1912 and began practising law.
- Joined the Indian national movement and became a close associate of Mahatma Gandhi.
- Died: 27 May 1964, New Delhi.

### 2. Early nationalist activities

- Attended the Bankipore Session of the Indian National Congress (1912).
- Associated with the Home Rule Movement (1916).
- Met Mahatma Gandhi at the Lucknow Congress Session (1916).
- Participated in the Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–1922).
- Became General Secretary of the All India Congress Committee (1923).
- Advocated complete independence rather than Dominion Status.
- Became a prominent leader of the younger generation of Congress nationalists.

### 3. Madras Congress Session (1927)

- President: M. A. Ansari.
- Jawaharlal Nehru moved a resolution advocating complete national independence.
- Opposed the all-British Simon Commission, appointed in 1927.
- Supported the boycott of the Commission.

Prelims Point: The independence resolution at Madras (1927) was moved by Jawaharlal Nehru, but the session was presided over by M. A. Ansari.

### 4. Lahore Congress Session (1929)

- Year: 1929.
- Place: Lahore.
- President: Jawaharlal Nehru.
- Congress formally adopted Purna Swaraj (Complete Independence) as its objective.
- Rejected Dominion Status as an adequate goal.
- Authorised the launching of a Civil Disobedience Movement.
- 31 December 1929: Nehru hoisted the national flag on the banks of the Ravi River.
- 26 January 1930: Observed as Independence Day throughout India.

Prelims Point: The Purna Swaraj resolution was adopted at the Lahore Congress Session (1929), not the Lahore Session of 1893 or the Calcutta Session of 1928.

### 5. Congress Presidency

| Year            | Session / Circumstance                                 |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1929            | Lahore                                                 |
| 1936            | Lucknow                                                |
| 1936 (December) | Faizpur (commonly listed as the 1937 Congress session) |
| 1946            | Elected Congress President                             |
| 1951–1954       | Served as Congress President after independence        |

### Faizpur Congress Session (1936)

- President: Jawaharlal Nehru.
- Held in December 1936 at Faizpur, Maharashtra.
- Recognised as the first regular annual Congress session held in a rural area.
- Focused attention on agrarian problems and the interests of peasants.

Important distinction: The Faizpur Session is frequently labelled 1937 in Congress presidency lists, although it actually took place in December 1936.

### 6. National Planning Committee (1938)

- Year: 1938.
- Established during the Congress presidency of Subhas Chandra Bose.
- Chairman: Jawaharlal Nehru.
- Sought to formulate plans for India's economic and industrial development.
- Favoured economic planning, industrialisation and a stronger role for the state.

Prelims Point: The National Planning Committee (1938) was chaired by Jawaharlal Nehru, but established under the Congress presidency of Subhas Chandra Bose.

### 7. Individual Satyagraha (1940) and Quit India Movement (1942)

### Individual Satyagraha (1940)

- Launched by Mahatma Gandhi in 1940.
- First Satyagrahi: Vinoba Bhave.
- Second Satyagrahi: Jawaharlal Nehru.
- Protested the denial of India's freedom to express its position on the Second World War.

### Quit India Movement (1942)

- Nehru participated in the movement launched in August 1942.
- Arrested along with other Congress leaders.
- Imprisoned at Ahmednagar Fort.
- Wrote much of The Discovery of India during his imprisonment.

### 8. Interim Government (1946)

- Formed: 2 September 1946.
- Head: Jawaharlal Nehru.
- Served as Vice-President of the Viceroy's Executive Council, effectively heading the Interim Government.
- Also held responsibility for External Affairs and Commonwealth Relations.
- The Muslim League initially did not join the Interim Government but entered it in October 1946.

Prelims Point: Nehru headed the Interim Government of 1946, before becoming the first Prime Minister of independent India in August 1947.

### 9. Role in the Constituent Assembly

- Tenure: 15 August 1947 to 27 May 1964.

## B. R. Ambedkar (1891–1956)

Dr. Bhimrao Ramji Ambedkar, popularly known as Babasaheb Ambedkar, was an Indian jurist, economist, social reformer, political leader and the Chairman of the Drafting Committee of the Constituent Assembly.

He is particularly remembered for the Mahad Satyagraha (1927), Poona Pact (1932), founding the Independent Labour Party (1936) and Scheduled Castes Federation (1942), and serving as independent India's first Law Minister (1947–1951).

He is widely regarded as the principal architect of the Indian Constitution.

### 1. Early life and education

- Born: 14 April 1891, Mhow, Central Provinces (present-day Dr. Ambedkar Nagar, Madhya Pradesh).
- Father: Ramji Maloji Sakpal.
- Mother: Bhimabai Sakpal.
- Belonged to the Mahar community, which faced caste-based discrimination.
- Studied at Elphinstone College, Bombay.
- Pursued higher education at Columbia University, New York, and the London School of Economics.
- Studied law at Gray's Inn, London.
- His important areas of study included economics, law, politics and public finance.
- Died: 6 December 1956, Delhi.

### 2. Important organisations and political parties

| Organisation                          | Year | Association                                                                      |
| ------------------------------------- | ---- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Bahishkrit Hitakarini Sabha           | 1924 | Founded to promote education and welfare of the Depressed Classes                |
| Samata Sainik Dal                     | 1927 | Organisation for social equality and mobilisation                                |
| Independent Labour Party              | 1936 | Founded to represent workers, peasants and disadvantaged communities             |
| All India Scheduled Castes Federation | 1942 | Founded to represent Scheduled Castes politically                                |
| Republican Party of India             | 1957 | Established after Ambedkar's death, based on his plans for a new political party |

Prelims Point: Ambedkar founded the Independent Labour Party in 1936 and the Scheduled Castes Federation in 1942. The Republican Party of India was formally established after his death, in 1957.

### 3. Important Social Reform Movements

### Mahad Satyagraha (1927)

- Date: 20 March 1927.
- Place: Mahad, Bombay Presidency (present-day Maharashtra).
- Leader: B. R. Ambedkar.
- Objective: Secure the right of the Depressed Classes to access water from the public Chavdar (Chowdar) Tank.
- Challenged caste-based restrictions on access to public facilities.
- 25 December 1927: Ambedkar and his supporters publicly burned the Manusmriti at Mahad as a protest against caste discrimination.

### Kalaram Temple Entry Movement (1930)

- Year: 1930.
- Place: Nashik, Maharashtra.
- Leader: B. R. Ambedkar.
- Objective: Secure temple-entry rights for people excluded on caste grounds.
- The movement challenged restrictions on religious access and social equality.

Prelims distinction: Mahad Satyagraha (1927) concerned access to public water, while Kalaram Temple Satyagraha (1930) concerned temple entry.

### 4. Round Table Conferences (1930–1932)

- Ambedkar participated in all three Round Table Conferences held in London.
- Represented the interests of the Depressed Classes.
- Demanded political safeguards, including separate electorates for the Depressed Classes.

| Conference | Year | Ambedkar | Gandhi         |
| ---------- | ---- | -------- | -------------- |
| First      | 1930 | Attended | Did not attend |
| Second     | 1931 | Attended | Attended       |
| Third      | 1932 | Attended | Did not attend |

Prelims Point: Ambedkar attended all three Round Table Conferences, whereas Gandhi attended only the Second Round Table Conference (1931).

### 5. Communal Award and Poona Pact (1932)

### Communal Award (1932)

- Announced: 16 August 1932.
- British Prime Minister: Ramsay MacDonald.
- Provided separate electorates for the Depressed Classes, among other communities.
- Gandhi opposed separate electorates for the Depressed Classes and began a fast in Yeravada Jail.

### Poona Pact (1932)

- Date: 24 September 1932.
- Agreement between representatives of the Depressed Classes led by Ambedkar and caste Hindu representatives, including Madan Mohan Malaviya.
- Gandhi approved the agreement while imprisoned.
- Replaced separate electorates for the Depressed Classes with reserved seats in joint electorates.
- Increased reserved seats in provincial legislatures from 71 under the Communal Award to 148 under the Poona Pact.
- Provided for a system of primary elections to select candidates from the Depressed Classes.

### Communal Award vs Poona Pact

| Feature                       | Communal Award (1932)          | Poona Pact (1932)                        |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------- |
| Announced / negotiated by     | Ramsay MacDonald               | Ambedkar and caste Hindu representatives |
| Depressed Classes electorates | Separate electorates           | Joint electorates                        |
| Provincial reserved seats     | 71                             | 148                                      |
| Political representation      | Separate electoral arrangement | Reserved seats within joint electorates  |

Prelims Point: The Poona Pact did not abolish political reservation for the Depressed Classes; it replaced separate electorates with reserved seats in joint electorates.

### 6. Role in Labour and Economic Reforms

- Ambedkar was an economist with a particular interest in public finance, monetary policy and labour welfare.
- Submitted evidence to the Royal Commission on Indian Currency and Finance (Hilton Young Commission) in 1925.
- Served as Labour Member of the Viceroy's Executive Council (1942–1946).
- Supported labour welfare measures, social security and protections for workers.
- His economic writings examined the rupee, currency management, provincial finance and agriculture.

Prelims Point: Ambedkar served as Labour Member of the Viceroy's Executive Council before independence, and as Law Minister after independence.

### 7. Role in the Constituent Assembly

- First elected to the Constituent Assembly in 1946 from Bengal.
- Subsequently elected from Bombay Presidency in 1947, following Partition.
- 29 August 1947: Appointed Chairman of the Drafting Committee.
- Played a central role in preparing, presenting and defending the Draft Constitution.
- Supported constitutional guarantees of equality, liberty, social justice and protection against discrimination.

### Important constitutional provisions

| Article    | Provision                                          |
| ---------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Article 14 | Equality before law and equal protection of laws   |
| Article 15 | Prohibition of discrimination on specified grounds |
| Article 16 | Equality of opportunity in public employment       |
| Article 17 | Abolition of untouchability                        |
| Article 23 | Prohibition of trafficking and forced labour       |
| Article 32 | Right to constitutional remedies                   |

- Ambedkar described Article 32 as the heart and soul of the Constitution.
- The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949 and came fully into force on 26 January 1950.

Prelims Point: Ambedkar was Chairman of the Drafting Committee, whereas Rajendra Prasad was President of the Constituent Assembly and B. N. Rau was Constitutional Adviser.

### 8. First Law Minister of Independent India (1947–1951)

- Became independent India's first Law Minister on 15 August 1947.
- Supported the Hindu Code Bill, which sought reforms in Hindu personal law, including marriage, divorce, inheritance and women's rights.
- Resigned from the Union Cabinet in 1951, partly because of disagreements over the progress of the Hindu Code Bill.
- Subsequently served as a Rajya Sabha member from 1952 until his death in 1956.

### 9. Conversion to Buddhism (1956)

- Date: 14 October 1956.
- Place: Deekshabhoomi, Nagpur.
- Ambedkar publicly converted to Buddhism, along with a large number of followers.
- His conversion was associated with the Navayana Buddhist movement.
- Died: 6 December 1956, Delhi.
- His death anniversary is observed as Mahaparinirvan Diwas.

## Bhogaraju Pattabhi Sitaramayya (1880–1959)

Bhogaraju Pattabhi Sitaramayya, commonly known as B. Pattabhi Sitaramayya, was an Indian nationalist leader, physician, historian and prominent member of the Indian National Congress.

He is particularly remembered for losing the Congress presidential election to Subhas Chandra Bose in 1939, serving as Congress President at the Jaipur Session (1948) and authoring The History of the Indian National Congress.

### 1. Early life and political associations

- Born: 24 November 1880, Gundugolanu, in present-day Andhra Pradesh.
- Profession: Physician; later became a full-time nationalist leader.
- Associated with the Indian National Congress and the freedom struggle in the Andhra region.
- Supported Mahatma Gandhi's political leadership and participated in the national movement.
- Played an important role in the movement for a separate Andhra province.
- Died: 17 December 1959.

### 2. Tripuri Congress Presidential Election (1939)

- Year: 1939.
- Congress Session: Tripuri, Central Provinces (present-day Madhya Pradesh).
- Candidates: Subhas Chandra Bose and Pattabhi Sitaramayya.
- Pattabhi Sitaramayya was supported by Gandhi and several senior Congress leaders.
- Result: Subhas Chandra Bose defeated Pattabhi Sitaramayya.

| Candidate            | Votes |
| -------------------- | ----- |
| Subhas Chandra Bose  | 1,580 |
| Pattabhi Sitaramayya | 1,377 |

- Gandhi subsequently described Pattabhi's defeat as more his own defeat than Pattabhi's.
- Differences over the Congress Working Committee and political strategy intensified.
- Bose resigned from the Congress presidency in April 1939.
- Rajendra Prasad succeeded Bose as Congress President.

Prelims Point: Pattabhi Sitaramayya was defeated by Subhas Chandra Bose in 1939, not in 1938. Bose had presided over the Haripura Session (1938) before his re-election at Tripuri.

### 3. Congress President — Jaipur Session (1948)

- Year: 1948.
- Session: Jaipur.
- President: B. Pattabhi Sitaramayya.
- Became Congress President after India's independence.
- Served as Congress President during 1948–1950.

Prelims Point: Pattabhi Sitaramayya lost the presidential election in 1939, but later became Congress President in 1948.

### 4. Role in the Andhra Movement

- Advocated the creation of a separate Andhra province based on the Telugu language.
- Supported the linguistic reorganisation of provinces.
- Wrote The Redistribution of Indian Provinces on a Linguistic Basis (1916).
- Was subsequently appointed to the JVP Committee (1948).

- Recognised that the Andhra question could be considered separately under appropriate conditions.

Prelims Point: The JVP Committee is different from the Dhar Commission (1948) and the States Reorganisation Commission (1953).

### 5. Banking and cooperative activities

- Founded Andhra Bank in 1923 at Machilipatnam.
- Promoted cooperative banking, insurance and Indian-owned financial institutions.
- Associated with the cooperative movement in Andhra.
- Encouraged economic self-reliance as part of the nationalist movement.

### 6. Constituent Assembly and Governor of Madhya Pradesh

- Elected to the Constituent Assembly in 1946.

## Muhammad Ali Jinnah (1876–1948)

Muhammad Ali Jinnah, popularly known as Quaid-e-Azam, was a lawyer and political leader who played a central role in the All-India Muslim League and the creation of Pakistan in 1947.

He is particularly remembered for his role in the Lucknow Pact (1916), proposing the Fourteen Points (1929), leading the Muslim League during the Lahore Resolution (1940), and becoming the first Governor-General of Pakistan (1947–1948).

### 1. Early life and political associations

- Born: 25 December 1876, Karachi, Sindh (then British India).
- Profession: Barrister.
- Studied law at Lincoln's Inn, London.
- Began his legal career in Bombay.
- Associated with the Indian National Congress from 1906.
- Joined the All-India Muslim League in 1913, while initially retaining his Congress association.
- Supported Hindu–Muslim political cooperation during the early phase of his career.
- Later became the principal leader of the Muslim League's demand for Pakistan.
- Died: 11 September 1948, Karachi.

### 2. Early nationalist activities

- Attended the Calcutta Congress Session (1906).
- Associated with the Moderate constitutional approach to political reform.
- Opposed the use of separate electorates as a divisive measure during his early political career.
- Became a member of the Imperial Legislative Council in 1910.
- Supported legislative reforms and constitutional methods.
- Gopal Krishna Gokhale was an important political influence on Jinnah's early career.

Prelims Point: Jinnah was associated with both the Indian National Congress and the All-India Muslim League during the early part of his political career.

### 3. Lucknow Pact (1916)

- Year: 1916.
- Place: Lucknow.
- Parties: Indian National Congress and All-India Muslim League.
- Jinnah played an important role in bringing the two organisations together.
- The Congress accepted separate electorates for Muslims as part of the agreement.
- The two organisations agreed on common constitutional demands to present to the British government.
- Sarojini Naidu described Jinnah as an Ambassador of Hindu–Muslim Unity.

Prelims Point: The Lucknow Pact (1916) was an agreement between the Congress and Muslim League, not between Gandhi and Jinnah personally.

### 4. Opposition to the Non-Cooperation Movement (1920)

- Jinnah opposed Gandhi's programme of mass Non-Cooperation.
- Preferred constitutional and legislative methods over Gandhi's approach of mass civil disobedience.
- Left the Indian National Congress in 1920.
- Continued his political activities through the Muslim League.

Prelims Point: Jinnah opposed the Non-Cooperation Movement (1920), although he had earlier supported Congress–League cooperation.

### 5. Delhi Muslim Proposals (1927)

- Year: 1927.
- Associated with Muhammad Ali Jinnah and other Muslim leaders.
- Sought a constitutional compromise concerning Muslim political representation.
- Offered conditional acceptance of joint electorates, provided certain safeguards were accepted.

### Important proposals

1. One-third Muslim representation in the Central Legislature.
2. Separation of Sindh from Bombay Presidency.
3. Constitutional reforms in the North-West Frontier Province and Baluchistan.
4. Muslim representation safeguards, including protection of Muslim majorities in Punjab and Bengal.

Prelims Point: Jinnah's Delhi Proposals (1927) conditionally accepted joint electorates, whereas his Fourteen Points (1929) demanded the continuation of separate electorates unless communities voluntarily agreed to abandon them.

### 6. Jinnah's Fourteen Points (1929)

- Year: 1929.
- Proposed by: Muhammad Ali Jinnah.
- Formulated in response to the Nehru Report (1928), chaired by Motilal Nehru.
- Intended to safeguard Muslim political representation and provincial autonomy under a future constitutional system.

### Important demands

1. A federal Constitution, with residuary powers vested in the provinces.
2. Uniform autonomy for all provinces.
3. Adequate and effective minority representation in legislatures.
4. One-third Muslim representation in the Central Legislature.
5. Continuation of separate electorates unless communities voluntarily agreed to joint electorates.
6. Protection of Muslim majorities in Punjab, Bengal and the North-West Frontier Province.
7. Full religious liberty for all communities.
8. No territorial redistribution that would adversely affect Muslim majorities.
9. Separation of Sindh from Bombay Presidency.
10. Constitutional reforms in the North-West Frontier Province and Baluchistan.
11. Adequate Muslim representation in public services.
12. Constitutional safeguards for Muslim culture, education, religion and personal law.
13. At least one-third Muslim representation in central and provincial cabinets.
14. Constitutional amendments to the federal arrangement to require the concurrence of the constituent units.

### Nehru Report vs Jinnah's Fourteen Points

| Feature                         | Nehru Report (1928)                 | Jinnah's Fourteen Points (1929)                                    |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Principal personality           | Motilal Nehru                       | Muhammad Ali Jinnah                                                |
| Constitutional structure        | Federation with a strong Centre     | Federation with substantial provincial autonomy                    |
| Residuary powers                | Centre                              | Provinces                                                          |
| Electorates                     | Joint electorates                   | Continuation of separate electorates, unless voluntarily abandoned |
| Muslim representation at Centre | Did not accept the one-third demand | One-third representation demanded                                  |

Prelims Point: The Nehru Report (1928) assigned residuary powers to the Centre, whereas Jinnah's Fourteen Points (1929) proposed vesting them in the provinces.

### 7. Muslim League leadership and elections (1934–1937)

- Returned to active Indian politics in 1934, after spending several years mainly in Britain.
- Reorganised the All-India Muslim League.
- Led the League during the 1937 provincial elections under the Government of India Act, 1935.
- The League did not secure a majority of Muslim seats across British India in those elections.
- The subsequent Congress–League disagreements contributed to a widening political divide.

### Day of Deliverance (1939)

- Date: 22 December 1939.
- Called for by Jinnah following the resignation of Congress provincial ministries.
- The ministries had resigned in protest against Britain's decision to involve India in the Second World War without consulting Indian representatives.

### 8. Lahore Resolution (1940)

- Date adopted: 23 March 1940.
- Place: Lahore.
- Organisation: All-India Muslim League.
- Session President: Muhammad Ali Jinnah.
- Resolution moved by: A. K. Fazlul Huq, Premier of Bengal.
- Called for Muslim-majority areas in north-western and eastern British India to be grouped into independent states, with their constituent units autonomous and sovereign.
- Subsequently became popularly known as the Pakistan Resolution.

Important distinction: The Lahore Resolution was moved by A. K. Fazlul Huq, not by Jinnah, although Jinnah presided over the session.

### 9. Position on the Cripps Mission (1942)

- Cripps Mission: Sent to India in 1942 under Sir Stafford Cripps.
- Proposed Dominion Status after the Second World War and allowed provinces the option not to join the proposed Indian Union.
- The Muslim League rejected the proposals because they did not explicitly recognise its demand for Pakistan.
- The Indian National Congress also rejected the proposals, but for different reasons.

### 10. Gandhi–Jinnah Talks (1944)

- Year: September 1944.
- Place: Bombay.
- Participants: Mahatma Gandhi and Muhammad Ali Jinnah.
- The C. R. Formula, proposed by C. Rajagopalachari, formed an important basis for discussion.
- Gandhi and Jinnah differed over the recognition of Pakistan and the conditions under which partition could be considered.
- The talks ended without agreement.

Prelims Point: The C. R. Formula (1944) was proposed by C. Rajagopalachari, whereas the subsequent negotiations involved Gandhi and Jinnah.

### 11. Simla Conference (1945)

- Year: 1945.
- Viceroy: Lord Wavell.
- Based on the Wavell Plan, which proposed reconstituting the Viceroy's Executive Council.
- Jinnah insisted that the Muslim League should have the exclusive right to nominate Muslim members to the Executive Council.
- Congress did not accept the League's claim to represent all Indian Muslims.
- The conference failed to produce an agreement.

### 12. Cabinet Mission Plan (1946)

- Year: 1946.
- Cabinet Mission members: Lord Pethick-Lawrence, Sir Stafford Cripps and A. V. Alexander.
- Proposed an Indian Union with a limited Centre responsible mainly for defence, foreign affairs and communications.
- Provinces were to be organised into three groups.
- Rejected the demand for a separate sovereign Pakistan.
- The Muslim League initially accepted the plan but later withdrew its acceptance amid disputes over its interpretation and implementation.

### Direct Action Day (1946)

- Date: 16 August 1946.
- Called for by the All-India Muslim League under Jinnah's leadership.
- Followed the League's decision to pursue its Pakistan demand through Direct Action.
- The day was accompanied by severe communal violence in Calcutta, followed by further violence elsewhere.

Prelims Point: Direct Action Day was 16 August 1946, not 16 August 1947.

### 13. Partition and creation of Pakistan (1947)

- 3 June 1947: Mountbatten Plan announced, providing for partition.
- 18 July 1947: Indian Independence Act received Royal Assent.
- 14 August 1947: Pakistan became independent.
- 15 August 1947: India became independent.
- Jinnah became the first Governor-General of Pakistan.
- Liaquat Ali Khan became Pakistan's first Prime Minister.
- Jinnah remained Governor-General until his death on 11 September 1948.

## Madan Mohan Malaviya (1861–1946)

Pandit Madan Mohan Malaviya, popularly known as Mahamana, was an Indian nationalist leader, educationist, lawyer and social reformer.

He is particularly remembered for founding Banaras Hindu University (BHU) in 1916, serving as President of the Indian National Congress on four occasions, and playing an important role in the Poona Pact (1932).

### 1. Early life and political associations

- Born: 25 December 1861, Allahabad (present-day Prayagraj), Uttar Pradesh.
- Popular title: Mahamana.
- Profession: Teacher, journalist, lawyer and educationist.
- Studied at Muir Central College, Allahabad.
- Became associated with the Indian National Congress in 1886.
- Delivered a speech at the Calcutta Congress Session (1886), presided over by Dadabhai Naoroji.
- Supported constitutional reforms, national education and social reform.
- Died: 12 November 1946, Allahabad.

### 2. Indian National Congress Presidency

Malaviya was elected Congress President on four occasions.

| Year | Congress Session      | Important fact                                               |
| ---- | --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1909 | Lahore                | First Congress presidency                                    |
| 1918 | Delhi Special Session | Second presidency                                            |
| 1932 | Delhi                 | Elected president; session prohibited by British authorities |
| 1933 | Calcutta              | Elected president; session prohibited by British authorities |

Prelims Point: Malaviya is generally listed as having been elected Congress President four times, although the 1932 and 1933 sessions could not function normally because of government repression.

### 3. Banaras Hindu University (BHU)

- Founded: 1916.
- Location: Varanasi, Uttar Pradesh.
- Principal founder: Madan Mohan Malaviya.
- Important associate: Annie Besant, whose Central Hindu College became part of the university.
- Legislation: Banaras Hindu University Act, 1915.
- Foundation ceremony: 4 February 1916, performed by Viceroy Lord Hardinge.
- Malaviya served as Vice-Chancellor from 1919 to 1939.
- S. Radhakrishnan succeeded him as Vice-Chancellor.

BHU sought to combine modern scientific and technical education with Indian intellectual and cultural traditions.

Prelims Point: The BHU Act was passed in 1915, while Banaras Hindu University was established in 1916.

### 4. Role in the National Movement

- Associated with the Moderate tradition of the Indian National Congress.
- Supported constitutional reforms, representative institutions and national education.
- Opposed the introduction of separate electorates for Muslims under the Morley–Minto Reforms (1909).
- Disagreed with the acceptance of separate electorates in the Lucknow Pact (1916).
- Opposed the repressive Rowlatt legislation (1919).
- Participated in the Civil Disobedience Movement and faced arrest.
- Attended the Second Round Table Conference (1931) in London.

### 5. Poona Pact (1932)

- Date: 24 September 1932.
- Place: Poona (present-day Pune).
- Principal signatories: B. R. Ambedkar and Madan Mohan Malaviya, representing the Depressed Classes and caste Hindu representatives respectively.
- Gandhi, who was imprisoned in Yeravada Jail, approved the agreement.
- The Pact followed Gandhi's opposition to separate electorates for the Depressed Classes under the Communal Award (1932).

### Main provisions

- Replaced separate electorates for the Depressed Classes with reserved seats in joint electorates.
- Increased reserved seats in provincial legislatures from 71 to 148.
- Provided a system of primary elections for selecting candidates belonging to the Depressed Classes.

Prelims Point: The Poona Pact was signed by representatives including Ambedkar and Malaviya. Gandhi was central to the negotiations but was not a signatory.

### 6. Hindu Mahasabha and Political Associations

- Malaviya was associated with the early development of the Hindu Mahasabha and served in its leadership.
- The organisation's roots lay in provincial Hindu associations; the all-India organisation emerged in 1915.
- Supported Hindu religious and educational institutions.
- In 1934, Malaviya and M. S. Aney formed the Congress Nationalist Party, following disagreements over communal representation.

Prelims distinction: The Congress Nationalist Party (1934) associated with Malaviya and M. S. Aney was different from the Swaraj Party (1923) founded by C. R. Das and Motilal Nehru.

### 7. Journalism and Newspapers

Prelims Point: The Leader (1909) and Abhyudaya (1907) are important newspaper associations of Madan Mohan Malaviya.

Do not confuse the Indian Opinion associated with Malaviya's early journalism in India with Gandhi's Indian Opinion, established in South Africa in 1903.

### 8. Social and Educational Contributions

- Advocated expansion of modern education, including scientific and technical education.
- Supported the promotion of Hindi and Sanskrit.
- Opposed untouchability and supported greater access to religious and educational institutions.
- Encouraged Swadeshi and Indian industries.
- Established the All India Swadeshi Sangh in 1932, promoting the purchase of Indian-made goods.
- Participated in public service and relief activities.

### 9. Bharat Ratna

- Awarded the Bharat Ratna posthumously in 2015.
- The award was announced on 24 December 2014.
- Atal Bihari Vajpayee was the other recipient announced on the same date.

Prelims Point: For questions about the award year, remember 2015; for questions about the announcement, remember 2014.

## J. B. Kripalani (1888–1982)

Jivatram Bhagwandas Kripalani, popularly known as Acharya Kripalani, was an Indian freedom fighter, Gandhian leader, educationist and politician.

He is particularly remembered for his association with the Champaran Satyagraha (1917), serving as General Secretary of the Indian National Congress (1928–1945), becoming Congress President during India's independence in 1947, and chairing the Fundamental Rights Sub-Committee of the Constituent Assembly.

### 1. Early life and political associations

- Born: 11 November 1888, Hyderabad, Sindh (then British India; now Pakistan).
- Full name: Jivatram Bhagwandas Kripalani.
- Popular title: Acharya Kripalani.
- Profession: Teacher, educationist and politician.
- Associated with Mahatma Gandhi and the Indian National Congress.
- Supported Gandhian principles, including non-violence, Swadeshi and village development.
- Wife: Sucheta Kripalani, who later became the first woman Chief Minister of an Indian state.
- Died: 19 March 1982, Ahmedabad.

### 2. Association with Champaran Satyagraha (1917)

- Kripalani was teaching at Muzaffarpur when Gandhi arrived in Bihar in 1917.
- Helped Gandhi during the Champaran Satyagraha.
- Assisted in organising support and connecting Gandhi with local people.
- Champaran concerned the exploitation of indigo cultivators under the Tinkathia System.

Prelims Point: J. B. Kripalani was an important associate of Gandhi during the Champaran Satyagraha (1917), alongside leaders such as Rajendra Prasad and Brajkishore Prasad.

### 3. Role in National Education

- Associated with the development of national educational institutions during the freedom struggle.
- Joined Gujarat Vidyapith, established by Mahatma Gandhi in Ahmedabad in 1920.
- Served as a teacher and later as its principal.
- Acquired the popular title Acharya, meaning teacher.
- Supported education based on Gandhian principles, Swadeshi and self-reliance.

Prelims Point: J. B. Kripalani was associated with Gujarat Vidyapith, whereas Madan Mohan Malaviya was associated with Banaras Hindu University.

### 4. Role in the Indian National Congress

- Participated in the Non-Cooperation Movement and Civil Disobedience Movement.
- Served as General Secretary of the Indian National Congress from 1928 to 1945.
- Played an important organisational role in Congress activities.
- Participated in the Quit India Movement (1942) and was imprisoned.
- Became Congress President in 1946.

### Meerut Congress Session (1946)

- Year: November 1946.
- Place: Meerut, United Provinces (present-day Uttar Pradesh).
- President: J. B. Kripalani.
- Predecessor: Maulana Abul Kalam Azad.
- Successor: Rajendra Prasad (1947).

Kripalani was therefore President of the Indian National Congress when India attained independence on 15 August 1947.

### Important distinction

| Event                                                        | Congress President      |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------- |
| Quit India Movement (1942)                                   | Maulana Abul Kalam Azad |
| Independence of India (15 August 1947)                       | J. B. Kripalani         |
| Congress presidency following Kripalani's resignation (1947) | Rajendra Prasad         |

Prelims Point: The Congress President at independence was J. B. Kripalani, not Jawaharlal Nehru or Mahatma Gandhi.

### 5. Role in the Constituent Assembly

- Kripalani was a member of the Constituent Assembly of India.
- Served as Chairman of the Fundamental Rights Sub-Committee.
- The Sub-Committee functioned under the larger Advisory Committee on Fundamental Rights, Minorities and Tribal and Excluded Areas, chaired by Vallabhbhai Patel.
- Contributed to discussions concerning fundamental rights and constitutional protections.

### Important Constituent Assembly committees

| Committee                                                                          | Chairman          |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------- |
| Fundamental Rights Sub-Committee                                                   | J. B. Kripalani   |
| Advisory Committee on Fundamental Rights, Minorities and Tribal and Excluded Areas | Vallabhbhai Patel |
| Drafting Committee                                                                 | B. R. Ambedkar    |
| Union Powers Committee                                                             | Jawaharlal Nehru  |
| Provincial Constitution Committee                                                  | Vallabhbhai Patel |

Prelims Point: J. B. Kripalani chaired the Fundamental Rights Sub-Committee, not the main Advisory Committee.

### 6. Resignation from Congress Presidency (1947)

- Kripalani resigned as Congress President in November 1947.
- Differences arose over the relationship between the Congress organisation and the government headed by Jawaharlal Nehru.
- Kripalani favoured a significant role for the Congress organisation in political decision-making.
- Rajendra Prasad succeeded him as Congress President.

Prelims Point: Kripalani's resignation from the Congress presidency occurred after independence in 1947.

### 7. Kisan Mazdoor Praja Party (1951)

- Founded: 1951.
- Founder: J. B. Kripalani.
- Formed after his departure from the Indian National Congress.
- Advocated democratic socialism, agrarian interests and Gandhian principles.
- Participated in India's first general elections of 1951–1952.

### Formation of Praja Socialist Party (1952)

- In 1952, the Kisan Mazdoor Praja Party (KMPP) merged with the Socialist Party.
- The merger resulted in the formation of the Praja Socialist Party (PSP).
- Important Socialist Party leaders included Jayaprakash Narayan and Acharya Narendra Deva.

Prelims Point: The Kisan Mazdoor Praja Party was founded in 1951 by J. B. Kripalani. The Praja Socialist Party emerged from a merger in 1952.

### 8. First No-Confidence Motion in the Lok Sabha (1963)

- Year: 1963.
- Moved by: J. B. Kripalani.
- Prime Minister: Jawaharlal Nehru.
- It was the first no-confidence motion moved against a government in the Lok Sabha.
- Introduced in the aftermath of the Sino-Indian War (1962).
- The motion was defeated, and Nehru's government retained the confidence of the Lok Sabha.

### Constitutional provision

- Article 75(3): The Union Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha.
- A no-confidence motion is moved only in the Lok Sabha, not the Rajya Sabha.
- Its adoption requires a simple majority of members present and voting.

Prelims Point: The first no-confidence motion in Lok Sabha was moved by J. B. Kripalani against Jawaharlal Nehru's government in 1963.

### 9. Sucheta Kripalani

- Wife of J. B. Kripalani.
- Prominent freedom fighter associated with the Quit India Movement.
- Member of the Constituent Assembly.
- Became Chief Minister of Uttar Pradesh in 1963.
- Served until 1967.
- Was the first woman Chief Minister of an Indian state.

Prelims Point: Sucheta Kripalani was the first woman Chief Minister of an Indian state, whereas Sarojini Naidu was the first woman Governor of an Indian state after independence.

## Periyar E. V. Ramasamy (1879–1973)

Erode Venkatappa Ramasamy, popularly known as Periyar and Thanthai Periyar, was an Indian social reformer, rationalist and political leader associated with the Self-Respect Movement (1925) and the development of the Dravidian movement in South India.

He is particularly remembered for his participation in the Vaikom Satyagraha (1924–1925), founding the Self-Respect Movement (1925), opposing caste discrimination and compulsory Hindi education, and establishing the Dravidar Kazhagam (1944).

### 1. Early life and political associations

- Born: 17 September 1879, Erode, Madras Presidency (present-day Tamil Nadu).
- Full name: Erode Venkatappa Ramasamy.
- Popular titles: Periyar (meaning the great one) and Thanthai Periyar (Father Periyar).
- Wife: Nagammai; later married Maniammai.
- Associated with the Indian National Congress during the early phase of his political career.
- Later became a prominent leader of the non-Brahmin and Dravidian movements.
- Advocated rationalism, social equality, women's rights and opposition to caste hierarchy.
- Died: 24 December 1973, Vellore, Tamil Nadu.

### 2. Association with the Indian National Congress

- Joined the Indian National Congress around 1919.
- Participated in Gandhi's Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–1922).
- Supported the promotion of khadi and prohibition.
- Opposed caste discrimination within society and political organisations.
- Demanded representation for non-Brahmin communities in education, public employment and political institutions.
- Left the Congress in 1925, following disagreements over caste-based representation and social reform.

Prelims Point: Periyar was initially associated with the Indian National Congress, but left it in 1925.

### 3. Vaikom Satyagraha (1924–1925)

- Year: 1924–1925.
- Place: Vaikom, Travancore (present-day Kerala).
- Objective: Secure the right of oppressed-caste communities to use public roads surrounding the Vaikom Mahadeva Temple.
- The movement began on 30 March 1924.
- Important early organisers included T. K. Madhavan, K. P. Kesava Menon and K. Kelappan.
- Periyar joined the movement in 1924 and was arrested for his participation.
- His contribution earned him the title Vaikom Veerar (Hero of Vaikom).

### Important distinction

The Vaikom Satyagraha concerned access to public roads around the temple, not initially entry into the temple itself.

| Movement                  | Year      | Main objective                              |
| ------------------------- | --------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Vaikom Satyagraha         | 1924–1925 | Access to public roads surrounding a temple |
| Guruvayur Satyagraha      | 1931–1932 | Temple-entry rights                         |
| Kalaram Temple Satyagraha | 1930      | Temple-entry rights; led by B. R. Ambedkar  |

Prelims Point: Periyar was an important participant in the Vaikom Satyagraha, but he did not initiate the movement.

### 4. Self-Respect Movement (1925)

- Founded: 1925.
- Founder: E. V. Ramasamy (Periyar).
- Region: Madras Presidency.
- Objective: Promote social equality, dignity, rationalism and opposition to caste discrimination.

### Important features

1. Opposed caste hierarchy, untouchability and Brahminical social dominance.
2. Promoted rationalism and questioned religious orthodoxy.
3. Advocated women's education, widow remarriage and women's property rights.
4. Opposed child marriage and caste restrictions on marriage.
5. Promoted Self-Respect Marriages, conducted without compulsory priestly rituals or caste-based restrictions.
6. Encouraged social equality and representation of disadvantaged communities.

### First Provincial Self-Respect Conference

- Year: 1929.
- Place: Chengalpattu, Madras Presidency.
- Adopted resolutions concerning caste equality, women's rights and social reform.

Prelims Point: The Self-Respect Movement was founded by Periyar in 1925, whereas the Justice Party was established in 1916.

### 5. Justice Party and Non-Brahmin Movement

### Justice Party (1916)

- Originated from the South Indian Liberal Federation, established in 1916.
- Important founding personalities included:
  - T. M. Nair
  - P. Theagaraya Chetty
  - C. Natesa Mudaliar
- Sought greater representation for non-Brahmin communities in education, government employment and politics.
- Became popularly known as the Justice Party, after its newspaper Justice.

### Periyar's association

- Periyar was not a founder of the Justice Party.
- Became its leader in 1938.
- In 1944, the Justice Party was reorganised as the Dravidar Kazhagam under his leadership.

Prelims Point: The Justice Party was founded in 1916, while Periyar became its leader much later.

### 6. Anti-Hindi Agitation (1937–1940)

- The Congress ministry headed by C. Rajagopalachari came to power in the Madras Presidency in 1937.
- The ministry introduced compulsory Hindi instruction in selected schools in 1938.
- Periyar became a prominent leader of the anti-Hindi agitation.
- Opposed the compulsory teaching of Hindi, arguing that it disadvantaged Tamil-speaking communities.
- Was imprisoned during the agitation.
- The compulsory Hindi policy was withdrawn in 1940, after the Congress ministry had resigned.

Prelims Point: The anti-Hindi agitation of 1937–1940 was associated with Periyar, while the compulsory Hindi policy was introduced under C. Rajagopalachari's ministry.

### 7. Dravidar Kazhagam (1944)

- Year: 1944.
- Leader: E. V. Ramasamy.
- Organisation: Dravidar Kazhagam (DK).
- Formed through the transformation of the Justice Party.
- Advocated social equality, rationalism and Dravidian identity.
- Supported the demand for a separate Dravida Nadu during this phase of the movement.
- Functioned primarily as a social and political movement rather than an electoral party.

### Formation of DMK (1949)

- Year: 1949.
- Founder: C. N. Annadurai.
- Party: Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam (DMK).
- Formed following a split from Periyar's Dravidar Kazhagam.
- Differences concerned political strategy, electoral participation and Periyar's marriage to Maniammai.

### DK vs DMK

| Feature                    | Dravidar Kazhagam                   | Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam               |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Year                       | 1944                                | 1949                                    |
| Principal leader / founder | Periyar                             | C. N. Annadurai                         |
| Origin                     | Transformation of Justice Party     | Split from DK                           |

## Vinoba Bhave (1895–1982)

Vinayak Narahari Bhave, popularly known as Acharya Vinoba Bhave, was an Indian freedom fighter, Gandhian social reformer and spiritual leader.

He is particularly remembered for being the first Individual Satyagrahi (1940) selected by Mahatma Gandhi, launching the Bhoodan Movement (1951) and promoting the Gramdan Movement.

He was a prominent advocate of Sarvodaya, meaning the welfare of all.

### 1. Early life and political associations

- Born: 11 September 1895, Gagode village, Kolaba district, Bombay Presidency (present-day Raigad district, Maharashtra).
- Original name: Vinayak Narahari Bhave.
- Popular title: Acharya Vinoba Bhave.
- Political and philosophical influence: Mahatma Gandhi.
- Joined Gandhi's ashram at Kochrab, Ahmedabad, in 1916.
- Participated in the Indian freedom struggle and Gandhian constructive activities.
- Advocated non-violence, village self-reliance, social equality and voluntary redistribution of land.
- Died: 15 November 1982, Paunar, Wardha district, Maharashtra.

### 2. Association with Mahatma Gandhi

- Met Gandhi in 1916 and became a close follower of Gandhian principles.
- Associated with the ashram movement and constructive programmes.
- Emphasised khadi, village industries, education and social service.
- Was entrusted with important Gandhian organisational responsibilities.
- Became associated with the Paunar Ashram, near Wardha, Maharashtra.

Prelims Point: Vinoba Bhave was a prominent Gandhian follower, especially associated with the Individual Satyagraha and post-independence land reform movements.

### 3. Individual Satyagraha (1940)

- Launched: October 1940.
- Initiated by: Mahatma Gandhi.
- First Individual Satyagrahi: Vinoba Bhave.
- Second Individual Satyagrahi: Jawaharlal Nehru.
- Third Individual Satyagrahi: Brahma Datt.
- First Satyagraha speech: 17 October 1940, at Paunar, Maharashtra.

### Objective

- Protest against the British government's decision to involve India in the Second World War without consulting Indians.
- Assert the right to express opposition to the war through non-violent means.
- Unlike the Non-Cooperation and Civil Disobedience movements, it was not initially organised as a nationwide mass movement.

Prelims Point: Gandhi selected Vinoba Bhave as the first Individual Satyagrahi in 1940, not Jawaharlal Nehru.

### 4. Bhoodan Movement (1951)

- Launched: 18 April 1951.
- Place: Pochampally, Nalgonda district, Hyderabad State (present-day Telangana).
- Founder: Vinoba Bhave.
- Meaning of Bhoodan: Gift of land.
- Objective: Persuade landowners to voluntarily donate land for redistribution to landless families.

### Origin of the movement

- During a visit to Pochampally, Vinoba Bhave met landless families seeking agricultural land.
- Local landowner Vedre Ramachandra Reddy offered approximately 100 acres of land.
- This donation marked the beginning of the Bhoodan Movement.
- Vinoba subsequently travelled across India on foot to encourage voluntary land donations.

Prelims Point: The Bhoodan Movement began at Pochampally in present-day Telangana, not at Wardha or Champaran.

### 5. Gramdan Movement

- Meaning: Gift of a village or collective dedication of village land to the community.
- Developed from the Bhoodan Movement in the early 1950s.
- Sought to encourage collective village-level arrangements for land ownership and management.
- Emphasised cooperation, social equality and village self-governance.
- Mangroth village, in present-day Uttar Pradesh, is generally identified as the first Gramdan village in 1952.

### Bhoodan vs Gramdan

| Feature             | Bhoodan                                                  | Gramdan                                   |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Meaning             | Gift of land                                             | Gift of village land to the community     |
| Beginning           | 1951                                                     | 1952                                      |
| Initial association | Pochampally, Telangana                                   | Mangroth, Uttar Pradesh                   |
| Main objective      | Redistribute voluntarily donated land to landless people | Promote community-based land arrangements |
| Principal leader    | Vinoba Bhave                                             | Vinoba Bhave                              |

Prelims Point: Bhoodan concerned voluntary land donations, whereas Gramdan extended the principle to village-level collective arrangements.

### 6. Sarvodaya Movement

- Meaning: Welfare or upliftment of all.
- The concept was strongly associated with Mahatma Gandhi and later developed through the work of Vinoba Bhave.
- Gandhi used the title Sarvodaya for his Gujarati rendering of John Ruskin's Unto This Last.
- Vinoba promoted Sarvodaya through voluntary land redistribution, non-violence and village self-reliance.

### Important principles

- Non-violence and peaceful social transformation.
- Voluntary sharing of resources.
- Welfare of all sections of society.
- Decentralisation and village self-governance.
- Cooperation instead of class conflict.

Prelims Point: Vinoba Bhave's Bhoodan and Gramdan movements were associated with Sarvodaya and Gandhian constructive work.

### 7. Sarva Seva Sangh and Constructive Work

- Sarva Seva Sangh: Established in 1948 as an organisation associated with the Gandhian Sarvodaya movement.
- Vinoba Bhave was a major figure in the movement.
- Worked to promote rural reconstruction, voluntary social service and non-violent change.
- The Bhoodan and Gramdan programmes became important activities of the wider Sarvodaya movement.

### 8. Important Writings and Publications

### 9. Awards and Recognition

- 1958: Received the Ramon Magsaysay Award for Community Leadership.
- 1983: Awarded the Bharat Ratna posthumously.
- Died: 15 November 1982, at Paunar, Maharashtra.

Prelims Point: Vinoba Bhave received the Bharat Ratna in 1983, after his death in 1982.

## M. N. Roy (1887–1954)

Manabendra Nath Roy, originally known as Narendra Nath Bhattacharya, was an Indian revolutionary, communist leader, political thinker and founder of the Radical Humanist movement.

He is particularly remembered for his association with revolutionary nationalism in Bengal, founding the Mexican Communist Party (1919), participating in the Communist International (Comintern), helping establish the Communist Party of India at Tashkent (1920), and developing the philosophy of Radical Humanism.

### 1. Early life and political associations

- Born: 21 March 1887, Arbelia, Bengal Presidency (present-day West Bengal).
- Original name: Narendra Nath Bhattacharya.
- Popular name: M. N. Roy (Manabendra Nath Roy).
- Early political association: Revolutionary nationalist movement in Bengal.
- Later became associated with international communism.
- Subsequently rejected orthodox communism and developed Radical Humanism.
- Died: 25 January 1954, Dehradun.

### 2. Association with Revolutionary Nationalism

- Initially associated with revolutionary nationalist activities in Bengal.
- Had connections with the Jugantar revolutionary network.
- Worked with revolutionary leader Bagha Jatin (Jatindranath Mukherjee).
- Participated in efforts to obtain foreign assistance and arms for an uprising against British rule during the First World War.
- These activities were connected with the wider Indo-German Conspiracy (1914–1917).
- Left India in 1915 in search of foreign assistance for the revolutionary movement.
- Subsequently travelled through several countries, including the United States and Mexico.

Prelims Point: M. N. Roy's political career began in revolutionary nationalism, before his association with communism.

### 3. Mexican Communist Party (1919)

- Year: 1919.
- Country: Mexico.
- Principal founder: M. N. Roy.
- Roy helped establish a communist organisation in Mexico that became known as the Mexican Communist Party.
- It was among the earliest communist parties established outside Soviet Russia.
- His activities in Mexico brought him into contact with the international communist movement.

Prelims Point: M. N. Roy was associated with the establishment of the Mexican Communist Party in 1919, before his role in organising Indian communists at Tashkent.

### 4. Communist International (Comintern)

- Comintern established: 1919, Moscow.
- Principal leader associated with its establishment: Vladimir Lenin.
- Objective: Promote international communist revolutionary movements.
- M. N. Roy participated in the Second Congress of the Communist International (1920).
- Developed an important debate with Lenin regarding colonial and national liberation movements.

### Roy's Supplementary Theses (1920)

- Lenin presented his Theses on the National and Colonial Questions.
- Roy prepared Supplementary Theses on the National and Colonial Questions.
- Roy emphasised the importance of revolutionary movements among workers and peasants in colonial countries.
- He was more sceptical than Lenin about relying on the colonial nationalist bourgeoisie.

Prelims Point: M. N. Roy's Supplementary Theses on the National and Colonial Questions are associated with the Second Comintern Congress (1920).

### 5. Communist Party of India — Tashkent (1920)

- Date: 17 October 1920.
- Place: Tashkent (present-day Uzbekistan).
- Important leader: M. N. Roy.
- A communist organisation known as the Communist Party of India was formed by Indian revolutionaries and communists abroad.
- Other associated personalities included Abani Mukherji and Evelyn Trent.

### Important distinction: 1920 vs 1925

| Year | Event                                                     | Important association                                                                |
| ---- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1920 | Formation of an Indian communist organisation at Tashkent | M. N. Roy and other émigré communists                                                |
| 1925 | Communist Conference at Kanpur                            | Organisational formation of CPI within India; Satyabhakta was an important organiser |

The Kanpur Communist Conference (December 1925) is commonly treated in Indian communist party history as the founding conference of the CPI within India.

Prelims Point: Remember both Tashkent (1920) and Kanpur (1925). They refer to different stages in the development of the communist movement in India.

### 6. Communist Movement in India

- Roy encouraged the spread of Marxist and communist ideas among Indian workers and intellectuals.
- Associated with international communist efforts to organise anti-colonial movements.
- Influenced early communist discussions on Indian nationalism and the working-class movement.
- His writings circulated among Indian communists even while he was living abroad.

### Important early communist personalities

| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |

### 7. Return to India and Kanpur Conspiracy Case

- Roy returned secretly to India in 1930.
- Was arrested by British authorities in 1931.
- Tried in connection with the Kanpur Bolshevik Conspiracy Case, originally instituted in 1924.
- Sentenced to imprisonment and released in 1936.

### Kanpur Bolshevik Conspiracy Case (1924)

- The British government prosecuted communist leaders for allegedly conspiring to overthrow British rule.
- Important accused included S. A. Dange, Muzaffar Ahmad, Shaukat Usmani and Nalini Gupta.
- M. N. Roy was named among the accused but was abroad during the original proceedings.

Prelims Point: The Kanpur Bolshevik Conspiracy Case (1924) must not be confused with the Kanpur Communist Conference (1925).

### 8. Association with the Indian National Congress

- After his release from prison in 1936, Roy joined the Indian National Congress.
- Advocated socialist and radical political ideas.
- Formed the League of Radical Congressmen in 1939.
- Developed differences with the Congress leadership over the Second World War.

### Position during the Second World War

- Roy regarded the struggle against fascism as internationally important.
- Supported the Allied war effort against fascist powers.
- This differed from the Congress leadership's insistence that India's participation in the war should be linked to political freedom.
- Left the Congress in 1940.

Prelims Point: M. N. Roy supported the anti-fascist Allied war effort during the Second World War, unlike the Congress leadership's prevailing position.

### 9. Radical Democratic Party (1940)

- Founded: 1940.
- Founder: M. N. Roy.
- Organisation: Radical Democratic Party (RDP).
- Supported democracy, individual liberty, rationalism and opposition to fascism.
- Later became associated with Roy's movement away from Marxism towards Radical Humanism.
- The party was dissolved in 1948.

Prelims Point: The Radical Democratic Party (1940) was founded by M. N. Roy, not Jayaprakash Narayan or Acharya Narendra Deva.

### 10. Radical Humanism

- Developed by M. N. Roy during the later phase of his political career.
- Also known as New Humanism.
- Emphasised human freedom, rational thought, scientific inquiry and ethical responsibility.
- Rejected authoritarian political systems.
- Criticised both orthodox Marxism and religious dogmatism.
- Favoured decentralised democracy and the active participation of individuals in public affairs.

### Evolution of Roy's political thought

Prelims Point: M. N. Roy's later philosophy was Radical Humanism, not orthodox Marxism.

- Attended the Second Round Table Conference (1931) in London.
- Opposed separate electorates on the grounds that they would deepen communal divisions.
- Helped establish the Congress Nationalist Party in 1934, following disagreements over the Communal Award and related political questions.&#x20;
