# Emergence of New Forces in the Indian National Movement (1920s–Early 1930s)

UPSC Civil Services Prelims — Modern Indian History Study Notes

The 1920s witnessed the emergence of new political forces in India's freedom struggle: socialist and communist organisations, student and youth movements, trade unions, peasant movements and revolutionary groups increasingly influenced by socialism.

These developments followed the suspension of the Non-Cooperation Movement in 1922 and were influenced by the Russian Revolution of 1917, growing working-class organisation and dissatisfaction with existing nationalist strategies.

The notes below cover the important developments in your textbook, including their continuation into the early 1930s.

## 1. Emergence of Socialist and Marxist Ideas

### 1.1 Background

- The Russian Revolution of 1917 and the establishment of Soviet power encouraged the spread of socialist and Marxist ideas.
- Socialists increasingly emphasised the connection between anti-imperialism and social justice.
- They argued that political independence should also address exploitation by landlords and capitalists.
- Jawaharlal Nehru and Subhas Chandra Bose emerged as prominent representatives of the left-wing tendency within Congress during the late 1920s.
- Younger nationalists increasingly demanded Purna Swaraj (complete independence) rather than limited constitutional reforms.

### 1.2 Communist Party of India

| Year | Development                                                                               |
| ---- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1920 | Communist organisation established at Tashkent by M.N. Roy, Abani Mukherji and associates |
| 1924 | Kanpur Bolshevik Conspiracy Case                                                          |
| 1925 | Communist Conference at Kanpur; foundation of the CPI formalised in India                 |
| 1929 | Meerut Conspiracy Case                                                                    |

Important distinction: The Tashkent formation in 1920 and the Kanpur conference of 1925 are separate milestones in the history of Indian communism.

### 1.3 M.N. Roy

- Full name: Manabendra Nath Roy.
- Associated with the establishment of the communist organisation at Tashkent in 1920.
- Participated in the international communist movement.
- Associated with the Communist International (Comintern).

### 1.4 Kanpur Bolshevik Conspiracy Case, 1924

Important accused included:

- S.A. Dange
- Muzaffar Ahmad
- Shaukat Usmani
- Nalini Gupta

The British government prosecuted communist activists over allegations of conspiracy to overthrow colonial rule.

### 1.5 Meerut Conspiracy Case, 1929

- British authorities arrested and prosecuted communist leaders, trade unionists and labour organisers.
- The case concerned alleged attempts to organise revolutionary activity and undermine British authority.
- It became an important episode in the repression of the communist and labour movements.

Prelims distinction: Kanpur Bolshevik Conspiracy Case — 1924; Meerut Conspiracy Case — 1929.

## 2. Youth and Student Movements

- Student leagues and conferences expanded during the 1920s.
- Youth organisations encouraged political participation, anti-imperialism and demands for complete independence.
- Jawaharlal Nehru presided over the All Bengal Students' Conference in 1928.
- Youth mobilisation also supported revolutionary organisations and the growth of socialist ideas.

### Naujawan Bharat Sabha, 1926

- Established in Lahore, Punjab.
- Closely associated with Bhagat Singh.
- Intended to mobilise students, workers, peasants and other young people.
- Advocated anti-imperialism, secularism and political awakening.
- Opposed communal organisations and encouraged religious tolerance.

Bhagat Singh and Sukhdev were also associated with the Lahore Students' Union.

## 3. Growth of Trade Unionism

### 3.1 All India Trade Union Congress (AITUC)

Established: 1920

| Position / association  | Personality         |
| ----------------------- | ------------------- |
| First President         | Lala Lajpat Rai     |
| First General Secretary | Dewan Chaman Lal    |
| Important supporter     | Bal Gangadhar Tilak |

- AITUC became a major all-India organisation of the labour movement.
- It provided a common platform for organised workers and trade unions.
- The labour movement increasingly interacted with nationalist and socialist politics.

### 3.2 Major labour struggles during the 1920s

| Centre     | Industry                      |
| ---------- | ----------------------------- |
| Bombay     | Textile mills                 |
| Jamshedpur | Tata Iron and Steel Works     |
| Kharagpur  | Railway workshops             |
| Madras     | Buckingham and Carnatic Mills |

- The Bombay textile workers' strike of 1928 involved approximately 1.5 lakh workers and continued for several months.
- A major wave of strikes occurred in 1928.

### 3.3 First May Day celebration in India

- Year: 1923
- Place: Madras
- Associated leader: Singaravelu Chettiar (M. Singaravelar)
- Significance: An important early public celebration of International Workers' Day in India.

## 4. Peasant Movements

Peasant movements in the 1920s addressed high rents, indebtedness, eviction, oppressive tenancy conditions and restrictions on customary rights.

| Region                         | Important demands / developments                         |
| ------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| United Provinces               | Lower rents, tenancy reform, protection against eviction |
| Rajasthan                      | Opposition to agrarian exploitation                      |
| Bombay and Madras Presidencies | Agrarian grievances in ryotwari areas                    |
| Rampa / Godavari Agency        | Tribal resistance to colonial restrictions               |
| Bardoli, Gujarat               | Revenue-related peasant Satyagraha, 1928                 |

### Bardoli Satyagraha, 1928

- Leader: Vallabhbhai Patel.
- Region: Bardoli, Surat district, Gujarat.
- Immediate issue: Increase in land revenue assessment.
- Peasants organised a disciplined campaign against the enhanced revenue demand.
- The movement strengthened Patel's standing as a nationalist leader.

Prelims distinction: The Bardoli decision of February 1922 concerned the suspension of mass civil disobedience after Chauri Chaura. The Bardoli Satyagraha of 1928 was a separate peasant movement.

## 5. Caste and Social Movements

The 1920s also witnessed movements challenging caste hierarchy, social exclusion and unequal political representation.

### Self-Respect Movement, 1925

- Founded by E.V. Ramasamy (Periyar).
- Opposed caste hierarchy and Brahminical social dominance.
- Promoted social equality, rationalism and self-respect.
- Challenged discriminatory customs and supported women's rights.

### B.R. Ambedkar and the Mahars

- Ambedkar organised movements against caste discrimination and untouchability.
- The Mahad Satyagraha (1927) asserted the right of untouchable communities to access public water resources.
- The Bahishkrit Hitakarini Sabha (1924) worked for the advancement of socially excluded communities.

## 6. Revival of Revolutionary Activity After 1922

### 6.1 Background

- Several revolutionary groups had suspended their activities or participated in Non-Cooperation.
- The withdrawal of the movement in February 1922 disappointed sections of nationalist youth.
- Some rejected both the Swarajists' legislative strategy and the No-Changers' constructive programme.
- Revolutionary organisations were revived, particularly in northern India and Bengal.

### 6.2 Important influences

1. Russian Revolution, 1917.
2. Spread of Marxist and socialist ideas.
3. Expansion of trade unionism.
4. Dissatisfaction with the withdrawal of Non-Cooperation.
5. Revolutionary literature and journals.

### Important publications

| Publication                | Association                         |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Bandi Jiwan                | Sachindra Nath Sanyal               |
| Pather Dabi                | Sarat Chandra Chattopadhyay         |
| The Philosophy of the Bomb | Bhagwati Charan Vohra               |
| Revolutionary              | Publication associated with the HRA |

Revolutionary journals included Atmasakti, Sarathi and Bijoli.

## 7. Hindustan Republican Association (HRA)

### 7.1 Formation

- Year: 1924
- Important organising centre: Kanpur
- Founders / organisers: Ram Prasad Bismil, Sachindra Nath Sanyal and Jogesh Chandra Chatterjee.
- Objective: Overthrow British rule through armed revolution and establish a republican political system.

The HRA envisaged a Federal Republic of the United States of India, based on adult franchise.

### 7.2 Kakori Train Action, 1925

Date: 9 August 1925

- HRA revolutionaries intercepted a train near Kakori, close to Lucknow.
- They seized government money being transported by railway.
- The action was intended to secure funds for revolutionary activities.
- The subsequent British crackdown severely weakened the organisation.

### Four revolutionaries executed in the Kakori Case

| Revolutionary        | Association                                |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Ram Prasad Bismil    | HRA leader                                 |
| Ashfaqulla Khan      | HRA revolutionary                          |
| Rajendra Nath Lahiri | HRA revolutionary                          |
| Roshan Singh         | Revolutionary convicted in the Kakori case |

Prelims distinction: All four were executed in 1927. Chandrashekhar Azad was not among those executed.

## 8. Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA)

### 8.1 Reorganisation in 1928

- Date: September 1928.
- Place: Feroz Shah Kotla, Delhi.
- HRA was reorganised as the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA).
- The organisation adopted socialism as an explicit objective.
- Chandrashekhar Azad played a leading role.

Important participants included Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev, Bhagwati Charan Vohra, Shiv Verma, Bejoy Kumar Sinha and Jaidev Kapur.

### 8.2 HRA and HSRA — comparison

| Feature                    | HRA                                              | HSRA                                             |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Formation / reorganisation | 1924                                             | 1928                                             |
| Important location         | Kanpur                                           | Feroz Shah Kotla, Delhi                          |
| Major early leaders        | Bismil, Sanyal, Jogesh Chandra Chatterjee        | Azad, Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev and others           |
| Political objective        | Overthrow colonial rule and establish a republic | National liberation and socialist transformation |
| Major activities           | Kakori action                                    | Saunders assassination, Assembly bomb action     |

The adoption of socialism did not mean that HSRA immediately abandoned armed methods. Its leaders increasingly recognised the importance of mass political mobilisation.

## 9. Important Revolutionary Events, 1928–1931

### 9.1 Death of Lala Lajpat Rai and Saunders Assassination

- Lala Lajpat Rai led protests against the Simon Commission in Lahore in October 1928.
- He was injured during a police lathi charge and died on 17 November 1928.
- On 17 December 1928, revolutionaries including Bhagat Singh, Rajguru and Chandrashekhar Azad participated in the action in which police officer J.P. Saunders was killed.
- Saunders was killed in an operation intended to avenge Lajpat Rai's death.

Important distinction: The revolutionaries had intended to target police officer James A. Scott, whom they held responsible for the lathi charge, but Saunders was killed instead.

### 9.2 Central Legislative Assembly Bomb Case, 1929

Date: 8 April 1929

- Revolutionaries: Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt.
- Place: Central Legislative Assembly, Delhi.
- Bills protested: Public Safety Bill and Trade Disputes Bill.
- The bombs were intended as a political demonstration rather than an attempt to cause mass casualties.
- Bhagat Singh and Dutt deliberately allowed themselves to be arrested.
- They intended to use the trial to publicise revolutionary ideas.

### 9.3 Lahore Conspiracy Case

- Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev and Rajguru were tried in connection with revolutionary activities, including the Saunders killing.
- Revolutionaries protested discriminatory prison conditions.
- They demanded better treatment and recognition as political prisoners.

### 9.4 Jatin Das

- Participated in a hunger strike while imprisoned in Lahore.
- Died on 13 September 1929, after a 63-day hunger strike (often described as dying on its 64th day).
- His death generated widespread public sympathy.

### 9.5 Chandrashekhar Azad

- Important leader of the HRA and HSRA.
- Died during a confrontation with police at Alfred Park, Allahabad, on 27 February 1931.

### 9.6 Execution of Bhagat Singh, Rajguru and Sukhdev

- Date: 23 March 1931.
- Place: Lahore Central Jail.
- All three were executed in connection with the Lahore Conspiracy Case.

## 10. Revolutionary Activity in Bengal

### 10.1 Major organisations

- Anushilan Samiti
- Yugantar
- Chittagong revolutionary group led by Surya Sen

Several Bengal revolutionaries maintained connections with Congress and Swarajist political activity.

Following the death of C.R. Das in 1925, Bengal Congress politics witnessed factional divisions.

### 10.2 Gopinath Saha, 1924

- Attempted to assassinate Calcutta Police Commissioner Charles Tegart.
- Another man, Ernest Day, was mistakenly killed.
- Gopinath Saha was subsequently executed.

## 11. Chittagong Armoury Raid, 1930

### 11.1 Leadership and organisation

- Date: 18 April 1930.
- Place: Chittagong, Bengal Presidency (present-day Bangladesh).
- Leader: Surya Sen, popularly known as Masterda.
- Organisation: Indian Republican Army — Chittagong Branch.

Important associates included:

- Anant Singh
- Ganesh Ghosh
- Lokenath Bal

### 11.2 Objectives

The revolutionaries planned to:

1. Capture the principal armouries in Chittagong.
2. Seize weapons.
3. Disrupt railway communication.
4. Cut telephone and telegraph connections.
5. Challenge British authority through an organised armed uprising.

Surya Sen proclaimed a provisional revolutionary government during the operation.

### 11.3 Fate of Surya Sen

- Arrested in February 1933.
- Executed on 12 January 1934.

## 13. Ideological Evolution of Revolutionary Nationalism

### 13.1 From individual revolutionary actions to socialism

Bhagat Singh and his associates increasingly argued that political independence alone would not eliminate exploitation.

Their objectives expanded to include:

- Ending imperialist rule.
- Ending exploitation by landlords and capitalists.
- Organising workers and peasants.
- Establishing a socialist social order.
- Promoting secularism and rational thought.

### 13.2 Bhagat Singh's political ideas

- Influenced by Marxism and socialist thought.
- Emphasised the importance of mass movements rather than relying solely on individual revolutionary actions.
- Opposed communal politics.
- Advocated criticism, independent thinking and rationalism.
- Viewed revolution as a transformation of the political and socioeconomic order.

### 13.3 Important writings and publications

| Work                       | Author / association        |
| -------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| The Philosophy of the Bomb | Bhagwati Charan Vohra       |
| Why I Am an Atheist        | Bhagat Singh                |
| Bandi Jiwan                | Sachindra Nath Sanyal       |
| Pather Dabi                | Sarat Chandra Chattopadhyay |

The revolutionary movement's ideological evolution is important for Prelims because HRA/HSRA nationalism was increasingly connected with socialism, secularism and the organisation of workers and peasants.

## Section I: Socialist and Communist Movements

### Q1. Consider the following statements regarding the emergence of socialist ideas in India during the 1920s:
