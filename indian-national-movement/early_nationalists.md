# Early (Pre-Congress) Nationalists in Indian National Movement

## Mahadev Govind Ranade (1842–1901)

Mahadev Govind Ranade (M. G. Ranade) was an Indian social reformer, judge, economist and early nationalist leader. He was a prominent figure in the social reform movement in Maharashtra and a **political mentor of Gopal Krishna Gokhale**.

He is particularly associated with the **Poona Sarvajanik Sabha (1870)**, **Prarthana Samaj** and Indian National Social Conference (1887).

### 1. Early life

- Born: 18 January 1842, Niphad, Nashik district, Maharashtra.
- Studied at Elphinstone College, Bombay.
- Served in the judicial services of British India.
- Appointed a judge of the Bombay High Court in 1893.
- Known for advocating social reform, economic development and gradual political change.
- Died: 16 January 1901.

Prelims Point: M. G. Ranade was not the founder of Prarthana Samaj. It was founded by Atmaram Pandurang in Bombay in 1867.

### 3. Role in Prarthana Samaj

- Founded: 1867, Bombay.
- Founder: Atmaram Pandurang.
- Important leaders: M. G. Ranade and R. G. Bhandarkar.
- Advocated monotheism and reform of Hindu religious and social practices.
- Supported women's education, widow remarriage and opposition to caste discrimination.
- Opposed child marriage and rigid caste restrictions.
- Emphasised social reform through education and persuasion.

### 4. Poona Sarvajanik Sabha (1870)

- Established in 1870 at Pune.
- Important early leaders included Ganesh Vasudeo Joshi (Sarvajanik Kaka) and M. G. Ranade.
- Sought to represent public grievances before the British administration.
- Submitted petitions and representations concerning taxation, administration and economic conditions.
- Became an important political association before the formation of the Indian National Congress in 1885.

Prelims Point: The Poona Sarvajanik Sabha was established in 1870, whereas the Indian National Congress was founded in 1885.

### 5. Indian National Social Conference (1887)

- Founded: 1887.
- Important founders: M. G. Ranade and Raghunath Rao.
- Established to promote social reforms alongside the growing national political movement.
- Its meetings were initially held alongside the annual sessions of the Indian National Congress.
- Addressed issues such as child marriage, widow remarriage, women's education and caste restrictions.
- It was a social reform organisation, not a political wing of the Congress.

### 6. Economic ideas and contributions

- Advocated industrialisation and economic development in India.
- Supported government assistance for industries and infrastructure.
- Emphasised agricultural improvement, banking and credit facilities.
- Criticised excessive dependence on agriculture.
- Helped establish the Industrial Association of Western India (1890).
- Considered economic development essential for India's progress.

Important UPSC distinctions:

- M. G. Ranade: Prarthana Samaj leader, Poona Sarvajanik Sabha, Indian National Social Conference and economic reform.
- Atmaram Pandurang: Founder of Prarthana Samaj (1867).
- Ganesh Vasudeo Joshi: Important early organiser of Poona Sarvajanik Sabha (1870).
- Gopal Krishna Gokhale: Political disciple of Ranade; founder of the Servants of India Society (1905).
- Bal Gangadhar Tilak: Contemporary nationalist who differed with Ranade over the priority and methods of social reform.
