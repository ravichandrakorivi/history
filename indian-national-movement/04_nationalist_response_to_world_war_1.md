
## First World War and Nationalist Response in India (1914–1918)

## 1. First World War and India's Response

### 1.1 Basic Facts

| Feature                     | Important fact                                                                     |
| --------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Duration of First World War | 1914–1918                                                                          |
| Allied Powers               | Britain, France, Russia and others; Italy joined in 1915, USA in 1917              |
| Central Powers              | Germany, Austria-Hungary, Ottoman Empire and Bulgaria                              |
| Viceroy at outbreak         | Lord Hardinge II                                                                   |
| Viceroy from 1916           | Lord Chelmsford                                                                    |
| India's position            | Participated as part of the British Empire, without an independent Indian decision |

### 1.2 Three Broad Nationalist Responses

| Group                       | Response                                                                                           |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Moderates                   | Supported British war efforts, regarding assistance as a duty and hoping for political concessions |
| Extremists, including Tilak | Generally supported British war efforts while pressing for self-government                         |
| Revolutionaries             | Attempted to exploit Britain's wartime difficulties to organise an armed uprising                  |

Prelims distinction: Extremist nationalists and revolutionary nationalists did not necessarily adopt the same approach to the war.

### 1.3 Wartime Conditions in India

- Increased taxation and government expenditure.
- Rising prices and economic hardship.
- Growing dissatisfaction with British colonial policies.
- Disillusionment with the limited Morley–Minto Reforms of 1909.
- Increased nationalist demands for self-government.

## 2. Home Rule League Movement (1916–1918)

### 2.1 Origin and Objective

- Inspired by the **Irish Home Rule Movement**.
- Demanded **self-government for India within the British Empire**.
- Led principally by **Bal Gangadhar Tilak** and **Annie Besant**.
- **Two separate Home Rule Leagues** were established in **1916**.
- The Leagues sought to maintain political agitation throughout the year, unlike the Congress's largely annual-session-centred activities.

Prelims trap: The Home Rule Movement initially demanded self-government within the British Empire, not immediate complete independence.

### 2.2 Factors Responsible for the Movement

1. Dissatisfaction with the Morley–Minto Reforms of 1909.
2. Wartime inflation, taxation and economic hardship.
3. Growing belief that political concessions required sustained public pressure.
4. Wartime rivalries between European powers weakening the prestige of imperial rule.
5. Tilak's return to active politics after his release in June 1914.
6. Annie Besant's efforts to organise a campaign for Indian self-government.

### 2.3 Tilak's League vs Annie Besant's League

| Feature                        | Tilak's Home Rule League                                                  | Annie Besant's Home Rule League                   |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Formation                      | April 1916                                                                | September 1916                                    |
| Founder                        | Bal Gangadhar Tilak                                                       | Annie Besant                                      |
| Important founding centre      | Belgaum                                                                   | Madras                                            |
| Headquarters                   | Poona                                                                     | Adyar, Madras                                     |
| Territorial coverage           | Maharashtra excluding Bombay city, Karnataka, Central Provinces and Berar | Rest of India, including Bombay city              |
| Important associates           | N.C. Kelkar, Joseph Baptista                                              | George Arundale, B.P. Wadia, C.P. Ramaswami Aiyar, Sir S. Subramania Iyer |
| Organisation                   | More closely organised                                                    | Comparatively loosely organised                   |

### 2.4 Additional Demands of Tilak's League

Apart from self-government, Tilak's League advocated:

- Formation of linguistic states.
- Education through vernacular languages.

### 2.5 Important Publications

| Publication            | Associated personality |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------- |
| New India              | Annie Besant           |
| Commonweal             | Annie Besant           |
| Kesari (Marathi)       | Bal Gangadhar Tilak    |
| The Mahratta (English) | Bal Gangadhar Tilak    |

### 2.6 Methods of the Home Rule Leagues

- Public meetings, lectures and conferences.
- Political education through libraries and reading rooms.
- Pamphlets, posters, newspapers and illustrated postcards.
- Political classes for students.
- Plays and songs.
- Local branches and participation in public affairs.

The movement also expanded political activity into areas such as Gujarat and Sindh.

## 3. Government Repression of the Home Rule Movement

| Event                         | Important fact                                                       |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Restrictions on students      | Students in Madras were prohibited from attending political meetings |
| Restrictions on Tilak         | Barred from entering Punjab and Delhi                                |
| Proceedings against Tilak     | Government action against him was overturned by the High Court       |
| Internment of Annie Besant    | June 1917                                                            |
| Associates interned           | B.P. Wadia and George Arundale                                       |
| Protest by S. Subramania Iyer | Renounced his knighthood                                             |
| Release of Annie Besant       | September 1917                                                       |

- Besant's internment generated nationwide protests.
- Tilak advocated passive resistance against repression.
- The agitation strengthened Besant's political standing.

Prelims trap: Sir S. Subramania Iyer renounced his knighthood in protest against the internment of Annie Besant and her associates.

## 4. Decline and Later Developments of the Home Rule Movement

### 4.1 Reasons for Decline

1. Weak organisational structure.
2. Communal disturbances during 1917–1918.
3. Moderate leaders becoming less active following the Montagu Declaration.
4. Differences between Moderates and Extremists over passive resistance.
5. Annie Besant's uncertain response to the Montagu–Chelmsford reforms.
6. Tilak's departure for England in September 1918.
7. Growing popularity of Gandhi's methods of mass mobilisation.

### 4.2 Tilak and the Chirol Libel Case

- Valentine Chirol wrote Indian Unrest.
- Tilak filed a libel case against Chirol.
- Tilak travelled to England in 1918 in connection with the case.

### 4.3 Home Rule League After 1919

- In 1920, Mahatma Gandhi accepted the presidency of the All India Home Rule League.
- The organisation was renamed Swarajya Sabha.

Prelims distinction: Gandhi did not establish the Home Rule Leagues in 1916. His association with the presidency of the All India Home Rule League came in 1920.

## 5. Moderate–Extremist Reunion

### 5.1 Important Chronology

| Year | Event                                                                     |
| ---- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1907 | Surat Split                                                               |
| 1915 | Congress constitutional changes facilitated the readmission of Extremists |
| 1916 | Reunion consolidated at the Lucknow Congress session                      |

### 5.2 Factors Facilitating Reunion

- Both groups recognised the disadvantages of continued division.
- Annie Besant and Tilak actively promoted reconciliation.
- Tilak expressed support for constitutional agitation and distanced himself from revolutionary violence.
- The deaths of Gopal Krishna Gokhale and Pherozshah Mehta in 1915 reduced opposition to reunion.

### 5.3 Lucknow Congress Session (1916)

| Feature                | Fact                                        |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Year                   | 1916                                        |
| Place                  | Lucknow                                     |
| Congress president     | Ambica Charan Majumdar                      |
| Important developments | Moderate–Extremist reunion and Lucknow Pact |

Prelims trap: The Congress–Muslim League agreement and the reunion of Moderates and Extremists were two distinct developments associated with the same Lucknow session.

## 6. Muslim League's Changing Political Position

### 6.1 Background

The All-India Muslim League, established in 1906, gradually moved toward cooperation with Congress during the years preceding the Lucknow Pact.

### 6.2 Important Factors

| Development                                                                 | Significance                                                                                                |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| British attitude toward Turkey during the Italo-Turkish War and Balkan Wars | Increased dissatisfaction among sections of Indian Muslims                                                  |
| Annulment of Bengal Partition in 1911                                       | Disappointed groups that had supported the partition                                                        |
| Aligarh university question                                                 | British refusal to grant the proposed university all-India affiliating powers alienated some Muslim leaders |
| Younger nationalist leadership                                              | Favoured cooperation with Congress                                                                          |
| Wartime repression                                                          | Increased anti-colonial sentiment                                                                           |

### 6.3 Important Chronology

| Year | Development                                                                                     |
| ---- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1912 | Calcutta session reflected a growing willingness to cooperate with other political groups       |
| 1913 | Muslim League adopted self-government as an objective, subject to safeguarding Muslim interests |
| 1916 | Congress–Muslim League Lucknow Pact                                                             |

### 6.4 Publications and Personalities

| Publication | Personality                 |
| ----------- | --------------------------- |
| Al-Hilal    | Maulana Abul Kalam Azad     |
| The Comrade | Maulana Muhammad Ali Jauhar |

Both publications faced British government suppression.

Prelims distinction: The Muslim League's move toward cooperation with Congress preceded the Lucknow Pact of 1916.

## 7. Lucknow Pact (1916)

### 7.1 Basic Facts

| Feature                 | Fact                                                                           |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Year                    | 1916                                                                           |
| Place                   | Lucknow                                                                        |
| Parties                 | Indian National Congress and All-India Muslim League                           |
| Congress president      | Ambica Charan Majumdar                                                         |
| Important personalities | Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Muhammad Ali Jinnah and other Congress and League leaders |
| Nature                  | Joint political agreement on constitutional reforms                            |

### 7.2 Important Constitutional Demands

| Demand                      | Proposed provision                                                                              |
| --------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Self-government             | British government should declare its intention to grant self-government at an early date       |
| Legislative councils        | Expansion of central and provincial councils with elected majorities                            |
| Legislative powers          | Greater powers for the councils                                                                 |
| Council tenure              | Five years                                                                                      |
| Executive councils          | At least half the members to be Indians, elected by elected members of the legislative councils |
| Secretary of State's salary | To be paid from the British treasury rather than Indian revenues                                |
| Muslim representation       | Fixed representation in central and provincial legislatures                                     |
| Separate electorates        | Accepted by Congress                                                                            |

### 7.3 Muslim Representation

- Muslims were proposed to receive one-third representation in the Imperial Legislative Council.
- Provincial representation was to follow agreed allocations rather than one uniform percentage.
- Separate electorates were accepted as part of the joint constitutional scheme.

### 7.4 Important Distinctions

- Separate Muslim electorates were introduced under the Indian Councils Act, 1909, not by the Lucknow Pact.
- Congress accepted separate electorates in the Lucknow Pact of 1916.
- The pact was a political agreement, not legislation.

## 8. Montagu Declaration (20 August 1917)

### 8.1 Important Facts

| Feature               | Fact                                                       |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Date                  | 20 August 1917                                             |
| Announced by          | Edwin Samuel Montagu                                       |
| Position              | Secretary of State for India                               |
| Place of announcement | British House of Commons                                   |
| Viceroy               | Lord Chelmsford                                            |
| Nature                | British government policy statement                        |
| Objective             | **Progressive realisation of responsible government in India** |

### 8.2 Main Features

The declaration announced the policy of:

1. Increasing Indian participation in every branch of administration.
2. Gradually developing self-governing institutions.
3. Progressively realising responsible government in India as an integral part of the British Empire.

### 8.3 Indian Objections

- No definite time frame was specified.
- The British government retained control over the pace and extent of constitutional reforms.

### 8.4 Important Constitutional Distinctions

| Feature                     | Morley–Minto Reforms, 1909                               | Montagu Declaration, 1917        | Government of India Act, 1919                            |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Nature                      | Legislation                                              | Policy declaration               | Legislation                                              |
| Responsible government      | Not established or promised as the object of the reforms | Adopted as an eventual objective | Limited provincial ministerial responsibility introduced |
| Separate Muslim electorates | Introduced                                               | Not introduced                   | Continued                                                |
| Provincial dyarchy          | Not introduced                                           | Not introduced                   | Introduced                                               |

Prelims trap: The Montagu Declaration did not itself introduce dyarchy. Provincial dyarchy was introduced under the Government of India Act, 1919.

## 9. Revolutionary Response During the First World War

### 9.1 Ghadar Movement

| Feature             | Fact                                                                 |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Formation           | 1913                                                                 |
| Organisation        | Pacific Coast Hindustan Association, popularly known as Ghadar Party |
| Principal centre    | San Francisco, USA                                                   |
| Founding president  | Sohan Singh Bhakna                                                   |
| Important organiser | Lala Hardayal                                                        |
| Headquarters        | Yugantar Ashram                                                      |
| Publication         | Ghadar                                                               |
| Objective           | Overthrow British rule through armed revolution                      |

- Ghadar revolutionaries returned to India during the war to organise an uprising.
- They attempted to secure support from Indian soldiers.
- A major uprising was planned for February 1915.
- The plans were discovered and suppressed.

Important personalities: Kartar Singh Sarabha, Vishnu Ganesh Pingle and Rash Behari Bose.

### 9.2 Lahore Conspiracy Cases

- Associated with the attempted Ghadar uprising of 1915.
- Several revolutionaries were prosecuted.
- Kartar Singh Sarabha and Vishnu Ganesh Pingle were executed in 1915.

### 9.3 Berlin Committee (1914)

| Feature           | Fact                                                             |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Formation         | 1914                                                             |
| Place             | Berlin                                                           |
| Also known as     | Indian Independence Committee                                    |
| Important leaders | Virendranath Chattopadhyaya, Chempakaraman Pillai and associates |
| Objective         | Obtain German assistance for an anti-British uprising            |

### 9.4 Provisional Government of India, Kabul (1915)

| Feature             | Fact                                                    |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Established         | 1 December 1915                                         |
| Location            | Kabul, Afghanistan                                      |
| President           | Raja Mahendra Pratap                                    |
| Prime Minister      | Maulana Barkatullah                                     |
| Important associate | Ubaidullah Sindhi (Home Minister)                                       |
| Objective           | Secure international assistance for Indian independence |

### 9.5 Bagha Jatin

- Real name: Jatindranath Mukherjee.
- Associated with the Jugantar revolutionary network.
- Involved in attempts to obtain foreign arms during the war.
- Died in September 1915 following an encounter near Balasore.

### 9.6 Singapore Mutiny (1915)

- Occurred in February 1915.
- Involved Indian soldiers stationed in Singapore.
- Took place during the First World War.
- Was suppressed by British authorities with assistance from allied forces.

Prelims distinction: The Singapore Mutiny and the planned Ghadar uprising occurred in the same year but were not the same incident.

| Feature | Fact                                                      |

| Year    | 1915                                                      |
| Context | First World War                                           |
| Purpose | Suppression of revolutionary and anti-colonial activities |
| Nature  | Extraordinary wartime security legislation                |

The legislation provided for extraordinary powers, including restrictions on suspects and special procedures for specified offences.

### Important Comparison

## 12. Ottoman Empire and Indian Muslim Political Opinion

- The Ottoman Empire entered the First World War on the side of the Central Powers in 1914.
- The Ottoman Sultan claimed the position of Caliph.
- The war generated concern among sections of Indian Muslim political opinion.
- The Ali brothers—Maulana Muhammad Ali and Maulana Shaukat Ali—were prominent Muslim political figures during this period.

Important distinction: The organised all-India Khilafat agitation developed mainly after the First World War, from 1919. It should not be confused with the Ottoman Empire's entry into the war in 1914.
