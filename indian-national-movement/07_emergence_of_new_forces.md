# Emergence of New Forces: Socialism, Youth Power and Trade Unionism

## 1. Background: Political Developments During the 1920s

Two developments significantly influenced Indian politics during this period:

- **Russian Revolution (1917):** Encouraged the spread of socialist and communist ideas.
- **Withdrawal of the Non-Cooperation Movement (1922):** Contributed to dissatisfaction among sections of the nationalist youth and the revival of revolutionary activities.

Consequently, the national movement witnessed the growing influence of:

- Socialists and communists.
- Trade unions and organised labour.
- Youth organisations.
- Revolutionary nationalist organisations.

The emerging left-wing tendency increasingly connected **political independence with economic and social equality**.

## 2. Socialist and Communist Movements

### 2.1 Spread of socialist ideas

Socialism influenced Indian nationalism through its emphasis on:

- Opposition to imperialism.
- Rights of workers and peasants.
- Opposition to economic exploitation.
- Redistribution of economic and political power.

**Jawaharlal Nehru and Subhas Chandra Bose** were prominent representatives of the emerging left wing of the Indian National Congress.

Both supported complete independence rather than Dominion Status.

### 2.2 Communist Party of India

| Year | Development |
|---|---|
| 1920 | Communist organisation established at Tashkent, associated with M.N. Roy and Abani Mukherji |
| 1924 | Kanpur Bolshevik Conspiracy Case |
| 1925 | Communist conference at Kanpur |
| 1929 | Meerut Conspiracy Case |

**Important distinction:** The 1920 Tashkent initiative and the 1925 Kanpur conference represent different stages in the development of the Indian communist movement.

### 2.3 M.N. Roy

- Prominent Indian communist revolutionary.
- Associated with the establishment of the communist organisation at Tashkent in 1920.
- Participated in the **Communist International (Comintern)**.

### 2.4 Kanpur Bolshevik Conspiracy Case, 1924

The British government prosecuted communist activists for their alleged revolutionary activities.

Prominent accused included:

- S.A. Dange.
- Muzaffar Ahmed.
- Shaukat Usmani.
- Nalini Gupta.

### 2.5 Meerut Conspiracy Case, 1929

- Involved the prosecution of communist and trade union activists.
- Reflected the colonial government's efforts to suppress communist influence within the labour movement.

### 2.6 Workers' and Peasants' Parties

These organisations emerged during the 1920s to:

- Spread socialist and Marxist ideas.
- Organise workers and peasants.
- Connect economic struggles with anti-imperialist politics.

**Prelims distinction:** Workers' and Peasants' Parties were political organisations, whereas AITUC was a trade union federation.

## 3. Trade Union Movement

### 3.1 All India Trade Union Congress (AITUC), 1920

| Feature | Fact |
|---|---|
| Established | 1920 |
| First session | Bombay |
| First president | Lala Lajpat Rai |
| First general secretary | Dewan Chaman Lal |
| Nature | All-India trade union federation |

AITUC was an important development in the organisation of India's industrial working class.

### 3.2 Important labour developments

| Year | Development |
|---|---|
| 1918 | Madras Labour Union established |
| 1920 | AITUC established |
| 1923 | First May Day celebration in India, at Madras |
| 1926 | Trade Unions Act |
| 1929 | Trade Disputes Act |

**Madras Labour Union (1918):** Associated with B.P. Wadia; generally recognised as one of India's earliest organised modern trade unions.

**First May Day celebration (1923):** Associated with Singaravelar in Madras.

### 3.3 Trade Unions Act, 1926

Important provisions:

- Provided for registration of trade unions.
- Gave registered trade unions legal recognition.
- Provided specified immunities from civil and criminal liability in connection with legitimate trade union activities.

**Important distinction:** Registration under the Act was voluntary; it did not make membership of a trade union compulsory.

### 3.4 Trade Disputes Act, 1929

The Act introduced restrictions and procedures concerning industrial disputes, including certain strikes.

It was criticised by sections of the labour movement.

## 4. Youth Movement

### 4.1 Naujawan Bharat Sabha, 1926

| Feature | Fact |
|---|---|
| Established | 1926 |
| Place | Lahore |
| Prominent founder | Bhagat Singh |
| Nature | Open political youth organisation |

### Objectives

- Mobilise youth against imperialism.
- Promote secularism.
- Oppose communalism.
- Encourage political awareness among workers and peasants.

**Important distinction:** Naujawan Bharat Sabha was an open political organisation. The HSRA was a clandestine revolutionary organisation.

## 5. Revolutionary Nationalism During the 1920s

### 5.1 Hindustan Republican Association (HRA), 1924

| Feature | Fact |
|---|---|
| Established | 1924 |
| Place | Kanpur |
| Important founders | Ram Prasad Bismil, Sachindranath Sanyal and Jogesh Chandra Chatterjee |
| Objective | Overthrow British rule and establish a federal republic |

The HRA envisaged a **Federal Republic of the United States of India** based on adult franchise.

### 5.2 Kakori Train Action, 1925

- **Date:** 9 August 1925.
- **Place:** Kakori, near Lucknow.
- **Organisation:** HRA.
- **Purpose:** Seizure of government railway funds for revolutionary activities.

Four revolutionaries were executed in connection with the case:

1. Ram Prasad Bismil.
2. Ashfaqulla Khan.
3. Rajendra Nath Lahiri.
4. Roshan Singh.

### 5.3 Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA), 1928

- The HRA was reorganised in **September 1928**.
- The meeting took place at **Feroz Shah Kotla, Delhi**.
- Socialism became an explicit organisational objective.

Prominent revolutionaries included:

- Chandrashekhar Azad.
- Bhagat Singh.
- Sukhdev.
- Bhagwati Charan Vohra.

### 5.4 Saunders Assassination, 1928

- **Date:** 17 December 1928.
- **Place:** Lahore.
- **Organisation:** HSRA.
- Bhagat Singh, Rajguru, Chandrashekhar Azad and Sukhdev were associated with the operation.
- Scott was the intended target; Saunders was killed.

The action followed the death of Lala Lajpat Rai after injuries sustained during an anti-Simon Commission protest.

**James A. Scott was the intended target, but J.P. Saunders was killed.**

### 5.5 Central Legislative Assembly Bombing, 1929

| Feature | Fact |
|---|---|
| Date | 8 April 1929 |
| Place | Delhi |
| Revolutionaries | Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt |
| Organisation | HSRA |
| Political context | Public Safety Bill and Trade Disputes Bill |

The revolutionaries intended the action as a political demonstration and deliberately submitted to arrest.

### 5.6 Revolutionary prisoners

**Jatin Das**

- Participated in a hunger strike demanding better treatment of political prisoners.
- Died on **13 September 1929**.

**Bhagat Singh, Rajguru and Sukhdev**

- Executed on **23 March 1931**.

**Chandrashekhar Azad**

- Died on **27 February 1931** at Allahabad.

## 6. Revolutionary Movement in Bengal

### 6.1 Chittagong Armoury Raid, 1930

| Feature | Fact |
|---|---|
| Date | 18 April 1930 |
| Place | Chittagong |
| Leader | Surya Sen |
| Popular name | Masterda |
| Organisation | Indian Republican Army, Chittagong Branch |

The revolutionaries attempted to:

- Capture government armouries.
- Disrupt railway and communication networks.
- Challenge colonial authority.

**Surya Sen was executed in January 1934.**

### 6.2 Important women revolutionaries

| Revolutionary | Important association |
|---|---|
| Pritilata Waddedar | Pahartali European Club attack, 1932 |
| Kalpana Dutt | Chittagong revolutionary movement |
| Shanti Ghosh | Comilla District Magistrate assassination, 1931 |
| Suniti Chaudhury | Comilla District Magistrate assassination, 1931 |
| Bina Das | Attempted to shoot Bengal Governor Stanley Jackson, 1932 |

## 7. Ideological Transformation of Revolutionary Nationalism

The revolutionary movement gradually developed a stronger socialist orientation.

**Bhagat Singh's political ideas** increasingly emphasised:

- Complete independence.
- Socialism.
- Organisation of workers and peasants.
- Opposition to communalism.
- Ending economic exploitation.

### Important revolutionary writings

| Work | Associated author |
|---|---|
| *Bandi Jiwan* | Sachindranath Sanyal |
| *The Philosophy of the Bomb* | Bhagwati Charan Vohra |
| *Why I Am an Atheist* | Bhagat Singh |
| *Pather Dabi* | Sarat Chandra Chattopadhyay |
